# Allergy & Immune Health Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: Allergy is not "weak immunity" and not "overpowered immunity" — it is a repeated **mistaken-identity incident** in which the immune system mislabels a harmless protein as an enemy. The real management spine is not "boosting immunity" but four things: **recognize it (diagnosis), avoid it (avoidance), quiet the symptoms (medication), re-educate the immune system (immunotherapy)** — plus one life-saving bottom line: **in anaphylaxis, epinephrine (adrenaline) is the only answer.**
>
> **How to read this volume**: ① build the correct immune picture (mechanism) → ② the nose (allergic rhinitis) → ③ the mouth (food allergy and the IgG scam) → ④ life and death (anaphylaxis) → ⑤ the skin (hives and eczema) → ⑥ auditing the "immunity-boosting" supplement market → ⑦ red flags and the master falsification list.

## ① Mechanism face: allergy is "mistaken identity", not "weakness"

**The immune system has exactly one job: tell friend from foe (FACT · immunology consensus)**

The immune system's task is to recognize "non-self" and decide whether to open fire. It fields two main weapon classes:

- **Antibodies such as IgG/IgM plus phagocytes**: these fight bacteria and viruses — this is the "immunity" most people imagine;
- **IgE**: whose original evolutionary assignment points at **parasites** (large pathogens such as worms).

The tragedy of allergy is that this IgE heavy weapon **aimed at the wrong target**. Pollen proteins, dust-mite proteins, peanut proteins — harmless by themselves — get registered by IgE as parasite-grade enemies (**sensitization**: after first exposure the body produces allergen-specific IgE, which stands sentinel on the surface of mast cells). On the **next** exposure to the same protein, hundreds of IgE molecules are cross-linked at once, and mast cells dump histamine and other inflammatory mediators into tissue within minutes — itchy nose, sneezing, hives (wheals), airway swelling, vasodilation... That is the full chain from "mistaken identity" to "open fire" (FACT · mechanism consensus).

Hence two iron laws of allergic reactions: **the first exposure usually causes no reaction** (it only sensitizes); **reactions strike from the second exposure onward**. Understanding this explains why "I ate it before and was fine" does not mean forever fine, and why the early-introduction prevention logic (see ③) works.

**Allergy vs intolerance vs "sensitivity": a strict three-way split (FACT · guideline framing, WHO/EAACI aligned)**

Everyday language blends these into one pot, but they are handled completely differently:

| Dimension | Allergy | Intolerance | "Sensitivity" (lay usage) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Immune mechanism (mostly IgE-mediated) | **Non-immune** (e.g., enzyme deficiency) | No unified definition |
| Dose | Tiny amounts can trigger | Usually dose-related (a little is fine; a lot is misery) | Arbitrary |
| Danger | Can be fatal (anaphylactic shock) | Unpleasant but not lethal | — |
| Objective testing | Skin prick / specific IgE can assist | e.g., lactose hydrogen breath test | Essentially none |

**Classic case: cow's milk.** Milk **allergy** = the immune system attacks milk protein (common in infants; can cause eczema, blood in stool, shock). Milk **lactose intolerance** = insufficient small-intestine lactase; lactose ferments in the gut (bloating, diarrhea, gas); common in adults. The former requires strict avoidance plus shock preparedness; the latter only needs lactase replacement or dose control — **completely different handling**. Conflating them means either needless hunger or real risk. Most commercial "food intolerance tests" (IgG) measure neither true allergy nor true intolerance (see the ③ special chapter).

**Falsifying the "stronger immunity is always better" frame (CONTRADICTED → rebuilt as MODEL)**

Folk thinking imagines immunity as a one-dimensional slider: "strong" = healthy, "weak" = sick. The real immunological picture is **multi-dimensional balance**: how accurately it identifies (specificity), how hard it fires (magnitude), whether it stops when it should (regulation), and whether it spares self (tolerance). Allergy and autoimmune disease are precisely what immune **oversensitivity / misidentification** looks like — one mistakes harmless proteins for enemies, the other mistakes the body's own tissue for enemies. Therefore:

- The problem in allergic individuals is not immunity that is "not strong enough" but an IgE pathway tuned too high (TH2 skew, relatively insufficient regulatory T-cell function — mechanism detail at FACT·relayed level);
- "Boosting immunity" style supplementation aims in the wrong direction — for allergic people, "immune-enhancing" supplements may pour fuel on the fire (see ⑥).

The "hygiene hypothesis" (less childhood microbial exposure → higher allergy rates) is one mainstream candidate framework for the rise of modern allergy (MODEL · epidemiological evidence supports the direction; mechanism not settled). It points the same way: what immunity needs is **education and balance**, not mindless reinforcement.

## ② Allergic rhinitis face: not "yet another cold", and no need to just endure it

**How common (FACT · verified)**

- Global framing: allergic rhinitis affects roughly **10%–30%** of people (long-standing WAO/ARIA framing, FACT·relayed).
- China framing: self-reported prevalence in major cities **rose significantly** across 2005→2011 (Wang XD et al., Allergy 2016;71:1170-80, multi-center telephone sampling in 11 cities, PMID 26948849) — the year-by-year percentages are body-text data of the original paper and are not re-quoted here unverified (UNKNOWN · pending direct verification); the verified conclusion is the rising trend itself.

**Telling it apart from a cold (FACT · clinical consensus, one table)**

| Feature | Common cold (viral nasopharyngitis) | Allergic rhinitis |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | Most **self-resolve in 7–10 days** | **>2 weeks** without closing, or year-round recurrence |
| Nasal discharge | Clear early → thickens to yellow-green | **Persistently watery** |
| Itchy eyes | Uncommon | **Common** (naso-ocular reflex; rubbing, blinking) |
| Sneezing | Scattered | **Paroxysmal clusters** (seven or eight in a row) |
| Fever / sore throat | Common | Absent |
| Pattern | None | Patterned: mornings, specific seasons (pollen), specific environments (dust-mite bedding / pets) |

Mnemonic: **"a cold has an end date; allergy has a pattern."** A child who "catches a cold four times a month, two to three weeks each time," with itchy eyes and sneezing fits, most likely does not have recurrent colds but rhinitis — the right door is ENT / allergy (immunology) clinic, not repeated cold medicine.

**Dust mites and mite control: which evidence is strong, which is weak (FACT → honestly graded)**

Dust mites feed on shed human skin flakes, love warmth and humidity (mattresses, pillows and sofas are their main nests), and are the **leading perennial allergen** in China and worldwide (FACT·relayed, sensitization-spectrum studies agree).

Evidence grading of mite-control measures (key point: **not all "anti-mite" measures are equal**):

- **Supported by real-world effect**: **hot-water laundering** (wash and dry bedding regularly at ≥55–60°C — kills mites and removes allergen); controlling indoor humidity (relative humidity below 50% is unfavorable to mites); reducing carpets and plush accumulation in bedrooms. All three physically kill or remove mites; mechanism and effect line up (FACT·relayed).
- **Evidence downgraded**: **mite-proof bedding covers / tightly woven encasements** — the updated Cochrane systematic review (Nurmatov et al., Allergy 2012; PMID 22103686) concluded that mite-proof bedding showed **no demonstrated clinical benefit** for perennial allergic rhinitis symptoms (as a standalone measure). Users need not be discouraged, but it cannot be expected to solve the problem alone; "bought a cover = did mite control" is a common error.
- **Demoted**: "one spritz of anti-mite spray and done" — see ⑦ falsification item 5.

**Medication ladder: nasal corticosteroids are first line — don't fear the word "steroid" (FACT · ARIA 2024–2025 revision, PMID 41324154)**

The ARIA-EAACI guideline 2024–2025 revision (Part I intranasal therapy; Part II oral and ocular) and domestic rhinology consensus point the same way:

1. **First line = intranasal corticosteroid sprays** (e.g., budesonide / mometasone furoate class): effective across the board for congestion, runny nose, sneezing, nasal itch; first choice for moderate-to-severe disease. **"Steroid fear" correction**: the local dose of a nasal spray is in the nanogram scale with very low systemic bioavailability (mostly orders of magnitude below 1% of oral steroid exposure, FACT·relayed); used as directed, systemic risk is far smaller than the media image of "steroids." Two usage points are frequently done wrong — **onset takes hours to days** (not a rescue drug; use it regularly and persistently), and **aim at the outer sidewall of the nose, not the septum** (less local irritation).
2. **Second-generation oral / intranasal antihistamines** (loratadine, cetirizine, azelastine nasal spray, etc.): fast onset, good for sneezing and itch, weaker for congestion; **first-generation antihistamines (chlorpheniramine class) have well-documented drowsiness and cognitive effects and are not first choice** — the generation gap is not a "new-old marketing gimmick" (FACT·relayed).
3. **Leukotriene receptor antagonists**: additive value in those with coexisting asthma (physician's weighing).
4. **Saline nasal irrigation**: a foundational adjunct with an excellent safety record, friendly in pregnancy and childhood; it can sit at the base of every ladder rung.

One-line principle: **escalate stepwise driven by symptoms; after regular-use control, de-escalate only on a clinician's assessment** — not on-off use of "spray when miserable, stop when fine."

**Allergen immunotherapy (AIT): the only path that changes the disease course (FACT · guideline level)**

Medication "presses symptoms"; immunotherapy (subcutaneous injection / sublingual tablets) "**re-educates the immune system**" — through regular, incrementally rising allergen exposure it turns the IgE response down and induces tolerance. It is currently the **only intervention that can change the natural course of allergy** and that lowers the risk of new allergen sensitization and of rhinitis progressing to asthma (FACT·relayed, WAO/ARIA aligned). Three honest disclosures:

- **Course of 3–5 years** — this is a marathon, not a "one summer and done" quick shot; quitting midway forfeits the gains;
- **It has risks**: local and even systemic allergic reactions can occur after dosing; subcutaneous injections must be given at properly equipped facilities with emergency capability, with observation before leaving;
- **It has indication boundaries**: evidence is most mature for airborne allergens such as dust mites and pollens; routine desensitization to food allergens is not a standard recommendation (fast-moving research; defer to clinician assessment).

## ③ Food allergy face: from "source of anxiety" to "most can make peace"

**The cast list: the eight major allergens (+ the ninth) (FACT · FDA framing, directly verified)**

The US FALCPA law defines **eight major** food allergens: **milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans**; the 2021 FASTER Act added **sesame** as the ninth (mandatory labeling from 2023). Note: **peanut is not a tree nut** (it is a legume), but it is usually listed alongside them. Allergen spectra differ across populations (e.g., sesame / shellfish weigh more in parts of Asia), but this list covers the great majority of food allergy.

The burden of food allergy in children is clear: for milk allergy, referral-population prevalence is about **2%–3%** (Skripak 2007, below); overall food-allergy rates are rising in multi-country surveillance (FACT·relayed).

**Natural-tolerance history: hard data that relieves anxiety (FACT · directly verified, abstracts checked this round)**

To the parental dread "is my child's milk / egg allergy a life sentence?", cohort data answer rather gently:

- **Milk**: the Johns Hopkins cohort of 807 children with IgE-mediated milk allergy (Skripak et al., JACI 2007; PMID 17935766): **19% tolerant by age 4, 42% by 8, 64% by 12, 79% by 16**. Most children make peace with milk, with doors still opening into adolescence — so children with milk allergy should have **regular follow-up re-evaluation**, not a casual lifetime sentence.
- **Egg**: the same team's natural-history study (Savage et al., JACI 2007; PMID 18073126): Kaplan-Meier estimates of tolerance **4% at age 4, 12% at 6, 37% at 10, 68% at 16**. Slower, but most still achieve tolerance.
- The counter-examples, stated honestly: natural tolerance rates for **peanut, tree nuts, and shellfish** are far lower, and persistence into adulthood is the norm — the comfort "most grow out of it" **applies only to the milk-and-egg family**; applying it to peanut can kill (see ⑦ falsification item 2).

**Diagnosis: screening is not diagnosis, and there is only one gold standard (FACT · Muraro 2014 EAACI guideline, PMID 24909706)**

- **Skin prick test, specific IgE (sIgE)** = **screening / adjunct**: high sensitivity but limited specificity — a positive only says "the body holds IgE targeted at it" (a sensitized state), **not that eating it causes reactions**. The population contains many people who "test positive yet have eaten it for decades without incident."
- **Oral food challenge (OFC)**: incremental feeding under emergency-capable, physician-supervised conditions — the **diagnostic gold standard**. Whether a challenge is needed, and when, is weighed by allergy specialists.
- **Self-test kits are no substitute**: "one drop of blood for a hundred allergens," "hair analysis," "bioresonance" — none has diagnostic validity (see ⑦ item 3).

**Special chapter: IgG "food intolerance testing" — one of the biggest pseudoscience businesses in the checkup market (CONTRADICTED · guideline-level rejection)**

Checkup providers and wellness markets commonly sell "90- or 100-item food intolerance tests" for thousands of yuan, with a rainbow "avoid list" that makes families treat food like an enemy. The truth has three layers:

1. **Mechanism layer**: rising IgG/IgG4 antibodies are a **marker of normal food exposure** — for whatever you have eaten, the gut immune system may have produced IgG. It is the footprint of "having met the world," not evidence of "poison." Interpreting IgG positivity as "allergy / intolerance" is like saying "I know this person, therefore this person is an enemy" (FACT · immunology consensus).
2. **Authoritative position**: the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI) task-force position paper's title is its conclusion — **"testing for IgG4 against foods is not recommended as a diagnostic tool"** (Stapel et al., Allergy 2008; PMID 18489614, directly verified this round). National allergy societies line up behind this.
3. **Consequence layer**: expensive "avoid lists" drive unnecessary dietary restriction (nutritional risk in children), miss the real cause, and close a placebo loop of "I avoided the foods and do feel better." **True lactose intolerance has nothing to do with IgG** (it is an enzyme deficiency). One line: **to investigate food allergy, use the IgE route and clinician assessment; the IgG report belongs in the shredder.**

**Prevention update: "delay allergenic foods to prevent allergy" is falsified — the LEAP trial reversal (FACT · RCT directly verified, abstract checked this round)**

Older guidelines once advised high-allergy-risk infants to **delay** introducing peanut and other readily allergenic foods. The 2015 LEAP randomized trial (Du Toit et al., NEJM 2015;372:803-813, PMID 25705822, n=640 infants at high risk due to severe eczema and/or egg allergy) **reversed the old guidance**:

- Skin-prick **negative** group (n=530): peanut allergy prevalence at age 5 — avoidance arm **13.7%** vs regular-consumption arm **1.9%** (P<0.001);
- Skin-prick **positive** group (n=98): **35.3%** vs **10.6%** (P=0.004);
- Rates of serious adverse events did not differ significantly between the arms.

In one line: **in high-risk infants, early regular peanut consumption cut peanut allergy at age 5 by roughly seventy to eighty percent (relative risk)**. Guidelines subsequently shifted to "early introduction, diverse complementary foods." Mind the boundary: high-risk infants (severe eczema / existing egg allergy) **should be clinician-assessed before introduction** (screening first may be needed, with the first introduction in a setting that can handle reactions) — "early introduction" does not mean a family-run dose-challenge.

## ④ Anaphylaxis face: everyone should read this chapter once

**Recognition: fast, multi-system, severe (FACT · WAO 2020 guideline, Cardona et al., World Allergy Organ J 2020; PMID 33204386, directly verified this round)**

The typical picture of anaphylaxis: within **minutes to half an hour** of exposure, skin/mucosal signs (widespread hives, angioedema) **combined with** at least one of — respiratory involvement (throat swelling with hoarse voice, stridor, breathing difficulty), circulatory involvement (dizziness, pallor, confusion, low blood pressure), or severe gastrointestinal symptoms (violent vomiting). It can also start with no visible rash and go straight to respiratory / circulatory collapse (more dangerous). Those with known allergy (especially peanut / tree nuts / insect venom) must treat every exposure as suspect.

**Intramuscular epinephrine = the only life-saving drug; the golden window is minutes (FACT · WAO 2020)**

- First-line and only cause-directed treatment is **epinephrine injected into the outer thigh** (mid-thigh, front-outer aspect; it can be injected through clothing); it directly reverses airway swelling and low blood pressure, **the earlier the better** — hesitation equals letting the disease run unchecked;
- Antihistamines and steroids are all **supporting cast** in the emergency hierarchy — see the fatal error below;
- After injection **call 120 immediately** (mainland China's medical emergency number; elsewhere your local emergency number — translator calibration), and go to hospital for observation even if things improve.

**The fatal error: "take an antihistamine and watch for a bit" (CONTRADICTED)**

Antihistamines can only ease itchy skin and hives; **they have almost no reversing effect on throat swelling or low blood pressure**, and oral ones act slowly — using an antihistamine as the shock rescue drug is like fighting a house fire with a wet towel. The common script in fatal anaphylactic shock: reaction → family gives antihistamine → "let's watch a little longer" → airway closes completely. The correct sequence is always: **epinephrine (if available) → call emergency → lie flat with legs raised (comfortable position if breathing is hard) → never leave the person alone**.

**Biphasic reactions: better ≠ safe (FACT·relayed; empirical literature consistent)**

A subset of patients relapse **hours after the first episode settles (a window that can extend to 24–72 hours)** (biphasic reaction; e.g., Langlois et al., Clin Transl Allergy 2022; PMID 35734270 reports higher biphasic risk with high baseline tryptase, asthma, and loss of consciousness). This is the hard reason why "feeling better still means medical observation" — riding it out at home leaves the second wave outside the rescue circle.

**Amplifiers: co-factors that turn "usually fine" into "a big event" (FACT·relayed)**

- **Exercise + food**: some people are fine eating the food alone and exercising alone, but react when they **exercise within 2–4 hours after a specific food** (food-dependent, exercise-induced anaphylaxis);
- **Female hormone cycle**: the reaction threshold may drop around menstruation;
- Others: alcohol, NSAIDs, acute infection, emotional stress — all tailwinds that lower the threshold.

**Accessibility: the on-the-ground reality, stated honestly (FACT·relayed + UNKNOWN)**

The **epinephrine auto-injector** standard in Europe and North America has long lacked formal approval for the mainland China market (as of this volume's writing, UNKNOWN · policy moves fast; confirm current availability with your doctor at visit). The realistic domestic path: **those with a history of severe allergic reactions obtain injectable epinephrine by physician prescription and learn emergency use** (some households keep ampoules and syringes under medical guidance), while **using it immediately + calling 120** always takes priority over any self-stocked plan. Nursery and school settings should be informed in writing and trained.

## ⑤ Skin allergy face: hives, contact dermatitis, and the "allergic constitution" label

**Urticaria: six weeks is the watershed (FACT · international urticaria guideline 2022, Zuberbier et al., Allergy 2022; PMID 34536239, directly verified this round; a 2026 edition, PMID 41649409, now coexists)**

- **Acute urticaria** (<6 weeks): common triggers are infections (especially in children, mostly viral), drugs, foods — fast in, fast out; mostly self-limited.
- **Chronic urticaria** (≥6 weeks, wheals weekly or every other day): the key cognitive reversal — **in most cases no external allergen is found**. The drivers are largely autoimmune factors (autoantibodies against the IgE receptor) and a lowered mast-cell "irritability threshold," **not "eating something dirty."** So the most common behavioral error in chronic urticaria is "endless food avoidance + repeated allergen testing": large-scale avoidance has no supporting evidence and risks malnutrition; the evidence-supported path is standard antihistamine therapy (dose escalation as clinician-assessed), keeping a **symptom diary** instead of holding food trials, and when needed stepping up to omalizumab-level therapy (physician's decision).
- **Psyche–immune face** (MODEL): stress and sleep deprivation can lower mast-cell thresholds and worsen chronic urticaria — the "more anxious, itchier; itchier, more anxious" loop has clinically consistent evidence. Managing stress is not mysticism; it is part of treatment.

**Contact dermatitis: the delayed-type "allergy" — a completely different mechanism (FACT·relayed)**

A wrist rash under a metal watch strap, a swollen scalp face after hair dye — this is **type IV delayed hypersensitivity** (T-cell mediated, appearing 1–3 days after contact, unrelated to IgE). Classic culprits: nickel, fragrances, dyes, rubber accelerators. Diagnosis is by **patch testing** (not IgE testing); management is avoiding the trigger (e.g., jewelry in surgical steel / pure gold). It shares the word "allergy" with immediate-type allergy, but mechanism, testing, and handling are three separate lines.

**Atopic dermatitis and the "allergic constitution" label (interlinked, not repeated)**

The full mechanism–skincare–treatment picture of atopic dermatitis is canonically covered in the skin volume and is not repeated here; only two things are anchored: ① atopic dermatitis is the entry station of the "atopic constitution" — infants and toddlers with eczema are a high-risk group for food allergy and allergic rhinitis (the atopic-march directional link is FACT·relayed; individual causality not settled); ② the everyday phrase "I'm allergic-constituted" mostly has never been diagnosed — **atopy in medicine specifically means a constitution tendency with evidence of IgE sensitization**, not a loose term for "thin skin / flushes easily." Label misuse pushes many non-allergic problems into an allergy narrative and bypasses the true cause.

## ⑥ The "immunity" chaos face: dissecting the supplement sales pitch

**"Immunity-boosting" supplements = a marketing concept with no metric and no evidence (CONTRADICTED as marketed)**

No line in any checkup report reads "immunity high/low." Immunoglobulin levels, blood counts, lymphocyte subsets are **adjacent diagnostic parameters in specific contexts**, not an "immunity score"; "catching colds easily = low immunity" lacks evidence in most healthy adults (2–4 colds a year is the population norm). Thus "boosting immunity" becomes an **unfalsifiable, unverifiable** marketing universal — which is exactly its commercial value. What immunity actually needs is **balance and education** (see ①), not "reinforcement."

**Probiotics: an honest downgrade of the evidence tier (MODEL, actively downgraded)**

There is a visible gap between probiotic research heat and evidence strength: for **specific strains and specific indications** (e.g., shortening some acute infectious diarrhea, preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea) systematic-review support exists (FACT·relayed); but the generalized claim "take probiotics to boost immunity / prevent allergy" has inconsistent evidence and limited effect sizes — several authoritative societies hold a reserved attitude toward routine use in healthy people. Money spent on year-round probiotics buys far less than sleep, exercise, and diet structure. For allergy prevention: probiotics in mothers during pregnancy / early infancy are **not** a standard preventive recommendation (guideline framing · FACT·relayed).

**"Pediatric tuina massage boosts immunity" [TCM·Traditional]**

Pediatric tuina is a traditional Chinese medicine external therapy with a practice tradition and user experience in child care and some functional symptoms (e.g., functional constipation, sleep soothing); this volume labels it honestly as a [TCM·Traditional] practice. What must be separated is this: the mechanism claim that tuina "raises immunity in the modern immunological sense" currently has **no** support meeting modern evidence grading. Parents may choose it as a parenting-care practice on tradition and experience, but **it must not replace vaccination, nor should it be expected to change allergic constitution**. Separate labeling is not disparagement — it lets tradition be tradition and evidence be evidence.

**The only evidence-backed "immunity face" is four plain things (FACT · interlinked with existing canons)**

1. **Sleep**: sleep deprivation measurably weakens immune responses (vaccine antibody responses drop, with experimental evidence) — sleep canon (sleep volume);
2. **Regular exercise**: moderate, regular exercise lowers respiratory infection incidence (J-curve model);
3. **Vaccines**: the most direct, highest-efficiency "immune education" — letting the immune system rehearse under safe conditions (child-health volume · vaccine face);
4. **A balanced diet**: shortfalls in protein, vitamin D and the like genuinely impair immunity — but that is "filling a deficiency," not a "more is better" reinforcement logic; blind high-dose supplementation can itself harm (vitamin / trace-element canon interlink).

## ⑦ Red flags + falsification face

**Red flags (act on appearance; watchful waiting is the wrong option)**

1. **Any combination of "skin symptoms + breathing difficulty / hoarse voice / throat tightness / dizziness-fainting / violent vomiting"** = suspected anaphylaxis: inject epinephrine immediately if available, **call 120 at the same time**, lie flat with legs raised (self-chosen comfortable position if respiratory distress), never leave the person alone;
2. **Every suspected exposure** in those with known peanut / tree nut / insect-venom allergy — re-exposure reactions can escalate; do not self-soothe with "last time wasn't that bad";
3. Food allergy **with uncontrolled asthma** — asthma is one of the strongest independent risk factors for death in anaphylaxis (WAO 2020); uncontrolled wheeze must be pressed down first;
4. Chronic urticaria with **lip / tongue / eyelid angioedema** or a throat-tightening sensation;
5. Allergic rhinitis with **recurrent cough-wheeze / night cough >4 weeks** — rhinitis and asthma are two exits of one airway; evaluate for asthma (resp volume interlink);
6. An infant with **repeated vomiting / blood in stool / worsening eczema after a specific food** — food allergy to be ruled out; see pediatrics / allergy specialist before deciding the avoidance spectrum.

**Falsification face (folk claims × evidence verdicts; counter-example history is never deleted)**

1. **"Allergy means weak immunity; take some tonics to boost it" — CONTRADICTED**. Allergy is immune **misrecognition and imbalance**, not "weakness"; blind "immune boosting" aims the wrong way (see ①⑥).
2. **"My child's allergy will fix itself with age" — half true; use only with limits**. Milk (79% tolerant by 16) and egg (68% by 16) indeed resolve mainly via natural tolerance (Skripak/Savage 2007, directly verified); but peanut, tree nuts and shellfish have low tolerance rates and can persist for life — offering this comfort to peanut-allergy families while relaxing avoidance is dangerous.
3. **"One drop of blood / a pinch of hair / a bioresonance machine tests a hundred allergens" — CONTRADICTED**. No diagnostic validity; even proper screening (prick / sIgE) needs clinician interpretation against history — positive ≠ allergic.
4. **"IgG testing = checking food allergy / intolerance" — CONTRADICTED · guideline level**. The EAACI 2008 position paper explicitly does not recommend it (Stapel, PMID 18489614); IgG positivity means "you ate it," not "you're allergic."
5. **"Anti-mite spray once over / buy an anti-mite cover and done" — demoted**. The updated Cochrane review does not support mite-proof bedding alone delivering clinical benefit (Nurmatov 2012, PMID 22103686); what works is the combination of hot-water laundering + humidity control + less accumulation.
6. **"A breastfeeding mother avoids everything so her child won't get allergies" — CONTRADICTED (limit: infants without allergic symptoms)**. Universal maternal dietary restriction has no preventive evidence for infants without allergic manifestations (guideline framing · FACT·relayed) and risks the mother's nutrition; for a breastfed infant with confirmed cow's-milk-protein allergy, maternal milk-protein avoidance is a **treatment** measure under medical guidance — do not mix prevention with treatment.
7. **"Add egg and peanut later to prevent allergy" — CONTRADICTED**. The LEAP trial (PMID 25705822) showed early regular peanut intake in high-risk infants cut age-5 peanut allergy from 13.7% to 1.9% (SPT-negative group); the current guideline direction = timely introduction, diversity, with high-risk infants clinician-assessed first.
8. **"In anaphylactic shock, take an antihistamine first and observe" — CONTRADICTED · fatal-grade**. Antihistamines cannot rescue shock; intramuscular epinephrine + emergency call is the only correct opening (WAO 2020, PMID 33204386).
