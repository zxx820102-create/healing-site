## 0. The one-sentence overview

**There exists a stepwise descending "gradient" between social status and health — it is not a phenomenon that begins below the poverty line; it is equally steep in populations with universal health coverage, full employment, and no absolute poverty; the pathways are both material and psychosocial (sense of control is the largest explanatory item of the Whitehall gradient); for the individual it is not a verdict: health behaviors + social connection + not detaching from employment + education are the statistically movable part (under the Whitehall II longitudinal assessment, behavioral factors explain 72% · 95%CI 42-154% · the interval's width marked honestly).**

Epistemic labeling: the gradient exists = FACT (repeated across countries and cohorts); the division among pathways = MODEL (shares vary by population); the inequality–health hypothesis = CONTESTED (both sides standing, honestly · §3); the action cards = INFERENCE (extrapolated from population evidence to personal operables · not clinical advice).

## 1. The gradient evidence face (the hard core)

### 1.1 The two Whitehall studies — one of epidemiology's most startling and hardest findings (FACT)

**Whitehall I (from 1967)**: 18,000+ British male civil servants. **Occupational mortality differentials ran across a broad spectrum of diseases** — the lowest grade (messengers / doormen etc.) had a death rate roughly 3–4 times that of the highest grade (permanent secretaries and other executives) (coronary heart disease most of all). "Whitehall" thereby became the byword for the "social gradient".

**Whitehall II (baseline 1985-88)**: Marmot MG et al. *Lancet* 1991;337:1387-93 (**PMID 1674771** · abstract original text verified): a new cohort of **10,314 civil servants** (6,900 men · 3,414 women · aged 35-55) — twenty years after Whitehall I, "**the inverse association between grade and disease showed no attenuation**": angina, ECG ischemic evidence, and chronic-bronchitis symptoms all descended grade by grade; lower-grade participants self-rated worse health and reported more symptoms.

**Why Whitehall is "startling"** (FACT · the pivotal fact of the whole volume):
- The participants were **all employed, all above the poverty line, all covered by the UK's National Health Service (NHS, universal since 1948)** — no destitution, no uninsured subjects;
- Yet the gradient **persisted steeply grade by grade** — not a binary "poor people get sick" phenomenon but a **continuous slope** from the top to the bottom;
- Marmot thereby rewrote the conclusion from "poverty causes disease" to "**status itself covaries with health**" — the title of *The Status Syndrome* (2004) comes from this (book anchor · not PubMed).

**The gradient does not retire (FACT)**: Marmot MG, Shipley MJ. *BMJ* 1996 (**PMID 8916748**): the Whitehall I cohort's 25-year follow-up — **the grade-based mortality differential persisted after retirement**. Retirement removed workplace stress exposure yet did not remove the gradient — showing the gradient is "branded into" a longer causal chain (see §2.3 early programming); "leaving the workplace" alone does not settle it.

### 1.2 The income axis: 1.4 billion US tax records (FACT · the largest modern confirmation)

Chetty R et al. *JAMA* 2016 (**PMID 27063997** · abstract numbers verified verbatim):
- Sample: **1.4 billion person-years** of de-identified tax and mortality records, 1999-2014;
- **The life-expectancy gap, top 1% vs bottom 1% of income: 14.6 years for men (95%CI 14.4-14.8) · 10.1 years for women (95%CI 9.9-10.3)**;
- The key sentence (original): "higher income was associated with greater longevity **throughout the income distribution**" — the income–longevity relation runs **across the entire income distribution**, not snapping only at the bottom;
- Time trend (2001-2014): the top 5% of income gained 2.34 years (men) / 2.91 years (women) of life expectancy; the bottom 5% gained only **0.32 years (men) / 0.04 years (women)** — the gap is widening;
- Low-income people's life expectancy **varies hugely across regions** — the same income, and where you live makes years of difference (this is the setup for §4.1's "statistics, not verdicts").

### 1.3 Three axes: income · education · occupation — independent and stacked (FACT for existence · MODEL for shares)

| Axis | Anchor | Verification state |
|---|---|---|
| **Occupation** | the Whitehall grade gradient (PMID 1674771/9242799) | FACT · cited above |
| **Income** | Chetty 2016 (PMID 27063997) | FACT · cited above |
| **Education** | Mackenbach JP et al. *NEJM* 2008 (**PMID 18525043**): 22 European countries comparing mortality and self-rated health inequalities by education level and occupational class — **the education gradient is pervasive across Europe's welfare regimes** | FACT · title + abstract verified |
| **The China face** | *Lancet Public Health* 2024 (**PMID 39419059**): region-level SES mortality inequality from a national cohort — individual-level SES / behavior / metabolic factors mediated **39.5%** of the regional effect (individual SES the largest contributor) | FACT · abstract verified |

The three axes are highly correlated (high education → good occupation → high income); "which is more fundamental" among them is methodologically unresolved (**UNKNOWN · multivariable causal decomposition of the three axes is fragile under collinearity**). The operable reading: **education is one of the most upstream personally controllable variables among the three axes** (INFERENCE · §4).

**Global scale**: Marmot M. *Lancet* 2005 (**PMID 15781105**): the life-expectancy gap between countries is **48 years** · within countries **over 20 years** — "not inevitable" is the opening sentence of the WHO Commission on Social Determinants of Health (CSDH 2008).

### 1.4 "Universal coverage ≠ the gradient vanishing" — not only a matter of money for care (FACT → MODEL)

The Whitehall cohorts themselves prove it: **in a population where care access was fully equalized, the gradient remained steep** (§1.1). From this a key exclusion follows (INFERENCE · logically forced):
- **"The gradient is just poor people unable to afford care" is excluded by Whitehall** — access to care is a necessary condition, not the gradient's main carrier;
- The remaining explanatory space = material living conditions (housing / food / occupational exposure) + **psychosocial pathways** (§2.2) + early programming (§2.3);
- Mackenbach 2008's cross-national face adds: under different welfare-state regimes the gradient **exists universally but with different magnitudes** — institutions can tune the magnitude, not erase the gradient (FACT · qualitative).

> Note the boundary: this takes no position on any specific insurance policy (this canon touches no politics · it states cohort evidence only). The material pathway's share may be larger in contexts like China with higher out-of-pocket shares (**UNKNOWN · no same-framing decomposition**, marked honestly).

### 1.5 Subjective social status — the predictive power of the "ladder question" (FACT for association · UNKNOWN for causation)

**The ladder question (MacArthur ladder)**: draw a 10-rung ladder — "standing at the top = the most money, the most education, the best jobs, most respected; the bottom is the opposite; which rung are you on?"
- **The original anchor**: Adler NE, Epel ES, Castellazzo G, Ickovics JR. *Health Psychol* 2000 (**PMID 11129362** · abstract verified): 157 healthy white women — **subjective status associated more consistently and more strongly with psychological functioning and health-related factors** (self-rated health, heart rate, sleep latency, body-fat distribution, cortisol habituation to repeated stress) than objective measures (income / education); most associations **remained significant after controlling for objective status**.
- **The Whitehall II replication**: Singh-Manoux A, Adler NE, Marmot MG. *Soc Sci Med* 2003 (**PMID 12600368**): subjective status and its health associations are measurable in the same cohort.
- **The meta-analysis**: Cundiff JM, Matthews KA. *Health Psychol* 2017 (**PMID 28726474** · abstract verified): 31 studies, 99 effects — subjective SES carries an **independent but small** cumulative association beyond objective indicators (Z=.07 · SE=.01); strongest for **self-rated health**; the authors' own note: **almost all cross-sectional; strong causal inference impossible**; and the association strength differs between Black and white samples (ethnic heterogeneity stated as-is).

The honest reading (INFERENCE): subjective status is a **perception-layer filter** — how you perceive your position on the ladder carries signal beyond the objective indicators; but "change the perception and you change the health" has no evidence (§5.1 falsifies that chicken soup directly).

## 2. The mechanism face (layered as-is · pathways = MODEL · anchors = FACT)

> Mechanism shares are **incommensurable** across populations, eras and endpoints — this section gives only "the pathway exists + the verified explanatory quantity" and manufactures no grand total-share table.

### 2.1 The material pathway (MODEL · the pathway's existence is common-sense grade · its share within the gradient varies by context)

The material face beyond care access (already partly excluded as the main carrier by Whitehall, §1.4): housing quality (crowding / damp / heating), food quality and food safety, occupational physical exposures (injury / dust / night shifts — linking to `docs/healing/burnout-canon-v1-20260912.md`'s long-hours and night-shift sections), neighborhood environmental exposure. These pathways **need no proof** (FACT-grade existence); what is undecided is their **share ratio** against the psychosocial pathways within the total gradient (UNKNOWN · no cross-context robust decomposition). The Chinese regional cohort's mediation analysis (PMID 39419059): individual SES + behavior + metabolism together mediate 39.5% — **the remaining 60.5% of the regional effect was not absorbed by individual-level variables** (indirect evidence for the regional material and built-environment face · MODEL).

### 2.2 The psychosocial pathway — sense of control is the largest explanatory item of the Whitehall gradient (FACT · within-cohort mediation)

**The anchor (one of this canon's strongest single anchors)**: Marmot MG, Bosma H, Hemingway H, Brunner E, Stansfeld S. *Lancet* 1997 (**PMID 9242799** · abstract numbers verified verbatim):
- In Whitehall I, traditional coronary risk factors **explained only about a quarter of the gradient**;
- In Whitehall II's 5.3-year follow-up (n=7,372): **the largest contributor to the gradient was "low control at work"**, followed by height (an early-life factor) and the classic risk factors led by smoking;
- **After adjustment for all factors: incident CHD for the lowest grade moved from OR 1.5→0.95 in men · 1.47→1.07 in women** — the psychosocial work environment where low control sits "explained a large part of the CHD incidence gradient" (the original Interpretation, verbatim).

**The stress-physiology face (MODEL · pathway mechanism)**: McEwen BS. *NEJM* 1998 (**PMID 9428819**): the repeated mobilization of chronic stress mediators produces "**allostatic load**" — the cumulative wear of blood pressure / cortisol / inflammation / metabolism. Adler 2000's abstract-level associations of subjective status with heart rate, cortisol habituation and body-fat distribution (§1.5) are the individual-level visible end of this pathway (FACT for association).
**The interlink with the burnout canon**: the high-demand low-control job = job strain, with coronary heart disease **HR 1.23 (95%CI 1.10-1.37)** (IPD meta n=197,473 · Kivimäki M et al. *Lancet* 2012, **PMID 22981903** · 1.16 after publication-bias correction) — the mechanism face is already established in the occupational-health canon; the two canons are evidence neighbors (`wrk:工作要求-控制模型` / `wrk:工作Strain冠心病`).

**In one sentence**: the psychosocial pathway by which status affects health — **the core is not "wounded vanity"; it is the chronic wear of the sense of control and predictability** — leading directly to §4's controllable variables (the small self-domain where control is restored).

### 2.3 Early programming — childhood SES × lifetime health (MODEL · interfaces verified)

- **The height clue**: in Whitehall II, height (an integrated indicator of early nutrition and infection) makes a small independent contribution to the CHD gradient (PMID 9242799 · FACT, original);
- **The ACE interface**: Felitti VJ et al. *Am J Prev Med* 1998 (**PMID 9635069** · title verified): adverse childhood experiences dose-related to adult top-ten-cause-of-death risk — childhood adversity and low SES are highly co-occurring; the ACE score is this canon's interface with the trauma canon (FACT · dose-response);
- **The developmental-programming hypothesis** (the Barker line · fetal/infant nutritional imprinting of metabolic-disease risk): MODEL grade — the full chain from childhood SES through developmental programming to lifetime disease risk **has no single decisive evidence**; it rests honestly at MODEL;
- Marmot & Shipley 1996's "gradient persists after retirement" (§1.1) corroborates it: part of the gradient is loaded before adult exposures even begin (INFERENCE).

## 3. The relative-deprivation face — inequality × health (CONTESTED · both sides standing, honestly)

### 3.1 The affirmative side (pro)

- **The Wilkinson hypothesis**: income inequality between members of society (rather than between individuals) itself harms health — the more unequal the society, the worse the health of those below the median (the 1992 BMJ original paper could not be PMID-verified in this round of searching · listed without cited numbers · honest boundary);
- **The largest meta-analytic anchor**: Kondo N et al. *BMJ* 2009 (**PMID 19903981** · abstract numbers verified verbatim): per +0.05 in the Gini coefficient — pooled mortality in prospective cohorts **RR 1.08 (95%CI 1.06-1.10)** (9 cohorts · n=59,509,857); poor self-rated health **OR 1.04 (95%CI 1.02-1.06)** (19 cross-sections · n=1,280,211);
- **The causal review**: Pickett KE, Wilkinson RG. *Soc Sci Med* 2015, "Income inequality and health: a causal review" (**PMID 25577953** · title verified): argues the combined evidence supports a causal contribution.

### 3.2 The critical side (anti)

- **The early critique anchor**: Judge K. *BMJ* 1995, "Income distribution and life expectancy: a critical appraisal" (**PMID 7496240** · title verified): a systematic critique of the model sensitivity and data quality of the inequality–life-expectancy cross-national correlation;
- **The structural critique** (methodological · MODEL grade · this canon's synthesis): ① the cross-national Gini–health correlation is mainly an **ecological correlation** — the correlation lives at the "country" grain and cannot be pushed directly to individuals; ② sensitivity to model specification (income-per-capita terms / time effects / regional subgroups), with studies that flip conclusions under alternative specifications coexisting; ③ Kondo's own meta-regression also showed the effects are **heterogeneous** (the abstract's "Meta-regressions showed stronger ass[ociations in]…" supports only "there is moderation" up to where it is truncated).

### 3.3 This canon's verdict (CONTESTED — this is an epistemic state, not fence-sitting)

**"Income inequality → population health" has not passed the verified line under this system's causal gate (candidate → shadow → canary → verified): the association-face evidence is large and stable (Kondo RR 1.08); the causal-face evidence stands two-sided (Pickett & Wilkinson 2015 vs Judge 1995 + specification sensitivity).** Any single-valued assertion of the "inequality kills" type is uniformly down-weighted to CONTESTED in this system.
**The physiological face of social comparison** (the hypothesis's mechanism port · MODEL): unequal societies amplify the frequency of "upward comparison" and the sense of relative deprivation — linking to the subjective-status pathway (§1.5's Adler cortisol/heart-rate associations) and the self-esteem face. Relative deprivation as an **individual-level psychological process** has measurement and experimental support; as a **population-level causal engine** it has not passed the gate. The two sentences are stated separately, not merged.

## 4. The actionable face (the landing point · this canon's most important part)

### 4.1 Dismantle the fatalism first: the gradient is statistics, not a verdict (INFERENCE · legally inferred from FACT)

- **Group means ≠ individual fate**: the gradient describes **the slope of a population distribution**, not a personal schedule. Individual variance dwarfs the between-group difference: in Chetty's data, **people at the same income level differ by years of life expectancy depending on where they live** (PMID 27063997 Results, third point · FACT); in Whitehall, lifespans within the same grade are also highly dispersed. The slope is "elevated risk", not a "due date".
- **The gradient is a "probability cloud that raises risk"**: the correct mental model is "your position changes the distribution you face; your place within the distribution is still decided by a large number of controllable variables".
- **Said honestly in the other direction too**: this statistic is not decoration — it shows that the "adjust your mindset, not your exposures" chicken soup (§5.1) and the fully resigned "lie flat" (§5.3) are both wrong.

### 4.2 How much do behavioral factors explain? (quantified as-is · FACT)

**The anchor**: Stringhini S et al. *JAMA* 2010 (**PMID 20332401** · abstract numbers verified verbatim) — Whitehall II, 9,590 participants followed to 2009-04, 654 deaths:
- Lowest vs highest socioeconomic position: all-cause mortality **HR 1.60**;
- **Baseline one-off** measurement of health behaviors (smoking / alcohol / diet / exercise) attenuated the association by **42%**;
- **Repeated measurement during follow-up** (time-varying covariates) attenuated it by **72% (95%CI 42%-154%** · the CI wide enough to contain everything — marked as-is: a large explanatory share with equally large uncertainty);
- By component (baseline → longitudinal): diet 7%→17% · exercise 5%→21% · alcohol 3%→12%.

The reading (INFERENCE): **behavioral factors are one of the known largest "movable parts" of the gradient** — roughly four to seven tenths (depending on measurement), but by no means all: the remaining third down to double-digit shares come from beyond behavior (material / psychosocial / early programming · §2). So "manage your lifestyle and that's enough" and "lifestyle is useless" are **both vetoed by this set of numbers**.

### 4.3 The personal controllables list (listed separately · action card)

> Positioning: personal operables extrapolated from population statistics (INFERENCE); ordered by "evidence strength × operability". This list is not medical advice; care decisions belong to clinicians.

| # | Controllable variable | Evidence anchor | Suggested action (the minimal operable step) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The behavioral four: smoking · movement · food · alcohol** | Stringhini 2010: explanatory share 42%→72% (PMID 20332401) | quitting smoking is the single biggest; accumulate WHO-framed brisk-walking-class activity weekly; food and alcohol per the `docs/healing/` nutrition canons |
| 2 | **Not detaching from employment long-term** | Roelfs DJ et al. 2011: all-cause mortality HR **1.63** for the unemployed (42 studies n>20 million · covariate-adjusted · PMID 21330027) | the harm of long-term unemployment exceeds most cases of "enduring a toxic job for now"; changing jobs ranks before quitting with nothing lined up (see §5.3) |
| 3 | **Control at work** | Marmot 1997: low control = the gradient's largest explanatory item (PMID 9242799); job strain HR 1.23 (PMID 22981903) | inside an unchangeable position, expand the **micro-control domain**: autonomy over task order + a clear off-work boundary + recovery rituals (operation card links to `burnout-canon` §4 "psychological detachment recovery") |
| 4 | **Social connection (strong + weak)** | Holt-Lunstad 2010: strong social relations raise survival odds **+50%** (OR 1.50 · 95%CI 1.42-1.59 · 148 studies n=308,849 · PMID 20668659); weak ties: Sandstrom & Dunn 2014 (PMID 24769739 · title-level): daily interactions with weak ties correlate with same-day well-being | at least one high-quality face-to-face per week; treat weak ties as "micro-nutrients": don't skip the short interactions with shop staff / neighbors / fellow hobbyists |

| 7 | **An audit of subjective comparison** | subjective status independently associates with health (PMID 11129362/28726474) — but "change perception, cure disease" has no evidence | what can be done: **reduce the input frequency of upward comparison** (the social-media face) and **accumulate objective small wins** (fin: small-victories card) — the goal is to stop the comparison pathway's chronic wear, not self-hypnosis |
| 8 | **Community participation** | L3 belonging-layer linkage (`human-needs-map` L3-41); social connection's mortality protection (PMID 20668659) | volunteering / hobby communities = connection + meaning + weak ties in one — a face this system already has (the mindbody service-to-others cell) |

**The structural note (no politics · evidence only)**: items 1–8 are all "the maximum feasible within a given situation"; they can move **four to seven tenths** of the statistical gradient (the Stringhini framing) and **cannot move the slope itself** — the slope belongs to institutions and structures, to the domain of public policy. This canon's service to the individual reader ends here and does not cross the line (honest boundary · also this canon's political-neutrality discipline).

### 4.4 The buffering of community connection (FACT for association + INFERENCE for direction)

Social relations' protection against mortality (OR 1.50) is **partly independent** of the SES gradient — connection is an axis outside the gradient (within Holt-Lunstad's adjusted framing). The weak-ties research (Sandstrom & Dunn 2014) completes the micro-pathway that "introverts / low-resource people can also do". **Community is the part of "status" a person cannot choose at birth yet can actively weave** — it does not change your position on the income ladder, but it changes your position in the connection network, and the latter has its own mortality protection (INFERENCE · the reason for action).

## 5. The falsification face (five pieces of internet chicken soup · CONTRADICTED / downgraded phrasings)

| # | Chicken soup | Verdict | Basis and the correct statement |

| 4 | "Status anxiety is cured by consumption downgrading" | **downgraded to partially valid** | The valid side (the financial face): downgrading improves cash flow and the emergency buffer — a real contribution to the material pathway (linking to `financial-map` fin:money-happiness-curve/fin:emergency-buffer-card). The invalid side (the status face): status anxiety's pathway is **relative comparison** (§3.3); cutting spending does not cut the reference frame — the social-media upward inputs remain, and the comparison pathway wears on as before. **Correct statement**: downgrading manages money; changing the reference frame manages anxiety; don't let the two impersonate each other. |
| 5 | "Humble homes no longer produce scholars" (the fatalism of blocked mobility) | **CONTRADICTED (but the structural-fact part retained as-is)** | The retained side: slow intergenerational mobility is a structural fact (this canon does not unfold the political face). The abused side: ① the education gradient existing across 22 countries ≠ education is useless — education is the most upstream controllable variable of the three axes (§4.3#5 · PMID 18525043/29533059); ② the gradient is a distribution's slope, not a personal verdict (§4.1 · Chetty: same income, regions apart by years); ③ fatalism itself self-actualizes through the given-up feeling and behavioral disinvestment (isomorphic with §5.3). **Correct statement**: the probability cloud is there, and the people inside the cloud still have large degrees of freedom — statistics is a map, not a sentence. |
