# Sexual Health Canon v1 (adult-education orientation · medically serious register)

**Positioning**: sexual health is a regular branch of medicine (WHO lists it among its health topics); this volume upgrades it from the human-needs compass's **"blank × high-frequency"** first slot into an actionable canon. The whole volume is **educational in purpose**: physiology literacy + care-seeking pathways + medication safety + prevention action cards + rumor falsification, with zero erotic content; it substitutes for no in-person consultation and no medical order. The structured graph (all of this volume's nodes · for the weaving lane to consume) is at `data/healing/sexual-health-map-20260912.json`, and the receipt at `system/control/receipts/2026-09-12-sexual-health/FINDINGS.md`.

**Boundaries (declared up front)**: ① this canon is **adult** education — minors' questions are handled exclusively by guardians through school education / professional medical channels, and this canon does not unfold them; ② for anyone who has experienced non-consensual sexual acts / intimate-partner violence, the first priority is safety and medical care, via professional help lines (see the red-flag card in Section 7) — this canon refers only, and handles nothing; ③ all medication decisions (including PDE5 inhibitors and hormone therapy) require an in-person prescribing physician.

## Section 1. The physiology face: definition and the response cycle

### 1.1 The WHO working definition (FACT · verified against the source text)

WHO's formal working definition: sexual health is **a state of physical, emotional, mental and social well-being in relation to sexuality** — not merely the absence of disease, dysfunction or infirmity ("a state of physical, emotional, mental and social well-being in relation to sexuality…not merely the absence of disease, dysfunction or infirmity", WHO sexual health topic page · verified 2026-09-12). Implication: the evaluation dimensions of sexual health naturally include the psychological and relational faces; a framing of "examining only the organs while never asking about the psyche and the relationship" is incomplete.

### 1.2 Sexual response cycle models (MODEL frameworks · labeled honestly, not passed off as physiological fact)

| Model | Content | Epistemic state and use |
|---|---|---|
| Masters & Johnson linear four-phase (1966, *Human Sexual Response*) | excitement → plateau → orgasm → resolution | MODEL: the classic framework of physiological measurement; fits populations incompletely and has lagged behind clinical practice |
| Kaplan three-phase model | desire → excitement → orgasm | MODEL: clinically useful for typing (maps onto a three-way stratification of troubles) |
| Basson circular / intimacy-based model (2000 · cited 430; 2002) | in long-term relationships, desire can be **"responsive"** — sparked by intimacy and stimulation rather than arising spontaneously first; the starting point varies | MODEL: explains the normality of "intimacy first, desire follows," reducing misdiagnosis of "no-desire disease" |
| Dual control model (Bancroft & Janssen; 2009 review cited 217; 2023 scoping review) | sexual response = "excitation system (accelerator) × inhibition system (brake)", a double gate; anxiety/distraction/stress are the typical brakes | MODEL: the mainstream research framework that mechanizes "psychogenic troubles" |

**Honesty clause**: the above are **conceptual frameworks (MODEL)**, not the final draft of physiology; they do not exclude one another — pick the model the problem calls for in clinic.

### 1.3 The hormone face: testosterone / estrogen × desire — the relation and its limits

- **Testosterone × male desire**: low testosterone is associated with reduced sexual desire and fewer morning erections (FACT · association grade); in confirmed hypogonadism, testosterone treatment improves sexual function **small-to-moderately** (Bhasin & Snyder NEJM 2025 review; Cochrane 2018 "Testosterone supplementation in men with sexual dysfunction"). **Limits (FACT · equally important)**: not everyone with low desire has low testosterone; testosterone does not resolve psychological and relational factors; long-term cardiovascular safety is still under follow-up — use requires endocrinology/andrology assessment, and it is not a "supplement".
- **Estrogen × women**: after menopause estrogen falls → vaginal dryness/atrophy (now called GSM, the genitourinary syndrome of menopause) → intercourse discomfort (FACT · guideline grade); local low-dose estrogen is one of gynecology's standard directions of management (per gynecologic orders). **The desire face**: Basson's 2021 review "Are Androgens at Fault?" points out that androgens are not the main driver of women's sexual troubles — low desire in women ≠ missing hormones (MODEL / review grade); check the psychological and relational faces first.
- **Hyperprolactinemia / thyroid / diabetes**: all affect desire and function through endocrine pathways (FACT · mechanism grade) — this is exactly why an abrupt drop in desire calls for bloodwork rather than buying supplements directly (action card in Section 7).

### 1.4 The age spectrum of change (FACT-grade direction · wide individual variation)

- Men: stronger stimulation needed for erection / longer latency, the refractory period lengthens with age, and ED prevalence rises markedly with age (direction consistent between Laumann 1999 and later consensus).
- Women: lubrication falls and tissue changes around menopause (GSM); perimenopausal desire fluctuation is common.
- **The normal spectrum ≠ disease**: the changes above are part of the aging spectrum; healthy older adults maintaining a sexual life is common and positively associated with quality of life (consensus-direction grade). Only when the **speed and degree** of change are abnormal (e.g., an abrupt drop within months, or other symptoms accompanying) does the care-seeking pathway open.

## Section 2. The four classes of trouble: prevalence ranges and care-seeking pathways

### 2.1 Stratification and prevalence (FACT · citation grade · ranges as given)

| Trouble class | Content | Prevalence face (range) | First-line clinic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desire | low or absent sexual desire / interest | the most common class in women; the consensus gives ranges, not single values (McCabe 2016, the Fourth International Consultation on Sexual Medicine consensus · desire-class troubles rank first in women); Laumann 1999: 43% of US women / 31% of men aged 18–59 self-reported at least one trouble | psychotherapy / sex therapy → endocrine workup |
| Arousal | ED in men; lubrication / sustained-arousal difficulty in women | male ED prevalence climbs steeply with age (consensus-consistent direction); ED is extremely common in middle-aged and older men | andrology-urology (men) / gynecology + sexual medicine (women) |
| Orgasm | difficulty reaching orgasm / premature or delayed ejaculation | premature ejaculation is the commonest orgasm-class complaint in men (consensus-direction grade); rate ranges vary widely with definition | andrology-urology / sexual medicine |
| Pain | intercourse pain / vaginismus | self-reported intercourse pain at some time point is quite common in women (ranges vary widely with definition) | gynecology (organic causes excluded first) → pelvic floor / sex therapy |

**The key distinction (FACT · the DSM-5 system)**: a "trouble" (self-reported problem) ≠ a "disorder" (with significant distress / functional impairment + duration conditions). Most self-reported troubles do not constitute a disorder; whether to act turns on distress and functional impact, not on comparison against some inconsistent standard.

### 2.2 Care-seeking pathway action card (this canon's core deliverable)

```text
Step 1 Classify: low desire / arousal difficulty (ED · lubrication) / orgasm problems · premature ejaculation / pain
      → the first-line clinics in the table above
Step 2 Check the medicines: run through everything currently being taken (the medication face in §2.3)
      — a medication-induced effect is ruled out first; never stop a drug on your own
Step 3 Bloodwork: abrupt drop in desire/function → endocrine-face screening
      (testosterone / prolactin / thyroid / blood glucose)
      ED under age 40, or with cardiovascular risk → cardiovascular-metabolic assessment in parallel
      (see the red-flag card)
Step 4 Psychological and relational face: workup found no organic problem / anxiety-strained relationship
      accompanies → certified sex therapy / couples counseling (RCT-grade evidence exists; see Section 5)
Step 5 Review: any "enhancement" prescription drug must be evaluated by the prescribing physician;
      buying enhancement drugs online = a high-risk source (unknown ingredients × nitrate-contamination risk)
```

### 2.3 Medication-induced sexual dysfunction (listed honestly · action card "don't stop the drug on your own")

- **SSRI antidepressants**: treatment-emergent sexual dysfunction is a common side effect — the meta-analysis (Serretti & Chiesa 2009 · cited 357) shows it is common overall and **differs significantly between drugs** (drug choice and adjustment belong to the prescribing physician); post-SSRI sexual dysfunction (PSSD) literature exists and its mechanism has no verdict (FACT: reviews exist (a new 2026 review is in print) · mechanism UNKNOWN). **Action**: do not stop the drug on your own (relapse risk of depression) → discuss switching / dose / timing windows with the prescribing physician.
- **Antihypertensives**: certain classes are associated with sexual-function effects (older-generation thiazide diuretics / beta blockers have more association reports; ACEI/ARB relatively neutral — MODEL grade; the literature is not uniform, labeled honestly); switching decisions belong to cardiology / the prescribing physician.
- **Antipsychotics**: mechanism related to hyperprolactinemia (Serretti & Chiesa 2011 review face).
- **Long-term hormones / chemotherapy / opioids**: all can act through the endocrine axis (mechanism grade MODEL) — the medication list is a must-check item in any sexual-trouble consultation.

## Section 3. The food-drug / substance interaction face (interlinked with the food-drug canon)

### 3.1 Nitrates × PDE5 inhibitors = absolute contraindication (FACT · potentially fatal · red-flag card listed separately)

**Mechanism**: nitrates (nitroglycerin / isosorbide mononitrate etc., including sublingual emergency medication) and PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil / tadalafil etc.) run through the same NO-cGMP pathway — combined inhibition → **blood pressure can crash, fatally**. The cardiovascular-safety reviews (Cheitlin 2003; Jackson, Montorsi & Cheitlin 2006) speak with one voice: those on nitrates must not take PDE5 inhibitors. **Inhaled alkyl nitrites ("poppers") fall under the same nitrate contraindication** (consensus grade). The time window (how long after stopping a PDE5 inhibitor a nitrate may be used) is managed by emergency physicians — it is not a patient self-management item.

### 3.2 Alcohol: dose-dependent and two-sided (FACT · direction grade)

- Low dose: subjective disinhibition (cortical inhibition lifted · the subjective "loosened up") — this is the physiological root of the folk line "alcohol helps".
- High dose: **inhibits** every phase of the sexual response cycle (both erection and orgasm thresholds rise) — laboratory dose-response and population studies agree in direction; "A Meta-Analysis of Erectile Dysfunction and Alcohol Consumption" exists (Europe PMC title-level verification · 2026-09-12).
- Long-term heavy drinking: ED is frequent + gonadal-axis suppression; partly reversible after quitting (prospective follow-up studies of quitting exist · title-level verification).
- **One-line framing**: low-dose disinhibition × high-dose response inhibition = **dose-dependent and two-sided (FACT)**; at high dose, "alcohol as an aid" runs causally in the opposite direction.

### 3.3 Grapefruit juice × PDE5 (mechanism carried from the food-drug canon)

Sildenafil is metabolized by CYP3A4; grapefruit juice inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 → blood drug levels rise (same mechanism as the food-drug canon's A2 grapefruit entry · referenced, not copied) — while taking a PDE5 inhibitor, avoid large amounts of grapefruit juice (MODEL-grade quantification without a verdict · direction FACT).

### 3.4 The monitoring face of gender-affirming hormones (one line · the professional face defers to physicians)

Gender-affirming hormone therapy is regular endocrine treatment: sex hormone levels + blood count / lipids / thrombotic risk etc. are monitored on schedule per the guideline (Endocrine Society clinical practice guideline, Hembree 2017 · cited 1485) — this canon does not unfold doses or regimens; the professional face always defers to specialist orders.

## Section 4. The relationship and communication face

### 4.1 Communication > technique: the intervention evidence face

- Sexual troubles are mostly "two-person events": stress / relational strain / missing sexual communication are the core pathways of psychogenic troubles (the "brake" terms of the dual control model · MODEL).
- **Psychological interventions carry RCT-grade evidence**: Brotto et al. 2021 (J Consult Clin Psychol · mindfulness-based cognitive therapy vs supportive sex education · RCT in female sexual interest/arousal disorder · cited 44); Cochrane 2019 "Bibliotherapy for sexual dysfunction" (self-help reading interventions: evidence limited but present). **Contrast**: pure "technique-type product" claims (the efficacy scripts of courses/devices) are weakly evidenced (FACT: no qualified comparative evidence face).
- **Action card**: put "talking" first among interventions — talk with your partner about expectations / discomfort / boundaries (FACT grade: the communication-face intervention has an RCT anchor); technique comes after communication; when self-help fails and distress is significant → certified sex therapy / couples counseling.

### 4.2 HPV vaccine and screening (cancer-prevention FACT-grade action card)

- **Vaccine**: HPV vaccination programs lower community rates of HPV-related disease (Cochrane 2025 · Henschke et al. population program review) + network meta-analysis (Cochrane 2025 · Bergman et al.: vaccines prevent cervical cancer and HPV-related disease). **Action**: vaccinate within the eligible age per the local immunization program (valency / age expansion per local guidance); common local reactions / syncope reports exist, and the large-scale evidence face shows no new serious risk signal (Cochrane 2025 framing · as given).
- **Screening is not waived**: the vaccine does not cover all high-risk types — **the vaccinated still undergo cervical cancer screening per the guideline** (FACT · guideline-consistent; screening intervals follow the PG screening_schedule ledger face).

### 4.3 The contraceptive effectiveness spectrum: perfect use vs typical use (one of the most important public-health facts · FACT)

| Method | Perfect use (first-year failure rate) | Typical use | Where the gap comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Combined oral contraceptive | ~0.3% | ~7–9% (NSFG framing range) | must be taken correctly every day — missed pills are the typical outcome |
| Male condom | ~2% | ~13% (per 100 person-years) | must be used correctly, every time, the whole act |
| LARC (IUD / subdermal implant) | <1% | <1% | **no per-act operation needed** — typical ≈ perfect |
| Withdrawal / fertility-awareness | (conditional entry) | notably higher (highly variable) | depends on per-act correctness and cycle prediction |

(Trussell 2011, Contraception · cited 889; Sundaram 2017 NSFG update; consistent with CDC / authoritative public-education framing, verified.)

- **How to read the table (this section's real deliverable)**: method effectiveness must be **read in both columns** — "the label's effectiveness" is perfect use; "the population's actual effectiveness" is typical use; **the entire gap comes from the operational burden of "getting it right every time"**. When choosing, treat the operational burden as the primary variable.
- **Dual protection**: the condom is the only contraceptive that also significantly lowers STI risk (FACT · consensus grade) — "a contraception plan" and "STI protection" are two questions; answering the first does not necessarily answer the second.

## Section 5. The verification / falsification face: five internet rumors

| # | Rumor | Verdict | Evidence and falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Abstinence preserves vitality and lengthens life (a drop of semen is ten drops of blood)" | **No evidence (rumor CONTRADICTED)** | Semen is mostly water / protein / fructose; ejaculation does not deplete any "vital essence" (physiology commons grade); the observational direction runs opposite or has no verdict: Rider 2016 (Eur Urol · cited 57): the ≥21 ejaculations/month group had **lower** prostate cancer risk (HR≈0.80 · observational); Leitzmann 2004 (JAMA · cited 59): in the younger cohort, the high-frequency group had higher risk — **the two studies stand side by side honestly = causal direction UNKNOWN**; but whichever the direction, neither supports "preserving semen pays". falsifier: any prospective intervention showing abstinence improves lifespan/prostate outcomes |
| 2 | "Viagra damages the heart and is addictive" vs "Viagra is a health booster anyone can take" (a bidirectional rumor pair) | **Both CONTRADICTED** | The one real red line is singular and clear: **combined use with nitrates = a fatal contraindication** (FACT); for people without nitrates, use evaluated by prescription has a good cardiovascular-safety record (Cheitlin 2003 / Jackson 2006 review face); it is not an enhancer — off-label misuse without indication + online-buy sources (unknown ingredients) are the real risk face. falsifier: an RCT-grade cardiovascular-harm signal in nitrate-free populations using it as prescribed |
| 3 | "Alcohol aids sex / alcohol is a booster" | **Dose-dependent and two-sided · opposite direction at high dose (CONTRADICTED)** | Low-dose disinhibition (subjective) × high-dose response inhibition (physiological · lab dose-response + population concordance); long-term heavy drinking carries high ED rates with partial reversibility after quitting (prospective studies). falsifier: robust evidence that the high-dose group's erection/orgasm measures beat controls |
| 4 | "The HPV vaccine causes infertility / serious sequelae" | **CONTRADICTED (evidence face)** | Cochrane 2025 population program review + network meta-analysis: preventive efficacy established; the harms face shows no new serious risk signal (local reactions / syncope reports exist · as given); "premature ovarian failure" case reports have not become a causal evidence face. falsifier: a qualified controlled study confirming ovarian function damage |
| 5 | "Testosterone supplements = a universal booster / an anti-aging miracle drug" | **CONTRADICTED (limits face)** | In hypogonadal men, sexual-function improvement is small-to-moderate (NEJM 2025 review / Cochrane 2018); in men with normal testosterone, supplement benefit is weakly evidenced + long-term cardiovascular safety still under follow-up; low desire → find the cause first (prolactin / thyroid / glucose / psychological) rather than supplementing directly. falsifier: long-term RCTs in normal-testosterone populations showing anti-aging / functional benefit |

## Section 6. One-page action-card summary (print-oriented)

**Choosing where to seek care**: low desire → sex therapy / psychotherapy + endocrine bloodwork; ED → andrology-urology (under 40 or cardiovascular risk: parallel cardiology assessment); premature / delayed ejaculation → andrology-urology; intercourse pain / bleeding → gynecology; cause unclear → sexual medicine clinic.
**Three medication checks**: ① sexual-function change after a new drug → read the label's sexual-function side-effect entry and talk to the prescribing physician; ② antidepressant sexual side effects are common and adjustable (never stop on your own); ③ before taking a PDE5 inhibitor, ask yourself "am I on any nitrate?" — if yes, it is contraindicated.
**Three preventions**: HPV vaccine within the eligible ages (vaccination ≠ screening exemption); cervical screening on schedule; contraceptive choice read in both columns (perfect vs typical) + condoms for dual STI protection.
**Communication first**: sexual communication with your partner = the first intervention with an RCT anchor; significant distress or failed self-help → certified sex therapy.
**Lifestyle** (direction grade MODEL · not a sex-function specific remedy): regular exercise / glucose control / quitting smoking / limiting alcohol — vascular health and erectile function share one road (T2DM × ED: see docs/healing/deep-card-t2dm-20260912.md).

## Section 7. Red lines for seeking care (red-flag card · listed separately)

| Red flag | Action |
|---|---|
| On nitrates (including sublingual emergency medication) + want to take / taking a PDE5 inhibitor | **Contraindicated** — have cardiology re-evaluate the medication plan first |
| Priapism >4 hours (painful or not) | **Emergency department now** (ischemic injury risk · time-sensitive) |
| Acute severe testicular/scrotal pain | **Emergency department now** (torsion time window · hours-scale) |
| Postcoital bleeding / postmenopausal bleeding | Gynecology as soon as possible (cervical-screening red line) |
| Abrupt desire drop + galactorrhea / headache / visual-field defect | Endocrinology as soon as possible (hyperprolactinemia / pituitary assessment) |
| New ED + chest pain / shortness of breath / falling exercise tolerance | Cardiology first (ED can be a sentinel of vascular disease · MODEL-grade pointer) |
| Sexually active + painful urination / abnormal discharge / genital ulcers | STI testing (delay = complications + transmission) |
| Non-consensual sexual acts / intimate-partner violence | **Safety first**: police / Women's Federation / psychological crisis hotline / medical institutions — this canon refers only and handles nothing |
| Minors' sexual-health questions | Guardian + school education / professional medical channels (this canon is adult-oriented · not unfolded) |

## Section 8. The falsifier table (this canon's overall falsifiable face)

1. Prevalence numbers drift with definition / era / culture — citations must carry source and year; a new edition of the international consultation consensus recalculates the ranges.
2. If the Basson / dual control models are replaced by a stronger mechanistic framework (e.g., a new predictive-processing framework earning RCT-grade discriminant validity) → the models section is rewritten.
3. Testosterone's long-term cardiovascular safety defers to large ongoing RCTs — TRAVERSE-class results landing update the limits wording.
4. Typical-use contraceptive failure rates refresh with each round of national surveys (NSFG-class).
5. PSSD's mechanism rising from UNKNOWN (a settled mechanism + reproducible biomarkers) → §2.3 rewritten.
6. The rumor falsification table is alive: any rumor overturned by a qualified counter-study flips the verdict and is recorded (historical verdicts are never deleted).
