# Child Health Parent Handbook — English L1 (2026-09-13)

> English L1 — public education, **not a diagnostic tool**; zero case files. **Core: comfort plus the red lines, not the number.**

## ① Fever

**Definition (FACT · NICE NG143 + AAP)**: rectal temperature (肛温) ≥38.0°C (100.4°F); normal ~37±0.6°C. Ages 0–5: electronic under-arm thermometers (under 4 weeks), chemical-dot or infrared-ear from 4 weeks; forehead chemical strips unreliable (NICE verbatim). Parental perception is "valid and taken seriously" (NICE) — confirm with a thermometer. **Fever is immune response (FACT · AAP)** — the brain raises the thermostat; treat discomfort and dehydration, not the number. **Goal = comfort (FACT · NICE)**: never give an antipyretic (退热药) solely to lower the number; paracetamol (对乙酰氨基酚) or ibuprofen (布洛芬) only for distress. Over 6 months, never judge severity by the number alone: 39°C with the child playing happily < 38°C with the child gone limp. **Cooling (FACT)**: no tepid sponging (NICE); no alcohol rubs or ice packs (AAP); one clothing layer; fluids. **Bundling up ("捂汗", folk) is absolutely contraindicated (FACT)**: children "should not be over-wrapped" (NICE); overheating raises SIDS (婴儿睡眠意外) risk — one more layer than you. **Dosing (FACT + disclaimer)**: paracetamol ~10–15 mg/kg per dose every 4–6 h (under 2: clinician only); ibuprofen ~5–10 mg/kg every 6–8 h (label bans <6 months; caution: dehydration, kidney, GI disease). Single drug first; packaged dropper; dose by **weight**. **Every actual dose defers to the drug label and your doctor/pharmacist.**

## ② Respiratory

Cough is protective (FACT · AAP) — treat only if it blocks eating/sleep/comfort. OTC cough/cold medicines (FACT · AAP): under 4 not recommended; 4–6 physician advice only; 6+ by label. Combination cold medicines = overdose risk (FACT · FDA/MedlinePlus): stacked acetaminophen → liver injury (MODEL); action card (INFERENCE): one ingredient = one product/day; log doses. Honey (FACT · AAP): banned under 1 year (infant botulism (婴儿肉毒中毒)); from age 1, 2–5 mL for night cough; saline + suction; no half-dosed adult medicines. **Distress (FACT · NICE)**: chest indrawing (三凹征), grunting, flaring; rates >60/>50/>40 per min (0–5/6–11/>12 mo); cyanosis (口唇发青), SpO₂ ≤95%, unfinished sentences → **ED now**.

## ③ Gut

Diarrhea (腹泻) (WHO: ≥3 loose/watery stools/day); treat with **oral rehydration salts (ORS, 口服补液盐)** plus zinc 10–14 days (FACT · WHO). It kills via dehydration (脱水) — a top-three killer at ages 1–59 mo. Small frequent sips; keep feeding (no fasting). WHO first-line has **no antidiarrheal drug** (FACT+MODEL). **Dehydration signs (FACT · WHO/NICE)**: sunken eyes or fontanelle (囟门), no tears, dry mouth, skin pinch ≥2 s, drowsiness, far fewer wet diapers (normal ≥4–6/day), thirst. Any sign → same-day visit; severe → ED. **Red lines (FACT)**: bloody stool; persistent or green (bilious) vomiting; diarrhea >14 days; fixed right-lower abdominal pain → ED.

## ④ Rash

**Blanching is the fork (FACT · NICE)**: fever plus non-blanching rash (压之不褪色) → consider meningococcal disease → **ED immediately**. Glass-tumbler test (MODEL): spots not fading under pressed glass = non-blanching. Blanching scattered rash is usually viral — §6 flags still mean care now. Roseola (幼儿急疹) (FACT · Mayo): fever 3–5 days, rash appears **after** fever falls; common under 2. Hand-foot-mouth (手足口病) and scarlet fever (猩红热) (FACT · CDC): directional only; scarlet fever needs a clinician's throat swab.

## ⑤ Febrile seizure (热性惊厥) — on the spot (FACT · NINDS)

Time it (>5 min → EMS); slow recovery → also call; **protect, don't restrain**; **side-lying, nothing in the mouth** (*Nothing should ever be placed in the child's mouth*). Simple seizures (<15 min, once/24 h) cause no long-term harm; peak 6 mo–5 y. **First seizure → evaluation; antipyretics do not prevent recurrence** (NINDS/NICE).

## ⑥ Red-flag chapter (memorize this) — any one item → immediate care / ED

1. **Infant <3 months with ≥38.0°C — straight to the ED, no observation** (NICE high-risk; AAP: call now).
2. **Distress**: indrawing, grunting, >60/50/40, cyanosis, SpO₂ ≤95%.
3. **Abnormal mental state**: unrousable, won't stay awake, unresponsive, inconsolable, high-pitched cry (NICE red list).
4. **Dehydration**: sunken fontanelle or eyes, no tears, reduced urine, slow skin pinch, refusing fluids.
5. **Fever >5 days** → **Kawasaki disease assessment (川崎病, NICE)**; AAP: <2 y fever >24 h, ≥2 y >72 h → call.
6. **Non-blanching rash** (glass test positive) → ED.
7. **Seizure**: first-ever, >5 min, recurrent, or consciousness not returning.
8. **Repeated ≥40°C (104°F)**; fever after a hot environment → ED (heat stroke).
9. AAP list: stiff neck, severe headache, sore throat/earache, unexplained rash, repeated vomiting/diarrhea, immune problems (sickle-cell, cancer, steroids), heat exposure.
10. **Parental instinct = formal review indicator (FACT)** — NICE: "worse than when last seen", "more worried", "unable to cope"; perception "valid and taken seriously". (Quantified intuition studies unverified at item level — that layer MODEL.) No parent must prove medical knowledge to bring a child in.

## ⑦ Falsified folk beliefs (CONTRADICTED)

Bundling sweats it out — children's absolute taboo (NICE; SIDS risk). Alcohol rubs — poisoning risk (AAP). Aspirin for children — Reye syndrome (瑞氏综合征). Teething causes high fever — false (AAP). Toddler combination cold medicine — overdose risk (FDA). High fever cooks the brain — false; watch ≥41°C heat stroke (MODEL) and CNS-infection signs (§6). Antipyretics prevent seizures / fever must reach 37°C — false (NINDS/NICE). Higher number = sicker — false over 6 months (NICE).

## 8. Boundaries

Education, not diagnosis. **Dosage iron law: every actual dose defers to the drug label and your doctor/pharmacist** (concentrations differ across products). Zero case files; UNKNOWN not hidden; verify current guidelines before use.
