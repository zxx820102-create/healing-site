# Urology & Kidney Health Canon v1 (English) — Urine · Kidney · Prostate · Stones · Infections

> **In one sentence**: **the kidney is a silent filtering organ, not a "sexual-performance bank"; "kidney deficiency" is not kidney disease; the defining feature of kidney disease is "no sensation," and its greatest hope is "detectable, and manageable."**

---

## ① Concept-clarification face: "kidney deficiency" ≠ kidney disease (the foundation of the whole canon)

If this face is not stood up first, all seven faces after it will be misread. The greatest confusion in the Chinese-speaking context is pressing two entirely unrelated conceptual systems onto the same single word "kidney."

### 1.1 The "kidney" of Chinese medicine — a functional concept system [TCM·Traditional]

- The "kidney" of Chinese medicine is an **umbrella term for a functional system**: governing water, storing essence, governing reproduction and development, governing bone and generating marrow, opening into the ears and the two lower orifices [TCM·Traditional]. It roughly covers the functional projections, in modern medicine, of the urinary, reproductive, endocrine, and skeletal-metabolic systems — the name of "a whole class of functions," **not an anatomical organ** [TCM·Traditional].
- "Kidney deficiency" (shenxu) is a pattern description within that system (a grouped categorization of a set of non-specific presentations: sore lower back and knees, ear ringing, hair shedding, frequent night urination, changed libido, etc.) [TCM·Traditional]. **It is a descriptive categorization, not an organic disease diagnosis** — a sore back does not mean the kidney has grown a disease, just as "running slow" does not mean the CPU is broken.
- This canon keeps [TCM·Traditional] content on its own separate layer: this is **layering, not criticism**. Chinese medicine, as a traditional medical system, has its own theoretical and clinical-practice traditions; the problem has never been the concept itself, but the moment when **a traditional concept is used as an organic diagnosis, or annexed by supplement marketing**.

### 1.2 The kidney of Western medicine — a filtering organ

- The kidneys are two fist-sized filtering organs: filtering about 180 liters of blood into filtrate daily, finally concentrated into 1–2 liters of urine (physiology-textbook framing, MODEL); they also manage blood-pressure regulation, electrolytes, the blood-making signal (erythropoietin), and calcium-phosphorus balance. They are a "sewage plant + endocrine organ," **not a sexual-performance manufacturer**.
- The direct pathway of sexual function runs through blood vessels, nerves, hormones, and psychology (the mechanistic mainline of erectile function = vascular endothelial function) and has no one-to-one mapping to glomerular filtration rate. Late-stage kidney-disease patients can have sexual dysfunction (as part of their overall state), but **the quality of sexual function can never be used to infer renal function in reverse** — this falsification is face ⑧.

### 1.3 The real-harm case face: when "tonifying the kidney" meets unknown ingredients

When a traditional concept is annexed as an organic diagnosis, the most expensive bill is the organ itself:

- **Aristolochic acid nephropathy (AAN)**: in the 1990s a Belgian weight-loss clinic accidentally mixed aristolochic-acid-containing fangchi (guang fang ji) into a "slimming pill"; a group of healthy women who took it developed rapidly progressive renal interstitial fibrosis, a considerable share progressing to end-stage renal disease, with a high rate of urothelial carcinoma found at follow-up (Nortier et al., NEJM 2000); Debelle et al. (Kidney International 2008) characterized it in review as a global problem of "rapidly progressive interstitial nephritis → end-stage renal disease + urothelial malignancy"; a Taiwanese population study confirmed the causal link between aristolochic acid exposure and upper-tract urothelial carcinoma (Chen et al., PNAS 2012), and Taiwan banned aristolochic-acid-containing herbs in 2003. FACT — note: what these victims originally bought was a **"weight-loss supplement,"** not a medicine for treating disease.
- **Illegal additives in weight-loss products**: systematic reviews and testing studies repeatedly find that the most common undeclared additive in "natural" herbal weight-loss products is **sibutramine** (a prescription drug withdrawn from market for cardiovascular risk), plus phenolphthalein, **bumetanide (a loop diuretic)** and others (Phan et al. 2025 systematic review; IRCMJ 2014 detection study). FACT — "supplements" of unknown composition may contain prescription drugs and even diuretics, acting directly on the kidney and cardiovascular system.
- **The common structure**: all three classes of harm (heavy metals / aristolochic acid / illegal additives) come from the four characters "composition unknown," not from the two characters "Chinese medicine." Between properly processed, composition-verified medicines and "kidney-tonifying pills" of unknown provenance stretches an entire regulatory gulf. Telling these two apart is what is simultaneously fair to traditional medicine and to modern medicine.

**In short**: sore back ≠ kidney disease; sexual function ≠ renal function; "kidney-tonifying" supplements ≠ kidney protection. Real kidney protection is the blood pressure, blood sugar, urine protein, and medication list of faces ② and ③.

## ② The chronic kidney disease (CKD) face: the silent pandemic

### 2.1 Prevalence and unawareness

- Chinese multicenter cross-sectional survey (2009–2010, n≈47,204): adult CKD prevalence **10.8%** (95%CI 10.2–11.3), extrapolating to about **119.5 million** people (Zhang L et al., Lancet 2012;379:815-822). FACT. A new-round survey published in 2023 thereafter suggests prevalence is still rising (Wang L et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2023, relayed grade).
- CDC (US) framing: about **9 in 10 (87%) of CKD patients do not know they have it**; even at severe CKD, about **1/3 remain unaware** (CDC Chronic Kidney Disease in the United States, 2023). FACT.
- **Mechanism of the silent disease**: the kidney has a huge compensatory reserve — filtration can fall below half with no symptoms at all; by the time fatigue, swelling, and nausea appear, it is often already irreversible late stage. This is the structure of "10% prevalence, 90% unaware" — it is not that people skip check-ups, but that **routine check-ups often simply omit the two kidney-function items** (see 2.3).

### 2.2 Reading the lab sheet: three lines that decide the early window

| Marker | What it is | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| **Serum creatinine** | Muscle metabolic waste excreted by the kidney | Influenced by muscle mass, diet, age; **"within normal range" ≠ kidney is fine** (see falsification 6, face ⑧) |
| **eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate)** | Filtration function converted from creatinine (optionally with cystatin C), staged G1≥90 → G5<15 | KDIGO 2024 stages by CGA: cause + GFR (G1–G5) + urine albumin (A1–A3); eGFR<60 sustained >3 months, or any albuminuria positive + markers, enters the CKD track. FACT (KDIGO 2024 guideline) |
| **Urine protein / urine albumin** | Direct evidence of the kidney's filter "leaking protein" | Dipstick "+/-, +1 to +4" is semi-quantitative screening; diagnosis and grading use the **urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR)**: A1<30, A2 30–300, A3>300 mg/g. FACT (KDIGO / National Kidney Foundation framing) |

- A single "+" on the urine dipstick deserves to be taken seriously: it can be a transient of fever or exercise, or the first signal of glomerular injury — **re-testing uACR** is the watershed move.
- eGFR 45 with creatinine "within the reference range" can perfectly well coexist — reference intervals come from the mixed distribution of healthy populations, and early CKD hides inside the distribution.

### 2.3 Screening rhythm for high-risk groups

High-risk = diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, long-term painkiller users, family history of CKD, prior acute kidney injury. KDIGO 2024 and multiple guidelines in consistent direction (MODEL; specific frequency follows your doctor):

- **Diabetes**: eGFR + uACR at least once a year (diabetic kidney disease is the number-one cause of end-stage renal disease; cross-graph anchor diab:).
- **Hypertension**: yearly eGFR + uACR; blood pressure itself is a loop that is both cause and consequence of CKD.
- **Long-term painkillers / suspected nephrotoxic medications**: periodic kidney function; proactively give the doctor your medication list (see face ③).
- **General check-up**: one urinalysis + kidney function included covers the baseline; any of the above factors upgrades it to rhythmized screening.

### 2.4 Proteinuria: the most intervenable driver of progression

- Proteinuria is not just "a marker of kidney injury" — it is itself a **progression accelerator**: the higher the A2→A3 grade, the higher the relative risk of progressing to end-stage renal disease, independent of baseline GFR (KDIGO risk-heatmap grading framework, MODEL).
- Intervention mainline (direction-level MODEL; specific plan follows your doctor): blood pressure controlled in place (RAAS blockers' proteinuria-lowering evidence in the proteinuric population), blood sugar controlled in place, low salt, smoking cessation, weight control; in recent years the organ-protection evidence of SGLT2 inhibitors in CKD hard-endpoint trials has added a mainstay to the "kidney-protecting toolbox" (direction-level MODEL).
- **Low-protein diets have boundaries**: a low-protein diet in moderate-to-late CKD (on the order of 0.55–0.6 g/kg/day, KDOQI nutrition-guideline framing, MODEL) may slow progression, but **improper execution causes malnutrition** — it must be customized by a nephrologist + dietitian; "just eat less meat" on your own does not do it.

## ③ The kidney-harming-behavior face: avoidable bills

### 3.1 Long-term high-dose painkillers (NSAIDs) — the most common avoidable kidney injury

- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac, etc.) inhibit prostaglandin synthesis, dismantling, in the renal medulla, "the standby vasodilation for times of ischemia": acutely they can reduce renal perfusion → acute kidney injury; long-term cumulative exposure is associated with CKD progression (AJKD 2020 review; Am J Med 2006 elderly community cohort: high cumulative exposure associated with faster CKD progression). FACT.
- Classic "analgesic nephropathy": long-term heavy mixed use of combination painkillers (historically phenacetin-containing combinations) causing chronic interstitial nephritis (MedlinePlus/textbook framing, MODEL).
- **Risk portrait**: dehydration + strenuous exercise + high-dose ibuprofen is the classic triad scenario of acute kidney injury; elderly, pre-existing CKD, and concomitant diuretics/ACEi raise the risk further (the "triple whammy" combination, guideline framing, MODEL).
- Boundary (honesty clause): occasional use at label doses in people without contraindications carries very low risk; **the problem is "long-term, high-dose, self-dosing in an unassessed state."** Chronic pain management has its own channel (interlink: the pain canon — pain is managed neither by gritting your teeth nor by unsupervised long-term self-dosing).

### 3.2 Unknown-ingredient Chinese herbs and slimming teas — the heavy bill of the four characters "composition unknown"

- The aristolochic-acid case chain: face ① 1.3 (NEJM 2000 / Kidney Int 2008 / PNAS 2012 / Taiwan's 2003 ban). FACT.
- The illegal-additive spectrum of slimming teas/fit pills (sibutramine, phenolphthalein, bumetanide): face ① 1.3. FACT.
- Layered statement (no smearing, no varnishing): ① aristolochic-acid-containing herbs are banned across many regions worldwide — ingredients **already falsified and eliminated by modern evidence**; ② Chinese herbs with verified composition under regulation are outside this item's scope; ③ any product with "a vague ingredient list, an untraceable channel, promises of quick results" — whether called Chinese medicine, supplement, or enzyme drink — carries the same guilt and the same risk. This is **a composition problem, not a culture problem**.

### 3.3 Holding urine and urinary tract infection

- Long-term habitual urine-holding increases urine stasis and opportunities for bacterial ascent (mechanism-grade MODEL), associated with urinary tract infections and imbalanced bladder training; occupationally urine-holding groups (drivers, nurses) carry a higher UTI burden (observational-evidence grade). The workable solution = voiding on demand (a rhythm of roughly every 3–4 hours by day, MODEL); modest postponement for special occasions is fine — no long-term confrontation.

### 3.4 The real boundary of "drink more water to detox"

- The verifiable real benefits of drinking enough water are **stone prevention** (see face ④) and constipation improvement — not the abstract "detox"; a healthy kidney filters 180 liters a day, and **no linear amplification of "drink more, filter more, get cleaner" exists** (physiological boundary, MODEL).
- **Guzzling counter-example one: hyponatremia**. Large volumes of plain water in a short time (on the order of 3–4+ liters within hours) can dilute blood sodium; extreme cases cause water intoxication (physiology consensus, MODEL).
- **Guzzling counter-example two: fluid restriction in heart and kidney patients**. Heart-failure and late-stage CKD patients precisely must **restrict** intake (commonly 1.5–2 L/day including soups and porridge for severe heart failure, practice-guideline framing, MODEL) — "drink more water" is a physician-order-level contraindication for them. Water advice must carry population boundaries.

## ④ The kidney-stone face: from "the worst pain" to arithmetic-able prevention

### 4.1 Renal colic: painology's top-tier specimen

- Features: unilateral flank-abdominal **colic** (not an ache), coming in waves, radiating to the same-side lower abdomen/groin, often with nausea and vomiting and restless pacing that finds no relieving position (textbook framing, MODEL). Renal colic has long been listed among the most severe pains known to human experience (popularly ranked alongside childbirth and trigeminal neuralgia — **analogy-grade ANALOGY**; individual variation is large and it does not enter any serious "worst-pain tournament" comparison).
- Epidemiology: US lifetime prevalence about **13% in men**, about **7% in women**; **5-year recurrence about 35–50% after a first stone** (with the finer framing of the Rule cohort: 11%/20%/31% at 2/5/10 years; literature-range FACT, window taken honestly and noted).
- Differential diagnosis must be handed to a doctor: renal colic must be differentiated from **abdominal aortic aneurysm, appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy** and other lethal/emergency pains — "grit through it" is not an option; "self-diagnosing" even less so.

### 4.2 The prevention evidence for drinking more water — the kind with numbers

- **The Borghi randomized controlled trial (J Urol 1996)**: first-time calcium stone formers randomized; the high-water group targeted urine output >2 L/day (actually about 2.6 L/day at 5 years); **5-year recurrence 12.1% vs 27.0% in controls** (p=0.008). FACT — this is one of the few "drink more water" benefits in urology backed by RCT numbers.
- Framing conversion (MODEL): most guidelines point to "drink to a urine output ≥2–2.5 L/day," which converts to roughly 2.5–3 L of distributed drinking across the day (individualized; heart/kidney patients run in reverse, see 3.4).
- Observational supplement: low urine output (<1 L/day) is a clear risk factor for recurrence — "sedentary + no water + holding urine" is the standard recipe for stones.

### 4.3 Calcium oxalate dietary layering: moderation, not abstinence

- About 75–80% of stones contain calcium oxalate (composition textbook framing, MODEL). The common wrong move is "got stones → quit all spinach/tea, and stop calcium too" — **wrong on all counts**.
- **Calcium should be eaten normally**: Borghi 2002 (NEJM) five-year RCT — a diet of **normal calcium (1,200 mg/day) + salt restriction + reduced animal protein** versus the traditional low-calcium (400 mg/day) diet: fewer recurrences on the normal-calcium arm. Mechanism: food calcium binds oxalate in the gut and leaves with it; low calcium leaves oxalate absorbed bare. FACT.
- **High-oxalate foods in moderation + with meals**: spinach, strong tea, nuts, chocolate are high-oxalate; the strategy is **moderate amounts, blanched, eaten with meals** (the meal's calcium locks oxalate in the gut), not lifelong abstinence (nutrition-guideline framing, MODEL).
- Salt restriction is the hidden protagonist: high sodium drives calcium into the urine, raising urinary calcium and promoting stones (the salt-restriction arm of Borghi 2002; mechanism MODEL).

### 4.4 Stone composition decides recurrence prevention; interventions at direction level

- An expelled/extracted stone should undergo **composition analysis**: calcium oxalate (salt restriction + normal calcium + plenty of water + moderate oxalate), uric-acid stones (acidified urine; purine restriction + urine alkalinization direction), infection struvite stones (the infection source must be treated), cystine stones (hereditary; specialist management) — different compositions, different preventions (guideline framework, MODEL).
- Intervention choices (direction-level MODEL; specifics assessed by urology): extracorporeal shock-wave lithotripsy (ESWL) suits some renal-pelvis/proximal-ureteral stones <2 cm; ureteroscopic stone retrieval has high success for mid-to-distal stones and ESWL failures; large staghorn calculi may need percutaneous nephrolithotomy. **It is not "all stones first get pills and a jumping session"** — size, location, obstruction, and infection status decide the route.

## ⑤ The prostate face: aging, cancer, and the falsification of "night urination = kidney deficiency"

### 5.1 Enlargement (BPH) and cancer are two different things

- **Histological BPH is nearly the default configuration of male aging**: autopsy series (Berry et al. pooled analysis; StatPearls 2024 framing) — about 50–60% of men in their sixties, rising to 80–90% over 70. FACT. It is benign enlargement of the prostate's **transition zone**.
- Prostate cancer prefers the **peripheral zone**; BPH is not a precancerous lesion; the two can coexist but **arise by different mechanisms** (textbook-consensus MODEL). "Enlargement will turn into cancer" has no supporting evidence at the population level (direction MODEL).
- BPH's real trouble is **lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS)**: storage phase (frequency, urgency, nocturia), voiding phase (thin stream, hesitancy, straining), post-void (dribbling, incomplete-emptying feeling); severe cases: acute urinary retention, repeated infections, bladder stones, hydronephrosis (progression chain, MODEL). The IPSS questionnaire is the standard tool for symptom grading (tool-grade MODEL).

### 5.2 The PSA screening dilemma — written as it is

- The dilemma of PSA (prostate-specific antigen) blood screening is **numeric-grade**, not literary-grade:
  - False positives: elevated PSA ≠ cancer (enlargement and inflammation both raise it); a positive brings the burden of biopsy and further tests (CDC patient education lists false positives among screening's main harms). FACT.
  - Overdiagnosis: autopsy and screening studies repeatedly show a considerable share of "indolent" prostate cancers never harm in a lifetime; screening finds them, and they may be over-treated (USPSTF evidence-review framing, MODEL).
  - The benefit side: large randomized trials (ERSPC et al.) show screening can reduce metastasis and prostate-cancer death (effect size depends on population and age; review-grade MODEL).
- **Current authoritative framing (FACT)**: USPSTF 2018 — ages 55–69 **grade C (individual, shared decision with the doctor)**; ≥70 **grade D (do not screen routinely)**. The right answer to the dilemma is not picking a side in "test/don't test" but **shared decision-making**: age, family history, race, and values (attitudes toward biopsy/active surveillance) all go on the scale.
- One sentence for family: PSA is a tool for "more complete information," not an amulet of "test early, rest easy," and not a trap of "tested, then doomed."

### 5.3 "Frequent night urination means kidney deficiency" — falsified

The cause list of increased nocturia (≥2 times/night, sustained) looks nothing like the intuition of the "kidney deficiency" [TCM·Traditional] side, and does not point at "renal function" (StatPearls 2024 et al., FACT-grade cause framework):

1. **Nocturnal polyuria** (daytime fluid redistributing at night — **heart failure**, lower-limb venous return problems, evening heavy water/alcohol intake);
2. **Sleep apnea** (OSA: hypoxia and negative intrathoracic pressure → ANP secretion → more night urine; treating the breathing matters more than treating the bladder);
3. **BPH storage-phase symptoms** (men);
4. Overactive bladder, diabetes (osmotic diuresis), late-stage CKD with declining concentrating ability;
5. Plainly light sleep (waking and going along to the toilet ≠ a bladder problem).

Action line: nocturia **suddenly increasing**, or with swelling, shortness of breath, snoring-with-choking — check heart and breathing first, then prostate and bladder; kidney function is only one station on the way (red-flag chapter interlink).

## ⑥ The urinary-tract-infection face: women's high-frequency question, men's find-the-cause question

### 6.1 Women's high incidence and the evidence tiers of recurrence prevention

- About **50–60%** of women have at least one UTI in life; after a first cystitis **20–40%** recur, and 25–50% of those become recurrent repeaters (AUA recurrent-UTI guideline framing). FACT. Mechanism base: the female urethra is short, the urethral opening neighbors the perianal area, and E. coli is the lead actor (microbiology-consensus MODEL).
- Preventive measures **stratified by evidence tier** (honesty clause — do not promote a handy tip into a golden rule):
  - **Better evidence**: adequate water intake (observational + trial direction); vaginal estrogen (postmenopausal repeaters, prescription-grade); prophylactic medication regimens for confirmed repeaters (prescription per doctor, AUA guideline framework).
  - **Weak evidence, low risk**: **post-intercourse voiding** — "a popular belief held to prevent recurrence, but lacking support from rigorous studies" (the family-medicine literature says plainly no study confirms it; JFP review). Fine to do; do not deify it. INFERENCE / low-evidence tier.
  - **Direction-level**: avoiding spermicide contraception (observational evidence of increased recurrence), wiping direction after stool, avoiding long holds.
- **"Internal heat flares, so it inflames" — falsified**: the causal chain of UTI is **bacteria × anatomy × behavior**, unrelated to "too much heat" [folk concept]. Attributing a urinary tract infection to internal heat costs antibiotic-window time → ascent to pyelonephritis. The infection progresses as usual while herbal tea "cools the fire"; and some herbal/anti-dampness teas are precisely heavy-disaster zones of unknown composition (interlink with face ③). CONTRADICTED.

### 6.2 Male UTI: not a simple infection

- The male urethra is long and prostatic fluid has antibacterial components — **spontaneous simple cystitis is rare to begin with**; a confirmed UTI in a man often signals a structural/functional problem: prostatitis, obstructive enlargement of the prostate, stones, diabetes, catheter-related (guideline framework, MODEL).
- Behavior line: male UTI (especially febrile, recurrent) should be investigated for cause — urine culture + urinary-tract evaluation (residual urine/imaging direction), not ended with "three more days of pills" (direction MODEL).

### 6.3 Asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy: the exception that must be treated

- Asymptomatic bacteriuria is usually **not treated** in non-pregnant populations (no benefit, breeds resistance); **pregnancy is the clear exception**: untreated, up to **30%** progress to acute pyelonephritis, associated with low birth weight and preterm birth; antibiotic treatment significantly lowers that risk (Cochrane review, Smaill et al., 2015/2019 update). FACT.
- Hence early-pregnancy urine testing to screen for bacteriuria is a standard move (screening-guideline framing, MODEL) — "no symptoms, nothing to manage" is the wrong conclusion in a pregnant woman.

## ⑦ The red-flag chapter: these signals do not take the "observe" channel

| Red flag | Why it must be investigated |
|---|---|
| **Painless visible (gross) blood in urine** | **Red flag number one for bladder cancer (and upper-tract urothelial cancer)**. AUA/SUFU hematuria guideline framing: detection rate of urinary-tract malignancy in gross hematuria **13.2%** (microhematuria 3.1%); being painless throughout is precisely the typical rhythm of tumor bleeding (infection/stone bleeding usually hurts). Any single episode of gross hematuria (especially >40 years old, smokers) deserves completion of a urinary-tract evaluation (cystoscopy + imaging direction); blood urine "clearing up on its own" does not close the case. FACT |
| **Persistently foamy urine** | Screens the proteinuria signal: fine dense foam that lingers without dispersing → re-test uACR (face ②). Occasional large sparse bubbles (impact of the stream / cleaning agents) is not a signal (MODEL) |
| **Sudden increase in night urination** | The multi-fork junction of heart failure / sleep apnea / prostate / diabetes (face ⑤ 5.3); a sudden change is not "normal aging" |
| **Oliguria / anuria** | Clearly reduced 24-hour urine output, or near none = acute-kidney-injury-grade emergency (all three classes — obstruction / perfusion / renal parenchyma — must be triaged) |
| **Swelling + reduced urine combination** | Nephrotic/nephritic syndrome direction (heavy proteinuria, hematuria, hypertension combination); urine protein and kidney function need testing promptly |
| **Flank pain + fever** | **Pyelonephritis emergency**: the infection has ascended to the kidney; delay can lead to sepsis; with chills, nausea, vomiting — seek care immediately; same weight in pregnancy (FACT-grade consensus emergency) |
| (Appendix) Acute urinary retention | Lower-abdominal distension pain + complete inability to urinate (the classic emergency of men with BPH); hours-grade emergency catheterization |

## ⑧ The falsification face: eight recurring false claims

1. **"Back pain = bad kidneys"** — CONTRADICTED. The overwhelming majority of back pain comes from the musculoskeletal system (lumbar muscle strain, discs, facet joints); kidney-origin back pain holds a small share of total back pain; renal colic's signature is colicky waves + radiation (face ④), a completely different phase from "sitting-all-day ache." Interlink: **The pain canon** (the back-pain mainline is developed in the pain canon). Counter-evidence: taking "kidney-tonifying pills" for "bad kidneys" delays disc and musculoskeletal evaluation.
2. **"Kidney-tonifying, virility-boosting supplements"** — CONTRADICTED. The main pathways of erectile function are vascular endothelium / nerve / hormone / psychology, with no organic correspondence to "the kidney" (face ①); the supplements side's real bill is aristolochic acid nephropathy and illegal additives (sibutramine/bumetanide, face ① 1.3). Counter-evidence: the Belgian weight-loss clinic's healthy women → end-stage renal disease; "virility" products found to contain undeclared prescription drugs. Interlink: sxh:ed-arousal (the sexual-health canon's ED face).
3. **"Sexual function = kidney function"** — CONTRADICTED. Renal function (filtration/endocrine) and sexual function (vascular-neural-psychological) differ in anatomy and mechanism; late CKD can bring sexual decline (part of the overall state), but normal sexual function utterly fails to exclude pre-uremic kidney disease. Counter-evidence: a pre-dialysis patient's sexual function can be fine while GFR is already <15.
4. **"Flush stones out with beer"** — CONTRADICTED. Alcohol suppresses antidiuretic hormone (ADH/vasopressin) → diuresis → **dehydration afterward** (Roberts JAMA 1963 classic physiology study; StatPearls vasopressin entry); dehydration = concentrated urine = a stone-forming driving force. Beer also carries purines (raising uric acid — uric-acid-stone direction). "Peeing a lot while drinking" is draining water, not draining stones. FACT + mechanism.
5. **"Dark urine = internal heat, must be cooled"** — CONTRADICTED (with correction). Dark yellow urine means first of all concentration (insufficient water, morning urine) and foods/vitamins (B vitamins turn it bright yellow); the "cooling" posture changes nothing about why the color is there, and instead hides the real variable — water — behind herbal-tea sugar; unknown-composition teas stack face ③'s risks on top. Action = watch total volume and the urinalysis, not the "fire." MODEL + folk-concept layering.
6. **"Normal creatinine = kidneys fine"** — CONTRADICTED. Creatinine only climbs visibly out of its reference interval below eGFR 60 (the buffer of muscle reserve); early CKD can perfectly well be "normal creatinine + positive proteinuria"; KDIGO's two indicators together (eGFR + uACR) are the early window (face ②). Counter-evidence: China's 10.8% prevalence coexists with "most check-up takers have normal creatinine." FACT.
7. **"Chug water to detox"** — CONTRADICTED. A healthy kidney's filtration does not scale linearly with water intake; chugging risks hyponatremia, and heart failure/late CKD run in reverse with fluid restriction (face ③ 3.4). Correct posture = drink to pale-yellow urine / adequate distributed amounts; special populations follow the doctor's orders. MODEL.
8. **"Painless blood urine is internal heat / excess heat"** — CONTRADICTED. The malignancy detection rate of gross hematuria is 13.2% (face ⑦); attributing it to "internal heat" and watching and waiting = treating the bladder-cancer red flag as an everyday occurrence. FACT.
