# Diabetes and Blood-Sugar Management Canon v1

> **In one sentence**: diabetes is a disease of "slow soaking" — high glucose soaks blood vessels and nerves in warm water for years, and by the time you feel it, the damage has often already happened; but the story has another half: **between "blood sugar running high" and a diabetes diagnosis lies a genuinely reversible window**, and even diagnosed type 2 diabetes has, under specific conditions, the possibility of medication-free **remission**.
>
> **How to read this volume**: ① the master ledger first (how many are affected · how many don't know) → ② the diagnosis face (three criteria, one table) → ③ the prediabetes-reversal face (the Da Qing study · DPP · remission's real boundaries) → ④ the complication-screening face (a disease found by screening, not found by waiting) → ⑤ the eating face (how people with diabetes eat) → ⑥ the hypoglycemia face (the 15-15 rule) → ⑦ the medication face (direction level) → ⑧ the red flags and the falsification list.

## ① The master ledger face: a question for about half a billion, and the majority who "don't know they have it"

**China's burden (FACT · numbers verified by search)**:

- The 2015-2017 national cross-sectional survey (TIDE · published BMJ 2020): by ADA criteria, prevalence of **diabetes 12.8%** and **prediabetes 35.2%** among Chinese adults (FACT · BMJ 2020;369:m997 original abstract, verbatim).
- The newer national survey (2018-2019 · published JAMA 2021, 173,642 participants): diabetes prevalence **12.4%**, prediabetes **38.1%**; and prevalence rose significantly from 10.9% in 2013 (FACT · JAMA 2021 original abstract, verbatim, PMC8715349).
- IDF (International Diabetes Federation) 2024 page: diabetes prevalence among Chinese adults 11.9%, patient count first in the world (FACT · IDF official site).
- **Awareness is the most glaring number**: in the JAMA 2021 national survey, awareness among people with diabetes was only **36.7%**, treatment rate 32.9%, and of those treated, 50.1% reached glucose control (FACT · original abstract, verbatim). INFERENCE · about **two thirds of patients don't know they have it** — "half don't know" would be the conservative phrasing.
- **Combined magnitude (INFERENCE · transparent arithmetic)**: about 1.1 billion adults × (12.4% + 38.1%) ≈ 560 million; even on the conservative framing, prevalence + prediabetes is on the order of **half a billion** — roughly 1 in every 2-3 adults has abnormal blood sugar.

**What this means (MODEL · multi-source consistent directional framework)**: diabetes is not a "minority's disease" but an environment in which **most adults deserve a blood-sugar check once a year**. One fasting glucose or HbA1c costs a few dozen yuan and a few minutes — possibly one of the highest-value health investments there is.

## ② The diagnosis face: three criteria, one table, and "prediabetes = the reversible window"

### Three diagnostic criteria in one table (FACT · ADA/WHO standard framing; China's 2020 guideline has brought HbA1c into the table)

| Criterion | Normal | Prediabetes | Diabetes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fasting plasma glucose FPG** (no food for at least 8 hours) | <5.6 mmol/L (ADA) / <6.1 (WHO · the traditional Chinese framing) | 5.6-6.9 (ADA "impaired fasting glucose, IFG") | **≥7.0 mmol/L** |
| **Hemoglobin A1c HbA1c** (the "tape recording" of average glucose over the past 2-3 months) | <5.7% | 5.7-6.4% | **≥6.5%** |
| **OGTT 2-hour** (2 hours after 75 g of glucose drink) | <7.8 mmol/L | 7.8-11.0 ("impaired glucose tolerance, IGT") | **≥11.1 mmol/L** |

- The three criteria measure different sides of the same thing: FPG reads "basal secretion," OGTT reads "load handling," HbA1c reads "the average." **Without diabetes symptoms, diagnosis usually requires two abnormal results (which may be across criteria)**; with classic symptoms (excessive thirst, excessive urination, weight loss), one qualifying result suffices (FACT · the guidelines' common rule).
- HbA1c's unique value: it is unaffected by "did you eat breakfast today" — it is the average replay of 2-3 months of glucose — **one checkup's HbA1c is closer to the truth than one fasting glucose**. Anemia, hemoglobinopathies and the like can distort it; then the glucose criteria prevail (FACT·relayed, multi-source consistent).

### "Prediabetes" = the reversible window (progression rates as they are)

- Prediabetes is not "a phrase for scaring people": each year about **5-10% of people with prediabetes progress to diabetes** (FACT·relayed, multi-source long-term cohorts agree on the magnitude); conversely, **a substantial share also revert to normal each year** — the window is two-directional.
- Prediabetes itself is not zero-risk: some vascular damage has already begun in the prediabetic phase (MODEL · same mechanism spectrum as "no symptoms ≠ no damage").
- **The correct first step on a checkup report reading "blood sugar high"**: neither panic nor ignore, but ① work out from the table above which criterion is high, and by how much; ② get one HbA1c (or repeat the fasting test) to confirm it isn't a fluke; ③ start the prediabetes-reversal moves immediately (see face ③) and book a doctor's evaluation. The first step is "retest and confirm," not "scare yourself" or "next time."

### No symptoms ≠ no damage: the mechanism of the slow soak (MODEL · mechanism logic)

High glucose's damage is a **chemical slow soak**: excess glucose non-enzymatically glycates proteins in the vessel wall (proteins get "pickled," cross-linked, stiffened), and with oxidative stress and chronic low-grade inflammation, the large vessels (heart, brain, lower limbs) and microvessels (retina, glomeruli, nerve endings) **degenerate slowly, on a scale of years**. This process **never passes through the "pain" alarm** — the inner sock wears through silently, until it tears into the deep layer. That is why diabetes damage is almost always **found by screening, not found by waiting** (see face ④).

## ③ The prediabetes-reversal face: China's gift to the world, and the real boundaries of "doing it without drugs"

### The Da Qing study: lifestyle intervention still working 30 years on (FACT · evidence chain)

- Begun in 1986 in Da Qing, Heilongjiang: 577 people with impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) randomized, the intervention arm receiving **6 years** of diet/exercise lifestyle coaching. This was **the world's first** randomized controlled trial proving lifestyle intervention can prevent diabetes (FACT · the Lancet series' original papers).
- During the 6-year intervention: diabetes incidence **51% lower** than controls (FACT · the 30-year follow-up paper, Gong 2019 original).
- The effect persisted after the intervention stopped: 14- and 20-year follow-ups kept showing reduced incidence (20-year cumulative incidence about 80% intervention vs about 93% control, a relative reduction of about 43%) (FACT · Lancet 2008).
- **The 30-year follow-up**: median delay of diabetes onset **3.96 years** in the intervention arm (95% CI 1.25-6.67) (FACT · Gong 2019 original, verbatim); subsequent follow-up (Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2014) showed cardiovascular events, cardiovascular death and composite microvascular complications all significantly reduced (FACT·relayed, that paper's conclusion face).
- **In one sentence**: **"eat less, move more" is not motivational filler — it is world-class evidence bought with 30 years of follow-up by Chinese researchers.**

### DPP: how to hold the magnitude (FACT)

The US Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP · NEJM 2002, 3,234 people): lifestyle intervention (goal **≥7% weight loss** + **≥150 minutes per week** of moderate-intensity activity) cut diabetes incidence by **58%** (95% CI 48-66), and metformin by **31%**. The lifestyle intervention worked better in the older arm (FACT · NEJM 2002 original).

**Translating the magnitude (INFERENCE)**: 150 minutes a week = 30 minutes of brisk walking a day × 5 days; 5-10% weight loss = 4-8 kg for an 80-kg person. You don't need to become an athlete — you need **persistent moderate volume**.

### Remission: type 2 diabetes's real conditions and boundaries for "doing it without drugs" (FACT+MODEL)

- **The concept**: remission = blood glucose maintained long-term at normal levels without glucose-lowering medication (the common definition: HbA1c <6.5% for at least 3 months off medication) (FACT·relayed, international consensus framing).
- **The DiRECT trial (UK · primary care)**: newly diagnosed (≤6 years) overweight type 2 patients on intensive structured weight management: **at 1 year, 46% (68/149) achieved remission**; **at 2 years, 36%**; remission climbed stepwise with weight lost — **57% among those losing 10-15 kg, 86% at ≥15 kg** (FACT · Lancet 2018/2019 original abstracts, verbatim); the 5-year follow-up is published, with the maintained-remission share declining over time (FACT · Lancet 2024, the extension exists).
- **The real conditions (FACT+MODEL)**: ① **short disease duration** (newly diagnosed, especially ≤6 years); ② **obesity-related** (the pancreatic β cells are "overworked," not "dead" — after weight loss lifts the fat toxicity, function can recover — the "twin-cycle hypothesis" · MODEL · the Taylor hypothesis, multi-source consistent direction); ③ **large and sustained weight loss**.
- **The boundaries (equally important)**: long-standing disease (β cells already exhausted), type 1 diabetes, and special types **do not apply**; remission is not cure — weight regain can bring relapse, requiring continued monitoring; whether to attempt it and how (especially anything involving stopping medication) **must be physician-led evaluation** — never stop medication on your own.

## ④ The complication-screening face: a disease found by screening, not found by waiting

**The core logic**: complications are almost symptomless early, early intervention works, late stages are irreversible — with three conditions met, the answer is only one: **screen proactively on a schedule** (MODEL · screening logic, based on multi-source guideline-consistent frameworks).

| Screening item | Frequency | What and why |
|---|---|---|
| **Fundus (retinopathy)** | **Once a year** dilated fundus exam / fundus photography | The "direct-view window" on microvascular damage — the retina is the only place the body's microvessels can be seen directly; early on there is no sensation at all, and by the time vision drops it is often mid-late stage |
| **Urine albumin (UACR) + kidney function** | Once a year | One of the earliest signals of diabetic kidney disease; early detection can markedly delay it via glucose and blood-pressure control + medication |
| **Foot examination** | Exam at every visit + one full assessment a year (self-check daily) | Neuropathy (reduced sensation) + vascular disease (poor supply) = the breeding ground where small wounds drag into big ulcers |
| **Lipids + blood pressure** | Yearly (denser if not at target) | The cardiovascular risk multiplier for people with diabetes; controlling glucose without pressure and lipids = shutting only one door |

- **The dividing lines of HbA1c control, plain-words edition (FACT · guidelines' common rule + individualization principle)**: for most adults with type 2 diabetes the HbA1c target is **<7%** — a number balancing "benefit" against "hypoglycemia risk." **Young, short-duration, complication-free patients can be tighter (e.g., ≤6.5%); older, long-duration patients with hypoglycemia history or multiple comorbidities should be looser (e.g., <8% or looser)** — loosening is not giving up; it is **keeping the harm of hypoglycemic falls/cardiovascular events from exceeding the long-term harm of high glucose** (the individualization logic · MODEL). The concrete target **defers to your own doctor**.

## ⑤ The eating face: how people with diabetes eat

**The master principle (MODEL · multi-source consistent framework)**: there is no "diabetes menu"; there are four levers — "total amount, structure, order, rhythm." The goal of dietary adjustment is **flattening the post-meal glucose curve**, not starvation.

### Eating order: vegetables first → protein next → staple last (FACT · small-RCT consistent direction)

Multiple small randomized crossover trials and reviews agree: **vegetables first, then protein/fat, staple food last** — same food, same total calories, but lower post-meal glucose peaks and smaller swings (FACT·relayed, multi-source RCT consistent; a medium-sized effect). Mechanism (MODEL): vegetable fiber and protein "lay the mat" first, delaying gastric emptying and glucose absorption. **Zero cost, zero sacrifice — the best value-for-evidence micro-adjustment to eating.**

### The staple is not the enemy

- **Iso-caloric substitution** is the correct move: white rice swapped for mixed-grain rice / brown rice / oats / buckwheat noodles — a coarse-fine mix (start at 1/3-1/2 whole grain), similar total carbohydrate yield but slower absorption and denser nutrition (FACT·relayed, guidelines' common advice).
- **The "quit staples" trap (CONTRADICTED · counter-example filed)**: cutting staples entirely → ① hypoglycemia risk (especially for those also on sulfonylureas/insulin — severe hypoglycemia possible); ② ketosis risk (very-low-carb made long-term); ③ unsustainable → rebound. What staples get controlled is **amount and quality**, not existence.

### The "sugar-free foods" trap (CONTRADICTED · counter-example filed)

"No sucrose" ≠ "doesn't raise glucose": the main ingredient of sugar-free biscuits and sugar-free cereals is still **refined starch** — starch ultimately breaks down into glucose in the gut, with glucose-raising power on the same order as sucrose. **Total carbohydrate is the key**; the words "no sucrose" on the label promise almost nothing to the glucose curve (FACT · nutrition-mechanism common framing + multi-source consistent). Reading the ingredient list's order and the total carbs is more useful than reading the advertising line.

### The right posture for fruit

- Fruit is not forbidden — it is a **substitution**: within the total-carbohydrate budget, **replace** a staple/snack serving with a serving of fruit, rather than adding on top (FACT·relayed, guidelines' common rule).
- **The glycemic index (GI) concept**: fruits raise glucose at different speeds (most berries, apples, pears lower; watermelon, lychee, ripe banana higher). Choose low-GI, control the total, whole fruit over juicing (juice strips the fiber = sugar water) (FACT·relayed, multi-source consistent).
- Eating between meals, a fist-sized portion at a time, paired with nuts/yogurt — common practical buffers (MODEL · experience level).

## ⑥ The hypoglycemia face: the more urgent face than high glucose

**Threshold (FACT · ADA)**: blood glucose **<3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL)** is hypoglycemia and needs immediate action.

### The 15-15 rule (FACT · ADA/CDC standard handling)

1. Eat **15 g of fast sugar now**: 3-4 glucose tablets, or half a cup of juice/sugary soft drink, or 1 tablespoon of sugar/honey, or 3-4 sugar cubes.
2. Wait **15 minutes**, retest.
3. Still <3.9 → another 15 g, another 15 minutes, looping until back above the safety line.
4. If the next meal is far, add a carbohydrate-containing snack (e.g., crackers + milk) to prevent a second dip.
5. **Note**: chocolate/ice cream are fat-heavy and slow to raise glucose — not first choice; **nothing by mouth for anyone not fully conscious/unable to swallow** — emergency care now, via glucagon/emergency pathway (FACT · red line).

### Hypoglycemia unawareness: the more dangerous adaptation (MODEL · multi-source consistent)

Long-term repeated hypoglycemia lets the body's warning system "adapt" — the adrenaline alarm (racing heart, tremor, sweating) grows ever duller, and the first symptom may be **the cognitive impairment of a brain short of glucose**. That is one reason for the individualization principle of "rather loosen the target than run low often" (echoing face ④).

### Recognizing nighttime hypoglycemia (FACT·relayed + MODEL)

Clues: nightmares, sweat-soaked sleepwear, morning headache, paradoxically high morning glucose (the Somogyi effect — the body's stress rebound). Suspicious cases should add 2-4 a.m. glucose checks or use a continuous glucose monitor, and **feed the situation back to the doctor for dose adjustment**, instead of toughing it out.

### Extra caution for sulfonylurea/insulin users (FACT·relayed, safety face)

These two drug classes directly drive/supplement insulin secretion, with hypoglycemia risk significantly higher than other orals — older age, reduced kidney function, irregular meals form the high-risk combination. **Skipped meals, intense exercise, alcohol** — all three scenarios demand extra vigilance; recurrent hypoglycemia is an **immediate indication for medical evaluation and dose adjustment**, not something to "tough out."

## ⑦ The medication face (direction level · everything defers to your doctor; this face writes no prescriptions)

- **Metformin = first-line** (FACT · the global guidelines' standing position): cheap, effective, low hypoglycemia risk, the thickest long-term data. **"It harms the kidneys" is the most widely circulated misreading (CONTRADICTED · counter-example filed)**: metformin itself does not harm the kidneys; the real logic is **"how well the kidneys work decides whether it can be used"** — with severely reduced kidney function the drug's clearance is blocked and accumulation risk rises, and only then must it be stopped/avoided. First comes the kidney problem, then the drug's contraindication — the causal order cannot be flipped (FACT·relayed, guidelines consistent). Regular kidney-function checks while on it are routine monitoring, not "the drug hurt the kidney."
- **Insulin ≠ "final judgment" — it is an organ-support tool** (MODEL · directional framework): type 2 diabetes is progressive; β-cell function declines with the years; when orals stop being enough, adding insulin is **replacing a failing organ's function**, isomorphic to "glasses correcting vision." **"One injection and you're hooked/addicted" (CONTRADICTED · counter-example filed)**: insulin is a hormone the human body makes; there is no addiction. Clinically, "once started, never stopped" is indeed common — that reflects **disease need** (or, after short-term intensive treatment for new-diagnosis hyperglycemic crisis lets the β cells rest, some people can even withdraw back to orals — "need" and "addiction" are two different things).
- **SGLT2 inhibitors / GLP-1 receptor agonists: the new heart-kidney benefit evidence face** (FACT·relayed, large cardiovascular outcomes trials consistent in direction): beyond glucose-lowering, these two newer classes show additional benefit on **heart-failure hospitalization, kidney-disease progression, and cardiovascular events in some populations**, and have rewritten the medication landscape of many countries' guidelines (especially with coexisting heart/kidney disease). Mechanism logic: the former makes the kidneys "spill glucose"; the latter delays gastric emptying + centrally curbs appetite and protects the cardiovascular system. **Whether they fit and how to use them — defer to your doctor.**

## ⑧ Red-flag chapter (any of the following — do not wait for the next appointment)

| Red flag | Why it's urgent |
|---|---|
| **Glucose >16.7 mmol/L** or climbing steadily | The approach to ketotic/hyperosmolar crisis |
| **Glucose <3.9 mmol/L not rising after two rounds of 15-15**, or recurrent lows | Severe hypoglycemia can cause loss of consciousness within minutes |
| **Vomiting/abdominal pain + dehydration + fruity (rotten-apple) breath + deep rapid breathing** | **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) emergency** — especially type 1 or after stopping insulin; fatal within hours; emergency department now |
| **Foot wound not healing / blackening / pus** | Diabetic-foot ulcer infection escalation can end in amputation; the earlier it is handled, the more is saved |
| **Sudden vision change (abrupt drop / shadows / floater burst)** | The window of vitreous bleeding/retinal detachment; the earlier, the more sight is kept |
| **Confusion, drowsiness, incoherent speech** | Severe hypoglycemia or hyperosmolar state; bystanders get them to hospital now |

## ⑨ Falsification face: the six most widely circulated claims, one courtroom each

1. **"High glucose feels like nothing, so ignore it" (CONTRADICTED)**: the damage mechanism is the slow soak (face ②), bypassing the pain alarm; an awareness rate of 36.7% means most people accumulate damage inside "feels like nothing." Counter-example mechanism: by the time "something is felt," it is usually late-stage complications. Correct posture: screen on schedule; use numbers, not feelings.
2. **"Sugar-free foods, eat freely" (CONTRADICTED)**: no sucrose ≠ doesn't raise glucose; refined starch converts to glucose all the same; the "sugar-free" label ignores total carbohydrate. Correct posture: read the ingredient list + total carbs, and count them into the budget.
3. **"Glucose-lowering teas / supplements can replace medication" (CONTRADICTED)**: no supplement has ever passed an RCT of "replacing glucose-lowering drugs" caliber; replacing drugs with them = soaking the reversible window into irreversible complications. Correct posture: supplements are at most "lifestyle accessories"; medication and stopping it are decided only by a doctor.
4. **"One insulin injection and you're addicted/dependent" (CONTRADICTED)**: insulin is a self hormone, non-addictive; "can't stop" = disease need (face ⑦). The direct cost of stigmatizing "need" as "addiction": not injecting when needed → glucose uncontrolled → complications accelerating.
5. **"Thin people don't get diabetes" (CONTRADICTED)**: **normal-weight type 2 diabetes (lean-type diabetes)** exists in Asian populations — visceral-fat tendency, β-cell function differences and other mechanisms make Asians develop diabetes at lower BMI (FACT·relayed, Asian epidemiology multi-source consistent: a considerable share of Chinese patients have unremarkable BMI). Thinness is no exemption pass; with family history or symptoms, testing is needed all the same.
6. **"Home meters are inaccurate; the hospital is better" (CONTRADICTED · half counter-example)**: home meters are allowed a margin of error by ISO standards (usually ±15% order), but they are **accurate enough for trend management**. The correct comparison posture: compare against hospital venous blood drawn at the same time (fingertip = capillary whole blood, physiologically different from venous plasma to begin with, more so after meals); calibrate the trend with periodic hospital draws rather than declaring "the meter is dead" over a single differing number. The capillary vs venous plasma framing difference is a physical fact, not a meter error (FACT·relayed).
