# Stroke Rehabilitation Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: stroke rehabilitation is neither "wait for time to nurse the person back" nor "past a certain point, resign yourself" — it is **actively rewriting the brain's motor/language maps with large volumes of repeated, task-oriented practice inside the neuroplasticity window**, while using evidence-based secondary prevention to defuse the mine of "recurrence" and catch "post-stroke depression," the most common and most treatable complication. Recovery efficiency decays over time, but **recovery itself is never capped in a lifetime** — that is this canon's first sentence and its last.

## 0. The one-minute version

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | The systems engineering of recovery after stroke: early rehabilitation engagement + evidence-based training methods + a complication defense line (swallowing / depression / shoulder pain) + secondary prevention + a care system |
| How common | Stroke itself is one of the leading causes of long-term disability; a considerable share of survivors are left with motor / speech / swallowing / mood impairments (details in each face) |
| When to start | Begin early rehabilitation activity within 24–48 h of the condition stabilizing; "very early" ≠ "the harder the better" (AVERT: high-dose mobilization within 24 h gave worse 3-month outcomes) |
| How long is the golden window | Recovery is fastest in the 3–6 months after onset (natural-history data), then efficiency decays; but chronic-phase training still has demonstrable benefit — "lifelong plasticity" stated honestly |
| Most underestimated | Swallowing screening (preventing aspiration pneumonia, the number-one killer complication) and post-stroke depression (about 1 in 3 · treatable) |
| Most common misreading | "Rest and it will heal itself" · "past half a year it's useless" · "therapists are useless, just practice on your own" · "IV drips to flush the vessels prevent recurrence" — falsified one by one in face seven of this canon |
| Where to seek care | Stroke unit / rehabilitation medicine department (a multidisciplinary team of rehabilitation physicians + therapists + speech-language therapists + rehabilitation nursing); secondary prevention follows neurology follow-up |

## 1. Face one: the rehabilitation window — "early" and "lifetime" hold at the same time

### 1.1 Ultra-early (24–48 h): once stable, move — but dose is the key

Thrombolysis / thrombectomy in the hyperacute phase belongs to the first-aid canon; this face starts from "after the condition has stabilized."

- **Guideline-level fact (FACT)**: the 2016 AHA/ASA guideline on adult stroke rehabilitation and recovery (Winstein et al. · PMID 27145936) recommends: rehabilitation should begin **as early as possible** once the condition is stable; measures preventing bed-rest complications such as deep vein thrombosis should start in the acute phase. The recommendation content is the guideline's own framing; the directly verified citation is FACT.
- **Counter-intuitive but important (FACT · RCT)**: the AVERT trial (PMID 25892679 · Lancet 2015 · international multicenter RCT) found that **high-frequency, high-dose very-early mobilization started within 24 h gave worse 3-month outcomes**; post-hoc analysis suggested medium-to-low dose, gradually escalating is safer. The correct plain-language version: **"start early" and "don't start ferociously" hold at the same time** — the ultra-early window logic is "start once stable · begin small · escalate gradually," not "the harsher the better."
- **Epistemic labeling**: AVERT's "worse at 3 months" primary outcome is RCT-directly-verified FACT; "medium-low dose is better" comes from its meta/post-hoc analysis framing, labeled MODEL (this volume does not cite specific dose-threshold numbers that were not directly verified).

### 1.2 The golden window (3–6 months): the season of fastest recovery, and the season where training is most valuable

- **Natural-history evidence (FACT · cohort)**: the Copenhagen Stroke Study (Nakayama et al. · PMID 8172497 · Arch Phys Med Rehabil 1994) measured the natural history of upper-limb recovery: **the overwhelming majority of upper-limb functional recovery happens within about 3 months of onset** (the study's recovery curve plateaus at about 12–13 weeks). This is the empirical source of the "3–6 month golden window" saying.
- **The honest prognosis face (FACT)**: Kwakkel et al. (PMID 12907818 · Stroke 2003) measured that if the upper limb remains completely flaccid early after onset (within weeks), the probability of regaining hand dexterity later is low — **the early examination itself is prognostic information**. This is for honestly calibrating expectations, not for pronouncing abandonment (see 1.3).
- **The golden window does not mean "past the window, void"** — it means "for the same training volume, the return on investment is highest now" — so what should be invested in this period is rehabilitation training of the **correct dose and correct tasks**, not waiting and watching.

### 1.3 Lifelong plasticity: efficiency decays, but there is no cap

- **Chronic-phase benefit evidence (FACT · RCT)**: the EXCITE trial (PMID 17077374 · JAMA 2006) enrolled precisely **chronic-phase** patients (3–9 months post-stroke); constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT) still significantly improved real-life use of the affected arm, and the benefit was retained at 1 year (PMID 18077218 · Lancet Neurol 2008). "Past a few months there is nothing left to train" is falsified by this trial's design itself.
- **Model layer (MODEL)**: neuroplasticity (synaptic remodeling, cortical functional reorganization, use-dependent plasticity) is a lifelong biological process; "efficiency decays" and "never capped" coexist — understandable as **a learning curve that never reaches zero but flattens**. This is the simplest consistent model of existing plasticity and training evidence, not a measured single-mechanism constant.
- **UNKNOWN (honest)**: the "dose–response curve" of plasticity cannot be predicted at the individual level; any assertion that "after such-and-such month there will be no recovery" has no evidentiary support, and conversely "with enough practice recovery to normal is guaranteed" is equally unsupported. The honest statement: **the outcome distribution is wide, training shifts the distribution markedly to the right, and each individual's endpoint is unknowable in advance.**

## 2. Face two: the honest tier-list of evidence-based methods — every method carries its evidence grade

> This face's framing: **FACT** = a directly verified RCT / systematic review / guideline anchor exists; **MODEL** = a direct anchor exists but the inference goes beyond the source text or stands only at guideline-recommendation grade; no exaggeration, item by item.

### 2.1 Constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT) — the benchmark evidence for the chronic-phase upper limb

- **What it is (plain words)**: restrain the unaffected hand (mitt / splint), practice real-life tasks intensively with the affected hand under supervision, reversing the "disuse loop" into "use-driven remodeling."
- **Evidence tier (FACT · multicenter RCT)**: EXCITE (PMID 17077374): patients 3–9 months post-stroke with some active wrist/finger extension improved in daily-life use of the affected hand after two weeks of intensive treatment, retained at 1 year (PMID 18077218).
- **Limits (honest)**: (1) enrollment required the affected hand to **retain basic wrist and finger extension** — the original protocol does not apply to severe paralysis; (2) treatment intensity is high and home-adherence demands are heavy; (3) no extrapolation to other domains such as "walking / speaking." Modified versions (mCIMT) are lower intensity with a larger evidence body but smaller single-trial effect sizes (MODEL · this lane did not directly verify mCIMT single trials).

### 2.2 Task-oriented training — the first law of plasticity: "you get what you practice"

- **What it is (plain words)**: practice real tasks (sit-to-stand, picking up a cup, walking to the kitchen), not isolated "joint range" drills. Repetition + specificity + progressive load is the greatest common divisor of all motor-learning evidence.
- **Evidence tier (MODEL · guideline grade)**: the AHA/ASA guideline (PMID 27145936) supports task-oriented / repetitive training as the core means of motor recovery based on multiple RCTs (the guideline recommendation is directly verified; this lane did not directly verify each cited individual RCT, hence the overall MODEL label). Dose honesty: the effective dose is large (many repetitions within treatment time); "going to the rehab department once a week to run through a routine" does not constitute a task-oriented dose.

### 2.3 Body-weight-supported treadmill training (BWSTT) — the negative face of a popular method, written honestly

- **Evidence tier (FACT · Cochrane systematic review)**: the conclusion framing of Mehrholz et al.'s 2017 updated Cochrane review (PMID 28815562): compared with usual overground walking training, BWSTT **failed to demonstrate** a significant improvement in independent walking after stroke; it may help early in those unable to walk, but the evidence is uncertain.
- **Plain version**: this is a good machine, not a law — there is no evidence that "getting on the treadmill speeds recovery"; **walking returns because of practicing the task of walking itself**. This item is this canon's template for "no exaggeration": a method's negative evidence enters the canon with the same weight as its positive evidence.

### 2.4 Transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) / transcranial electrical stimulation — guidelines exist, but the clinical position is still frontier

- **Evidence tier (FACT · guideline anchor + MODEL · recommendation grade)**: the second edition of the rTMS evidence-based guideline (Lefaucheur et al. · PMID 31901449 · Clin Neurophysiol 2020) assigned recommendation grades to rTMS interventions for chronic post-stroke upper-limb motor recovery; but the same guideline makes explicit that effect sizes in this field are limited and parameters heterogeneous. For transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) in stroke, the guideline gives no strong recommendation.
- **Plain version**: non-invasive brain stimulation is a composite of **active research frontier + limited clinical gain** — worth discussing, not worth deifying. Before accepting it, ask three questions: what is the recommendation grade? How large is the effect size? Is it still investigational use? (In most settings the answer to the last question is UNKNOWN.)

### 2.5 Drug-promoted recovery: fluoxetine's full arc — from positive to triple negative

- **Arc start (FACT)**: the FLAME trial (Chollet et al. · PMID 21216670 · Lancet Neurol 2011): in acute ischemic stroke with motor deficits, the fluoxetine group's motor recovery was superior to placebo (a single-center, relatively small phase II trial).
- **Arc end (FACT · triple negative)**: the three large international validation trials — FOCUS (PMID 30528472 · Lancet 2019 · UK), AFFINITY (PMID 32702334 · Lancet Neurol 2020 · Australia/Asia), EFFECTS (PMID 32702335 · Lancet Neurol 2020 · Sweden) — all **failed to demonstrate** that fluoxetine improves post-stroke functional outcomes (and brought risk signals such as fractures; reported in FOCUS exploratory analyses).
- **Conclusion (MODEL · pooled judgment)**: fluoxetine for "promoting motor recovery" **does not constitute a routine recommendation**. This item is also this canon's methodological template: a single-trial positive must wait for independent replication — medicine has no "one trial settles it forever." Fluoxetine for post-stroke depression is a different matter (see face six).

### 2.6 One unifying framework (MODEL): the common denominator of rehabilitation methods

Plain model: **the brain rewrites itself through "use"; every effective method adds volume, specificity, or motivation to "use"** — CIMT removes fake use and adds real use; task-oriented training adds task use directly; BWSTT adds a safety scaffold to the use called "walking"; brain stimulation adds noise-gating assistance to use-driven remodeling (that mechanism is MODEL). Any passive promise unconnected to "adding use" (pure massage to "unblock meridians," pure machines "activating" in place of active training) should be down-weighted — this is the simplest consistent model into which all items of face two converge.

## 3. Face three: swallowing and speech — block the number-one killer, find the words back

### 3.1 Swallowing: a single sip of water can take the wrong door

- **Why first priority (MODEL · mechanism)**: the most lethal acute complication of stroke is not "the brain itself" but **aspiration pneumonia** — after the swallowing muscles fail, food / saliva / gastric contents enter the airway. Pneumonia markedly raises death and disability risk in the stroke population (mechanism layer is MODEL; the epidemiological weight is described in the guideline).
- **Screening evidence (FACT)**: Hinchey et al. (PMID 16109909 · Stroke 2005): in quality-improvement projects, hospitals that performed **formal dysphagia screening** had significantly lower pneumonia rates. "Everyone gets a swallow check; no oral intake before the check" is one of the single most cost-effective rehabilitation defense lines.
- **Operational common sense (MODEL · guideline grade)**: those who fail screening temporarily take nothing by mouth and switch to alternative feeding; head-of-bed elevation, oral care, and food-texture modification (thickeners / soft diets) are graded by the speech-language therapist / rehabilitation team.
- **Feeding route (FACT · RCT)**: the FOOD trial (Dennis et al. · PMID 15733717 · Lancet 2005): among patients with dysphagia, **early (within days) percutaneous gastrostomy (PEG) feeding was not superior to** nasogastric tube feeding (no improvement in death / poor outcomes); no need to place a PEG prematurely; meanwhile early nutritional-support strategy did not improve outcomes. "Early PEG = faster recovery" is falsified.
- **UNKNOWN (honest)**: which screening tool is best, the optimal texture tiers for thickeners and similar details differ across guidelines; this volume does not impersonate a verdict.

### 3.2 Speech and aphasia: practice works, but "substitute practice" is not treatment

- **Treatment effectiveness (FACT · Cochrane)**: Brady et al.'s Cochrane systematic review (PMID 27245310 · 2016 update): speech-language therapy shows a trend of improvement for post-stroke aphasia, but effect sizes are small and evidence certainty limited, and **treatment dose / intensity is the key variable** — comparison evidence that formal speech-language therapy outperforms informal conversational companionship exists but is of limited strength.
- **The honest counter-anchor (FACT · RCT)**: Big CACTUS (Palmer et al. · PMID 31397288 · Lancet Neurol 2019): in chronic aphasia, **adding** self-administered computerized speech practice on top of usual care showed no meaningful additional functional benefit — there is no evidence that "buy a software and practice alone to replace the therapist" (interlocks with face seven, item 6).
- **The pathway (MODEL · guideline grade)**: assess aphasia type and severity in the acute phase → early high-intensity speech-language therapy → adapt the home communication environment (low-pressure communication, partner training) → long-term follow-up with adjustment. The fine numbers (optimal hours per week) are UNKNOWN.

## 4. Face four: family and caregivers — the main battlefield of rehabilitation is at home

### 4.1 Home modification and fall prevention

- **Risk (MODEL · background)**: hemiplegia + reduced sensation + neglect (unilateral neglect) stack to make fall rates in stroke survivors markedly higher than same-age peers; the general face of the fall–fracture chain is in the aging-care canon (cross-graph anchor agc:fall_fracture_chain).
- **Modification common sense (MODEL · guideline grade)**: grab bars (toilet / shower), non-slip flooring and removal of loose rugs, night lighting paths, frequently used items within reach on the unaffected side, bed/chair heights suited to older bodies, handrails on both sides of stairs. Each is a low-cost, low-risk, high-common-sense engineering item — but the cluster-RCT evidence that "modification lowers fall rates in the stroke subgroup" is limited within stroke (hence MODEL, not impersonating FACT; RCT evidence for home-hazard intervention exists in older populations, though this lane did not directly verify that single item).
- **Caregiver burden and respite care (with UNKNOWN)**: caregivers' depression / exhaustion risk is higher than the general population's (MODEL · background evidence grade); **RCT evidence that respite services (short-term substitute care) improve caregiver outcomes is insufficient, and regional availability varies enormously — honestly labeled UNKNOWN**. The caregiver-health face of the aging-care canon is available (cross-graph anchor agc:caregiver_health).

### 4.2 Shoulder pain and spasticity: the two most common "secondary disasters"

- **Shoulder-pain prevention (MODEL · guideline grade)**: post-stroke shoulder pain is mostly related to traction injury, rotator cuff injury, shoulder subluxation, adhesion / freezing, central pain, and spasticity. Three items of common sense: **never pull on the affected arm** (pulling the affected limb when rising / transferring is the number-one action error in family care); position and support the limb on the affected side in lying and sitting; avoid letting the affected arm dangle for long periods. Whether to use a sling depends on subluxation and the individual (guidelines differ · MODEL).
- **Spasticity management (FACT+MODEL)**: the ladder of spasticity care: positioning and stretching (foundation) → oral medications (systemic side-effect trade-offs) → **local botulinum toxin injection** (Brashear et al. · PMID 12167681 · NEJM 2002 · RCT in wrist/finger flexor spasticity improving tone and passive function) → surgical assessment for severe cases. The evidence that botulinum toxin improves muscle tone is FACT; the downstream extrapolation "better tone = better daily self-care" is limited (MODEL).
- **UNKNOWN (honest)**: the best indications for shoulder slings, and the benefit of "preventive" spasticity injections — this volume does not impersonate a verdict.

## 5. Face five: recurrence prevention — everything in rehabilitation rests on "no second stroke"

> Recurrence outranks every training technique: each recurrence can zero out existing recovery. This face is ordered by evidence strength.

### 5.1 Blood pressure at target — first place, no contest

- **Evidence (FACT · RCT)**: PROGRESS (PMID 12958329 · Stroke 2003 · the disability and dependency endpoint analysis of the blood-pressure-lowering trial in post-stroke/TIA populations): a perindopril-based regimen (with optional indapamide) reduced stroke recurrence and disability-dependency; the main trial's main paper is Lancet 2001 (this lane directly verified the trial's Stroke 2003 endpoint-analysis paper; direct verification of the main paper's citation was not completed — see the receipt's honest list — so this item cites as FACT "PROGRESS demonstrated the benefit of blood-pressure lowering," and does not cite relative-risk numbers that were not directly verified).
- **Plain version**: **"blood pressure at target" is the hardest-evidence, most cost-effective item in secondary prevention.** Targets are individualized (follow neurology / cardiology orders · targets differ with intracranial artery stenosis and similar scenarios); this canon writes no universal number. Home blood-pressure self-measurement + taking medication on time + never stopping on your own are the three things caregivers can directly hold.
- **Cross-graph interlink**: the full chain of vessels and atherosclerosis is in the cardiovascular canon (cross-graph anchor card:canon_v1 · the target graph cardio-map-20260912 actually uses the cardio: prefix · the bridge mapping is in the weave map).

### 5.2 Statins (FACT · RCT) — SPARCL

The SPARCL trial (Amarenco et al. · PMID 16899775 · NEJM 2006): in recent stroke/TIA patients without known coronary disease, **high-dose atorvastatin lowered recurrent stroke risk**. Key points: secondary-prevention statins are a long-term, risk-stratified regimen, not temporary lipid-lowering; benefits and monitoring (liver function / muscle pain) are discussed together. "My lipids are normal so I don't need a statin" is a common misreading — secondary prevention reads risk, not a single lab value (medication decisions follow the doctor · this canon writes no prescriptions).

### 5.3 Blood glucose and metabolism (MODEL · guideline grade)

Diabetes is a major risk factor for stroke recurrence; guideline recommendations exist for glucose management in secondary prevention (this lane did not directly verify specific RCT anchors; labeled MODEL). Cross-domain common sense: the canon of glucose-control methods is on the metabolic line (this graph sets no diab anchor · not in the whitelist).

### 5.4 Antithrombotics: atrial fibrillation is the watershed that forces a weapon change (MODEL · guideline grade)

- Non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke: long-term antiplatelet therapy (aspirin / clopidogrel etc.) is the guideline-grade standard (MODEL).
- **With atrial fibrillation**: oral anticoagulation is significantly superior to antiplatelet therapy (MODEL · guideline-consensus grade; this lane did not directly verify specific RCT anchors such as RE-LY/ARISTOTLE, honestly labeled). "Aspirin alone is enough for AF" is a fatal misreading.
- The balance of bleeding and ischemic risk is assessed by physicians; this canon provides no prescription-level advice.

### 5.5 FAST: the family's first-response protocol for recurrence

**F**ace (facial droop) · **A**rm (arm drift) · **S**peech (slurred speech) · **T**ime (immediately) — at any sign of recurrence, **call emergency services at once — call 120 (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number — *(translator calibration)*)**; record the time symptoms appeared (it determines thrombolysis eligibility); **do not give water or medication, do not drive to hospital yourself bypassing the emergency system** (the emergency system reaches the stroke unit directly). The general canon of emergency recognition and scene response is on the first-aid line; this item is the stroke-specific entrance (MODEL · common-sense and guideline grade; this lane did not directly verify FAST public-campaign effectiveness studies).

## 6. Face six: post-stroke depression (PSD) — the shadow illness of about one third, and it is treatable

### 6.1 How common (FACT+MODEL)

- Hackett & Pickles's updated systematic review (PMID 25117911 · Int J Stroke 2014): pooling observational studies, depression prevalence at any time point after stroke is around thirty percent — **"about 1/3"** is the most stable framing of that literature family (point estimates to the decimal fluctuate with instrument / time point / threshold · this passage is FACT (the review's existence and magnitude) + MODEL (the precise value)).
- Risk factors (FACT · systematic review): early severe paralysis, cognitive impairment, prior depression history and others are associated with PSD (Hackett 2005 predictors systematic review · PMID 16179565).
- Mechanism (MODEL): biological (lesions disrupting monoamine circuits / inflammation) + psychological (sense of loss) + social (roles and economics), stacked in three layers — "post-stroke depression is not weak-mindedness" is the first thing this face says to families.

### 6.2 It is treatable (FACT · Cochrane)

- Treatment: Allida et al., Cochrane (PMID 37417452 · 2023): SSRIs and psychological interventions improve post-stroke depressive symptoms; effect sizes are small-to-moderate and drug side effects (gastrointestinal, bleeding tendency) need weighing.
- Prevention: Allida et al., Cochrane (PMID 32390167 · 2020): antidepressants can lower PSD incidence in non-depressed stroke survivors but carry adverse events; Robinson et al. (PMID 18505948 · JAMA 2008): escitalopram or problem-solving psychotherapy prevented PSD in non-depressed stroke survivors.
- **Interlock with face two §2.5 (honesty clause)**: fluoxetine for "treating/preventing depression" and fluoxetine for "promoting motor recovery" are two different things — the former has Cochrane support (small-to-moderate effect), the latter was negated by three large trials (§2.5). **You cannot negate PSD treatment because the "recovery promotion" failed, nor hype "recovery promotion" with PSD evidence.**
- **Interlink with the mood canon**: PSD is the mood-disorder spectrum instantiated in the stroke setting — assessment tools, suicide red flags, and treatment frameworks share origins with the mood canon (cross-graph anchors mood:mood_vs_disorder · mood:exercise_nma; the exercise-for-mood evidence chain runs through both canons).
- **Red flags (MODEL · guideline grade)**: any post-stroke suicidal ideation, food refusal, or day-night reversal lasting over two weeks → treat as a medical problem; "keeping company and chatting" cannot solve it. PSD is heavily under-diagnosed; active screening (PHQ-9 and similar tools) is something families may request of the medical team.

## 7. Face seven: the falsification face — the six most widely circulated claims, each with its falsifier

> Format: claim → verdict → falsifier (what evidence would prove this verdict wrong) → evidence anchor. Verdict epistemic states: except where labeled, all are FACT (a negation supported by directly verified anchors) or MODEL (pooled judgment).

### 7.1 "Rehabilitation is just resting; bed rest is best"

- **Verdict**: wrong (FACT · directional negation + MODEL · dose details).
- **Why wrong**: prolonged bed rest is itself a complication factory (thrombosis / pneumonia / contracture / disuse — mechanism MODEL, guideline-consensus grade); early activity once stable has RCT-lineage support (guideline anchor PMID 27145936). But **this item's reverse trap must also be stated**: AVERT (PMID 25892679) showed that ferocious high-dose movement within 24 h is equally harmful — "pure rest" and "ferocious training" are both wrong; the correct answer is "start once stable · escalate gradually."
- **Falsifier**: if a high-quality RCT shows the long-term bed-rest group's functional outcomes are non-inferior (or superior) to standard early rehabilitation after stabilization, this verdict is downgraded to CONTRADICTED.

### 7.2 "Past six months it's useless; don't waste money training"

- **Verdict**: wrong (FACT).
- **Why wrong**: EXCITE (PMID 17077374+18077218) enrolled precisely 3–9-month chronic-phase patients and the benefit was retained at 1 year; plasticity is lifelong and efficiency decays (face one §1.3's model framing). Six months changes the **rate of return**, not the **existence** of benefit; "golden window" is investing-timing vocabulary, not sentencing vocabulary.
- **Falsifier**: if systematic reviews of chronic-phase (>6 months) interventions consistently show effect size = 0 after excluding publication bias, this verdict is downgraded.

### 7.3 "Take supplements / Angong Niuhuang Wan to prevent recurrence"

- **Verdict**: supplements for recurrence prevention — no evidence (FACT · absence-of-evidence verdict); Angong Niuhuang Wan — positioning and evidence status written separately and honestly (below).
- **[TCM·Traditional] independent labeling**: Angong Niuhuang Wan is one of the "three treasures" of cooling-opening (releasing) formulas (liangkai sanbao) of traditional Chinese medicine, positioned through the dynasties as **emergency standby medicine for high fever with unconsciousness**, not as a stroke-prevention or rehabilitation drug. The modern evidence status (this lane's TinyFish retrieval log · 2026-09-12): clinical expert consensuses exist (experience/consensus layer); mechanism research concentrates on animal experiments and network pharmacology (preclinical layer); clinical RCTs are few and generally of limited methodological quality; publicly reported placebo-controlled clinical studies have shown **no result superior to placebo** (work associated with the PUMC-team direction · publicly reported framing). **Efficacy verdict: UNKNOWN** — it does not impersonate "ineffective," still less "effective"; and it cannot replace any single item of face five's evidence-based secondary prevention.
- **The supplements part**: no supplement has ever completed an RCT showing reduced stroke recurrence; the four pillars of secondary prevention (blood pressure / statin / glucose / antithrombotic) each have RCT or guideline-grade anchors — at the recurrence-prevention layer, the null hypothesis holds for the whole "bu (supplement)" family.
- **Falsifier**: Angong Niuhuang Wan — if a preregistered, multicenter, placebo-controlled, adequately powered RCT shows improved stroke outcomes, this UNKNOWN upgrades to FACT (positive); same logic for supplements.

### 7.4 "Someone had a stroke — first bleed them / press the philtrum"

- **Verdict**: wrong, and harmful (FACT · mechanism-level negation + absence of evidence).
- **Why wrong**: the only correct first action in stroke emergency is **call 120 (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number) and record the onset time**; bloodletting / philtrum pressure has no evidence of improving stroke outcomes (absence of evidence), and its real cost is **delaying the thrombolysis/thrombectomy time window** — time is brain tissue (every minute of delay has quantifiable neuronal loss · that quantitative framing lives on the first-aid line; this graph does not cite unverified numbers). Needling-to-bleed has no known mechanistic pathway to the cerebrovascular event itself.
- **Falsifier**: if a rigorous RCT shows such interventions improve functional outcomes at discharge, this verdict is downgraded.

### 7.5 "Periodic IV drips to flush the vessels prevent recurrence"

- **Verdict**: wrong (FACT · absence of evidence + mechanism MODEL).
- **Why wrong**: periodic IV infusions in the name of "flushing the vessels" (commonly seasonal "promoting blood circulation" infusions) have no RCT evidence of lowering stroke recurrence (absence of evidence); the pathological basis of recurrence is the **chronic** management of atherosclerosis / embolism / bleeding mechanisms (mechanism MODEL) — a chronic disease does not run on "a few days of infusions," it runs on "at target every day." Infusions themselves also carry administration risks: acute heart failure / allergy / infection.
- **Falsifier**: if a high-quality RCT shows some periodic infusion regimen significantly lowers recurrence rates and guidelines adopt it, this verdict is downgraded.

### 7.6 "Therapists are useless; practicing at home alone is fine"

- **Verdict**: half wrong — "practicing on your own" is necessary but not sufficient (MODEL · pooled).
- **Why wrong**: (1) **direction**: wrong compensation patterns (lifting the shoulder to substitute for arm raise) are extremely costly to correct once trained in — the therapist's first role is "practice it right," only then "practice it enough"; (2) **dose and task design**: effective rehabilitation is a high-dose, task-specific, progressively overloaded prescription (face two's common denominator, MODEL); "moving around casually each day" is not a dose; (3) **empirical speech domain**: self-help computerized aphasia training substituting for formal therapy showed no additional benefit (Big CACTUS · PMID 31397288 · FACT); (4) safety details like swallowing / spasticity / shoulder protection need professional assessment.
- **Stated honestly at the same time**: the supply of rehabilitation professionals really is insufficient, and home practice really is the long-term main body — the right answer is **"therapist sets the prescription + family executes at volume + periodic re-evaluation and adjustment"**, not either-or.
- **Falsifier**: if high-quality evidence shows unsupervised self-training is non-inferior to professionally guided training on functional outcomes (including safety endpoints), this verdict is downgraded.

## Closing

Stroke takes away some abilities, but it cannot take away the prospect of using them — provided one **starts early and steadily, executes at dose, holds the four pillars of blood pressure / statin / glucose / antithrombotic, catches the two defense lines of swallowing and depression, and lets the caregivers be cared for too**. Behind every "can" in this canon stands a directly verified PMID; every "cannot be determined" is honestly written as UNKNOWN — this is both evidence discipline and the greatest respect for those who live through it: never hand out false hope, and never steal real hope.
