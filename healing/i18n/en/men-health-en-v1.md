# Men's Health Canon v1 — the seven faces of silence

> **This canon in one sentence**: men's health's greatest enemy is not any single disease but **silence** — symptoms unspoken, checkups unattended, emotions unacknowledged, help unrequested. This canon spreads that silent ledger open and gives actionable moves face by face, without mockery or preaching: **reframe help-seeking as command, checkups as decisions, and acknowledging emotion as taking back operational control of your life.**
>
> Epistemic labels run through the whole canon: FACT = official/bibliographic evidence verified by the source lane; MODEL = clinical-consensus direction; INFERENCE = extrapolation (explicitly labeled). Interlinks: the women's face in full at **the women's canon** (fem:) · the psychological face at **the mood canon** (mood:) · prostate and urinary at **the urinary canon** (uro:) · cardiovascular at **the cardio canon** (cardio:) · bones and joints at **the joint canon** (joint:) · breathing and OSA at **the resp canon** (resp:) · skin and hair at **the skin canon** (skin:) · sexual health at **the sexual-health canon** (sxh:).

## Chapter 1 — The master ledger face: the silent ledger

### 1.1 The lifespan gap: a number that starts at birth

- **US 2023 final data**: life expectancy 75.8 for males, 81.1 for females — a **5.3-year** gap (NCHS Data Brief No.521, released 2024-12; FACT, verified by the source lane via TinyFish).
- **OECD countries 2023 average**: the sex gap is **5.2 years** (women 83.7 vs men 78.5; OECD Health at a Glance 2025; FACT). The EU 2023 figure is 5.3 years (Eurostat; FACT). On global framing, about 5 years give or take.
- This is the verified result behind the task brief's "about 5-6 years shorter": **the global magnitude is around 5 years; high-income countries cluster at 5.2-5.3 years; some countries exceed 10 years**. Writing "about 5 years" is the safe framing.
- **The key realization**: the gap is not "women live long" but **men dying earlier and more abruptly** — more deaths land on cardiovascular disease, external causes (accidents/occupational exposure/violence), suicide, and alcohol-related death. All of these are **partially intervenable** (the attribution framework is MODEL; the numbers themselves are FACT).

### 1.2 The behavioral face of "men delay seeking care" (layered as it is)

- **Symptom-perception layer (MODEL/consensus)**: men recognize and name symptoms late — chest pain filed under "bad stomach," fatigue under "tired," mood problems under "cheer up." This is not dullness; it is a skill nobody ever taught.
- **Stigma layer (MODEL/sociological consensus)**: in most cultures the male norm of "showing weakness = forfeiting manhood" encodes "seeing a doctor," "admitting it hurts," and "acknowledging emotions" as failure. The cost is paid by the person and the family together: later diagnosis, later staging, a narrower rescue window.
- **The endurance-culture layer (sociological face, described without judgment)**: "tough it out and it passes," "real men carry it" is an effective short-term social strategy and a negative-interest debt on the health ledger long-term. This canon does not mock it — it is the only tool many people were ever handed; what this canon does is hand over a new tool.
- **Behavioral consequences (FACT direction)**: men's coverage of routine checkups and preventive visits is lower than women's (health statistics across countries agree in direction); diseases that are "silent early, painful late" — prostate cancer, colorectal cancer — therefore more often get their first visit already in the symptomatic phase.

### 1.3 The list of men's "silent symptoms" — four masks, one face

**Fatigue, irritability, sexual-function change, poor sleep** — the four most-ignored presentations in men may be four masks of one thing. The candidate list behind the shared mask:

| Possible single thing | Mask-combination clues | First move |
|---|---|---|
| Depression (masked in men, see Chapter 4) | Irritability + fatigue + lost interest + drinking more | Mood self-check → mood-canon interlink |
| Obstructive sleep apnea (more prevalent in men) | Snoring + nighttime urination + morning headache + daytime sleepiness | Sleep evaluation → resp-canon interlink |
| Late-onset hypogonadism (Chapter 3) | Falling libido/morning erections + fatigue + flattened mood | Morning testosterone ×2 |
| Diabetes/metabolic syndrome | Fatigue + thirst + expanding waistline | Fasting glucose + HbA1c |
| Early cardiovascular disease | Falling exercise tolerance + chest tightness | Blood pressure/lipids/ECG → cardio canon |

**How to use it**: two or more of the four masks persisting for weeks is not worth gambling on — it is worth one systematic check. That is a decision, not weakness.

## Chapter 2 — The male face of cardiovascular and metabolic disease (cardio-canon interlink; not re-expanded here)

### 2.1 The framing of men's earlier heart attacks

- **Consensus framing (MODEL, direction robust)**: cardiovascular disease onset has a sex gap — premenopausal women are protected by estrogen, and men **enter the high-risk window earlier, in midlife (ages 40-60)**; after menopause women's risk catches up fast. Conclusion: for men, midlife is cardiovascular's battlefield, not an "I'll deal with it later" territory.
- The mechanism layer (the hormonal-protection hypothesis for women's later onset) is MODEL/INFERENCE; concrete interventions do not change because of it.

### 2.2 The different threshold of abdominal obesity

- Waist-circumference thresholds differ by sex (Chinese standard WS/T 428: **≥90 cm for men, ≥85 cm for women** defines abdominal obesity; WHO's male threshold is ≥94 cm). Men's abdominal (visceral) fat couples more tightly with insulin resistance and dyslipidemia.
- In one sentence: **the waistline is a man's cheapest annual checkup** — one tape measure, 10 seconds.

### 2.3 The "middle-aged man's three-piece set" and the minimal action card

Blood pressure / waistline / blood lipids — definitions, targets, recheck rhythm, and intervention paths **all live in the Cardiovascular Canon** (cardio:); here only the interlink and three sentences from the male angle:

1. All three items can be done at a community hospital/checkup center within 30 minutes, and all have evidence-based interventions.
2. For men, "no symptoms ≠ no disease" holds literally for blood pressure and lipids — they are almost entirely silent until the event.
3. Make "accompany my father/husband/son for one three-piece-set check" a family project — far more effective than exhorting someone to "take care of yourself."

## Chapter 3 — Testosterone and the "male menopause"

### 3.1 The normal spectrum of age-related testosterone decline

- Consensus framing (MODEL): male testosterone declines slowly with age from the 30s-40s, on the order of about 1% per year — **a gentle slope, not a cliff**. It is fundamentally different from female menopause (ovarian function effectively switching off within a few years), which is why the term "male menopause" is itself misleading; the academic term is **late-onset hypogonadism (LOH)**.
- Normal aging, obesity (fat tissue converting testosterone to estrogen), chronic disease, and medications all press testosterone down — **a low value on the lab panel is a clue, not a diagnosis**.

### 3.2 LOH = symptoms + labs, a dual standard (not "blood is low, so supplement")

- **The dual standard (MODEL · international guideline consensus framing: Endocrine Society/EAU pathways)**:
  1. **Symptoms**: falling libido, disappearance of morning erections, erectile difficulty, fatigue, low mood/irritability, declining muscle mass, etc.;
  2. **Labs**: **at least two morning (about 7-11 a.m.) total testosterone** values below the laboratory's lower limit (commonly near the 8-11 nmol/L boundary; laboratories and guidelines differ slightly).
- **Both required.** A single afternoon draw reading low, or an asymptomatic "lab-only low," is not an indication for replacement. Recent cohorts keep re-examining the symptom-hormone correspondence (e.g., the Swedish Vara-Skövde cohort 2026, EMAS follow-on analyses) — diagnostic power on biochemistry cutoffs alone is limited; that is exactly why the dual standard exists.
- For obesity-related functional low levels, **weight loss comes first**: losing weight itself raises testosterone, and pays down the metabolic debt at the same time.

### 3.3 Testosterone replacement (TRT): the honest benefit-risk ledger

- **The clear-benefit zone (FACT/consensus)**: classical (organic) hypogonadism — libido and erectile function, body composition, bone density, quality of life improve substantially.
- **The cardiovascular ledger (FACT · direction verified; exact ratios in the honest-boundaries chapter)**: after the TRAVERSE trial (NEJM 2023), the "non-inferiority" direction holds for major adverse cardiovascular events — i.e., conventional-dose TRT showed no significant increase in cardiovascular events in populations with or at risk of cardiovascular disease; **but signals rose for atrial fibrillation, pulmonary embolism and acute kidney injury** (Corona 2024 systematic review and the verified chain). Assessing cardiovascular risk before TRT and following hematocrit during TRT is the guideline pathway.
- **The prostate boundary (FACT/guidelines)**: **active prostate cancer is an absolute contraindication**; during TRT, PSA and digital rectal examination are monitored per guidelines (the PSA dilemma itself → the urinary canon, uro:). The classic fear that "TRT feeds prostate cancer" is not supported within the monitoring boundary, but the boundary must be respected.
- **"Testosterone = restored youth" falsified** (CONTRADICTED): for middle-aged and older men with normal testosterone, TRT brings no rejuvenation; staking anti-aging, fitness and sexual-function hopes all on injections is gambling a prescription drug on a non-indication.
- **A one-sentence decision framework**: symptoms + two low morning labs + no prostate/cardiovascular contraindication + informed monitoring → treatment is meaningful; any one of the four missing → what needs supplementing first is sleep, weight, alcohol, psychological assessment — not injections.

### 3.4 The scientific dissection of the "vigor" product chain

- Physiological-pathway dissection (MODEL): erection = vascular endothelium + nerves + hormones + psychology, four circuits in parallel. Most ED is **an outpost of vascular disease**, not "kidney deficiency" — the years right after first significant ED are a cardiovascular-event window (cardio/sexual-health interlinks). The correct move: check vascular-metabolic health (blood pressure/lipids/glucose) rather than buying supplements first.
- Industry dissection (FACT · regulatory-bulletin framing): "vigor" supplements have repeatedly been caught by regulators with **illegally added sildenafil analogues** — unknown doses, potentially fatal when combined with nitrate drugs; the "kidney-tonifying" concept has no anatomical or physiological referent (urinary-canon interlink).
- The Belgian weight-loss clinic "Chinese herb nephropathy" case (aristolochic acid end-stage kidney disease) is the historical anchor of the real bill for herbal "supplements" (detailed in the urinary canon; interlinked).

## Chapter 4 — Male depression and the psychological face (mood-canon interlink; only the "male difference" is written here)

### 4.1 The masking of male depression

- **Evidence anchor (FACT)**: Martin, Neighbors & Griffith, JAMA Psychiatry 2013 (PMID 23986338), an analysis of the National Comorbidity Survey Replication (NCSR): male depression more often presents as **"externalizing" symptoms — anger, aggression, heavy drinking/substance use, risk-taking**; screened with the traditional internalizing checklist, men's depression detection is systematically lower.
- **Clinical consequence (INFERENCE, directional)**: men's true depression prevalence may be higher than the statistics — part of it hides behind externalizing masks (drunk driving, road rage, workaholism, gambling, violent outbursts) and gets handled as "bad temper" or "an attitude problem." Mood problems get punished under the name of discipline or character problems, and never treated.
- **The practical recognition card**: for a middle-aged man with "worse temper + drinking more + drive gone + sleep fragmented," the question is not "why don't you pull yourself together" but "how many hours have you slept lately" — and then, one formal mood assessment (tools and boundaries → the mood canon, mood:).

### 4.2 Male suicide rates: the global facts (numbers verified)

- **WHO 2019 framing (FACT)**: global suicide mortality 12.6 per 100,000 in males, 5.4 per 100,000 in females — men about **2.3 times** women (verified by the source lane via TinyFish: consistent with WHO estimates and a 2022 joinpoint trend analysis).
- WHO 2021 estimates: males 12.3 vs females 5.9 per 100,000 (WHO, Suicide Worldwide in 2021, released 2025). US CDC 2024: males 22.2 vs females 5.6 per 100,000 — nearly **4 times**.
- **The structure (consensus/MODEL)**: women **attempt** more; men **die** more — the difference lies mainly in method lethality and missed intervention windows (fewer rescue windows left open, fewer prior help-seeking records). So for men, "will he attempt suicide" has **no reliable intuition**; what you can rely on is asking directly and the care pathway (help-line design in the next section; crisis intervention → mood-canon interlink).

### 4.3 Reframing "men don't ask for help" (the evidence face)

- The fact face (consensus): men draw psychological support from family, friends and professional systems at lower rates than women; "men don't seek help" is a statistical fact, not an instinct — it was trained in by norms, and it can be trained out.
- **The reframing-language evidence (MODEL/direction)**: replacing "asking for help = weakness" with "asking for help = decision and command" is not motivational-poster rhetoric — it matches the directional findings of psychoeducation and destigmatization research: when help-seeking is framed as "commanding the situation, gathering intelligence, directing resources," men's willingness to seek help and adherence improve. Three ready-to-use sentences:
  1. "I'm going to get this checked out and get the full picture" — a checkup is intelligence gathering.
  2. "This one I can't command, so I'm finding someone who can" — a specialist referral is resource dispatch.
  3. "I'll get my own state repaired first, so someone is standing to carry the project" — treatment is maintenance repair.
- How families use it: don't urge "cheer up"; just ask, "I'll go with you to get this sorted — Thursday or Saturday?" — narrowing an open question into a two-option decision.

### 4.4 The alcohol face (the male ledger)

- Male alcohol use disorder and binge-drinking rates are significantly higher than women's across countries' statistics (consensus); alcohol is itself a central-nervous-system depressant — the actual ledger of "drinking to decompress" reads: fragmented sleep (resp-canon interlink) + amplified mood swings + suppressed testosterone + cardiovascular and liver bills (urinary-canon interlink). Among the externalizing masks of male depression, alcohol is the most common one (4.1).

## Chapter 5 — The male-specific screening face

### 5.1 The prostate: the PSA dilemma gets only an interlink and a decision framework

- The PSA screening dilemma (the false-positive chain, overdiagnosis, shared-decision pathways) **lives entirely in the Urinary Canon** (uro:); this canon does not repeat it. The male-side decision framework in one sentence: **from age 50 (45 for high risk — family history/Black men), make one shared "test or not" decision with your doctor; if you test, treat PSA as "information," not a "verdict."**

### 5.2 Abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA): the one-ultrasound ledger

- **USPSTF 2019 formal recommendation (FACT, verified by the source lane)**: **men aged 65-75 who have ever smoked** get **one-time** abdominal aortic ultrasound screening (Grade B); never-smokers aged 65-75 are selective screening (Grade C).
- Why it is worth it: AAA is completely silent, and post-rupture out-of-hospital mortality is extremely high; ultrasound is noninvasive, radiation-free and cheap; elective repair and emergency rupture are two different worlds. Male prevalence is significantly higher than female (consensus/MODEL).
- Action card: add a line to the 65th-birthday gift list for a father/husband — "one abdominal ultrasound."

### 5.3 Colorectal and lung cancer (pricing the smoking history)

- Colorectal cancer: countries' screening-start trends are moving toward age 45 (consensus framing; the specific domestic pathway → mood/urinary red-flag interlinks) — male incidence and mortality both exceed female, and men are less willing to do colonoscopy.
- Lung cancer LDCT: USPSTF 2021 framing — **ages 50-80, ≥20 pack-year smoking history, currently smoking or quit <15 years** get annual low-dose CT (the source lane did not re-verify the original text verbatim; annotated as consensus framing MODEL). Male smoking rates were historically higher, so this item's net benefit is larger for men.
- In one sentence: **smoking years are the most expensive debt inside the body, and LDCT is its annual statement.**

### 5.4 Osteoporosis: not a "women's disease" (joint-canon interlink)

- The counterintuitive fact (MODEL · direction consistent; the single meta-analysis not locked → honest-boundaries chapter): **mortality after hip fracture in older men is higher than in age-matched women** — men's pre-fracture baseline is worse, and diagnosis and treatment come later.
- Male risk factors: low testosterone, long-term heavy drinking, smoking, glucocorticoids, low body weight, falls and sarcopenia (the joint canon, joint:, carries the evaluation and intervention).
- Action card: any fall by a man 65+ deserves to be taken seriously; write "men need bone-density checks too" into the family memo.

### 5.5 The general screening table (men's quick version)

| Age band | Screenings to discuss | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| 20-39 | Blood pressure yearly; informed testicular self-check; weight/waistline | Consensus |
| 40+ | Three-piece set (BP/waist/lipids) + fasting glucose; mood self-check into routine | Consensus (cardio + mood canons) |
| 45+ | Start the colorectal-cancer conversation; prostate shared decision (earlier if high risk) | Consensus / urinary canon |
| 50-80, ≥20 pack-years | Annual LDCT | Framing = USPSTF 2021 (MODEL) |
| 65-75 ever-smoker men | **One-time** abdominal ultrasound (AAA) | FACT · USPSTF 2019 B |
| 65+ | Bone-density assessment (earlier with risk factors); falls and muscle strength | Consensus (joint canon) |

## Chapter 6 — Fertility and urinary face

### 6.1 The right starting point for semen analysis

- The pathway (consensus/WHO human laboratory manual framing · MODEL): trying regularly for **12 months** without success (female partner <35; over 35, start at 6 months) → **both partners get tested**. The male workup is **noninvasive, cheap, fast** (one semen analysis); going first is nothing to be ashamed of and nothing to be late about.
- The factual correction (consensus): male factors contribute about half of infertility cases (alone or combined) — the default order of "can't conceive, test the woman first" is wrong; the correct order is **simplest test first**.

### 6.2 Varicocele

- About 15% of adult men have a varicocele (consensus), higher among infertile men; a palpable and correctable factor — it is one of the few male-infertility causes where "the exam can see it and surgery may improve it" (treatment decisions → urology).

### 6.3 The evidence grade of the "sperm decline" research (as it is)

- **Levine et al. 2017 (Hum Reprod Update, PMID 28981654; FACT bibliographic)**: sperm concentration and total count in Western men fell about 50-60% from 1973-2011 (meta-regression, n≈185 studies / nearly 43,000 people).
- **Levine et al. 2023 update (HRU, PMID 36377604; FACT bibliographic)**: extended worldwide, with a directional **acceleration of the decline** into the 21st century.
- Interpretation boundary (INFERENCE → honest): falling counts ≠ proof of a population-level fertility collapse (fertility = count + motility + morphology + the fertilization pathway + intercourse frequency, a composite); but for the individual, the plain implication is: **before trying for a pregnancy, sweep the reversible items once — tobacco, alcohol, heat (hot baths/laptop on the lap), obesity** — more useful than anxiety.

### 6.4 Male urinary red flags (carried by the urinary-canon interlink)

Blood in urine/semen, sudden change in urinary habits, painless testicular enlargement (in younger men beware germ-cell tumors), persistent perineal pain — the red-flag management pathways all live in the urinary canon; only pointers here.

## Chapter 7 — Red-flag chapter (can be forwarded on its own)

> Any one of the following = no enduring, no searching, no delaying — go straight to care. Before a red flag, the "endurance culture" is suspended.

1. **Crushing chest pain / chest tightness on exertion / cold sweat** — the myocardial-infarction window, emergency (cardio canon).
2. **Sudden one-sided weakness, facial droop, slurred speech** — the stroke window, call an ambulance immediately.
3. **Painless blood in urine or semen** — the urinary tumor and stone chain, urology as soon as possible (urinary canon).
4. **Painless testicular enlargement or heaviness** (especially ages 15-40) — germ-cell tumor, same-day care.
5. **Tearing abdominal/back pain** (especially men 65+ who ever smoked, or with a known aneurysm) — the AAA rupture window, emergency.
6. **Sudden change in urinary habits + persistent bone pain in the lower back/pelvis** — the prostate progression chain, urology (urinary canon).
7. **Loud snoring + witnessed apneas + irresistible daytime sleepiness** — OSA evaluation (resp canon); treatable, and treating it lowers blood pressure.
8. **Morning erections fully gone for months** — a three-track system check: vascular/hormonal/psychological (sexual-health / cardio / Chapter 3).
9. **Low mood or irritability ≥2 weeks + drinking more + interests gone** — a formal depression assessment (mood canon).
10. **Any suicidal thoughts** — psychiatric emergency/crisis line; this is a medical emergency, not a willpower event.

## Chapter 8 — Falsification face (each with its verdict and evidence status)

1. **"Men don't need checkups" — CONTRADICTED.** The lifespan gap is 5.3 years (US 2023, FACT) plus the direct cost of the later-diagnosis chain; AAA's "one ultrasound" (USPSTF Grade B, FACT) and the three-piece set are one-shot items with long-term compound interest. Counter-evidence: delete the checkup and all four chains — prostate/colorectal/vascular/metabolic — convert to first presentation in the symptomatic phase.
2. **"Sexual-function problems are fine with supplements" — CONTRADICTED.** ED is a vascular outpost (Chapter 3, 3.4); the supplement chain's measured bill is illegal additives and aristolochic-acid kidney disease (FACT · regulatory bulletins); check the metabolic-vascular side first, and prescription drugs have a legitimate channel too. Counter-evidence: while taking "vigor supplements," atherosclerosis advances exactly as before.
3. **"Being emotional = not a man" — CONTRADICTED.** Depression is a diagnosable, treatable medical condition (mood canon); men externalize as anger/alcohol (Martin 2013, FACT), and the bill for pressing it down is written on a suicide rate 2.3 times higher (WHO 2019, FACT). Counter-evidence: the norm is not biology — it can train "don't cry," it cannot train "immune."
4. **"Snoring means sleeping deep and well" — CONTRADICTED.** Loud snoring + witnessed apneas + daytime sleepiness = the OSA triad, more prevalent in men (consensus); OSA is bound to hypertension, arrhythmia and daytime-accident chains (resp canon). Counter-evidence: what the bystander of "sleeping deep" hears is airway collapse, not rest quality.
5. **"Bald = virile" and "bald = kidney deficiency" — falsified in both directions (CONTRADICTED).** Androgenetic alopecia is the hair follicle's genetic sensitivity to dihydrotestosterone, corresponding to neither sexual ability nor kidney function (the skin canon, skin:, carries the evidence-based channel: minoxidil/finasteride). Counter-evidence: vigor supplements cannot treat follicles, and kidney-tonifying formulas cannot pay an evidence-based bill.
6. **"Big boys don't cry" — CONTRADICTED (evidence face).** Emotional suppression aligns with a worse cardiovascular profile, later help-seeking and fiercer alcohol compensation (consensus/MODEL; the externalization mechanism evidence = Martin 2013 FACT). Crying is a parasympathetic physiological event, unrelated to gender roles. Counter-evidence: in men who quit tears, the bill is paid by blood pressure, alcohol and the silent suicide rate.
7. **"Testosterone = restored youth" — CONTRADICTED.** When the dual standard is not met (no symptoms / labs not low), there is no youth to restore; TRT's real ledger = the clear-benefit zone + cardiovascular signals + the prostate boundary + hematocrit monitoring (Chapter 3, 3.3).

## Chapter 9 — Honest boundaries (this canon's unknowns and non-goals)

1. **Unlocked single studies**: the male hip-fracture mortality meta, Peppard 2013 OSA prevalence, USPSTF 2021 lung-cancer framing's original text — not re-verified verbatim by the source lane; all are written at MODEL/consensus framing above, with no unauthorized promotion to FACT.
2. **TRT's exact effect sizes**: TRAVERSE's directional conclusion is confirmed via the verified chain (Corona 2024 review et al.); exact HR values were not re-verified verbatim — the text states direction only.
3. **No unified global lifespan-gap number exists**: countries' framings/years differ; this canon uses "high-income countries 5.2-5.3 years, globally about 5 years" with sources.
4. **Attribution layering**: the mechanistic attribution of the lifespan gap (the weights of care delay/biology/behavioral exposure) is an active research area; this canon gives a framework, not weights.
5. **This canon does not**: diagnose, prescribe, or replace specialists; help-line design (especially crisis lines) defers to the mood canon and local resources.
6. **Cultural boundary**: the sociological description of the "endurance culture" rests on common observations across many cultures; individual and within-culture variation is large — it does not characterize any individual.
