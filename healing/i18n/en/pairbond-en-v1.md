# Couple Relationship Science Canon v1 (English)

> **Positioning**: this volume answers "what does a good relationship look like, how do relationships form, and how are they maintained". It is the positive counterpart of the relationship-violence canon (the protective face) — the violence canon covers the red lines; this volume covers the baseline. The reader = an adult who wants to use research findings to improve their own relationship (adult education, not a therapy manual).
> **First-hand proposition**: a relationship's long-term quality is decided mainly by "what happens in the day-to-day of being together", not by "which person was chosen". This is not a consolation — it is the machine-learning aggregate conclusion of 43 longitudinal couples studies (Joel 2020, FACT-grade anchor; see §2.5).

## §1 The relationship-formation face: how people connect

### 1.1 The real lineage of attachment research (infancy → adulthood)

| Node | Finding | Epistemic state |
|---|---|---|
| Bowlby attachment theory | An infant's attachment to a caregiver is an evolution-shaped behavior system; security comes from "caregiver accessible + responsive" | MODEL (theoretical framework; developmental-psychology mainstream) |
| Ainsworth strange situation | Infant observation yields three patterns — secure / anxious-ambivalent / avoidant (disorganized added later) — with distribution ratios around 6:2:2, floating | FACT (the observational categories exist) + the distribution figure is a common literature range |
| Hazan & Shaver 1987 | Brought attachment research into adult romance: adult romantic love is isomorphic with infant attachment (PMID 3572722); self-reported three-category proportions approximated the infant distribution | MODEL (the isomorphism proposition; pioneering but methodologically rough) |
| Fraley, Waller & Brennan 2000 | The psychometric verdict: adult attachment is not discrete "four types" but **two continuous dimensions** — anxiety (fear of abandonment) × avoidance (discomfort with closeness); the four "types" are only the four corners of the dimensional space | FACT (the IRT / taxometric psychometric conclusion) |

**The falsification of the viral four-label taxonomy (CONTRADICTED face)**:
- "You're secure / preoccupied / dismissive / fearful, and I'm type X, so we're incompatible" — **compressing continuous dimensions into horoscope-style personality labels is a usage explicitly rejected by psychometrics**. Most people sit in the middle ground of the dimensions, and the same person's position moves across different relationships and different periods (stability is moderate, not fixed).
- "Childhood attachment decides a whole life" — **overdetermination**. Longitudinal evidence supports "tendency continuity", not "lifetime sentence"; relationship experiences in adulthood themselves update one's attachment position (corrective experiences; research face INFERENCE).
- Correct usage: treat the two ECR-style dimensions as **coordinates for self-awareness** (roughly where am I on the fear-of-abandonment / discomfort-with-closeness axes, and toward which end do I slide in which situations) — not as labels to slap on people.

### 1.2 Three pillars of attraction research: proximity, similarity, reciprocity

| Pillar | Finding | Epistemic state |
|---|---|---|
| Proximity | Physical and functional distance decides the "meeting pool" — classic studies and online-era data agree: most relationships begin in settings of repeated contact (classmates / colleagues / neighbors / shared communities) | FACT (repeated observation) |
| Similarity | Similarity of attitudes and values supports liking and long-term harmony (the "similarity attraction" effect is robust); complementarity is weak as an evidence-based source of attraction | FACT (similarity) / MODEL (complementarity = an unverified folk narrative) |
| Reciprocity | "Knowing the other person likes me" is one of the strongest amplifiers of liking (repeatedly replicated since Festinger) | FACT |
| Looks and initial attraction | Appearance dominates first impressions and fast mate-selection contexts; but initial attraction's predictive power for long-term satisfaction is limited (in the Joel 2020 face, the individual-difference terms run consistently weak) | FACT (initial) / INFERENCE (long-term) |

**Operational implication**: if you want to meet a relationship, "let yourself repeatedly show up in small groups with shared activities" is the single best-evidenced move — the meeting pool precedes the matching algorithm.

### 1.3 "Good-enough" matching vs the perfect match

- The **predictive validity of ideal partner preferences has been repeatedly meta-analytically tested as weak**: the mate standards people claim (the ideal picture) have small predictive power over their evaluations of, and liking for, a real person in actual interaction (Eastwick, Finkel & Luchies 2013, Psychological Bulletin, PMID 23586697). FACT.
- The Joel 2020 machine-learning aggregate shows the same: **individual-difference variables (personality, attachment, preferences) predict relationship quality far more weakly than "within-relationship perception" variables** (see §2.5). INFERENCE→built into a face.
- The mechanism explanation of "good enough": long-term satisfaction comes more from interaction quality, responsiveness, and joint coping (next section) than from finding "a zero-friction perfect choice". Finding "a person willing to process disagreements together with you" beats finding "a person who will never disappoint you" — the latter is a narrative, not a purchasable spec.

## §2 The relationship-quality prediction face (this canon's hard core)

### 2.1 The Gottman longitudinal studies: what was observed, what was found

Observational design: couples talk for 15 minutes about a contentious topic in the "love lab"; emotional and interaction behaviors are coded second by second; follow-up runs for years to over a decade (Gottman, Coan, Carrère & Swanson 1998, Journal of Family Psychology; Carrère & Gottman 1999, Family Process — coding just the first 3 minutes of newlyweds' conflict conversations was enough to sort couples into groups predicting later stability / dissolution, with high in-sample predictive accuracy).

**The Four Horsemen** — four interaction patterns significantly associated with later divorce / dissolution in longitudinal data (FACT: the predictive associations recur across multiple longitudinal studies):

1. **Criticism**: escalating a specific problem into a rejection of the person ("you always / you're just this kind of person"). The contrast: a complaint targets the matter itself.
2. **Contempt**: condescending mockery, eye-rolling, belittling — **the strongest divorce predictor in the longitudinal research** (reported at multiple points in the Gottman face, including the physiological layer: couples high in contempt also show worse health indicators such as illness rates over follow-up — the physiological association is INFERENCE).
3. **Defensiveness**: counter-attacking justification and "it's not me, you started it" — escalating conflict into a blame contest.
4. **Stonewalling**: withdrawing from interaction, a blank face, shutting down responsiveness — commonly the behavioral freeze after physiological flooding.

Honest boundary: the Four Horsemen are **risk markers, not causal verdicts** — longitudinal observation proves "early high frequency predicts later dissolution", but the causal proposition "removing the Four Horsemen prevents divorce" has only indirect support from intervention studies (RCTs are hard to run; see §4.1).

### 2.2 Repair attempts: more important than not fighting

- The Gottman line's core distinction: stable and dissolving couples **do not differ significantly in total conflict volume**; the difference lies in the **repair moves** during and after conflict — a self-deprecating joke, a concession signal, a "did we just say that too hard". Successful repair attempts = the relationship can heal itself (book + longitudinal observation face; MODEL-level proposition · the observation face is consistent).
- Whether repair succeeds depends on **whether the receiving side receives the signal** — that is, the relationship's emotional account (the savings of everyday kind interactions) decides how repair signals are decoded: when the account runs a deficit, even a neutral move gets read as hostility (observation face).
- Operational implication (action card B): do not chase "never fighting"; build a **pause / make-up code word both sides recognize**, and bank goodwill daily.

### 2.3 The demand-withdraw pattern

- Definition: one party presses for discussion / change (demand), the other avoids / goes silent (withdraw). Papp, Kouros & Christensen 2009 (JCCP, PMC3218801), a diary study of families: both directions (husband-demands–wife-withdraws / wife-demands–husband-withdraws) occur at **comparable** frequency at home; both patterns are associated with stronger negative emotion during conflict and lower problem-solving rates; the conflict initiator sits more often in the demanding position.
- Falsification of the internet-simplified "only women nag and men flee" (CONTRADICTED: the directional difference is exaggerated; gender is only a mediator of topic type — issues about the relationship itself more easily trigger demanding).
- Mechanism face (MODEL): demand-withdraw is a **complementarily locked system** — the harder one side demands, the farther the other withdraws, and vice versa; breaking it requires either side to unilaterally change moves (the demander switches to a soft startup, the withdrawer switches to a timed commitment to respond).

### 2.4 Long-term satisfaction trajectories: happy newlyweds ≠ happy forever

- Trajectory research (Lavner & Bradbury et al., incl. PMC8153381, a multi-sample newlywed trajectory study): post-marriage satisfaction on average follows a **declining trajectory**, and the decline mostly happens in the first few years — "newlywed bliss" has limited predictive power for long-term outcomes; high early satisfaction does not exempt one from the later slide. FACT (repeated observation).
- The Joel 2020 supplement: the strong predictors of cross-sectional surveys (who is satisfied right now) and of longitudinal prediction (who is still satisfied later) are not the same — **"satisfied now" cannot serve as a guarantee of "satisfied forever"**.
- Warning (INFERENCE explicit): taking the passion level of the dating period as the long-term baseline is the main cognitive source of the "satisfaction cliff" feeling; the other half of the research face's truth is that most long-term couples' satisfaction curves flatten after declining rather than falling off a cliff — trajectories are heterogeneous; there is no single destiny.

### 2.5 Joel 2020: the machine-learning aggregate anchor (this canon's strongest FACT face)

Joel et al. 2020, PNAS 117(32):19061-19071 (PMID 32719123, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1917036117): pooled 43 longitudinal couples studies (samples 10,000+, 11,000+ partners), machine-learning screening of self-report variables.

- **The strongest predictors are within-relationship perceptions**: perceived partner responsiveness, intimacy, gratitude / appreciation of the partner, sexual satisfaction, commitment, subjective well-being.
- **Individual-difference variables (personality, attachment style, self-reported preferences, etc.) run consistently weak in predictive power** — "choosing the right person" explains far less than "what being together looks like".
- The same analysis's honest boundary: even with the strongest variable combination, **individual-level prediction remains imprecise** — relationship outcomes carry a large unpredictable component; any "scientific matchmaking" product claiming high individual precision exceeds the evidence.

## §3 The conflict-science face: conflict itself is not a death sentence

### 3.1 Conflict volume ≠ breakup rate

- Most long-term couples carry **a small set of recurring perpetual problems** (values / housework / money / intimacy frequency / in-laws, etc.) — the Gottman face's commonly cited figure is that about 69% of problems are "perpetual, unsolvable, only manageable" (**a book-level proposition, epistemic state MODEL**: not a peer-reviewed settled figure; the direction is consistent with the research face).
- The difference between stable and dissolving couples is not whether conflict exists, nor conflict frequency, but **how conflict is handled** (§2.1-2.3) and the capacity for repair (§2.2). FACT face (longitudinal replication).
- The function face of conflict (MODEL / INFERENCE): conflict is the developer fluid of difference — zero conflict can mean stonewalling rather than harmony; couples who "know how to argue" argue about the problem; couples coming apart argue about the person.

### 3.2 How to argue: operational action cards

**Action card A · Soft startup**
- Replace "you + character verdict" with "I + specific situation + feeling + request".
  - Counter-example (criticism): "You never care about this family."
  - Correct example (complaint + request): "I've done all the dishes this week and I'm a bit tired. Could we work out a rota?"
- Longitudinal face: the direction of a conversation's first few minutes can separate later outcome groups (Carrère & Gottman 1999) — the soft startup is one of the few "rewrite the first 3 minutes" moves an ordinary person can directly copy.

**Action card B · Pause signals and physiological calming**
- Agree on a pause code word both sides recognize (a word / a gesture); the trigger condition = either party feels their heart rate clearly accelerating, or wants to yell or slam a door.
- A pause = a timed leave (e.g., 20-30 minutes doing something distracting), **with a committed return** — no time limit = a variant of stonewalling.
- Evidence face: stonewalling often stems from physiological flooding (once arousal exceeds threshold, the quality of rational conversation drops sharply · Gottman observation face); the calming window is the precondition for successful repair attempts (MODEL / observation face).

**Action card C · The repair conversation (within 24 hours of the conflict)**
- Three steps: ① each states their own slice of responsibility in the conflict (no demand that the other accept theirs) ② classify this round's disagreement — solvable (find a concrete plan) or perpetual (convert to management: catch it with humor / express acceptance / set a boundary) ③ close with one specific small connecting action.
- Evidence face: the quality of repair attempts separates stable from dissolving couples (§2.2); perpetual problems need "management", not a solution chase (§3.1).

**Action card D · Daily gratitude deposits**
- Once a week at a fixed time, express one specific appreciation / thank-you to the other (pointing at behavior, not personality).
- Evidence face: perceived partner responsiveness and gratitude are among Joel 2020's strongest predictors; the accumulation of everyday goodwill decides the decoding direction of repair signals in conflict (§2.2). FACT association → the action stays within its epistemic state.

## §4 The evidence face's honest boundaries (required reading)

### 4.1 Causal strength: mainly longitudinal observation; RCTs are hard to run

- Randomly assigning couples to a "mutual contempt group" is ethically impossible; intervention RCTs can only measure "change in relationship indicators after teaching communication skills" — complex variables, high attrition. Therefore **the overwhelming majority of this canon's propositions are longitudinal observations or cross-sectional associations** — "the Four Horsemen predict divorce" = a predictive association, not the settled causation "the Four Horsemen cause divorce". INFERENCE explicitly labeled.
- Reverse causation and selection effects stay open: unhappy → more contempt, versus contempt → unhappy; the two paths have not been experimentally separated; the most honest reading is an **evidence face of a two-way vicious cycle** (MODEL).

### 4.2 The boundary between Gottman commercial products and the academic evidence

| Face | Academic evidence | Commercial claim |
|---|---|---|
| Predictive associations of the Four Horsemen + repair attempts | Multiple peer-reviewed longitudinal studies (1998 JFP, 1999 Family Process, etc.) | Same as left |
| ">90% prediction accuracy" | Specific samples, in-sample fitting / retrospective prediction framing; cross-sample extrapolation and individual-level precision are low | Often marketed down to "fortune-telling at a glance" |
| The Seven Principles workshops / app / assessments | The tools' design basis is the research face above | Product efficacy claims mostly un-settled by independent RCTs |
| Couple therapy itself | Meta-analysis face shows a positive effect (e.g., the PubMed-indexed couple therapy meta-analysis · PMID 32551734 et al.; effect significant but moderate in magnitude, floating by therapy type and outcome) | Not equivalent to any single branded course |

Usage discipline: citing the Four Horsemen for **self-awareness and conversation improvement** is safe; citing marketing numbers to make "predict this relationship of mine" decisions is not.

### 4.3 Falsification of the viral misuse of the four attachment labels (summary)

See §1.1. Three verdicts: continuous dimensions, not discrete labels (FACT · psychometrics); label-pairing "we're incompatible" has no evidence (CONTRADICTED); childhood determinism exceeds the evidence (tendency continuity ≠ lifetime sentence).

### 4.4 What this canon leaves blank (UNKNOWN explicit)

- The precise "69% perpetual problems" figure has not been independently peer-review replicated (book-level).
- Cultural extrapolation: the mainstream samples are North American romantic couples; extrapolation to long-term commitment / multi-generation households / unmarried cohabitation structures = UNKNOWN.
- The mechanism behind "perceived responsiveness" as a strong predictor (does a good relationship cause good perception, or does good perception cause a good relationship) is unseparated (UNKNOWN).

## §5 When to seek couples therapy (the care-seeking line · listed separately)

**The red-line face (interlinked with the relationship-violence canon)**: where the relationship contains **fear, control, threats, violence, or coercive sexual behavior**, this canon's action cards do not apply — this is not a communication problem, it is a safety problem. Follow the violence canon (docs/healing/intimate-violence-canon-v1-20260912.md) help-seeking path: safety first — police / Women's Federation / crisis hotlines / medical institutions; conjoint couples counseling in an abusive relationship is regarded by professional consensus as **not recommended, even dangerous** (the abusive party may retaliate after the counseling).

**Signals for seeking couples therapy (couples therapy)** (non-urgent; observe for a few weeks without improvement, then act):
1. The same conflict loop (typically demand-withdraw) replays repeatedly and both sides are powerless to break it unilaterally (§2.3's complementary lock-in mechanism — the research face is exactly that such patterns respond relatively well to therapy).
2. Repair attempts failing in a row: no reconciliation after fights, or rapid relapse after reconciling.
3. Stonewalling as the default state: daily non-transactional conversation has largely vanished.
4. After a major life event (childbirth / bereavement / unemployment / discovery of an affair) both want to continue but cannot talk it through.
5. One or both show persistent low mood / insomnia and other psychosomatic symptoms entangled with the relationship state (connects to the mb:M2 emotion-regulation face; the depression × demand-withdraw association is at Papp 2009).

**The realistic care-seeking path**: the clinical psychology / psychosomatic medicine department of a proper hospital; or a licensed psychotherapist (confirm training in an evidence-based couples-therapy system — EFT / ICBT / Gottman-method orientations all qualify, but go by "trained + supervised", not by brand); couples counseling presumes "both willing"; if one refuses to attend, individual counseling can come first.
