# Women's Health Whole-Life-Cycle Canon v1

**Written for**: anyone who wants to understand their own body (or that of someone they love). Half of humanity will menstruate for about forty years, be pregnant some number of times (or choose not to be), and spend a third of life in menopause — this mainline deserves a canon free of scaremongering, shame and salesmanship.

**Legend**: [FACT·anchor] = literature-anchored with sentence-level correspondence; [MODEL] = textbook/guideline consensus or multi-source consistent; [GUIDELINE] = institutional guideline framing (ACOG/WHO/ADA/EMA/NAMS etc.); [CONTRADICTED] = broken by evidence; [TCM·Traditional] = traditional-context concept, kept separate from the evidence face; red ⇒ = medical red flag (see Chapter 6).

## Chapter 1 The menstruation face

### 1.1 What counts as "normal"
- Cycle length = day 1 of this period to day 1 of the next. The traditional textbook normal range is **21–35 days** with 2–7 days of flow; FIGO 2018's newer framing writes normal frequency as 24–38 days — the two framings coexist, and practice defers to the guidelines of your region [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- **Variation itself is the norm**: the first years after menarche and the menopausal transition vary more; an occasional cycle a few days early or late is not disease. What warrants workup is a **change in regularity persisting ≥3 cycles**, or any single item of FIGO abnormal uterine bleeding (see 6.1) [MODEL].
- What menstrual discharge is made of: endometrium (proliferating and shedding under cyclic estrogen-progesterone control) + blood + cervical mucus + vaginal epithelial cells. **There is no "toxin" to expel** (falsified at 7.1) [FACT · physiology].

### 1.2 Dysmenorrhea: the dividing line between primary and secondary
- **Primary dysmenorrhea**: no pelvic organic lesion; the mechanism is excess endometrial prostaglandins (PGF2α et al.) → over-strong uterine contraction + ischemia. Features: appears within 1–2 years of menarche, worst on days 1–2 of flow, cramping lower-abdominal pain, no other signs. First-line handling = NSAIDs (better taken just before the period or at first pain) + hormonal contraception (combined short-acting pills / the levonorgestrel intrauterine system) [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- **Secondary dysmenorrhea**: an organic cause exists (endometriosis, adenomyosis, uterine fibroids, pelvic inflammatory disease, cervical stenosis, etc.). Warning features: **progressive worsening**, painful intercourse, non-menstrual pelvic pain, intermenstrual bleeding, failure despite full-dose NSAIDs, dysmenorrhea beginning years after menarche. Any one item ⇒ gynecological workup for cause (ultrasound ± MRI; laparoscopy ± pathology is endometriosis's gold standard) [MODEL].
- The dividing line in one sentence: **the pain gets heavier, worse over time, and not only during periods — that is not "normal cramps", it is a signal.**

### 1.3 Endometriosis: the diagnosis delayed for years
- Common prevalence estimates span about 2%–10% of reproductive-age women (imaging/surgical framings differ widely; the true burden is higher) [MODEL].
- **The real burden: an average diagnostic delay of 6.7 years** — a ten-country multicenter study of 1,418 patients aged 18–45 with endometriosis; the delay happens mainly in primary care [FACT · PMID 21718982 · Nnoaham 2011 Fertil Steril]. The same study confirmed its impact on quality of life and work productivity is on a par with major illnesses such as premature aging and heart failure (that cohort's quantified result) [FACT · same anchor].
- Why the delay: progressive period pain is treated as "every woman just endures it", painful intercourse as unspeakable, adolescent abdominal pain as something else. **The countermeasure is not self-reassurance — it is memorizing the warning features of 1.2.**

### 1.4 Premenstrual syndrome (PMS) and premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD), graded
- **PMS**: physical/mood/behavioral symptoms arising in the luteal phase (from 5–11 days before the period), easing within days of menstrual onset, and affecting daily life [MODEL].
- **PMDD**: an independent DSM-5 diagnosis = more severe symptoms, dominated by mood symptoms (marked irritability / loss of emotional control / anxiety / hopelessness), and **clearly interfering with work/relationships/life**. Global meta-analysis (50,659 participants): **diagnosed-PMDD prevalence 3.2% (95%CI 1.7–5.9%); symptom-level (provisional-diagnosis framing) 7.7%** [FACT · PMID 38199397 · J Affect Disord 2024].
- The diagnostic core is not "it feels like it" — it is a **prospective symptom diary across ≥2 cycles** (symptoms must appear in the luteal phase and vanish in the follicular phase) — which prevents booking every mood disorder under menstruation, and also prevents real PMDD being dismissed as "drama" [GUIDELINE · DSM-5].
- A real treatment ladder exists: lifestyle/calcium have moderate evidence → SSRI after diary-confirmed diagnosis (continuous or luteal-phase-only) → hormonal suppression (drospirenone-containing compounds / GnRH agonists with add-back) as the severe-options tier [MODEL·GUIDELINE].

## Chapter 2 The contraception face

> Interlink with the sexual-health canon: sexually transmitted infection protection, consent and communication live at sxh: (v1 · 20260912); this chapter only covers "not getting pregnant".

### 2.1 Typical use vs perfect use: the gap is the truth
Methodological anchor: US NSFG 2006–2010 cycle data (15,728 contraceptive use intervals / 6,683 women; Kaplan-Meier estimates of first-year failure rates) [FACT · PMID 28245088 · Sundaram/Trussell 2017]. Per-method numbers follow the Contraceptive Technology/Trussell table system [MODEL · textbook table; not re-verified cell-by-cell in this lane's EPMC — verification boundary in the receipt]:

| Method | Typical-use failure/year | Perfect-use failure/year |
|---|---|---|
| Subdermal implant | ~0.05% | ~0.05% |
| Copper intrauterine device | ~0.8% | ~0.6% |
| Levonorgestrel intrauterine system | ~0.1–0.4% | ~0.1–0.4% |
| Combined short-acting oral pill | ~9% | ~0.3% |
| Male condom | ~18% | ~2% |
| Contraceptive injection | ~4% | ~0.2% |
| Withdrawal | ~22% | ~4% |
| Fertility-awareness methods | ~24% | ~0.4–5% (method-dependent) |
| Spermicides and other topicals | ~28% | ~18% |

In plain words: **"the drug is near-perfect; people are not"** — the short-acting pill shows a 30-fold gap between perfect and typical use, and the gap is missed doses. For high effectiveness that does not depend on daily memory, choose a LARC (next item). Double protection (short-acting pill or intrauterine system + condom) solves contraception and sexually transmitted infection protection at once.

### 2.2 Emergency contraception: the real windows and effectiveness
- **Levonorgestrel (LNG)**: within **72 hours** of unprotected intercourse, the sooner the better; effectiveness falls off markedly with time; beyond 72 hours it is not recommended to rely on it [GUIDELINE · WHO/ACOG; anchor PMID 21568368 (Drugs 2011 review)].
- **Ulipristal acetate (UPA)**: usable within **120 hours (5 days)**, with relatively stable effectiveness throughout — better sustained effectiveness than LNG (especially in the 72–120 h band) [FACT · PMID 21568368 · Glasier et al., pharmacology framing].
- **Copper intrauterine device**: inserted within **5 days** — the most effective of all emergency contraceptives (failure <0.1%), and it then converts directly into routine highly effective contraception [GUIDELINE · ACOG].
- Shared boundaries: emergency contraception is a **backstop, not a routine method**; further unprotected intercourse later in the same cycle is not covered by the earlier dose; vomiting (within 3 hours of the dose) requires a repeat dose; it does not cause infertility and does not interrupt an implanted pregnancy (the mechanism is mainly delaying/inhibiting ovulation) [GUIDELINE]. "At most three times a year" is a rumor (falsified at 7.7).

### 2.3 Intrauterine devices: the long-underestimated reversible king
- Highly effective (failure on the same order as sterilization), **the best reversibility** (fertility returns immediately on removal), one insertion lasts 3–10+ years (copper device / LNG system differ), hassle-free and cheap over time (long-run amortization) [GUIDELINE · ACOG/WHO].
- Why it was underestimated historically: memories of infection complications from early devices (modern evidence: insertion-period infection risk is brief and low), the outdated notion "only for those who have given birth" (current consensus: usable in the never-pregnant; pain management available at insertion), and concerns about bleeding patterns (copper device may increase menstrual volume vs the LNG system's reduction — both directions can be chosen by need) [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- The real drawbacks, written honestly: insertion/removal is a medical procedure; insertion pain (local anesthesia/analgesia can be requested); the copper device can worsen cramps and flow; spotting in the first 3–6 months of the LNG system is common [MODEL].

### 2.4 The thrombosis risk of combined pills: absolute numbers, not scaremongering
- EMA official framing: venous thrombosis (VTE) risk for combined hormonal contraceptive (CHC) users varies by progestin type, **about 5–12 cases per 10,000 women per year of use** [FACT · EMA CHC referral page (verified at search-summary level via the tinyfish channel)]; baseline in non-users about 2/10,000 women-years [MODEL · EMA communication's common framing, snippet not re-verified verbatim] — i.e., a relative rise of about 2–4-fold, but the **absolute magnitude is "single digits per 10,000 women per year"**.
- The comparison set: **pregnancy and the puerperium themselves carry higher VTE risk than pill use** (the physiological hypercoagulable state) [MODEL]. In plain words: from about 2 per 10,000 up to 5–12 per 10,000 — a real rise, not zero risk; but multiplier-style propagation of "the pill = clots = terrifying" buries "the absolute value is small and smaller than pregnancy itself".
- Risk management: combined pills are contraindicated in smokers over 35 (this factor amplifies risk far more than the drug itself); contraindications such as hypertension, prior VTE, migraine with aura are assessed item by item; transdermal or progestin-only routes can serve as alternatives [GUIDELINE · ACOG]. Combined pills also carry non-contraceptive benefits (dysmenorrhea relief, acne, reduced ovarian/endometrial cancer risk) — both benefits and risks belong on the table [MODEL·GUIDELINE].

## Chapter 3 The pregnancy face

### 3.1 Preconception folate: the evidence and the time window
- The neural tube closes at **4–6 weeks** of pregnancy (about 2–4 weeks after the last menstrual period) — **most people have not yet discovered the pregnancy at that point**. So folate must be started **1–3 months before conception**, 0.4 mg (400 μg) daily, at least through the end of the first trimester; prior neural-tube-defect pregnancy history / high-risk cases use higher doses under medical guidance [GUIDELINE · CDC/WHO/ACOG].
- Evidence face: the folate-deficiency → NTD causal chain was established by randomized controlled trials; policy evidence that national folic-acid fortification programs lower NTD incidence keeps accumulating [FACT · PMID 42280401 (Nutrients 2026 fortification-policy review)].
- In one sentence: **this is one of the few hard windows where "starting after you are pregnant" is already too late.**

### 3.2 First-trimester bleeding and hyperemesis: where the boundaries sit
- Common causes of first-trimester bleeding: implantation bleeding, cervical contact bleeding, threatened miscarriage, embryonic demise, **ectopic pregnancy**. The boundary: **severe one-sided lower-abdominal pain, shoulder pain, rectal pressure, dizziness or syncope ⇒ red flags of ruptured ectopic pregnancy — emergency care immediately** (it can be fatal); painless light bleeding → seek care early for ultrasound + serial HCG, no self-administered "pregnancy-preserving" remedies [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- Hyperemesis gravidarum ≠ "morning sickness to endure": persistent vomiting + weight loss ≥5% + ketonuria/electrolyte disturbance/dehydration ⇒ hyperemesis gravidarum, which needs medical intervention (fluids, antiemetics, nutritional support when necessary); delay harms mother and fetus [GUIDELINE]. The boundary in one sentence: **can't keep fluids down, scant urine, weight falling — it is no longer "endure it".**

### 3.3 Gestational diabetes and hypertension: the screening logic
- **GDM**: OGTT at **24–28 weeks** recommended (one-step 75 g or two-step; guidelines differ by region). Logic: the insulin-antagonist effect of placental hormones (hPL et al.) peaks in mid-late pregnancy — **the screening time point = just before the physiological risk peak** [GUIDELINE · ADA/ACOG]. High-risk (obesity / prior GDM / family history of diabetes) → screen at the first prenatal visit [GUIDELINE].
- **Gestational hypertension / preeclampsia**: the logic of checking blood pressure + urine protein at every visit = preeclampsia is a **progressive, mild-to-severe** multi-system disease; the warning combination (hypertension + proteinuria/organ involvement) is at 6.5. High-risk cases: low-dose aspirin from 12–16 weeks is preventive [GUIDELINE · ACOG].

### 3.4 Postpartum depression: a real illness, not "drama"
- It is a disease, with a measurable symptom spectrum (persistent low mood / loss of interest / insomnia beyond the infant's needs / thoughts of self-harm or of harming the infant), it is screenable (**the EPDS Edinburgh scale**, ≥10 as the clue score), and multi-point screening antenatally + postnatally is advised; distinguish it from the postpartum "maternity blues" (about 50–80% of mothers, self-resolving within 2 weeks); not easing by 2 weeks, or thoughts of self-harm or harming the infant at any time point ⇒ seek professional help immediately [GUIDELINE · ACOG/USPSTF].
- Onset need not be "immediately after birth" — it can occur any time within the postpartum year; fathers can also have perinatal depression [MODEL].
- Treatment works: psychotherapy first line; medication for moderate-to-severe cases (with lactation-compatible options) [GUIDELINE].

### 3.5 Miscarriage ≠ the mother's fault
- Among clinically confirmed pregnancies, miscarriage is about 10–20% (mostly first trimester) [MODEL · commonly cited framing].
- **The main axis of causation is random embryonic chromosomal events**: in karyotype analysis of 778 miscarriage tissue specimens, **55.5% (432/778) were chromosomally abnormal**, trisomy the most common (47% of the abnormal), rising with maternal age and earlier gestational week [FACT · PMID 41062999 · BMC Pregnancy Childbirth 2025]. These are **random meiotic errors**, unrelated to "drank coffee / carried a box / didn't stay in bed".
- Clarification (aimed at universal self-blame): the overwhelming majority of miscarriages are not preventable and not attributable to any of the mother's daily behaviors; after one miscarriage, most subsequent pregnancies go well; only **≥2–3 consecutive losses** triggers the recurrent-miscarriage systemic workup (immunological/anatomical/endocrine/both partners' chromosomes) [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- The grief face: miscarriage is a real loss; grieving is allowed, and partners carry it together; needing psychological support is not weakness (interlink with the need: human-needs graph).

## Chapter 4 The menopause face

### 4.1 Perimenopause: the symptom spectrum and the timeline
- The transition usually begins in one's 40s (4–8 years before menopause): cycles **shorten first**, then turn erratic; vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes/night sweats, VMS); fragmented sleep; mood swings; vaginal dryness; joint aches; changed flow patterns; menopause is defined = 12 months after the final period, established retrospectively [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- **The timeline (the SWAN multi-ethnic cohort, measured follow-up of 1,449 women)**: median **total vasomotor-symptom duration 7.4 years**; still persisting a median of **4.5 years** after the final menstrual period; women with frequent, early-onset hot flashes a median of **>11.8 years** [FACT · PMID 25686030 · Avis 2015 JAMA Intern Med].
- Translation: menopause is not "a spell"; for a substantial share of women it is a transition on the **order of seven to ten years** — which is why "to treat or not" is a real quality-of-life question, not a fuss.

### 4.2 The MHT benefit–risk window: a decision framework (not a prescription)
- **The timing hypothesis, stated as it is**: for **symptomatic women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause**, starting MHT has the most favorable benefit–risk balance; starting later, the risk profile worsens. This is the state of a hypothesis plus a multi-source consistent evidence face, not a transcendent law [MODEL · NAMS 2022 position framing].
- **WHI's historical misreading must be told**: WHI enrolled **50–79-year-olds, mean age 63**, testing the use of "hormones to prevent chronic disease in older women generally"; its headline risk conclusions cannot be transplanted to "younger perimenopausal women treated for symptoms". **The 50–59 subgroup showed more favorable results (including endpoints such as all-cause mortality)** [FACT · PMID 24084921 · Manson 2013 JAMA (intervention phase + extended post-stop follow-up, full texts)]. Across two decades, countless women were denied legitimate treatment by the headline misreading — this is the evidence base of the falsification face at 7.6.
- **The breast cancer face**: an individual-participant-data meta-analysis (worldwide epidemiological evidence pool): MHT use is associated with excess breast cancer risk, **the risk rises with duration of use; earlier start (e.g., around age 50) carries a smaller absolute excess, which declines after stopping**; estrogen-alone differs in magnitude of excess from estrogen-plus-progestin [FACT · PMID 31474332 · Lancet 2019 collaborative-group meta-analysis].
- **The decision framework (no prescriptions written)**: four items on the table = ① symptom burden and duration (the 7.4 years of 4.1) ② individual baseline risks (breast/cardiovascular/thrombosis/hepatobiliary) ③ route-and-regimen differences (transdermal estrogen's lower thrombosis face, etc.) ④ whether the start point falls inside the window. The decision = made jointly by the woman and her doctor, re-evaluated periodically (e.g., yearly) on whether to continue [GUIDELINE · NAMS]. Local (vaginal) low-dose estrogen for genitourinary symptoms belongs to another risk magnitude [GUIDELINE].
- **Non-hormonal options**: SSRI/SNRI class, gabapentin, cognitive behavioral therapy, weight management and cool environments — all effective for VMS (magnitude smaller than MHT); vaginal moisturizers/lubricants are first line for genitourinary symptoms [GUIDELINE].

### 4.3 Bone density and cardiovascular: the silent step-up
- Estrogen is a protective axis for bone: in the 5–10 years after menopause bone loss accelerates, and **hip/vertebral fracture risk steps up**; routine DXA bone density is recommended at 65, earlier for high risk (early menopause / long-term glucocorticoids / fracture history / very low body weight) [GUIDELINE · NOF/USPSTF framing].
- Cardiovascular: before menopause women's coronary risk is markedly lower than age-matched men's; after menopause the gap closes and reverses — lipids/blood pressure/glucose generally worsen across the peri-to-post-menopausal segment, the signal to **move the prevention window earlier** (same screening logic as 3.3) [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- In one sentence: menopause management = symptoms (4.1/4.2) + **the two things that don't cry out in pain: bones and blood vessels**.

## Chapter 5 The screening face

### 5.1 Cervical cancer: HPV vaccine + screening — complementary, not interchangeable
- The vaccine (interlink with the vaccine canon, vax:hpv, details not expanded) covers high-risk types; **the vaccine does not cover all carcinogenic types, and those already exposed before vaccination do not benefit** — so **even the vaccinated follow the screening rhythm** [GUIDELINE · WHO].
- The rhythm (general logic; local guidelines' rhythms differ and local rules prevail): no screening ≤21; from 21–29, TCT every 3 years; 30+ may choose HPV testing every 5 years / co-testing every 5 years / TCT every 3 years; with an adequate prior history, screening may stop at 65 (low-risk population); **after total hysterectomy (for benign reasons), cervical screening stops** [GUIDELINE · USPSTF/ACS].
- Most abnormalities found by screening are not cancer — they are an intervenable precancerous stage; **screening's whole meaning is precisely "precancer is treatable"** [MODEL].

### 5.2 Breast cancer: self-exam is not screening; imaging is
- **Self-examination has weak/negative mortality evidence**: the Shanghai randomized trial of 266,000 textile workers (134,000 in the self-exam instruction arm vs 133,000 controls, 10–11 years of follow-up) — **intensified self-exam instruction did not lower breast cancer mortality** (no difference in breast cancer death between arms; more benign biopsies in the self-exam arm) [FACT · PMID 12359854 · Thomas 2002 JNCI final results].
- Self-exam's correct positioning: **breast self-familiarity** (knowing your own normal, so changes are noticed) + change → seek care (path at 6.6); it is not a screening tool, and "no lump felt = all clear" is an illusion [MODEL·GUIDELINE].
- **Screening = imaging**: mammography is the screening cornerstone for middle-aged and older women (regular from 40+; starting ages/intervals differ by country); Asian women have a higher proportion of dense breasts, and ultrasound and mammography complement each other (dense tissue lowers mammographic sensitivity) [GUIDELINE·MODEL].
- **The density face**: breast density is both a mammography miss factor and an independent risk factor; "dense" itself usually calls for no extra panic-level testing, but know that it changes how much a "normal mammogram" is worth — confirm your density category with your doctor [MODEL·GUIDELINE]. High-risk groups (BRCA and other gene mutations / significant family history) take the enhanced-screening path (MRI etc.), interlinking the brca causal line [GUIDELINE].

### 5.3 General rules of screening
- Screening is **systematic examination of the asymptomatic**; symptoms (bleeding/lump/pain) go through the Chapter 6 red-flag path, not the waiting-for-screening path.
- Every screening carries false-positive and overdiagnosis costs — participate informed, rather than "screened so certainly fine" or "unscreened so certainly safe" [MODEL].

## Chapter 6 The medical red-flag chapter (shareable on its own)

> red ⇒ = be seen the same day; ⇒⇒ = emergency care immediately — call 120 (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number — *(translator calibration)*).

### 6.1 The definition of abnormal bleeding (FIGO framing)
Meeting any one item is abnormal — seek care rather than "observe for half a year" [GUIDELINE · FIGO 2018/PALM-COEIN framework]:
- Frequency abnormal: cycle <21 days or >38 days (FIGO framing; crossing the traditional 21–35 range also warrants care);
- Irregular: >7–9 days' difference between longest and shortest cycle, across ≥3 consecutive cycles;
- Prolonged flow: >8 days;
- Heavy flow: interferes with life / soaks through protection within 2 hours / continuously heavy with anemia symptoms (dizziness, palpitations, fatigue);
- **Intermenstrual bleeding, postcoital bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding (see 6.2)**.
The causal framework = PALM-COEIN (Polyp / Adenomyosis / Leiomyoma / Malignancy + Coagulopathy / Ovulatory dysfunction / Endometrial / Iatrogenic / Not otherwise classified) — "no structural problem found" does not equal "normal"; the functional face also needs handling [GUIDELINE].

### 6.2 Postmenopausal bleeding = must be seen
- Meta-analysis (40,790 women): **91% of endometrial cancer patients (95%CI 87–93%) had postmenopausal bleeding** — it is endometrial cancer's most common and earliest signal [FACT · PMID 30083701 · Clarke 2018 JAMA Intern Med].
- The direction must be stated precisely: 91% is the proportion "of cancer patients who bled"; **reversing it into "bleeding most likely means cancer" is wrong** — most postmenopausal bleeding has benign causes such as atrophy or polyps [MODEL]. But precisely because it is the main early signal and the workup is simple (endometrial thickness/biopsy), **any single episode of postmenopausal bleeding (including spotting) should be evaluated — never explained away as "my old friend came back"** (the folk euphemism for menstruation returning).

### 6.3 Severe pelvic pain
- Sudden severe pelvic pain / severe pain after intercourse / pain with fever ⇒ be seen the same day (time-sensitive causes: adnexal torsion, ruptured ectopic pregnancy, pelvic abscess, appendicitis, etc.) [MODEL]; progressively worsening period pain follows the 1.2 secondary path for cause-finding.

### 6.4 The pregnancy red-flag combinations
- **Markedly decreased fetal movements** (especially a changed pattern) ⇒ be seen the same day [GUIDELINE];
- **Headache + blurred vision/flashing lights + upper abdominal pain + swelling (mainly face and hands)** — any combination ⇒ be seen the same day: preeclampsia red flag, can progress to seizures (eclampsia) [GUIDELINE];
- Vaginal fluid (suspected ruptured membranes preterm), active bleeding, regular contractions (preterm) ⇒ same day; one-sided severe pain/shoulder pain/syncope ⇒⇒ emergency (3.2 ectopic pregnancy) [MODEL].

### 6.5 The breast-lump handling path
New lump / fixed hard knot / skin dimpling or peau-d'orange change / bloody nipple discharge / new nipple retraction / enlarged axillary lymph nodes ⇒ care with **imaging (ultrasound ± mammography, by age and density) ± targeted biopsy** — not "anti-inflammatory pills and watch" (especially for a non-inflammatory lump without redness, warmth or fever). Redness-warmth-pain during lactation is usually mastitis with a different, anti-infective path — **"a lump" and "an inflammatory mass" take two roads; both roads go through a doctor** [GUIDELINE·MODEL].

### 6.6 The universal red-flag line
Chest pain / breathing difficulty / altered consciousness / major hemorrhage (soaking through hourly / continuous unstoppable) / any thought of self-harm ⇒⇒ emergency assistance immediately. Thoughts of self-harm or of harming the infant at any postpartum time point = an obstetric-emergency-level call for help, not weakness (3.4).

## Chapter 7 The falsification face (each item with its verdict and evidence state)

1. **"Menstruation is detox"** [CONTRADICTED]. Menstrual discharge = endometrium + blood + mucus (1.1); the body's metabolic waste exits via liver/kidney/bile routes, nothing to do with the uterus. Falsifier: if it held, people with amenorrhea or past menopause should be "poisoned" — reality is the opposite. Derivative rumors (no hair-washing/exercise/cold drinks during periods) have no evidence; manage by comfort [MODEL].
2. **"A cold womb = infertility; warming the womb = fertility" over-attribution** [TCM·Traditional · kept separate from the evidence face]. "Cold womb" is a traditional functional-pattern description, a self-contained language of care; but the **evidence face**: infertility has workable organic/functional causes (ovulatory disorders, tubal factors, semen factors, endometriosis, etc.), and the standard path = systematic cause-finding first (the endometriosis of 1.3 is often swallowed by the "cold womb" narrative); attributing all infertility to a cold womb and only warming = delaying treatable causes. Falsifier: "driving out the cold" does not open blocked fallopian tubes.
3. **"An acidic constitution is the root of women's illnesses; alkaline constitutions are the way of health"** [CONTRADICTED]. Blood pH is homeostatic at 7.35–7.45; diet cannot change blood acidity; the theory's founder was convicted of practicing without a license and ordered to pay a huge judgment (2018, USA). Falsifier: any commercial test claiming to measure "constitutional acidity" has no diagnostic validity.
4. **"Lymph-detox massage enlarges breasts"** [CONTRADICTED·UNPROVEN]. Breast tissue volume is set by fat/gland; external massage does not add gland; "detox" has no anatomical object; rough massage can injure tissue and **delay care for a real lump** (6.5). Falsifier: no controlled evidence of any kind shows massage enlarges breasts; when "massage worked", first exclude a pre-existing lump that was overlooked.
5. **"Ovary-care essential-oil massage delays menopause"** [CONTRADICTED]. The ovaries sit deep in the pelvis (about thumb-sized); abdominal massage physically cannot reach them; menopause timing is set by the remaining follicle stock, with no mechanism for massage to delay; some "essential oils" contain phytoestrogen-like components that add uncertainty — **this service category is a mature beauty-marketing scam**. Falsifier: no cohort evidence of any kind shows menopause age differing between "ovary-care" recipients and controls.
6. **"Menopause is a disease / should be endured in silence"** [CONTRADICTED]. Menopause is a physiological transition, but **VMS lasting a median 7.4 years, >11.8 years in frequent cases** (4.1 · SWAN measurements) significantly damages sleep and quality of life; for symptomatic women inside the window (<60 / within 10 years of menopause) MHT is a legitimate option with a favorable benefit–risk balance, and non-hormonal options genuinely exist (4.2). The WHI headline's misreading of younger subgroups has been clarified at the [FACT · PMID 24084921] level. "Enduring" is not a virtue; it is handing back seven years of sleep to no one.
7. **"Emergency contraception at most three times a year / frequent use causes infertility"** [CONTRADICTED]. There is no medical "three-times-a-year" cap; current evidence does not show emergency contraception damages long-term fertility (the mechanism is delayed ovulation; it stays out of "routine contraception" only for lower effectiveness and lesser convenience, not toxicity); needing it often = the signal that routine contraception should be upgraded (2.2) [GUIDELINE · WHO].
8. **"Intrauterine devices only suit those who have given birth / it will 'go to the head' and cause trouble"** [CONTRADICTED · outdated notion]. Modern consensus: usable in the never-pregnant; infection risk low and brief; expulsion slightly more likely in the never-pregnant but the absolute value small (2.3) [GUIDELINE · ACOG].
