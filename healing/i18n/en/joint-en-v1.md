# Joint Canon v1 (English) — Bone and Joint Health

> **In one sentence**: **joint pain is not one disease, and joint disease is not one prescription**. The worn-out (OA), the inflamed (RA), the crystallized (gout), the infected (septic arthritis) differ completely in mechanism, and their treatment logics can even contradict each other — telling them apart is the first step of every joint problem.

---

## ① Master face: osteoarthritis vs rheumatoid — the dividing line between "wear" and "inflammation"

The two largest, and most easily conflated, categories of joint pain. Treating OA as RA, or dragging RA along as OA, are the two costliest misreadings in the real world.

| Dimension | Osteoarthritis (OA) | Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) |
|---|---|---|
| Essence | **Mechanical degeneration + low-grade inflammation**: cartilage wears, osteophytes grow; inflammation is secondary | **Autoimmune disease**: the immune system wrongly attacks the body's own synovium; inflammation is primary |
| Typical sites | Weight-bearing, heavily used joints: knees, hips, lumbar spine, distal finger joints (DIP) | Symmetric small joints: wrists, metacarpophalangeal, proximal interphalangeal (PIP); large joints can also be involved |
| Morning stiffness | Short, usually <30 minutes; quickly "loosens up" with activity | Long, usually >30 minutes, typically >1 hour |
| Pain pattern | Hurts with use, eases with rest; late stages hurt even at rest | Worst on waking, eases with activity; rest brings stiffness, not relief |
| Systemic features | Essentially none | Fatigue, low-grade fever, anemia, weight loss possible — it is a **systemic disease** |
| Laboratory | Inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP) usually normal | ESR/CRP often elevated; rheumatoid factor / anti-CCP can be positive |
| X-ray | Osteophytes, uneven joint-space narrowing | Bone erosions, juxta-articular osteoporosis |
| Treatment mainline | Exercise, weight loss, braces, analgesic injections when needed | **Early DMARDs** (systemic immunotherapy), treat-to-target strategy |
| First-visit department | Orthopedics / rehabilitation | **Rheumatology-immunology** (not orthopedics) |

**The cost of the generalized misreading**: "joint pain = arthritis" lumps everything into the single word "arthritis" in everyday speech. An older person's knee pain defaults to the folk "old cold legs" (laohan tui) label / "wear and tear" — years without a check, the 3–6-month RA treatment window missed, joint destruction irreversible. Conversely, draping OA in an "immune problem" and taking "immune-regulating" folk remedies long-term wastes the correct path just the same.

Epistemic labeling: the table above is textbook-consensus grade (FACT); "low-grade inflammation participates in OA progression" is the recent mainstream model (MODEL; mechanistic details still under study).

## ② The knee OA face: exercise is treatment, not damage

### 2.1 Scale: the leading source of mobility disability in older adults

Knee osteoarthritis is the leading cause of pain and mobility disability in the elderly. Prevalence must be stated by stratum — a single number misleads:

- **Symptomatic** knee OA (counting only those with symptoms): at age 60+, about **10% of men, 13% of women** (Zhang & Jordan, Nat Rev Rheumatol 2010).
- **Radiographic** framing (counted if seen on imaging, including the symptom-free): rises continuously with age; roughly on the order of **1/3 at age 65+** (multiple elderly cohorts).
- **Care-seeking share**: among ages 45–64, about 1/3 of women and nearly 1/4 of men have sought care for OA; **approaching half at 75+** (NICE CKS summary of the UK framing).

So "about half of those over 65" holds only barely, and only on the "radiographic/care-seeking" framings — symptomatic prevalence is far lower. When passing this along to family, state the framing; otherwise "has bone spurs on film" becomes "half of the elderly are crippled," manufacturing anxiety.

### 2.2 Core evidence: exercise is treatment

International OA guidelines (ACR/AF 2019, NICE NG226, OARSI) uniformly list **exercise therapy** as first-line core treatment for knee OA, at a strength no lower than any drug. Effective forms:

1. **Quadriceps strength training**: the muscles at the front of the thigh are the knee's "armor"; added strength directly lowers intra-articular stress and improves stability — for mild-to-moderate knee OA it can significantly reduce pain and improve function.
2. **Aquatic exercise**: buoyancy cancels body weight, load-bearing drops to nearly zero; the starting form for those who "hurt the moment they move" — from water walking and water aerobics, transitioning to land.
3. **Cadence adjustment**: a lighter, quicker step rate with a slightly shorter stride lowers per-step knee impact and cumulative load — the immediate move for "can't change your weight yet? change your gait first."

Exercise-prescription logic (not a prescription itself): **start in water/low load → progress strength → multiple times weekly, sustainable for life**. Intensity boundary: "next-day pain after a session does not exceed baseline pain."

### 2.3 The vicious cycle: the more it hurts, the less you move → the less you move, the more it hurts

Pain makes people afraid to move; not moving leads to muscle atrophy, joint stiffness, weight gain; weaker muscles load the joint more, it hurts more, and they dare to move even less. Most knee-OA disability is not the single straight line of "worn out," but the spiral descent of this loop. **The breaking point is almost always "start moving"** — that is also why guidelines list exercise as first-line: it cuts the loop at its very upstream, all at once.

### 2.4 Weight loss: the hardest intervention

The biomechanical ledger (Messier et al., Arthritis Rheum 2005):

- Walking on level ground, the reaction force through the knee joint is about **2–4 times body weight** (higher on inclines and stairs).
- Measured: **for every 1 pound (≈0.45 kg) of weight lost, the knee load per walking step drops by about 4 pounds** — the lever of weight loss is **multiples** of the pounds lost.
- The study's intuitive number: lose 10 pounds, and each knee bears about **48,000 pounds** less cumulative load per mile walked.

So "lose 5 kg" is not "carry 5 kg less" — it is about 20 kg less carried at every step, accumulated over several thousand steps a day. In clinical intervention studies, roughly 10% weight loss combined with exercise significantly reduces pain and improves function. For overweight knee OA, the evidence hardness and effect size of weight loss exceed the vast majority of drugs and injections.

### 2.5 "Exercise wears out joints" — falsified: moderate exercise is precisely cartilage feeding

Cartilage **has no blood vessels** — adult articular cartilage is avascular tissue, and chondrocytes depend entirely on **diffusive exchange with the synovial (joint) fluid** for nutrition; synovial fluid enters the cartilage matrix through **the joint's cyclical pressurization and unloading** (squeeze-recoil, like a sponge drinking and wringing). O'Hara et al.'s classic study (1990) directly confirmed: cyclic loading significantly promotes nutrient transport into cartilage.

In other words: **not moving = not feeding the cartilage**. A sedentary joint has the worst nutrient exchange; moderate, low-to-mid-impact exercise is the cartilage's nutrient pump. Epidemiology likewise slaps down "running wears the knees": Alentorn-Geli et al. (JOSPT 2017, systematic review + meta-analysis) hip/knee OA prevalence — **recreational runners 3.5% < sedentary people 10.2% < competitive runners 13.3%**. The arthritis prevalence of moderate runners is in fact on the order of one-third that of the sedentary; the risk curve is U-shaped — what raises risk is competitive-level training volume and prior major injury, not "running" itself.

Boundary (honesty clause): exercise protects cartilage only on the premise of **moderation + progression + no major structural injury** (after cruciate-ligament/meniscus injury, OA risk rises significantly — a separate matter); with obvious knee varus/valgus deformity or loose bodies, the exercise plan needs customization by rehabilitation/orthopedics.

## ③ Supplements and injections, an honest ledger

### 3.1 Glucosamine and chondroitin sulfate: the evidence, told as it is

- **Trial face**: the largest trial, GAIT (Clegg et al., NEJM 2006, 1,583 knee-OA cases, 24 weeks): four arms — glucosamine, chondroitin, combination, placebo — **no significant difference among arms in the overall population**; in the moderate-to-severe pain subgroup, "combination" was statistically better than placebo — but this was a **subgroup exploratory analysis** (the trial had no statistical power for that subgroup itself), and it has not been reliably replicated since.
- **Guideline face**: the American College of Rheumatology/Arthritis Foundation 2019 guideline **strongly recommends against** glucosamine for knee/hip/hand OA; chondroitin (alone or combined with glucosamine) is likewise not recommended. NICE also advises against routine use.
- **The ledger of "it hasn't hurt me, keep taking it"**: short-term safety is acceptable, but "hasn't hurt" is not "worth taking." The real cost is **opportunity cost**: real money each month, buying away attention and budget that belonged to exercise and weight loss; some formulations have reports of raised blood glucose; and the subtler cost is that the reassurance of "I'm already taking glucosamine" delays effective intervention.

Honest conclusion (mixed MODEL/CANDIDATE state): the "repairs cartilage" marketing does not hold; the exploratory signal in the moderate-to-severe subgroup is unconfirmed and does not justify building a long-term habit.

### 3.2 The "block injection": a short-term tool, not a long-term plan

The honest ledger of **intra-articular glucocorticoid injection**:

- **Positioning**: a **short-term tool** for acute flares, rapid pain relief, and "opening a window" for rehabilitation exercise. Systematic reviews show average analgesia of about **2–4 weeks**, a moderate effect (OARSI 2022 synthesis); NICE recommends it only "when short-term relief is needed."
- **Frequency**: common advice is **no more than 3–4 times per year into the same joint**, with two injections usually ≥3 months apart. "A few shots a year will do it (year after year, repeatedly)" is a misreading — the research face already carries the debate that "repeated injection may accelerate cartilage loss" (raised from Guermazi et al. 2020 onward); the evidence is still evolving, and caution itself is the guideline position.
- **The true boundary of use**: injecting without exercising equals buying only a few pain-free days; injecting then following with exercise/weight loss is how the shot earns its fare. What it buys is a window, not an outcome.

### 3.3 Hyaluronic acid (viscosupplementation): the controversy, told as it is

The controversy is real; both ends presented faithfully:

- **The not-recommended side**: NICE NG226 explicitly does not recommend it as routine treatment; ACR/AF 2019 strongly recommends against; most international guidelines are "conditionally against" (Australian Prescriber 2026 review framing) — rationale: small effect sizes versus placebo, limited clinical value.
- **The relatively favorable side**: some systematic reviews show good safety and some pain reduction within 6 months in certain patients; in practice it is often used as the alternative for "those unsuited to / unwilling to use steroids" (coverage policies vary by country's health system).

Honest conclusion: **the evidence does not support it as first-line**; if tried, set "did things actually improve after 2–3 injections" as the stop line, and do not refill endlessly. The drug face ends here — for actual medication, see your clinician in person.

## ④ The RA dedicated face: a systemic disease, not "old cold legs"

### 4.1 The clue formula

**Morning stiffness >30 minutes (typically >1 hour) + symmetric small-joint pain/swelling** (both wrists, metacarpophalangeal, proximal interphalangeal) — with both lines present, the RA workup path should start (rheumatology-immunology, inflammatory markers, rheumatoid factor/anti-CCP). Early RA can involve only 1–2 joints and can begin in large joints; when clues are few, "morning-stiffness duration" is the backstop.

### 4.2 RA is a systemic disease

Synovial inflammation is the joint-end manifestation of a body-wide immune dysregulation. Untreated RA destroys more than joints — **cardiovascular risk rises significantly** (chronic inflammation accelerates atherosclerosis; one of the leading causes of death in RA patients), **interstitial lung disease**, **dry eyes/eye inflammation/scleritis**, osteoporosis, anemia. What "endure until unbearable, then treat" endures away is more than joints.

### 4.3 The early-treatment window: the key before irreversible destruction

- Bone erosions can appear on X-ray within **3 months of symptom onset** (Wilson et al. 2013) — destruction starts very early.
- **The "window of opportunity"**: starting a DMARD (disease-modifying antirheumatic drug) **as early as possible after symptoms appear (striving for within 3 months)** is the modern RA treatment consensus (the European S3 guideline recommends within 3 months; Burgers et al. 2019 review). Treatment within the window: higher remission rates, less destruction; dragged past the window, the same drugs work less well — destruction is irreversible, and so is missing the window.
- The modern standard is **"treat-to-target"**: regular reassessment against a set target (clinical remission or low disease activity) with dynamic medication adjustment; most patients can reach **remission** — "rheumatoid means inevitable disability" is last century's narrative, not today's.

### 4.4 The care path

First visit: **rheumatology-immunology**, not orthopedics. Orthopedics handles structural destruction (late-stage corrective surgery); the immune disease itself belongs to immunology. Chasing RA as an orthopedic problem, film after film, is this disease's most common care detour.

## ⑤ The gout face: crystals, lab pitfalls, and the truth about dietary restriction

### 5.1 The mechanism in plain words

Uric acid too concentrated in the blood → precipitates into **needle-shaped crystals** → deposits in the peripheral joints where temperature is low and flow is slow (the first metatarsophalangeal joint of the big toe is the classic site) → one night an immune cell swallows a crystal and acute inflammation erupts — which is why a gout attack is "wakened at midnight by pain, joint red, swollen, hot, painful even at the touch of a bedsheet." It is essentially **crystal arthritis**, not bacterial infection and not "blocked qi and blood flow."

### 5.2 "Don't test uric acid during an acute attack" (the pit of normal values during a flare)

During an acute attack, about **1/3 of patients have blood uric acid in the normal range** (the inflammatory response of the flare promotes renal uric acid excretion, pulling the blood value down) — "the uric acid came back normal this time, so it's not gout" is a **real pit** and misses diagnoses. Correct posture: diagnose the acute phase clinically plus joint presentation; **recheck uric acid 2–4 weeks into the intercritical period** for staging to mean anything. Conversely, most people with hyperuricemia never have symptoms in their lifetime (high uric acid ≠ gout); diagnosis defers to the clinic.

### 5.3 Diet is only part: the endogenous share is the big one

The uric acid source ledger: **about 2/3 is synthesized by the body itself (endogenous), about 1/3 comes from dietary purines** (Ekpenyong 2015; Roman 2023 review); on the excretion side, about 2/3 goes via the kidney, 1/3 via the gut. Therefore:

- Diet's ceiling is limited — diet alone typically lowers blood uric acid on the order of 10–18%; most patients who need drugs **cannot reach target by diet alone**.
- What should be avoided really should be: **beer (alcohol + purine double hit), seafood (especially shellfish and sardines), organ meats, sugar-sweetened beverages and fruit juice (fructose is a confirmed urate-raising factor), spirits**.
- **The decision basis for urate-lowering therapy is not "arrows on the uric acid lab sheet" but the clinic**: ≥2 flares per year, tophi, damage already seen on imaging, comorbid moderate-to-late chronic kidney disease, uric acid >9 mg/dL — any one criterion met, and per the ACR 2020 guideline urate-lowering therapy is recommended, targeting blood uric acid **<6 mg/dL** (<5 with tophi/severe disease), **maintained long-term once target is reached**.

### 5.4 "Gout = the rich man's disease, grin and bear it" — falsified

The cost of enduring through the acute attack queues up behind you: **crystals keep depositing during the intercritical period** — tophi form (in bone-joints, tendons, under the skin of the ears), chronic gouty arthritis (continuous joint destruction and deformity), **uric-acid nephropathy and kidney stones** (uric acid injures the kidney at the same time), and it commonly trades cause and effect with hypertension, metabolic syndrome, and chronic kidney disease. The core cognition of modern gout management: **treating the acute flare is only putting out the fire; lowering uric acid in the intercritical period is the demolition**.

## ⑥ The red-flag chapter: when these appear, do not mute them with "observe a few days"

| Red flag | What it might be | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Single joint red, swollen, hot, painful, acute onset** | First visit must triage **septic arthritis** (infection) together with gout | Same-day/next-day visit; septic arthritis is an emergency — delay = permanent cartilage destruction |
| Joint swelling and pain after trauma, unable to bear weight | Fracture / ligament-meniscus injury | Imaging assessment; do not drag it out as a "sprain to rest" |
| Morning stiffness >30 minutes, symmetric small-joint swelling | RA window clue | Rheumatology-immunology, the earlier the better |
| **Fever + joint pain** | Infectious arthritis / systemic disease | Seek care early; fever with joint pain is not a minor illness |
| **Joint pain in a child: night-time single-limb pain, with limping/swelling/daytime pain/fever** | Possibilities beyond growing pains (arthritis, infection, blood disease, bone tumor) | Active evaluation; do not write it all off as "growing pains" |

**The differential boundary of childhood growing pains**: typical growing pains = both legs, evening/night episodes, completely normal play by day, gone by morning, no swelling/limping/fever — a picture matching this can be observed with confidence. **Any line crossed** (only one side hurts, hurts in the daytime, the joint itself is swollen, limping when walking, fever or weight loss) is not "a pain you grow out of" — pediatrics/pediatric rheumatology should investigate actively.

## ⑦ The falsification face (six + one)

1. **"Exercise wears out joints"** — falsified. Cartilage is avascular and is fed by synovial-fluid diffusion plus a loading "pump" of cyclic pressure (O'Hara 1990): moderate exercise is cartilage feeding; sedentariness is cartilage starvation. Recreational runners' hip/knee OA 3.5% vs 10.2% in the sedentary (Alentorn-Geli 2017). The real wear: competitive-level volume, major structural injury, deformity left unmanaged — not "moving," but "how you move + moving without correcting."
2. **"Glucosamine repairs cartilage"** — downgraded. GAIT was negative overall; "repair" has no evidence; guidelines strongly recommend against; the moderate-severe subgroup signal was exploratory and unreplicated. Put the money and the discipline into exercise and weight loss.
3. **"Bone broth builds bone/calcium"** — falsified. Calcium resides mainly in meat and bone tissue, barely dissolves into the broth; a bowl of bone broth is low in calcium, and its real yield is **fat and purines** (long-simmered broth is double-high in both — those with gout/high uric acid should drink even less). The right road to calcium: dairy, soy foods, leafy greens + calcium and vitamin D supplements when needed (the osteoporosis face is detailed in the aging-care volume).
4. **"Clicking joints = disease"** — a line to draw. Physiological clicking: painless, no swelling, no motion limitation, feels looser after the click (gas cavitation in the joint space — a normal phenomenon, no treatment needed). Pathological clicking: **with pain, swelling, locking (catching), giving way, or new onset after trauma** — that is a signal to seek care. The dividing line is not "does it click," but "is anything else traveling with the click."
5. **"Gout gone = cured"** — falsified. Treating the acute flare only puts out the fire; **the intercritical period is the window and the main battlefield for lowering uric acid**; "no pain, no pills" = crystals keep making deposits, until tophi and kidney injury. Reaching the urate-lowering target (<6 mg/dL) + long-term maintenance is the framing of "cured."
6. **"For joint pain, a medicated plaster is enough"** — downgraded + delay risk. Topical preparations do offer some symptomatic relief for superficial small joints (hands, superficial knee layers), **but they have zero coverage for any red flag (see chapter ⑥)**: plastering septic arthritis into sepsis, plastering RA past its treatment window — that is the plaster's most expensive cost. When red flags are present, diagnose first; plaster afterward is not too late.
   - **[TCM·Traditional] the plaster face (labeled separately)**: medicated plasters (black plasters, acupoint application, etc.) belong to the tradition of TCM external therapy, with their own theory and indications within the TCM system (pattern-differentiated use for wind-cold-damp bi obstruction, etc.) and traditional rules such as prohibition on broken or allergy-prone skin. This canon does not evaluate their efficacy-evidence grade here, and requires only: red flags in chapter ⑥ must still be excluded before use; broken skin and allergic constitutions handled per traditional rules and the product insert. The two lines run in parallel without conflict: evidence-based medicine governs diagnosis and red flags; the traditional face is used under its own rules.
7. (Appendix) **The generalized misreading "joint pain = arthritis"** — see chapter ①. Pain from the muscles, tendons, and bursae around a joint is often lumped under "arthritis"; mechanism and treatment differ completely — diagnosis first, the first step of every joint problem.

- FACT-grade: the OA/RA mechanism-division table, GAIT negative overall, ACR/AF 2019 recommendation against glucosamine and chondroitin, the Messier weight-loss/load lever, the Alentorn-Geli running meta-analysis, the ACR 2020 gout ULT indication framework, the typical growing-pain picture and its red flags.
- MODEL-grade: the details of OA's "low-grade inflammation participates" model, the causal debate over repeated steroid injection and cartilage loss, the clinical-value positioning of HA injection.
