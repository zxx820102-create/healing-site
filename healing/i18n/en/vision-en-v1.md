# Eye & Vision Canon v1 (English) (2026-09-12 source)

The eye is the only "window that lets light through" — a clinician can see living vessels and nerve directly without opening the body (retinal vessels are the only microvasculature in the whole body that can be viewed non-invasively; the optic nerve is an extension of the brain). So ophthalmology is not just about sight: it doubles as the observation post of systemic chronic disease. This canon compresses into seven faces: **myopia (preventable, irreversible) → the refractive family (hyperopia/astigmatism/presbyopia) → blinding red flags and chronic disease (glaucoma/cataract/diabetic retinopathy/AMD/high myopia) → dry eye and eye strain → the children's face (screening windows and screens) → red flags (emergency list) → falsification face (scam firewall)**.

Epistemic labels: FACT = authoritative-source data verified by retrieval this round; MODEL = model/framing estimates or standard guideline framing; INFERENCE = judgment derived from evidence; HYPOTHESIS = mechanism hypothesis (unconfirmed); UNKNOWN = not yet verified, not claimed. **This canon does not replace a doctor**: all medication, device, surgery, and screening decisions defer to your clinician.

## Face 1 · Myopia: preventable, progression-controllable — but not reversible

### 1.1 Epidemiology: a silent epidemic

China's official framing (National Health Commission, 2020 national child and adolescent surveillance): **overall myopia rate 52.7%** — 6-year-olds 14.3%, primary school 35.6%, junior high 71.1%, senior high 80.5% (FACT · NHC release + Xinhua report 2021-07-13, verified verbatim this round). Against the first-disclosed 2018 framing of 53.6% (age 6: 14.5% / primary 36.0% / junior 71.6% / senior 81.0%, FACT), the overall rate dipped slightly in two years but the structure held: **myopia is mainly grown during the 9 years from primary into junior high** — from 1 in 7 at age 6 to 4 in 5 at 18. Journal-side long-trend data for Chinese Han students aged 7–18 likewise show rising detection rates (FACT · two trend analyses, Chinese Journal of Epidemiology 2017 and Chinese Journal of Preventive Medicine 2023, PMID 28651391/36854441).

Looking globally: in 2000 roughly 1.406 billion people had myopia (22.9%) and 163 million high myopia (2.7%); extrapolating current trends, by 2050 this reaches about 4.76 billion (49.8%) and 938 million (9.8%) (FACT · Holden et al., Ophthalmology 2016 systematic review + model, 145 studies, 2.1 million people, PMID 26875007). **"Half of humanity myopic" is not alarmism — it is a peer-reviewed model projection** (note: the 2050 figures are MODEL-grade extrapolation; model boundaries stated honestly).

### 1.2 Mechanism: axial length is the sibling of "a bone that once grown cannot un-grow"

- **The body of true myopia is axial myopia**: the eye's front-to-back length (axial length) is too long, so the focal point falls in front of the retina. Axial growth is like height — **irreversible** (FACT · physiology consensus face). No method currently exists to shorten an already-elongated axis — this is the first physical boundary behind all later "myopia cure" scams.
- **"Pseudomyopia" clarified**: prolonged near work keeps the ciliary muscle contracted (accommodation spasm); after relaxation, the myopic manifestation can disappear — this "pseudo" part is a reversible functional state (FACT · reversibility of accommodation spasm · case and clinical literature face, PMID 42181498/40950295). But it does not mean "myopia precursors all resolve on their own": **separating true from false requires cycloplegic (dilated) refraction** — self-perception or ordinary auto-refraction cannot distinguish them (FACT · cycloplegic refraction is the standard pediatric refraction step · clinical consensus face, PMID 40996730). In Chinese-language marketing, "pseudomyopia" is often repurposed as "myopia can be cured" patter — remember: the false part is reversible; the true part (the axis) is not.
- **Why the axis grows**: heredity (parental myopia is a clear risk factor) + environmental drive (prolonged near work, insufficient outdoor time). The molecular-level "dopamine hypothesis" (outdoor light stimulating retinal dopamine release, inhibiting axial growth) is the current mainstream mechanism model (HYPOTHESIS · strong animal-model support, human-pathway evidence still accumulating — honestly labeled MODEL/HYPOTHESIS grade, not settled).

### 1.3 Two hours outdoors: the hardest-evidence prevention

**The Guangzhou cluster RCT (JAMA 2015, 12 primary schools, 1,903 first-graders)**: intervention schools added a 40-minute daily outdoor activity class and encouraged weekend outdoor time; controls carried on as before. 3-year cumulative myopia incidence: intervention **30.4%** vs control **39.5%** (difference -9.1%, 95%CI -14.1 to -4.1, P<0.001); spherical equivalent 3-year change -1.42D vs -1.59D (difference 0.17D); but **axial elongation showed no significant difference between groups** (0.95 vs 0.98 mm, P=0.07) (FACT · PMID 26372583, verified verbatim). Read it honestly: **outdoor time mainly prevents myopia onset (in children not yet myopic); its effect on progression in already-myopic children is weak** — it is a prevention tool, not a treatment. Adult cohorts agree: more childhood outdoor time, lower adult myopia risk (FACT · Lingham et al., Sci Rep 2021, PMID 33737652).

"Two hours outdoors daily" is the standard recommendation framing of domestic myopia-control guidance (MODEL · policy framing · defer to the latest local guideline); what the RCT directly tested was the "about 40 extra minutes at school daily" tier — the 2-hour figure is a policy target extrapolated from dose-response reasoning. The two evidence strengths differ and are not mixed.

### 1.4 The four progression-control tools: atropine / ortho-K / defocus spectacles / red light — each at its honest evidence tier

Once a child is myopic, the goal is **slowing axial growth** (every 1D slowed roughly lowers later fundus-complication risk — direction level). The four tools' evidence tiers:

**① Low-concentration atropine eye drops (prescription-only · follow your clinician)**
- LAMP1 (Hong Kong RCT, 438 children aged 4–12, 1 year): 0.05%/0.025%/0.01% vs placebo myopia progression of -0.27/-0.46/-0.59/-0.81D; axial growth 0.20/0.29/0.36/0.41 mm — **higher concentration, stronger effect; the 0.05% tier optimal** (FACT · Ophthalmology 2019, PMID 30514630).
- LAMP2 (474 children aged 4–9 not yet myopic, 2 years): 2-year myopia incidence 28.4% in the 0.05% arm, significantly below 45.9% (0.01%) and 53.0% (placebo); **0.01% showed no significant difference from placebo**; photophobia was the most common adverse event (12.9% in the 0.05% arm) (FACT · JAMA 2023, PMID 36786791). This directly corrects the market patter around "0.01% as a cure-all."
- Refractive rebound after stopping exists and is concentration-related (ATOM follow-up evidence face: FACT direction level, PMID 24315293); the LAMP 8-year follow-up (including tapering schemes) is published (FACT · JAMA Ophthalmol 2026, PMID 41885837 — the long-term face is still accumulating).
- When to ask a doctor: school-age children with fast progression should have concentration and course assessed by ophthalmology; do not purchase and use on your own.

**② Orthokeratology lenses (ortho-K)**: rigid lenses worn overnight temporarily flatten the cornea, freeing daytime glasses; multiple systematic reviews/meta-analyses show slowed axial growth (FACT · efficacy direction level · meta-analyses exist, PMID 42589879; combination-with-atropine comparative meta also accumulating, PMID 42568973). Costs and boundaries: contact-lens-related infectious keratitis risk (low probability, high consequence), high hygiene and adherence demands (the conformity meta likewise flags adherence issues, PMID 42645417); fitting is a medical act.

**③ Defocus spectacle lenses (DIMS and similar designs)**: a 2-year double-blind RCT (183 Chinese children aged 8–13): defocus-lens group progression -0.41D vs single-vision -0.85D (52% slower); axial growth 0.21 mm vs 0.55 mm (62% less) (FACT · Lam et al., BJO 2020, PMID 31142465). For parents this is the **contact-lens-risk-free** option, fitting younger children or constrained hygiene conditions — actual selection belongs with the clinician.

**④ Repeated low-level red light (RLRL)**: multi-center RCT evidence shows slowed progression (3-year multi-center efficacy data published — FACT exists, PMID 41558834), but **long-term retinal safety is undetermined**: a 2026 JAMA Ophthalmol safety assessment called for independent safety verification of 4 devices, and recorded that China has **reclassified red-light laser-type devices as Class III medical devices** under strict regulation (FACT · PMID 41642586 verified in original wording; see also PMID 42313390). Honest conclusion: **efficacy evidence is accumulating while regulation tightens — parents should not treat it as "risk-free physiotherapy"**; whether to use it, and which device, belongs to credentialed clinicians.

### 1.5 One-line collapse

**Outdoor light is the best free medicine (prevents onset) · cycloplegic refraction is the only sorter (separates true from false) · the four progression-control tools are speed bumps for the already myopic (slows progression) · the irreversible axis is the demon-revealing mirror for every sales pitch.**

## Face 2 · The refractive-error family: hyperopia, astigmatism, presbyopia

### 2.1 Hyperopia (farsightedness)

Parallel light focuses **behind** the retina. Young people with low hyperopia can compensate by accommodating and "feel nothing," at the price of eye strain (achy eyes and headache after long reading). **Moderate-to-high hyperopia in children is a risk factor for amblyopia and inward eye turn (esotropia)** (FACT · clinical consensus face) — this is the second reason children's screening must use cycloplegic refraction (without dilation, hyperopia is often missed). Adult hyperopia gradually "reveals itself" as accommodative reserve declines, stacking with presbyopia after 40.

### 2.2 Astigmatism

Uneven curvature of the cornea or lens (like a basketball surface vs a rugby ball surface) — focal points in different directions do not coincide → double vision/ghosting, squinting, eye strain. Mostly congenital; degree usually stable; correction includes spectacles (cylindrical power), toric contact lenses, and surgery. Childhood astigmatism past a certain degree likewise affects visual development and should be corrected per medical advice (direction-level consensus).

### 2.3 Presbyopia: the physiological turning point of 40+, not "weak eye muscles"

**The mechanism is the lens hardening and losing elasticity with age — when the ciliary muscle contracts, the lens can no longer "bulge," and accommodative amplitude declines year by year** — physical aging of the refractive media, not "weak muscles that training can fix" (FACT · physiology consensus face · also the root of falsification item 2). Scale: about **1.8 billion people** worldwide have presbyopia, a substantial share with near-vision impairment from being uncorrected (FACT · Fricke et al., Ophthalmology 2018 systematic review + model, PMID 29753495). Typical onset 40–45 (the phone starts being held farther away) — **this is a normal physiological turning point**: refraction and reading glasses / progressive lenses are the standard solution; new surgical and drop therapies exist but are medical decisions (not expanded here; follow your clinician).

## Face 3 · Blinding red flags and chronic disease: the "silent thieves" inside the eye

### 3.1 Glaucoma: the silent thief of sight

- Scale: glaucoma patients worldwide projected to reach **111.8 million** by 2040, growth concentrated in Asia and Africa (FACT · Tham et al., Ophthalmology 2014 meta, PMID 24974815).
- **Open-angle**: pressure gradually rises (or normal-tension variants) compressing the optic nerve — **peripheral vision quietly narrows first, central vision affected last**; because it is painless, the loss is irreversible (FACT · optic-nerve damage irreversible · consensus face), hence "the silent thief." By the time patients notice "the viewing window has shrunk," it is often mid-late stage.
- **The honest face of screening evidence**: the USPSTF-commissioned systematic review (83 studies) found insufficient evidence of benefit for screening generally asymptomatic adults — in a 616-person RCT, screening did not improve vision and vision-related quality of life, and fall risk rose instead (RR 1.31); OCT nerve-fiber-layer thickness screening sensitivity 0.79 / specificity 0.92 (FACT · JAMA 2022, PMID 35679438). In plain words: **no push for universal mass screening, but higher-risk groups (40+, family history, high eye pressure, high myopia, diabetes, long-term steroid use) should discuss individualized periodic fundus + eye pressure + visual-field checks with ophthalmology** (INFERENCE · the reasonable landing point derived from the screening-evidence structure). Clinician assessment prevails; policy content stays unfrozen.
- **Acute angle-closure**: abrupt onset — eye pain + severe headache + nausea and vomiting + blurred vision + rainbow rings around lights (halos); can blind within hours to days; an ocular emergency (see Face 6).

### 3.2 Cataract: the world's No.1 cause of blindness — but the surgically curable kind

Lens opacification. Mostly age-related; diabetes, steroids, trauma, UV accelerate. Symptoms: painless progressive vision loss, glare, ghosting, myopic drift of prescription. The only effective treatment is surgery (removal of the clouded lens + intraocular lens implant) — modern phacoemulsification is a routine high-volume outpatient procedure with good safety and visual outcomes (FACT · BMJ clinical review, PMID 16840470). **"Wait until it is fully ripe" is a leftover error from the old-surgery era** (falsification item 3): the current mainstream framing is "assess surgery when daily life is affected"; dragging it to over-ripeness raises surgical difficulty and complication risk (FACT direction level: clinical consensus face; exact timing is the clinician's judgment).

### 3.3 Diabetic retinopathy (DR): disease duration is the biggest annotation

Among people with diabetes globally: any-DR prevalence **34.6%**, proliferative 6.96%, macular edema 6.81%, vision-threatening VTDR 10.2% — about 93 million people; **prevalence rises monotonically with duration, HbA1c and blood pressure, and is higher in type 1 than type 2** (FACT · Yau et al., Diabetes Care 2012 pooled analysis · 35 studies, 22,896 people, PMID 22301125). Translation: diabetes is essentially "an eye time bomb plus a timer" — duration is the timer. Three control lines: control glucose, pressure, duration (systemic face); build a fundus record at diagnosis; periodic fundus screening on the clinician's rhythm (type 1 usually from within 5 years; type 2 at diagnosis — standard guideline framing, MODEL · latest guidelines and your clinician prevail). This face interlinks with the diabetes canon (diab: anchors).

### 3.4 Age-related macular degeneration (AMD): a top enemy of elderly central vision

Damages central vision (pages and faces distort; a central dark spot). The AREDS series is the evidence pillar:
- The original AREDS formulation (vitamin C + E + beta-carotene + zinc) was shown to **lower the risk of progressing to advanced AMD** (FACT · AREDS2 paper Importance-section citation, PMID 23644932).
- AREDS2 main trial (4,203 people aged 50–85, median follow-up 5 years): **adding** lutein/zeaxanthin or omega-3 on top of AREDS did **not significantly further** lower advanced progression (L/Z HR 0.90, P=0.12; omega-3 HR 0.97, P=0.70); meanwhile **the beta-carotene arm had more lung cancers** (2.0% vs 0.9%) (FACT · JAMA 2013, PMID 23644932).
- 10-year follow-up: **substituting L/Z for beta-carotene is the better solution** (L/Z vs beta-carotene advanced-AMD HR 0.85, 95%CI 0.73–0.98; L/Z vs no-addition 0.91) (FACT · JAMA Ophthalmol 2022, PMID 35653117).
- Honest reading: the formulation is for **slowing high-risk fundi already at risk of progression**, not an eye supplement — normal eyes gain nothing measurable (see falsification item 6).

### 3.5 High-myopia complications: myopia's real bill arrives 40 years later

In high myopia (usually ≥ -6.00D or axial length ≥26 mm), the fundus is stretched for decades: retinal thinning, peripheral lattice degeneration, **retinal detachment** risk rising with axial length (a Danish registry study confirmed axial length as an RD risk factor — FACT · Acta Ophthalmol 2026, PMID 41697099; the specific risk-gradient numbers were not verified item by item this round · UNKNOWN); **pathologic myopic maculopathy** (choroidal neovascularization, atrophy) is a leading cause of blindness in the working-age population (FACT · the pathologic myopia international consensus ICON-PM exists, Prog Retin Eye Res 2026, PMID 42476447). **Every episode of "myopia deepening" in adolescence books a charge against the 40-years-later fundus** — that is the entire meaning of Face 1.4's four progression-control tools.

## Face 4 · Dry eye and eye strain: the muscle-and-lubricant problems of the screen age

### 4.1 Dry eye disease

The TFOS DEWS II international consensus defines dry eye as **a multifactorial tear-film instability driven by hyperosmolar tears and ocular-surface inflammation**; symptoms include dryness, foreign-body sensation, burning, fluctuating blur, reflex tearing (FACT · consensus exists, Ocul Surf 2017 executive summary + definition/classification report, PMID 28797892/28736335). One core subtype: **evaporative dry eye** — main driver meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD), i.e., blocked/abnormal oil glands of the lid margin (FACT · 2011 international workshop, PMID 21450913). MGD and prolonged screen work (incomplete blinks, fewer blinks) feed each other.

**Artificial tears, direction level**: first line is lubricant replacement — preservative-free, longer-lasting artificial tears (e.g., sodium hyaluronate class) are common choices; where MGD dominates, the focus is warm compresses + lid-margin hygiene + clinician management when needed (antibiotic/anti-inflammatory/physical therapy), not frantically dosing "redness-remover" drops (INFERENCE · direction level · specifics with your clinician). **Do not self-use steroid or vasoconstrictor drops long-term** (see med: crossing).

### 4.2 Eye strain and the 20-20-20 rule: evidence tier stated honestly

Digital eye strain is highly prevalent among young screen users (systematic review exists: Prev Med 2023, PMID 36977430); mechanisms include reduced blinking, accommodative strain, and dry-eye overlay. **The "20-20-20 rule" (every 20 minutes, look 6 meters away for 20 seconds) is a reasonable behavioral prescription, but lacks RCT-grade direct validation** (MODEL · mechanistically plausible + low risk + no high-quality direct evidence — labeled honestly, neither inflated nor dismissed). What it actually buys is "interrupting continuous near accommodative load"; for myopia itself it is not a treatment. Screen height, brightness matching, conscious full blinks, and fully corrected refractive error are behavioral faces at the same evidence tier (INFERENCE).

## Face 5 · The children's face: screening windows and the screen-time framing

### 5.1 Screening rhythm: the relay from infancy to school age

- **Infants and toddlers**: pediatricians screen the eyes at routine visits — the **red reflex test** (a white reflection in the pupil = leukocoria clue: retinoblastoma/cataract etc., urgent referral), fixation and following, eye alignment (strabismus) assessment (FACT · AAP policy "Eye Examination in Infants, Children, and Young Adults by Pediatricians," Pediatrics 2003, PMID 12671132). Any parent observation of "one eye glowing white in photos" → go to a doctor directly, do not wait for a screening node.
- **The amblyopia window is a hard clock**: visual development has a critical (sensitive) period; amblyopia (brain-side suppression from strabismus/refractive error/deprivation) **treats well before age 8 and responds worse with age**; ages 7–12 still show RCT-confirmed treatment benefit but limited magnitude (FACT · PEDIG randomized-trial face, Arch Ophthalmol 2008, PMID 19064841; "post-critical-period plasticity" animal evidence is expanding — eLife 2021 cat/mouse models, PMID 34464258 — research frontier, not a clinical promise). **Amblyopia treatment is a race against the window closing** — building a vision record from age 3 and rechecking every 6–12 months per medical advice is the standard framing (MODEL · unfrozen).
- **School-age myopia monitoring**: the official surveillance system behind the 52.7% figure and the school screening network exist for this; the first step on noticing vision drop is **hospital cycloplegic refraction**, not going straight to an optical shop to buy glasses (Face 1.2's conclusion).

### 5.2 Screen time: WHO and AAP framings honestly side by side

| Framing | Recommendation | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| WHO 2019 (physical activity/sedentary/sleep guidance under age 5) | **Under 1 year: no screen exposure; ages 2–4: sedentary screen time ≤1 hour, less is better** | Official guideline text (FACT · WHO publication 9789241550536 + official news page, verified by retrieval this round) |
| AAP 2016 ("Media and Young Minds" policy) | Avoid before 18–24 months (video calls excepted); ages 2–5 limited to about 1 hour daily, emphasizing co-viewing and content quality | Pediatrics policy statement (FACT the policy exists: PMID 27940793; specific tiering numbers are standard policy framing, MODEL · original text prevails) |

The two framings agree in direction (**less is better + quality matters**) and differ in tier boundaries and wording hardness — presented side by side, not merged. For ages 5+ and school children, neither body sets a one-size-fits-all hour cap; the core shifts to sleep displacement and content/context management; **for myopia specifically, outdoor time matters more to manage than screen hours** (Face 1).

## Face 6 · Red flags: when these appear, do not wait for the next checkup node

Any of the following — treat as an emergency (hours to same-day care; acute glaucoma / retinal detachment / vascular occlusion count in hours):

1. **Sudden visual-field defect** — a fixed shadow / curtain sensation in the view: retinal detachment or retinal vascular occlusion (especially in high myopia).
2. **Flashes + a sudden surge of floaters** (a cloud of new black specks, frequent lightning streaks): retinal tear/detachment warning — dilated fundus exam within 24 hours.
3. **Eye pain + blurred vision + rainbow rings around lights + headache, nausea, vomiting**: acute angle-closure glaucoma attack, **can cause permanent vision loss within hours** — straight to the eye emergency department.
4. **Sudden painless vision loss in one eye** (seconds to minutes): central retinal artery occlusion and the like — handle as "a stroke of the eye," the earlier the better; systemically it flags same-source vascular risk (card: crossing).
5. **Red eye + pain + light sensitivity + vision loss** (not the ordinary pink-eye pattern): uveitis / keratitis / acute pressure problems.
6. **Leukocoria (white glow in photos) / new-onset eye misalignment in an infant or toddler**: urgent pediatric ophthalmology evaluation (Face 5 red reflex).
7. **Any vision change after trauma, or a penetrating injury**: do not press, do not rub; cover and rush to care.
8. **A sudden barrage of "firework" flashes with headache and a hemianopic (half-field) defect**: suggests a neurological event (men: crossing); treat as a neuro-emergency.

## Face 7 · Falsification face: seven "high-heat, low-evidence" claims, each with its falsifier

**① "Myopia surgery (laser/ICL) cured the myopia" — CONTRADICTED**
Surgery corrects the cornea / refractive power (bringing the focal point back onto the retina); **the axial length has not shortened by a millimeter**; the high-myopia fundus stretching, detachment risk, and pathologic-change risk **remain exactly as they were** (FACT · physiological boundary + ICON-PM consensus framework, PMID 42476447). Falsifier: post-op axial measurement and fundus photography still need lifelong follow-up — "ditching glasses ≠ ditching high-myopia fundus risk." Advertising that sells "freedom from lenses" as "cure" is non-compliant patter.

**② "Just grab any reading glasses off the rack" — CONTRADICTED as a safe practice**
Ready-made glasses have one uniform single power and one fixed pupillary distance; mismatched lens powers / astigmatism / PD → eye strain, dizziness; more crucially, **no refraction done = cataract, glaucoma, macular disease, and diabetic retinopathy masquerading as presbyopia go undetected** (INFERENCE · derived from the Face 3 disease structure). Falsifier: wear the ready-made and the fitted glasses one week each and compare symptoms, while getting one full eye exam — the difference is measurable. Presbyopia's root is lens hardening (Face 2.3); "train the eye muscles to reverse presbyopia" programs are equally unevidenced (UNKNOWN · no reliable RCT retrieved).

**③ "A cataract must be fully ripe before surgery" — CONTRADICTED**
Patter from the old-surgery era. Modern phacoemulsification has a small incision and fast recovery; **surgery should be assessed once daily life is affected**; dragging to over-ripeness hardens and swells the lens, can trigger secondary glaucoma, and raises surgical difficulty and complications (FACT direction level: BMJ review + clinical consensus, PMID 16840470; exact timing individualized by the clinician). Falsifier: no current guideline/review recommends "wait for ripeness"; the historical source is an old-technique constraint, not medical necessity.

**④ "Eye-friendly lamps / blue-light lenses prevent myopia and protect the eyes" — CONTRADICTED as a selling point**
A Cochrane systematic review (17 RCTs): blue-light-filtering lenses vs standard lenses showed **no difference** in eye-strain scores (low-certainty evidence), no difference in critical flicker-fusion frequency; small samples, high risk of bias; claims of "protecting the macula, improving sleep" equally unsupported (FACT · Cochrane 2023, PMID 37593770, original wording verified). Myopia's drivers are insufficient outdoor time + near-work load + axial growth (Face 1), not lamp-brand brightness or blue light. Falsifier: any "blue-light blocks myopia" product must show an RCT on myopia incidence — none exists to date. A bright, flicker-free lamp is a comfort matter; it does not carry the "myopia prevention" account.

**⑤ "Eye exercises (eye-protection gymnastics) cure or halt myopia" — efficacy unestablished ([TCM·Traditional] separately labeled)**
Chinese school eye exercises are a traditional campus health practice ([TCM·Traditional] label: may be kept as a relaxation / eye-care habit), but **retrieved systematic reviews and controlled studies have not established a myopia-improving effect** (FACT · a 2023 systematic review + two controlled studies exist, PMID 36969666/33177899/32164649; their exact efficacy face was not read to full conclusion this round — UNKNOWN stated honestly), and **"curing myopia" collides directly with the irreversible-axis physiological boundary** (FACT boundary). Falsifier: axial length before and after doing the exercises is unchanged — the axis is the hard ledger. Selling "lens-free courses" under a "traditional Chinese" banner is not permitted.

**⑥ "Lutein supplements restore vision / everyone should take them" — CONTRADICTED as a general supplement**
AREDS2 main trial: in those already with intermediate AMD, **adding** lutein/zeaxanthin did not significantly further lower advanced progression (HR 0.90, P=0.12); no benefit evidence in normal eyes; "restores vision" has no source at all (FACT · PMID 23644932). Its true position: **replacing beta-carotene in formulations for high-AMD-risk fundi** (10-year HR 0.85 vs beta-carotene, PMID 35653117) — an ingredient for risk groups, not a mass supplement. Falsifier: ask for an RCT of "vision improvement in normal people" — none exists.

**⑦ "Dilating drops harm the eyes / children don't need dilation" — CONTRADICTED as a dangerous claim**
Cycloplegic refraction is the standard pediatric refraction step — without dilation, accommodative interference inflates the myopic reading and masks hyperopia, and true vs false cannot be separated (FACT · clinical consensus face, PMID 40996730/40950295). Common reactions are hours-to-a-day of temporary light sensitivity and near blur; no evidence shows routine dilation damages the eye (MODEL · textbook framing; individual contraindications such as narrow-angle tendency are screened by the clinician). Falsifier: the "inflated power" on a no-dilation refraction printout can be compared against the dilated one — two measurements of the same child settle it. The "no-dilation express glasses fitting" is precisely the top source of wrong prescriptions.

## Collapse: the one-card version

**Prevent** (children): the daily-2-hours-outdoors framing + broken-up near work + annual dilated refraction with records. **Control** (already myopic): atropine / ortho-K / defocus lenses / red light — choose with the clinician; watch the axis, not the ads. **Check** (40+): eye pressure + fundus + visual field into the routine checkup package; fundus records at any diabetes diagnosis; eye exam before glasses fitting. **Emergency** (red flags): curtain shadow / flash-and-floater surge / painful rainbow halos / one-eye sudden blindness — same-day care. **Invariant**: the axis is irreversible — whoever claims to "cure myopia" is lying physically.

- Eye-exercise RCT-grade efficacy full-text conclusions: UNKNOWN (this round verified only that the review and original studies exist; conclusion sentences not captured).
