# Infectious Disease Canon v1 (English)

## ① Master outline: the infection triad, the three "periods," and the "inflammation = infection" error

### 1.1 The infection triad (the epidemiological triad)

An infectious disease is never the pathogen's solo act — it is the ensemble of three elements:

1. **The agent (pathogen)**: viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites, prions. The same pathogen, different subtypes, wildly different virulence (influenza A(H3N2) and A(H1N1) differ in their severe-disease spectrum).
2. **The host**: age, immune status, underlying disease, nutrition determine "after exposure — does illness develop, and how severe." The severe-illness high-risk groups named on WHO's influenza fact sheet = pregnant women, children <5, adults ≥65, people with chronic disease, the immunosuppressed [FACT · WHO influenza fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12].
3. **The transmission route (environment/pathway)**: respiratory droplets and aerosols (influenza, tuberculosis), fecal-oral (hepatitis A, norovirus, bacillary dysentery), blood-body fluid and mother-to-child (hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV), contact (skin fungi, scabies), vector-borne (dengue, malaria).

**Canon verdict**: cut any one corner and the triad breaks — this is the unified skeleton for understanding the three classes of public-health action, "isolate the sick, protect the high-risk, block the routes" [MODEL · epidemiology general framework].

### 1.2 Incubation period / communicable period / quarantine: three easily confused boundaries

| Concept | Definition | Start and end | Example | Epistemic state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Incubation period** | the span from infection to first symptoms | moment of infection → first symptom | influenza 1–4 days (WHO fact sheet original: "Symptoms begin 1–4 days after infection"); TB can be weeks to decades | FACT (influenza framing read directly) / the rest is textbook general knowledge |
| **Communicable period** | the span in which the host can transmit the pathogen to others | usually begins before symptoms appear, ends after they resolve | influenza is infectious from 1 day before symptoms appear (textbook general framing) | FACT·relayed (not verified word-for-word this round) |
| **Quarantine** | restriction and observation of those **exposed but not yet ill** | counted from the last exposure, usually taking the disease's longest incubation period | medical observation of close contacts | FACT · definitional |
| **Isolation** | separate management of **confirmed / suspected patients** | during confirmed / suspected illness | hospital isolation for pulmonary TB | FACT · definitional |

**The boundary mnemonic**: incubation = "the disease has not arrived"; communicable = "can transmit"; quarantine governs "not sick but possibly carrying"; isolation governs "already sick." Quarantine length keys on the longest incubation period, not the average [MODEL · reasoning framework].

### 1.3 The "inflammation = infection" error: sterile inflammation, told honestly

- Infection often causes inflammation, but **inflammation ≠ infection**. Redness, swelling, heat, pain, and loss of function are inflammation's five cardinal signs; their triggers can be pathogens — or purely physical / chemical / immune factors [FACT · pathology general knowledge].
- **Sterile inflammation** (no pathogen involved): trauma and sprains, gouty arthritis, autoimmune disease (rheumatoid), chemical irritation (bile / urate crystals), etc. For this kind of "inflammation," antibiotics are **useless** — the antibiotic's target is the pathogen, not the inflammatory response itself [FACT · mechanism general knowledge].
- Clinical meaning: not every "inflammation" calls for "anti-inflammation medicine / antibiotics"; conflating the two drug classes is precisely where misuse begins (see the renaming paragraph in face ④).

## ② The influenza face: the real global death count, the 48-hour window, and falsifying "flu = bad cold"

### 2.1 The real global annual death count

- The WHO fact sheet's current framing: **about 1 billion influenza cases every year, of which 3–5 million severe; 290,000–650,000 people die of respiratory-related deaths annually** (original: "It causes 290 000 to 650 000 respiratory deaths annually") [FACT · WHO influenza fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12].
- The academic source of this figure is the *Lancet Respiratory Medicine* 2018 special series estimating global seasonal influenza-associated respiratory mortality. This round, a Europe PMC title search verified the series exists (6 same-title records: PMID 29248256, 29193690, 29197174, 29197173, 29197161, 29660769 — all from this round's search results); the global-total estimate item was not hit in two targeted searches and, per the stopping rule, was downgraded to "series verified at series level + the global-estimate item not verified item-by-item this round" [INFERENCE → honest downgrade, see receipt].

### 2.2 High-risk groups (the "host" corner of the outline triad)

Pregnant women, children under 5 (especially <2), adults ≥65, underlying heart-lung disease, diabetes, immunosuppression — WHO original framing: "People at high risk or with severe symptoms should be treated with antiviral medications as soon as possible" [FACT · read directly]. 99% of deaths from influenza-related lower respiratory infection in children occur in developing countries [FACT · read directly].

### 2.3 The antiviral 48-hour window

- **The earlier the better; 48 hours is the key watershed.** This round's title-level verification: PMID **31929434** (Dai Z et al., 2020) — "Early Administration of Oseltamivir Within 48 Hours After Onset of Flulike Symptoms Can Reduce the Risk of…" (starting oseltamivir within 48 hours of onset reduces subsequent risk) [FACT · title verified this round].
- Clinical meaning: when people in high-risk groups develop influenza-like symptoms, they should be seen promptly for antiviral evaluation without waiting for pathogen confirmation; those past 48 hours with severe-illness risk can still be evaluated for treatment (a narrowed but present benefit) [MODEL · clinical reasoning].
- Mechanism-semantics note: the oseltamivir class are **neuraminidase inhibitors — they suppress viral replication and release; they do not directly "kill" the virus** (see face ⑦ falsification 7) [FACT · mechanism general knowledge].

### 2.4 Falsifying "flu = bad cold" (three faces broken at once)

| Face | Influenza | Common cold |
|---|---|---|
| **Symptom spectrum** | abrupt high fever (38.5–40°C), body-wide aching, marked fatigue and headache, dry cough; systemic symptoms overwhelm local ones | nasal congestion, runny nose, sneezing, sore throat dominate; fever rare or low-grade; local symptoms overwhelm systemic ones [FACT · general knowledge] |
| **Pathogen and course** | influenza A / B viruses; WHO framing: symptoms last about a week, severe cases weeks; 1 billion cases/year, 3–5 million severe [FACT · read directly] | rhinoviruses / coronaviruses and other multiple pathogens; self-limited 5–7 days, severe disease rare [FACT · general knowledge] |
| **Complications and death** | pneumonia (primary / secondary bacterial), myocarditis, encephalitis, myositis; 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths globally each year [FACT · read directly] | complications rare; almost no deaths in healthy adults [FACT · general knowledge] |

**Falsification sentence**: the cost of treating influenza as a "bad cold" is handing the three intervention points — "high-risk group + high fever + the 48-hour window" — back to the virus for nothing. The two are **different diseases** on four dimensions: etiology, symptom weighting, complication spectrum, and death magnitude [FACT, sources per face above].

**Prevention hand-off**: influenza vaccination is detailed in the vaccine volume (vax:flu_antigenic_drift_yearly · repaired 2026-09-13); this volume does not repeat it. Post-infection care-seeking decisions: see face ⑥ red flags.

## ③ The tuberculosis face: the top infectious killer, latent vs active, the resistance chain

### 3.1 The real global and China burden numbers

- WHO fact sheet, 2024 data framing: **in 2024 about 10.7 million people fell ill worldwide (5.8 million men / 3.7 million women / 1.2 million children), and 1.23 million people died of tuberculosis (of whom about 150,000 were people living with HIV)** [FACT · WHO TB fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12].
- **Tuberculosis remains the world's leading cause of death from a single infectious agent** ("TB is the world's leading cause of death from a single infectious agent"), and is also **one of the main drivers of AMR-related deaths** [FACT · read directly].
- Regional distribution: the South-East Asia Region accounts for 34% of new cases, the Western Pacific Region 27%, the African Region 25%; 87% of new cases concentrate in 30 high-burden countries [FACT · read directly].
- China-related: the WHO fact sheet names the top five countries by absolute TB-services spending in 2024 as Brazil, **China**, India, Russia, South Africa [FACT · read directly]; "China is among WHO's 30 TB high-burden countries (including the MDR/RR-TB high-burden list)" is a historical authoritative framing; the list page failed three channel attempts this round and was **not verified — recorded as a downgrade to UNKNOWN (pending)**, see receipt.

### 3.2 Latent infection vs active disease

| | Latent infection (LTBI) | Active tuberculosis |
|---|---|---|
| Symptoms | none | cough (sometimes with blood), chest pain, fatigue, weight loss, fever, night sweats — WHO symptom-spectrum original "prolonged cough (sometimes with blood)" [FACT · read directly] |
| Infectiousness | not infectious | spreads through the air (cough / sneeze / droplet nuclei) [FACT · read directly] |
| Natural course | a minority (general framing: about 5–10% lifetime risk) progress to active disease | without treatment, high risk of death [MODEL · textbook framing] |
| Handling | preventive treatment possible after evaluation | a combination of ≥2 sensitive drugs, full-course disciplined treatment [FACT · general knowledge] |

**"Cough >2 weeks → get checked"**: the suspected-symptom alert line of China's national TB-control program core framing (cough and sputum ≥2 weeks, or coughing blood, should trigger a sputum test and chest X-ray) [FACT·relayed — the program original not verified this round → downgrade recorded in receipt]; WHO's fact sheet corresponding wording is "prolonged cough + fever + unexplained weight loss should be seen early" [FACT · read directly]. The two framings point the same way.

### 3.3 Disciplined treatment: why self-stopping is forbidden

- **How resistance emerges** (WHO original framing): when anti-TB drugs are used improperly (under-dosing / adding a single drug / interrupting the course too early), the sensitive strains suppressed by the drugs are not fully killed, and resistant mutants are **selected** out and gradually become the dominant population — "Drug resistance emerges when TB medicines are used inappropriately…, through use in incorrect doses or durations" [FACT · WHO fact sheet read directly, sentence verified this round].
- **MDR status**: WHO framing — MDR-TB "remains a public health crisis and a health security threat"; in 2024, only about 2/5 of patients with drug-resistant TB received treatment [FACT · read directly]. China's drug-resistant burden has long sat near the top under WHO list framings (see the 3.1 downgrade note).
- **Canon verdict**: TB treatment is a "mopping-up campaign" at the level of the bacterial population — symptom improvement only means the dominant sensitive strains have been suppressed; the residual bacilli (including semi-dormant ones) still need months more of the combined regimen to clear. Stopping on your own = personally running a resistance-selection sieve for the pathogen [MODEL · mechanistic reasoning; falsifier same-source as face ⑦ falsification 3]. Cure ≠ immunity: see face ⑦ falsification 6 (PMID 15831840 — reinfection is an empirical event).

## ④ Antibiotic rationality: resistance as a global threat, three misuse scenes, renaming "anti-inflammation medicine," and AWaRe

### 4.1 Antibiotic resistance = the magnitude of a top-ten global health threat

- WHO framing: **"Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) is one of the top global public health and development threats"**; **in 2019 bacterial AMR directly caused 1.27 million deaths worldwide** (associated-death framings run higher) [FACT · WHO AMR fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12]. WHO's 2019 list of "ten threats to global health" names AMR among them (the list page not read directly this round → that list attribution is general-knowledge relay [FACT·relayed]; the fact sheet's threat-positioning sentence has been read directly and verified).
- Tuberculosis's resistant form (MDR-TB) is one of the main drivers of AMR's death burden [FACT · read directly; see face ③].

### 4.2 Three misuse scenes (each one giving bacteria a "selection-pressure" lesson)

1. **Antibiotics misused on viral infections**: colds, influenza, and most acute sore throats are viral; antibiotics (antibacterial drugs) have no effect on them — beyond the zero effect, they net two extra harms: killing the normal flora + selecting resistant bacteria [FACT · mechanism general knowledge; the WHO AMR page's "misuse and overuse" main-driver wording read directly].
2. **Self-stopping and leftover pills**: stopping the moment symptoms ease turns "a mopping-up that killed 90%" into "a training camp" — the surviving low-sensitivity flora breed en masse; saving leftovers to self-medicate next time miscalibrates both dose and course [FACT · mechanism general knowledge; same framing as WHO's "not complete the course" driver family].
3. **Livestock and agricultural overuse**: WHO original — the misuse and overuse of antimicrobials in humans, animals and plants is the **main driver** of resistant-pathogen development [FACT · read directly]. Growth-promotion antibiotics are the same class as human drugs, and resistance genes can spread horizontally across species [MODEL · mechanistic reasoning].

### 4.3 Renaming the "anti-inflammation medicine = antibiotic" language confusion

| Colloquial name | Actual pharmacological class | Acts on | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| "anti-inflammation medicine" (消炎药 — often mis-meaning antibiotics) | antipyretic-analgesic-anti-inflammatory drugs | inflammatory mediators (prostaglandins etc.) | ibuprofen, paracetamol (acetaminophen) |
| glucocorticoids | anti-inflammatory / immunosuppressive | inflammation and the immune response | prednisone (prescription-only; never self-medicated) |
| **antibiotics** | antibacterial drugs | **bacterial structure / metabolism** (cell wall, protein, DNA replication) | amoxicillin, azithromycin, levofloxacin |

**The renaming sentence**: antibiotics do not "reduce inflammation" — they kill bacteria. Inflammation receding is the **result** of the infection being brought under control, not the direct target. For sterile inflammation (outline 1.3), the antibiotic is a completely ineffective drug [FACT].

### 4.4 Tiered access: WHO AWaRe and China's three-tier management

- **The WHO AWaRe classification (Access / Watch / Reserve)**: to improve access to appropriate treatment and reduce inappropriate use, WHO sorts antibiotics into three tiers — **Access (first-line common use · broad coverage)**, **Watch (second-line · requires close monitoring, higher resistance risk, e.g., the fluoroquinolones and some macrolides), Reserve (last resort · restricted-use tier, e.g., the polymyxins)** [FACT · WHO AMR fact sheet AWaRe section, read directly 2026-09-12; section name original "Antimicrobial stewardship and AWaRe"].
- China's *Measures for the Clinical Application of Antibacterial Drugs* runs a parallel unrestricted-use / restricted-use / special-use three-tier management [FACT·relayed — the regulation original not verified this round → downgrade recorded].
- **Canon verdict**: tiering is not "rank discrimination"; it is saving the last resort for the last moment — the day the Reserve tier is squandered is the day there is no drug left to use [MODEL].

## ⑤ The viral hepatitis face: hepatitis B's three routes, falsifying discrimination, the curability of hepatitis C, the liver-cancer chain

### 5.1 Hepatitis B: three transmission routes and the non-transmission facts

- WHO 2024 framing: **240 million people worldwide live with chronic hepatitis B, with 900,000 new infections each year** [FACT · WHO hepatitis B fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12].
- Three transmission routes: **① mother-to-child vertical transmission** (at childbirth — the hepatitis B fact sheet repeatedly names the perinatal transmission route); **② sexual contact** (with blood / body-fluid exchange); **③ the blood route** (unsafe injection, shared needles, unscreened blood products, open-wound contact with an infected person's blood) [FACT · read directly; the page's three English phrases were verified by direct reading this round, Chinese relay as above].
- **The non-transmission face**: the hepatitis B virus does not spread through shared meals, handshakes, hugs, coughing or sneezing — everyday contact (WHO hepatitis Q&A page general framing; this page not verified word-for-word this round → **downgraded to FACT·relayed and recorded**). This is precisely the scientific basis for falsifying the "carriers are dangerous" discrimination.
- The mother-to-child block timing anchor: hepatitis B vaccine + hepatitis B immunoglobulin within 24 hours of birth blocks mother-to-child transmission with very high efficiency (the fact sheet contains the within-24-hours-of-birth wording [FACT · read directly]); vaccine details belong to the vaccine volume (vax:china_nip_children_free · repaired 2026-09-13).

### 5.2 Carriers and the falsification of social discrimination

- The logic in three steps: everyday contact does not transmit (5.1) → the risk of ordinary shared life is negligible → excluding people on the grounds of HBsAg positivity from schooling / employment / shared meals **has no epidemiological basis** [INFERENCE, premises FACT].
- The China layer: since 2010, hepatitis B testing has been removed from school-admission and employment physicals (the "hepatitis B five-panel" banned from employment screening) as settled policy [FACT·relayed — not verified this round → downgrade recorded].
- Discrimination's real cost: it forces carriers into a hidden state → delayed monitoring → missed early screening for cirrhosis / liver cancer. Discrimination is not a moral issue; it is public health's negative asset [MODEL].

### 5.3 Hepatitis C: a disease the curative sciences have conquered (but access has not)

- **DAA (direct-acting antivirals) can cure over 95% of hepatitis C infections, with 8–12-week courses and pan-genotypic regimens** — WHO original "cure more than 95% of persons with hepatitis C infection," "within 8–12 weeks of therapy in nearly all patients" [FACT · WHO hepatitis C fact sheet, read directly 2026-09-12].
- The access cliff (real numbers): of all people infected worldwide across 2015–2024, only 36% had been diagnosed; of those diagnosed, about 20% had received DAA treatment by the end of 2024 [FACT · read directly]. Hepatitis C **still has no vaccine** [FACT · read directly] (prevention hand-off: blood safety + injection safety).
- Transmission routes: mainly the blood route (unsafe injections / medical procedures / unscreened blood / shared needles in injection drug use) [FACT · read directly].

### 5.4 The liver-cancer chain: HBV → cirrhosis → hepatocellular carcinoma

- WHO 2024 framing: **in 2024 hepatitis B caused about 1.1 million deaths, mainly from cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)** [FACT · read directly]. Chronic hepatitis B → fibrosis / cirrhosis → HCC is a continuous disease chain, and every link on the chain has intervention points: antiviral suppression of replication, cirrhosis surveillance, HCC screening with ultrasound + alpha-fetoprotein every 6 months [FACT · general knowledge + MODEL].
- The chain's detailed expansion belongs to the liver volume (interlinks: liver:hbv [repaired 2026-09-13] / the cirrhosis link (liver volume's dedicated node not yet built · original anchor repaired 2026-09-13 · original ids in receipt) / liver:hcc-surveillance); this volume sets the anchor and does not repeat.

## ⑥ Care-seeking red flags: with these signals, no more "let's observe a little longer"

> The red flags' common undertone: the window in which an infectious disease slides from "controllable" to "critical" is often counted in **hours**. Any one of the following — seek care the same day; several together or poor mental state — go straight to the emergency department [MODEL · triage framework]. Interlink with the fa volume (fa:fever-redflags); this volume supplies the infection context.

| Red flag | Why it is dangerous (what it points to) |
|---|---|
| **High fever not falling >3 days** | ordinary viral fevers mostly fall back within 3 days; sustained high fever suggests bacterial infection / abscess / urinary infection and the like, needing re-evaluation of the cause [FACT · general knowledge] |
| **Altered consciousness** (drowsiness / delirium / not responding to calls / seizures) | meningitis / encephalitis / septic-encephalopathy signal; risk of deterioration in hours [FACT · general knowledge] |
| **Neck stiffness** (chin cannot touch the chest when bowing the head + headache + fever) | meningeal irritation signs — an emergency indication for bacterial meningitis [FACT · general knowledge] |
| **Rash + fever** (especially non-blanching petechiae / ecchymoses) | meningococcal disease / sepsis (ecchymoses) / measles / dengue and other rash illnesses; same-day differential needed [FACT · general knowledge] (rash details belong to the skin volume) |
| **Difficulty breathing / rapid breathing / chest pain / cyanotic lips** | pneumonia, sepsis, myocarditis signals (post-influenza secondary bacterial pneumonia is a main lethal pathway, see face ②) [FACT · general knowledge] (pneumonia interlinks to the resp volume) |
| **Diarrhea with dehydration signs** (little urine / dry mouth / sunken eyes / poor skin recoil / crying without tears) | children and the elderly can decompensate within hours; oral rehydration salts are the first life-saving drug [FACT · general knowledge] (diarrhea and ORS details interlink to the gi volume) |

**The red-flag usage rule**: red-flag logic holds for "everyone," and the threshold should be lowered further for high-risk groups (the five groups of outline 2.2) — high-risk + fever = straight onto the influenza care pathway, without waiting the 3 days [MODEL].

## ⑦ Falsification face (each item with its falsifier)

> Format: **rumor** → falsification (with mechanism) → falsifier (what evidence could overturn this falsification) → epistemic state.

**Falsification 1: "IV drips speed recovery"**
- Falsification: efficacy depends on the concentration-time curve of the drug reaching its target site, unrelated to the "ritual" of the administration route; for infections that can be treated orally (most upper-respiratory infections, most gastroenteritis), oral and intravenous outcomes do not differ — while the intravenous route additionally carries allergy / circulatory overload / phlebitis / particulate-contamination and infusion-reaction risks [MODEL · clinical-consensus level].
- Falsifier: if a high-quality RCT for the same indication showed intravenous administration significantly superior to oral in outcomes (rather than fever falling a few minutes faster), this falsification would be weakened.
- Epistemic state: MODEL (consensus relayed); **no title-level literature verification obtained this round; downgrade honestly recorded in the receipt**.

**Falsification 2: "antibiotics = anti-inflammation medicine"**
- Falsification: see the renaming table in face ④ 4.3. The antibiotic's target is bacteria, not inflammatory mediators; zero effect on sterile inflammation, zero effect on viral infection. Calling ibuprofen "anti-inflammation medicine," then taking "anti-inflammation medicine" as if it were an antibiotic, is a double semantic misplacement [FACT].
- Falsifier: if antibiotics were shown to have clinical benefit in sterile-inflammation models (e.g., gout flares) independent of their antibacterial action, this falsification would need rewriting (current evidence does not support it).
- Epistemic state: FACT (pharmacology general knowledge).

**Falsification 3: "symptoms gone means I can stop the antibiotic (the course length doesn't matter)"**
- Falsification: symptom relief ≠ the bacterial population cleared to zero. Stopping early leaves behind the low-sensitivity subpopulation selected by drug pressure — this is the textbook path by which resistant bacteria are "selectively bred"; for tuberculosis, interrupting the course directly manufactures MDR candidates (WHO read-direct framing in face ③ 3.3) [FACT].
- Falsifier: if large-sample RCTs established that, in specific infections (i.e., scenarios other than non-TB and non-complex upper respiratory infections), shorter-than-standard courses are non-inferior in both relapse and resistance rates and do not increase transmission, then "one-size-fits-all full courses" would need pathogen-by-pathogen revision (medicine does have a genuine short-course trend for some infections — hence this item's correct phrasing: "follow the doctor's prescribed course; do not self-adjudicate") [MODEL · boundary honesty].
- Epistemic state: FACT (mechanism) + MODEL (course-tier boundary).

**Falsification 4: "vaccines are inferior to immunity from being sick (natural infection is more 'real')"**
- Falsification: immunity from natural infection = "antibodies" bought at the price of **real probabilities of severe disease and death** — influenza's 290,000–650,000 annual respiratory deaths, tuberculosis's 1.23 million deaths, hepatitis B's 1.1 million deaths (all WHO read directly this round) are the global ledger of that price; a vaccine obtains same-direction immune protection at a controlled antigen dose and skips the severe-disease lottery. Division of labor between the two volumes: the risk ledger sits in this volume, the immunology in the vaccine volume (vax:natural_infection_price_tag interlink · repaired 2026-09-13) [FACT (death counts) + INFERENCE (comparative reasoning)].
- Falsifier: if some disease's natural-infection immunity proved so superior in durability / breadth as to offset its severe-disease risk gap (as in some contested data in the varicella-zoster field), then revision by disease would be needed — this falsification rests on the "risk asymmetry" skeleton and claims no all-around immunological domination.
- Epistemic state: FACT+INFERENCE.

**Falsification 5: "alcohol sponging / alcohol rubs can bring a child's fever down"**
- Falsification: children's skin is highly permeable and their surface-area / weight ratio large — alcohol can be absorbed through the skin, with reported risk of transdermal ethanol poisoning; alcohol sponging causes vasoconstriction and can instead worsen chills and raise temperature; pediatric consensus across countries (including the AAP) does not recommend alcohol sponging as an antipyretic method. The AAP clinical report on fever and antipyretic use in children, title verified this round: PMID **21357332** ("Fever and antipyretic use in children." *Pediatrics* 2011) [FACT · title verified this round; the report body not verified word-for-word this round → downgrade recorded]. Tepid sponging's fever-lowering value is itself limited (PMID 38322388, a systematic review comparing tepid sponging with paracetamol, from this round's search) [FACT · title]. Correct handling of childhood fever interlinks to the ch and fa volumes.
- Falsifier: if high-quality evidence appeared showing that alcohol sponging in children has a benefit-risk ratio superior to standard antipyretics with no transdermal-absorption toxicity, this falsification is void (current consensus and case reports all point the other way).
- Epistemic state: FACT (title-level) + downgrade note.

**Falsification 6: "cured of tuberculosis means lifelong immunity — you can't get it again"**
- Falsification: reinfection after cure is an empirically documented event — PMID **15831840** (Verver S et al., *Am J Respir Crit Care Med* 2005, title verified this round, doi:10.1164/rccm.200409-1200oc): after successful tuberculosis treatment, the **reinfection rate is higher than the general population's new-incidence rate** (a Cape Town high-transmission-setting cohort). Cure ≠ vaccine; renewed exposure can still mean renewed infection (BCG protection details belong to the vaccine volume — the vaccine volume has not built a BCG node · original anchor repaired 2026-09-13 · original ids in receipt) [FACT · title-level].
- Falsifier: if long-term cohorts in low-transmission settings established that post-cure reinfection rates are significantly below population incidence (i.e., substantial clinical immunity exists), this falsification would need revision by setting — Verver's result comes from an extremely high-transmission area; that is the extrapolation boundary [MODEL · boundary honesty].
- Epistemic state: FACT (title-level).

**Falsification 7: "antiviral drugs can 'kill' the virus"**
- Falsification: the disciplined semantics of clinical antivirals is **suppressing replication** (oseltamivir inhibits neuraminidase → blocks new virions' release from host cells; DAAs inhibit HCV protease / polymerase → the replication chain breaks); the virus's final clearance depends on the immune system + (in the hepatitis C scenario) sustained suppression driving the conversion to negative. The clinical cost of the semantic misplacement: treating "take the drug = kill the virus = two days is enough" as a reason to self-stop, or reading an unfinished course as "ineffective" while "the virus is still there" [FACT · pharmacology general knowledge; the 48h anchor PMID 31929434 and DAA cure rates read directly from WHO].
- Falsifier: if a class of antivirals truly acting by direct inactivation reached clinical validation in the body, the "suppress replication" semantics would need narrowing from "all" to "most existing classes."
- Epistemic state: FACT.

**Falsification 8 (bonus): "flu = bad cold"**: see face ② 2.4's three-faces-broken table [FACT]. Falsifier: if systematic evidence erased the influenza / common-cold difference on any one of symptom weighting, complication spectrum, or death magnitude, the "different diseases" verdict would be downgraded. Epistemic state: FACT.
