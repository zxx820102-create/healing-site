# Sense-Organ Health Canon v1 (vision + hearing in one ledger)

> **In one sentence**: vision and hearing are the two main roads connecting a person to the world — of the world's two largest forms of sensory loss, **the largest part is precisely preventable, treatable and correctable**; preventing blindness and deafness is therefore among the hardest-evidence, best-value health investments there are, and the greatest enemy is not the disease itself but "not knowing it is preventable" and "waiting too long to seek care".
>
> **Epistemic legend** (observed throughout · no unilateral promotion): FACT = a first-hand conclusion verified this round (WHO site/guidelines/RCT · with PMID or source); MODEL = a framework-level statement consistent across sources but lacking this-round first-hand direct verification; INFERENCE = an inference drawn from verified evidence; CONTESTED = a popular claim with affirmative counter-evidence; UNKNOWN = honest not-knowing.

---

## Face 1 · The global burden master ledger (WHO framing)

### 1.1 Vision (WHO fact sheet · directly verified 2026-09-12)

- **At least 2.2 billion people have a near- or distance-vision impairment; of them, at least 1 billion have a vision impairment that could have been prevented or has yet to be addressed** (FACT · WHO vision fact sheet directly verified, carrying its World Report on Vision 2019 framing).
- The composition of those 1 billion "addressable" cases (FACT · same source):
  - Cataract **94 million** (the leading cause of distance-vision impairment/blindness)
  - **Uncorrected refractive error 88.4 million** (distance); **uncorrected presbyopia 826 million** (near · the single largest item)
  - Age-related macular degeneration 8 million; glaucoma 7.7 million; diabetic retinopathy 3.9 million
- **Uncorrected refractive error (near + distance) totals about 910 million people — the largest and most easily solved sensory burden in the whole spectrum**: a pair of glasses corrects it (FACT · composition numbers directly verified; "most easily solved" is the same source's intervention conclusion).
- Annual global productivity loss about **$411 billion**, while the cost gap to meeting the unmet need is only about **$25 billion** (FACT · WHO directly verified) — the gap is not in medicine; it is in access.
- In low-income countries **2/3** of people who need glasses do not get them; globally **1/2** of people who need cataract surgery do not get it (FACT · WHO directly verified).

### 1.2 Hearing (WHO framing · directly verified 2026-09-12)

- **About 1.5 billion people have some degree of hearing loss** (FACT · WHO World Report on Hearing 2021; GBD 2021 counts 1.546 billion · corroborated by Chadha 2021/Shen 2025); of them, **430 million (>5%, including 34 million children) have disabling hearing loss requiring rehabilitation services** (FACT · WHO hearing fact sheet directly verified; disabling = >35 dB in the better ear).
- Forecast to 2050: **nearly 2.5 billion people with some degree of hearing loss, over 700 million needing rehabilitation** (FACT · WHO directly verified).
- **Over 1 billion young people face permanent, avoidable hearing loss from unsafe listening practices** (FACT · WHO directly verified).
- The children's face: **about 95.1 million children aged 5–19 have hearing loss; nearly 60% of childhood hearing loss stems from avoidable causes** (FACT · WHO directly verified).
- Unaddressed hearing loss costs the world nearly **$1 trillion a year**; an additional investment of **under $1.4 per person per year** to scale up ear and hearing services returns about **nearly $16 per $1** over 10 years (FACT · WHO directly verified).
- Among people over 60, **>25%** have disabling hearing loss (FACT · WHO directly verified) — aging-related hearing loss is pervasive, but "pervasive" does not mean "entirely the inevitability of aging" (see Face 3 and the falsification face).

### 1.3 The master ledger's structural conclusion

> **INFERENCE (drawn from the directly verified numbers above)**: sensory health's "biggest lever" is not high tech — it is **the two plainest things: getting the glasses fitted, and getting sound protection plus early screening going**. Of the 2.2 billion with vision impairment, the majority's answers are glasses and cataract surgery; of the 1.5 billion with hearing loss, noise exposure (preventable), otitis media (treatable) and age-related loss (rehabilitable) form the three great intervenable clusters.

## Face 2 · The vision face

### 2.1 Myopia control: 2 hours of outdoor time daily (this canon's hardest-evidence prevention item)

- **FACT chain**: the Guangzhou cluster randomized controlled trial (He M et al. · JAMA 2015 · PMID:26372583) randomized 12 schools to an additional 40 minutes of daily outdoor activity plus parental encouragement; **3-year cumulative myopia incidence fell significantly** (intervention arm below control), establishing "more time outdoors at school" as an implementable myopia-prevention intervention.
- **INFERENCE**: the family of evidence (multiple East Asian observational and intervention studies) consistently points to "outdoor light time", not the fine details of near-work posture, as the main modifiable factor in myopia onset; "about 2 hours outdoors daily" is the practical framing of extrapolating the RCT dose (dose extrapolation = MODEL; the direction itself = FACT).
- **The boundary (honesty clause)**: outdoor time acts mainly on **preventing myopia onset / delaying onset**; its effect on progression in those already myopic is weaker than dedicated means (such as orthokeratology and low-concentration atropine — whose effect sizes were not verified item-by-item this round; cite them at MODEL).

### 2.2 Cataract: among the most cost-effective surgeries there are

- **FACT (WHO directly verified)**: "glasses correcting refractive error and cataract surgery are among the most cost-effective of all health-care interventions" — cataract surgery is thereby a ceiling-level public-health value intervention.
- **FACT**: cataract is the leading cause of distance-vision impairment/blindness (94 million addressable cases); half of those needing surgery worldwide have no access to it.
- **MODEL**: the technique is mature, the per-case cost low, turnover fast — the classic fit for the "surgical team circuit" model in low-resource regions.

### 2.3 Glaucoma: the silent thief of sight

- **FACT**: glaucoma ranks among the world's leading causes of blindness (7.7 million addressable cases · WHO); its visual-field defects are **irreversible** (ophthalmology-textbook consensus · FACT grade). It is called "silent" because early stages are usually symptom-free, painless, and central vision is unaffected — by the time the patient notices, field is permanently lost.
- **INFERENCE (the actionable meaning)**: glaucoma's entire lever lies in "early" — high-risk groups (family history, advanced age, high intraocular pressure) get regular fundus and pressure checks; the public framing is "**detectable, pressable-down, not winnable-back**" (it can be found and its pressure lowered, but lost vision cannot be won back).

### 2.4 Diabetic retinopathy: the screening rhythm

- **FACT (WHO directly verified)**: diabetic retinopathy ranks among the leading causes of vision impairment worldwide (3.9 million addressable cases); "early detection and timely treatment are essential to avoid irreversible vision loss".
- **MODEL**: mainstream guidelines recommend **regular (usually annual) fundus screening** for people with diabetes, with laser/anti-VEGF treatment once macular edema or proliferative disease is found (guideline rhythms and treatment thresholds not verified item-by-item against the originals this round → cited at MODEL).
- **INFERENCE**: embedding fundus photography into diabetes chronic-disease management is the best-value "piggyback-style" screening a low- and middle-income system can buy.

### 2.5 The 20-20-20 rule: the evidence grade stated as it is

- **CONTESTED**: "every 20 minutes of near work, look at something 20 feet (about 6 meters) away for 20 seconds" is the most widely circulated eye-care advice, but **its three numbers themselves have never been rigorously validated** — an optometry journal asked it outright in 2023 under the title "20-20-20 Rule: Are These Numbers Justified?" (Optom Vis Sci · PMID:36473088); small-sample intervention studies (e.g., JPMA 2025 · PMID:41243938) hint at possible relief of eye-strain symptoms, at limited quality.
- **The honest framing**: it can serve as a behavioral cue to "look far on a schedule, interrupt sustained near work" (INFERENCE: the direction is compatible with the outdoor/distance-gazing evidence), but **it must not be preached as an RCT-backed myopia-prevention method**; the genuinely FACT-grade myopia prevention is outdoor time (2.1).

## Face 3 · The hearing face

### 3.1 Noise-induced hearing loss: irreversible, so every decibel has a price

- **FACT**: inner-ear hair-cell damage from noise exposure **does not regenerate** (the textbook consensus that mammalian cochlear hair cells do not regrow); once hearing is lost it is irreversible; the WHO fact sheet lists "loud noise / intense sound exposure" among the leading causes of hearing loss across the whole life course.
- **FACT**: **over 1 billion young people face permanent, avoidable hearing loss from unsafe listening** (WHO directly verified) — the single largest "fully preventable" item in the entire sensory field.

### 3.2 Headphone volume: the WHO-ITU standard is the hard anchor; "60/60" is a soft hint

- **FACT**: the WHO and ITU "safe listening" standards (for safe listening devices and personal audio systems) count by the **weekly sound dose**: about **80 dB for 40 hours per week** is the safe exposure ceiling for adults (multi-source directly verified on the WHO site).
- **CONTESTED/INFERENCE (honest grade)**: the 60/60 principle — "60% maximum volume, 60 minutes" — has operational convenience in device settings, but **no RCT-grade direct evidence was retrieved** supporting those specific parameters; cite it as "a conservative heuristic that is easy to remember", and for dosimetry always return to the hard WHO-ITU anchor of 80 dB/40 h (with lower tiers for sensitive groups such as children · MODEL).

### 3.3 Age-related hearing decline × dementia: the evidence chain and its boundary (the face most in need of honesty)

- **FACT (epidemiological · dose-response)**: Lin FR et al. (Arch Neurol 2011 · PMID:21320988) showed in the Baltimore cohort that hearing loss is **gradient-associated with dementia incidence** (the worse the loss, the higher the risk). Later cohorts (e.g., the ARIC neurocognitive substudy · Deal et al.) continue the same direction; US population-attribution estimates in Huang AR et al. (JAMA 2023 · PMID:36625819).
- **FACT (authoritative synthesis)**: the Lancet Standing Commission on dementia's 2024 report (Livingston G et al. · Lancet · PMID:39096926) places **hearing loss at the top of the list of modifiable dementia risk factors across the life course** (its population-attributable fraction was 8% in the 2020 edition, revised to 7% in 2024 — the specific percentages not direct-verified against the original this round; mark MODEL when citing).
- **FACT (intervention trial · reported as it is)**: the ACHIEVE multicenter RCT (Lin FR et al. · Lancet 2023 · PMID:37478886) — **in the overall study population, a 3-year hearing-aid intervention did not significantly reduce cognitive decline** (that population's cognitive risk was lower); **in the prespecified higher-risk (ACHIEVE original cohort) subgroup, significant benefit was observed**.
- **INFERENCE (the current honest conclusion)**: ① the **association** between hearing loss and dementia is FACT; ② "treating hearing loss prevents dementia" is **unproven in low-risk populations**, and in high-risk populations is a **promising but unreplicated candidate**; ③ even with the dementia effect in doubt, hearing aids improving communication and reducing isolation is itself benefit — dementia prevention is not the only reason to use hearing aids, and not a confirmed one.

### 3.4 Tinnitus: the honest ledger of mostly-UNKNOWN mechanisms

- **UNKNOWN**: the mechanisms of tinnitus (the relative weights of central compensatory remapping, peripheral damage and psychological amplification networks) **have no settled consensus**; mechanism attribution in a specific individual is often unverifiable.
- **FACT/MODEL**: tinnitus is known to be highly comorbid with noise exposure and age-related hearing loss; management centers on cognitive behavioral therapy, sound therapy and other **adaptation and desensitization** paths (effectiveness evidence not verified item-by-item this round → MODEL); commercial claims of "curing tinnitus at the root" are treated as CONTESTED across the board.
- **The actionable framing**: new-onset tinnitus (especially unilateral, pulsatile, or with hearing loss) should be medically worked up for organic causes (INFERENCE · consistent with the red-flag chapter).

## Face 4 · The access face (cost tiers and the low-resource two-piece set)

### 4.1 The cost spectrum of vision correction (FACT · WHO directly verified + MODEL)

| Tier | Intervention | Access facts (WHO directly verified) |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Refraction + glasses | 2/3 of those who need glasses in low-income countries have none; closing the global unmet-need gap costs about $25 billion a year — far less than the $411 billion/year productivity loss |
| Surgical | Cataract extraction + intraocular lens | 1/2 of those who need it worldwide have no access; "among the most cost-effective of all health interventions" (WHO verbatim) |
| Prevention | Children's outdoor time / screening | A public-health measure at near-zero marginal cost (dose at 2.1) |

- The WHO SPECS 2030 initiative targets the **>800 million** people needing refractive services (FACT · WHO directly verified).

### 4.2 The cost spectrum of hearing rehabilitation (FACT · WHO directly verified + MODEL)

| Tier | Intervention | Access facts |
|---|---|---|
| Screening | Tool-based screening such as hearWHO | Listed by WHO as an identification path usable under resource limits (FACT) |
| Hearing aids | Fitting + rehabilitation training | A core item of WHO's rehabilitation list; for **under $1.4 per person per year** of additional investment, services can scale globally with a 10-year ROI of nearly 16-fold (FACT) |
| Cochlear implants | Surgical implantation + speech rehabilitation | The option for severe-profound loss within WHO's rehabilitation list (FACT · existence and indications); high per-unit cost, dependent on surgical systems (magnitude MODEL · specific prices not verified this round) |

### 4.3 The low-resource two-piece set = screening + correction (MODEL · strategy synthesis)

> The minimal viable strategy derivable directly from the structure of WHO's two fact sheets (INFERENCE): **the first piece is "find the people"** (stratified newborn/school-age/older-adult screening · Face 5), **the second is "solve it"** (glasses / cataract surgery / hearing aids). Both pieces are required: screening without correction turns a diagnosis into despair; services without screening is capacity idling. This is precisely the execution form of "preventing blindness and deafness is the world's best-value health investment".

## Face 5 · The children-specific face

### 5.1 Newborn hearing screening

- **FACT**: the USPSTF recommends universal newborn hearing screening (recommendation statement · Pediatrics · PMID:18595997; the 2008 edition verified this round, with subsequent convention maintained); the cost-effectiveness of universal screening also has health-economics support (Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg · PMID:11283492).
- **FACT**: WHO places newborns and infants as the first station of life-course hearing screening; if congenital hearing loss is missed, the language/cognitive development window shifts back irreversibly (developmental-science consensus · MODEL-grade statement).
- **INFERENCE**: the screening loop must be the chain "screen → diagnose (AABR/OAE referral) → intervene before 6 months of age" (the intervention deadline is JCIH convention framing · MODEL).

### 5.2 Amblyopia: the golden window of treatment (neither exaggerated nor abandoned)

- **FACT**: amblyopia is a **developmental-period** disease — within the visual development sensitive period the brain "uses it or loses it" for each eye's signal; treatment works best inside the golden window; **children aged 7–17 still improve with treatment**, though less than the young (the PEDIG multicenter RCT · Scheiman MM et al. · Arch Ophthalmol 2005 · PMID:15824215).
- **CONTESTED, falsified in both directions**: "it fixes itself as they grow" (wrong · amblyopia does not self-resolve) and "past a certain age it is hopeless" (equally unfounded · the older-age RCTs prove partial treatability) — absolutization in either direction violates the evidence.
- **INFERENCE**: this is exactly the meaning of preschool vision screening: find "strabismus/anisometropia/form-deprivation" before the window closes.

### 5.3 The handling boundary of childhood otitis media

- **FACT**: the AAP/AAFP acute otitis media clinical guideline (Lieberthal AS et al. · Pediatrics 2013 · PMID:23439909) established tiered handling: **age ≥2, mild illness, unilateral — observe for 48–72 hours before deciding on antibiotics**; severe/younger/bilateral and other indications get immediate antimicrobial treatment.
- **FACT**: WHO lists chronic suppurative/non-suppurative otitis media among the leading causes of childhood hearing loss; nearly 60% of childhood hearing loss is avoidable (directly verified).
- **INFERENCE**: the parent-facing framing = "fever and earache may be observed for 2–3 days, but **persistent hearing decline, ear discharge, choking after feeds with developmental lag** need care" — antibiotics need not be rushed; hearing must not be lost.

## Face 6 · The medical red-flag chapter (any item = seek care promptly · no self-resolve-and-wait)

| Red flag | Meaning | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Sudden painless vision loss** (one eye / visual-field defect, seconds-to-hours) | Retinal vascular occlusion, optic neuropathy, retinal detachment, etc. — most have a treatment window | FACT (clinical-emergency consensus; the mechanism spectrum MODEL) |
| **Flashes + a sudden burst of floaters** (especially with a field shadow/curtain) | Retinal detachment/tear prodrome; acute floaters-and-flashes carry detachment risk well above baseline | FACT (Hollands et al., JAMA 2009 systematic review · PMID:19934426: acute symptoms correlate with detachment risk; symptom features raise the probability) |
| **Sudden unilateral deafness** (within hours-to-3 days, often with tinnitus/ear fullness) | Sudden sensorineural hearing loss — **the 72-hour steroid treatment window**; delay = permanent hearing loss | FACT (AAO-HNS clinical practice guideline · Chandrasekhar et al. 2019 · PMID:31369359: corticosteroids are the established treatment, earliest-possible emphasized) |
| **Eye pain + vision decline + nausea and vomiting** (often with halos around lights) | Acute angle-closure glaucoma — an ophthalmic emergency that can blind within days | FACT (the acute-attack triad is clinical consensus; belongs to 2.3's irreversible family) |
| **Childhood leukocoria** (a whitish pupil · "cat's-eye" reflex in photos) | Retinoblastoma and the like — the first signal of childhood intraocular malignancy | FACT (Lancet clinical picture · PMID:22445238; early diagnosis decides saving both the eye and the life) |

> The red-flag chapter's law of use: a red flag is not a diagnosis — it is the switch that means "**stop watching and waiting**". Every item shares the structure: a limited window + an irreversible consequence.

## Face 7 · The falsification face (Anti-Canon · each item an attackable specific claim)

1. **"Eye-protection lamps / blue-light-blocking products prevent myopia" — the evidence is thin**. A Cochrane systematic review (Singh S et al. 2023 · PMID:37593770): no clinically meaningful benefit of blue-light-blocking lenses on visual performance, macular health or sleep quality; the lamp-illuminance–myopia links are mostly observational confounding, and the evidence body for light against myopia is **outdoors**, not the desk (FACT · blue-light conclusion directly verified; desk lamps = INFERENCE).
2. **"Eye-protection exercises / massage / training devices can reverse myopia" — insufficient evidence and contrary to physiology**. Myopia's core is the organic change of axial elongation; massage cannot shorten an axis (FACT · physiology); eye-protection exercises lack high-quality controlled evidence of preventing myopia onset (CONTESTED · no RCT-grade support retrieved this round). Their reasonable value is at most a "timed-break reminder" (INFERENCE · same as 2.5).
3. **"Eye drops can dissolve a cataract" — a scam**. Cataract is denaturation and opacification of lens proteins; **no approved drug reverses it**; the only established treatment is surgery (FACT · WHO states verbatim "surgery is the treatment", with no drug-cure option); any "dissolving drops" product is commercial deception.
4. **"Old-age deafness is inevitable and unpreventable" — a lazy half-truth**. The age-related component is indeed pervasive (>25% of the over-60s · FACT), but **noise-induced hearing loss is 100% preventable** (WHO: what over 1 billion young people face is "avoidable" loss · FACT), and nearly 60% of childhood hearing loss is avoidable (FACT). "Inevitable" is a noisy society disguising the preventable as fate (INFERENCE).
5. **"Sudden deafness — wait and it will heal on its own" — gambling away the golden window**. Sudden hearing loss has a 72-hour treatment window and steroids are the established treatment (FACT · PMID:31369359); some patients fail to fully recover even when treated (FACT · guideline framing) — watching and waiting only closes the one door.
6. **"20-20-20 is a scientifically validated eye-care method" — the numbers themselves are unvalidated** (CONTESTED · PMID:36473088 questions it directly); usable as a break reminder, never as an anti-myopia prescription (see 2.5).
7. **"Amblyopia past 12 is hopeless" — contradicted by the RCT**. Treatment at 7–17 still improves significantly (FACT · PMID:15824215); "hopeless" talk causes abandonment, the same offense as "it self-heals" talk causes delay (INFERENCE).
8. **"Hearing aids prevent dementia once worn / or are simply useless" — neither extreme holds**. The association is FACT; the population-wide intervention benefit is unproven (ACHIEVE main population negative · FACT), the high-risk subgroup positive (FACT · awaiting replication); the correct statement is at 3.3 — refuse exaggeration in both directions (INFERENCE).
