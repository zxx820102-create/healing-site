# The Symptom Textbook v1 (2026-09-12)

**Readers**: self-learners and health workers. **How to use**: the section-end anchor ([L3:#n]) leads back to the evidence layer. The L3 discipline is "prefer an empty cell to an invented one": restrictions without evidence are tagged UNKNOWN, and TCM entries are tagged "traditional" — this book's wording is aligned to it verbatim, zero promotion.

---

## Chapter 0. The one-page version (L1 · zero-background readable)

> Abridged companion text: this chapter is a condensed transcription of `docs/healing/textbook/symptom-l1-80yi-20260912.md`, not word-identical; the companion is the fuller, supplemented version and can be distributed on its own.

**The doctrine: rule out danger first, talk restrictions second. Eating and drinking adjustments are the helper for symptoms, not the main operator that cures disease.** Red lines first: vomiting blood, black stools, blood in the stool, severe chest pain, sudden facial droop / limb weakness / slurred speech, thunderclap headache, fever above 40°C, difficulty breathing, throat tightness, thoughts of self-harm — seek medical care immediately; dietary advice does not apply at all (see Chapter 2).

**Seven action cards**: ① for diarrhea, replenish with oral rehydration salts first and keep eating (confirmed by research) ② constipation = fiber 25-30 g/day + enough water + exercise, with prunes and kiwifruit additionally backed by single trials (confirmed by research / fairly well established) ③ honey relieves night cough from age 1 up; high-dose vitamin C for preventing colds has no evidence (fairly well established / confirmed by research) ④ reflux = no food for 3 hours before bed, and for the overweight weight loss comes first (confirmed by research) ⑤ for period pain, ginger has small trials; avoiding iced drinks is unproven and only a personal self-trial (fairly well established) ⑥ insomnia: cut caffeine after midday, zero alcohol in the evening (confirmed by research) ⑦ after surgery, protein at every meal — do not drink only congee; unless an allergy is confirmed, do not restrict seafood and eggs in the name of "fa wu" (confirmed by research; the negative side on fa wu = the tier "not known").

**Myths and not-known aspects, same as the L1 page**: fa wu restrictions hollowing out protein, the unfounded red-date blood tonic, the spinach-and-tofu "mutual restraint" myth, the risks of broad-spectrum restriction, He Shou Wu liver injury, starving a fever and bundling up to sweat; and — not known — the efficacy of broad-spectrum restriction, iced and cold drinks, and quitting dairy for acne.

## Chapter 1. Concepts and terminology

**Learning objectives**: distinguish symptom from disease; read the dual sourcing; match the epistemic states.

### 1.1 A symptom is not a diagnosis
A symptom is a bodily signal; a disease is an entity a physician diagnoses — behind one symptom may lie different diseases. So every L3 entry carries a "cause-check line": chronic diarrhea lasting >4 weeks — check infection / inflammatory bowel disease first [L3:#10]; new-onset constipation + weight loss + blood in the stool = a cause-check red line [L3:#11]. **This book answers "how to eat day to day"; it does not answer "what disease do I have"** [L3:红线总纲].

### 1.2 Dual sourcing: separate first, then align
Each L3 symptom is written in two streams: [TCM · traditional] entries are always in the "traditional" state and make no RCT-grade claims; [modern · evidence-based] entries carry the five states. Points of agreement are listed separately, and conflicts are flagged [divergence point] (10 places) [L3:卷首]; the traditional never passes itself off as evidence-based, and the modern labels its tier honestly.

### 1.3 The epistemic-state crosswalk, four tiers (Table 1-1 · the in-table tier shorthand is defined in the table's second column)
| L3 epistemic state | Plain-language tier | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| FACT | confirmed by research (确) | backed by controlled trials / reviews or the guideline-facing side |
| MODEL | fairly well established (较) | mechanism clear, a single trial, or a surrogate indicator holding |
| INFERENCE | still being studied (研) | observational leads, causation unproven |
| UNKNOWN | not known (知) | do not believe any assertion |
| CANDIDATE | still being studied (earlier stage) | mechanism / single-case level only |
| traditional | the traditional-experience domain | the pattern-differentiation domain; does not pass itself off as evidence-based |

### 1.4 The three rules of use
① **Red lines first**: clear Chapter 2 before anything; ② **diet is the supporting role**: for dry skin the main cause is bathing [L3:#38], for dry eyes the mainline is eye-use rhythm [L3:#49], for a urinary tract infection flare medical care comes first [L3:#46]; ③ **if you take medication, check Chapter 9 first**: the drug conflict outranks any dietary therapy [L3:横切面].

### Glossary (in order of appearance)
1. **Symptom**: an abnormal bodily sensation; ≠ a disease diagnosis.
2. **Oral rehydration salts (ORS)**: a WHO-formula sachet drink designed specifically to replace the water lost in diarrhea.
3. **Osmotic diarrhea**: concentrated sugars / sugar alcohols dragging water into the gut (excess fruit juice, lots of chewing gum).
4. **FODMAP**: fermentable carbohydrates that readily produce gas and pull water (onions, legumes, lactose, and so on); low FODMAP = a self-trial method of restricting first, then reintroducing.
5. **Soluble fiber**: dissolves in water into a gel (psyllium, oats); firms the stool.
6. **Heme / non-heme iron**: the iron of meat, liver, and blood tofu (well absorbed) vs plant iron (poorly absorbed); vitamin C raises absorption of the latter 2-3 fold.
7. **fa wu**: a traditional concept — foods believed to trigger or worsen illness; not broadly confirmed by modern evidence.
8. **Pattern-differentiation treatment**: the traditional method of setting a plan by cold / heat / deficiency / excess.
9. **Red-line (alarm) symptoms**: danger signals requiring immediate medical care (see Chapter 2).
10. **Lower esophageal sphincter (LES)**: the anti-reflux valve between esophagus and stomach; caffeine, chocolate, mint, and alcohol relax it.
11. **Purines**: abundant in organ meats, concentrated meat broths, and some seafood; metabolized into uric acid.
12. **Glycemic index (GI)**: a population-average ranking of how quickly foods raise blood glucose.
13. **Medication-overuse headache (MOH)**: the chronic headache caused, in reverse, by painkillers taken >10-15 days/month.
14. **Tyramine**: an amine in aged cheeses and cured foods; × MAOI antidepressants = hypertensive crisis.
15. **Ferritin**: the iron-store indicator; **thalassemia**: a hereditary blood disorder common in southern China — the two together = the mandatory cause-check before iron supplementation.
16. **Trigger diary**: recording diet and symptom attacks to find one's own triggers with one's own data.
17. **RCT**: randomized controlled trial, the gold standard of causal evidence.

**Summary**: symptom ≠ diagnosis; traditional and modern written separately, then aligned; red line, behavior, and drug conflict — the three priorities.

## Chapter 3. The digestive group (#9-#18)

**Learning objectives**: the rehydration order for diarrhea; the hard measures for reflux; the constipation three-piece set.

### 3.1 Acute diarrhea [L3:#9]
Step one is always rehydration, first choice oral rehydration salts — reduces severe-case death (confirmed by research); cola and juice are hypertonic sugar drinks and osmotically worsen diarrhea (confirmed by research). Step two: keep eating (WHO: early feeding shortens the course, confirmed by research); long-term congee-only eating is a myth. Specific probiotic strains shorten the course by about one day (fairly well established). Infants and young children: low-osmolarity ORS + continued feeding + zinc for 10-14 days. The TCM toasted-rice soup and Chinese-yam lotus-seed congee are traditional astringent approaches (traditional), in slight tension with the modern "back to normal eating early" [L3:#9 分歧点].

### 3.2 Gastroesophageal reflux (heartburn) [L3:#13]
The two hardest items: **for the overweight, weight loss is the first intervention** (RCT/META, confirmed by research); **no food for 3 hours before bed** (acid exposure when lying flat, confirmed by research). The LES is relaxed by alcohol, caffeine, chocolate, mint, and carbonated drinks (fairly well established, individualized); high-fat big meals delay gastric emptying (fairly well established). The TCM "acid regurgitation: avoid sweet and greasy" points the same direction (traditional). Those on long-term acid suppressants: monitor B12 and magnesium.

### 3.3 Chronic constipation [L3:#11]
The three-piece set: **fiber 25-30 g/day** (whole grains, legumes, vegetables, fruit + prunes, confirmed by research) + **enough water** (otherwise the fiber instead causes bloating, confirmed by research) + **regular exercise and toilet routine** (confirmed by research); kiwifruit at 2 per day has an RCT (fairly well established). A low-fiber refined diet is the main negative cause (confirmed by research); iron supplements / calcium-containing antacids / opioids cause drug-induced constipation (confirmed by research). Add fiber slowly in the elderly. New-onset constipation + weight loss / blood in the stool / anemia = a cause-check red line. TCM black sesame, honey, and cassia seed are traditional bowel-moistening items (traditional).

### 3.4 The digestive quick-check table (Table 3-1)
| Symptom | Favor (tier) | Avoid (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic diarrhea [#10] | soluble fiber · fairly well established; low lactose · confirmed by research for the intolerant; check the cause first | sugar-alcohol gum · confirmed by research; excess juice, tea/coffee/alcohol · fairly well established |
| Stomach pain [#12] | regular meals, small frequent servings, low fat · fairly well established | greasy big meals, painkillers on an empty stomach, drinking on an empty stomach · confirmed by research |
| Nausea and vomiting [#14] | ginger · fairly well established, both streams agree; dry crisp carbohydrates in small frequent servings · confirmed by research | greasy heavy flavors, too long on an empty stomach · confirmed by research |
| Bloating and gas [#15] | low-FODMAP self-trial with gradual reintroduction · fairly well established; eat slowly and chew well · confirmed by research | carbonated drinks and gum, sugar alcohols · confirmed by research |
| Mouth ulcers [#16] | supplement after checking iron / zinc / B vitamins / folate · fairly well established; yogurt · still being studied | SLS toothpaste · fairly well established; individual triggers = not known |
| Hemorrhoids [#17] | high fiber + enough water, no long toilet sitting with a phone · confirmed by research | spicy food · fairly well established; alcohol · still being studied |
| Poor appetite [#18] | small frequent servings + energy density first · confirmed by research; zinc / B1 · fairly well established | snacks replacing meals, sweet drinks before meals · fairly well established |

[L3:#10-#18] **Summary**: after diarrhea, salt first, then food; heartburn relies on weight loss and the pre-bed empty window; constipation relies on fiber, water, and movement.

## Chapter 4. The head/face/respiratory group (#1-#8, #48, #49)

**Learning objectives**: the honey boundary; for rhinitis, "environment > diet"; the too-hot risk.

### 4.1 The common cold and acute cough [L3:#1/#4]
**Honey (age ≥1)** relieves night cough, comparable to common cough medicines (fairly well established); **honey is forbidden under age 1** (infant botulism risk, confirmed by research). Lukewarm liquids and soft foods help mucosal hydration (still being studied); chicken soup = traditional + observational relief (still being studied). **High-dose vitamin C for preventing colds: no prevention / shortening evidence in the general population (confirmed by research)**. Smoking, including secondhand smoke, is a clear avoid (confirmed by research). TCM divides wind-cold and wind-heat, each matched to its pattern (traditional). Red lines: fever >3 days, rapid breathing, coughing blood, cough >4 weeks with weight loss → seek care and check the cause.

### 4.2 Allergic rhinitis [L3:#6]
**Environmental control (dust mites / pollen) > diet** (guideline-facing, confirmed by research). Omega-3 deep-sea fish and fermented dairy are directional leads (still being studied); avoid alcohol (the congestion-worsening side, still being studied). **Divergence point**: the tradition lists shrimp, crab, and goose as "wind-provoking items", while the modern position is not to routinely restrict seafood unless an IgE-confirmed allergy exists — blind broad-spectrum restriction carries real nutritional risk [L3:#6 分歧点]. For confirmed allergy: strict avoidance + nutritional replacement (confirmed by research).

### 4.3 The head/face quick-check table (Table 4-1)
| Symptom | Favor (tier) | Avoid (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Influenza [#2] | water + electrolytes, easily digested energy · confirmed by research; antivirals within 48h take priority over diet | alcohol · confirmed by research |
| Acute sore throat [#3] | lukewarm liquids · confirmed by research; honey as a film · fairly well established | hotter than 65°C · an esophageal-cancer-risk association, confirmed by research; spicy and fried food |
| Fever [#5] | rehydration salts for water, light eating without fasting · confirmed by research; keep feeding young children | alcohol rubdowns, bundling up to sweat · forbidden in children, confirmed by research |
| Sinusitis [#7] | enough water, warm steam · still being studied; a symptom-specific restriction = not known | red line: red swelling around the eye / vision change → emergency |
| Bad breath [#8] | water + sugar-free gum to boost saliva · confirmed by research; green tea · still being studied | tobacco and alcohol, garlic and onion · confirmed by research |
| Dry mouth / excess thirst [#48] | water sipped evenly, sugar-free sour stimulation · confirmed by research / fairly well established | sugary drinks replacing water · confirmed by research; red line: excess thirst and urination + weight loss → check blood glucose |
| Dry eyes / eye strain [#49] | the 20-20-20 eye rule as the mainline · confirmed by research; Omega-3 · fairly well established | a symptom-specific restriction = not known |

[L3:#1-#8/#48/#49] **Summary**: honey has an age floor; for rhinitis manage the environment before the mouth; heat above 65°C is a clear risk.

## Chapter 5. The neuro/sleep/mood group (#19-#24)

**Learning objectives**: the hard measures for insomnia; the diary method for migraine.

### 5.1 Insomnia [L3:#19]
The strongest agreed items: **no caffeine after midday** (half-life about 5h; the evening residue disrupts sleep structure, confirmed by research); **zero alcohol in the evening** (it helps falling asleep but wrecks the second half of the night and fragments REM, confirmed by research); **dinner neither too full nor too late** (reflux disturbs sleep, confirmed by research — the TCM "when the stomach is not at peace, sleep is not at peace" arrives at the same point from the other side). Magnesium-containing foods at dinner have directional support (fairly well established, food-scale amounts).

### 5.2 Headache / migraine [L3:#20]
**There is no universal dietary trigger** (the population is highly heterogeneous; broad-spectrum restriction lacks evidence = not known) — the right tool is a **trigger diary** to locate one's own triggers (aged cheese / processed meat / MSG as candidates, evidence mixed, fairly well established). Regular meals, no empty stomach (low blood glucose is an accepted trigger, fairly well established); caffeine at regular, limited doses (the withdrawal-headache mechanism is clear, confirmed by research). Red line: painkillers >10-15 days/month cause medication-overuse headache in reverse (confirmed by research); thunderclap headache / neurological signs → emergency.

### 5.3 The group quick-check table (Table 5-1)
| Symptom | Favor (tier) | Avoid (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Mild anxiety [#21] | after-midday caffeine reduction, magnesium sources, Omega-3 · fairly well established / still being studied | alcohol as self-medication · rebound anxiety confirmed by research; red line: >2 weeks affecting functioning / thoughts of self-harm → professional assessment |
| Low mood [#22] | Mediterranean pattern · the SMILES trial, fairly well established; EPA-type Omega-3 · fairly well established | the ultra-processed-food pattern · still being studied; for those on MAOIs, limit tyramine · confirmed by research; red line: thoughts of self-harm → seek medical care immediately |
| Brain fog [#23] | B12 check · confirmed by research for vegetarians / the elderly / long-term metformin users; Mediterranean / MIND · fairly well established | a symptom-specific restriction = not known |
| Motion sickness [#24] | ginger slices / ginger capsules before departure, light dry food, neither empty nor overfull · fairly well established | greasy big meals before departure, reading and phone use in transit · confirmed by research |

[L3:#21-#24] **Summary**: for insomnia quit caffeine and alcohol; for migraine use a diary, not a restriction list; mood red lines always outrank dietary therapy.

## Chapter 6. The female-specific group (#25-#28, #50, #52)

**Learning objectives**: the first line for period pain and where ginger's evidence sits; evidence-based iron repletion.

### 6.1 Period pain (primary) [L3:#25]
The modern first line is NSAIDs such as ibuprofen (stomach-harming on an empty stomach, confirmed by research); on the dietary side **ginger** has small trials comparable to NSAIDs (fairly well established), and fatty fish 2-3 times a week (fairly well established). **The divergence point, written honestly**: "avoid cold and iced drinks" is a traditional cold-stasis pattern prohibition, and the modern side has not confirmed that iced drinks worsen period pain — treat it as an individual self-trial item, not a universal ban. Period pain that progressively worsens / pain with intercourse / pain outside periods → rule out endometriosis (red line).

### 6.2 Heavy menstrual bleeding and the anemic look [L3:#27/#52]
Evidence-based iron repletion: **heme iron** (red meat / small weekly servings of liver / blood tofu) + **vitamin-C fruits and vegetables in the same meal** (raises non-heme iron absorption 2-3 fold, confirmed by research); **keep tea and coffee about 1 hour apart from iron-containing meals** (tannins inhibit absorption, confirmed by research). **No blind iron supplements**: check a complete blood count + ferritin first — for people with thalassemia, iron supplementation is harmful (confirmed by research; a note for populations in southern China). **Divergence point**: red dates and longan are the traditional dietary-therapy side — low iron, poorly absorbed; they do not replace the evidence-based route [L3:#52 分歧点]. Months of heavy flow + fatigue and pallor → check the cause.

### 6.3 The group quick-check table (Table 6-1)
| Symptom | Favor (tier) | Avoid (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Premenstrual syndrome [#26] | calcium about 1000mg · RCT, fairly well established; pre-period salt limiting · a consensus point; complex carbohydrates in small frequent servings · fairly well established | pre-period caffeine, alcohol, high-salt processed food · still being studied / fairly well established |
| Pregnancy nausea and vomiting [#28] | ginger · fairly well established; small frequent servings, dry crisp carbohydrates before getting out of bed · confirmed by research | greasy heavy flavors, too long on an empty stomach; red line: weight loss >5% / ketonuria / unable to eat → seek care; zero alcohol throughout, caffeine <200mg/day |
| Cold hands and feet [#50] | iron check first, B12 / iodine substrates, adequate protein · fairly well established / confirmed by research | excessive dieting, tobacco · confirmed by research; "avoid cold and raw" = an individual self-trial, not a universal ban |

[L3:#25-#28/#50/#52] **Summary**: for period pain, recognize ginger first, then self-trial restrictions; iron repletion goes "liver and blood + vitamin C in the same meal + tea and coffee kept apart"; the pregnancy-vomiting red line watches weight and intake.

## Chapter 7. The skin group (#35-#39)

**Learning objectives**: for eczema, "barrier > restriction"; limiting sugar for acne; the He Shou Wu warning.

### 7.1 Eczema (atopic dermatitis) [L3:#35]
The mainline = **barrier repair (moisturizers) and environmental control**, diet as the adjunct (a weak consensus point). For confirmed food allergy (in children commonly milk / egg): avoidance + nutritional replacement (confirmed by research); **long-term restriction of many foods without a confirmed diagnosis = the risk of malnutrition / growth faltering outweighing the benefit (confirmed by research, most strongly in children), and requires dietitian supervision**. There is no universal "eczema diet" (not known); the probiotic evidence is mixed (still being studied). **This book's first divergence point**: the tradition restricts "fa wu" (shrimp, crab, egg) vs the modern side, which avoids only on confirmed allergy — eggs and milk are precisely children's quality protein; do not restrict them routinely unless allergy is proven [L3:#35 分歧点].

### 7.2 Acne [L3:#36]
The hardest-evidenced dietary item = a **low-GI structure** (RCT improving acne scores, fairly well established); limiting sugar and sweets is a consensus point of both streams. The skim-milk association (still being studied, mechanism unclear); whey-protein supplements (fairly well established, the association side); Omega-3 and zinc (still being studied). **Hard drug-conflict warning**: isotretinoin × vitamin A supplements = stacked toxicity + teratogenicity (confirmed by research; during treatment, vitamin A supplements are forbidden + strict contraception); tetracyclines × milk and calcium kept apart (confirmed by research).

### 7.3 The group quick-check table (Table 7-1)
| Symptom | Favor (tier) | Avoid (tier) |
|---|---|---|
| Urticaria (hives) [#37] | most chronic spontaneous cases are unrelated to food · confirmed by research; the diary method to locate individual triggers | broad-spectrum / low-histamine restriction · not known; alcohol and spicy food · a consensus point; red line: throat tightening → emergency |
| Dry skin [#38] | the main cause is bathing: water not too hot, no strong alkaline soap · confirmed by research; essential-fatty-acid and vitamin-A foods · fairly well established | a symptom-specific restriction = not known |
| Hair loss [#39] | palm-sized protein at every meal · confirmed by research; iron / zinc supplemented after checking · fairly well established; vitamin D by test results · still being studied | rapid weight-loss dieting · a typical trigger, confirmed by research; **He Shou Wu taken orally · liver-injury reports confirmed by research — no self-directed oral use**; biotin has no evidence and interferes with lab tests · fairly well established |

[L3:#35-#39] **Summary**: for eczema, moisturize first and do not restrict blindly (especially in children); for acne, lower GI first; among the "black nourishes black" items, the only one to stop is oral He Shou Wu.

## Chapter 8. The whole-body and chronic-disease group (#29-#34, #40-#47, #51)

**Learning objectives**: DASH and salt limiting; the gout three hard avoids.

### 8.1 Hypertension [L3:#29]
Strongest evidence: the **DASH pattern** (high vegetables-and-fruit + low-fat dairy + whole grains, a blood-pressure-lowering magnitude comparable to a single drug, confirmed by research); **sodium <5 g salt/day** (WHO, confirmed by research); high-potassium fruits and vegetables (premised on normal kidney function, confirmed by research); low-sodium salt substitutes (for those with normal kidney function, fairly well established). Avoid: alcohol (dose-dependent blood-pressure raising), high-sodium processed food (the main dietary sodium source — >70% comes from processing), and licorice preparations, which raise pressure and lower potassium (all confirmed by research). **Drug conflict**: those on ACEI/ARB/spironolactone — ask a doctor before touching low-sodium salt or potassium supplements (hyperkalemia risk, confirmed by research). Blood pressure >180/120 with chest pain, vision change, or speech problems → emergency. TCM chrysanthemum, cassia seed, and celery are traditional liver-calming items (efficacy unproven · does not replace medication, traditional).

### 8.2 Gout [L3:#31]
The three hard avoids (one of the two streams' strongest consensus items, confirmed by research): **alcohol (especially beer)**; **animal organs / concentrated meat broths / some seafood (high-purine)**; **fructose drinks**. Favor: low-fat dairy daily, enough water (fairly well established); cherry and coffee are only observational associations (still being studied) — do not treat them as therapy; in the intercritical period, urate-lowering medication as the doctor prescribes is the mainstay (confirmed by research). The TCM "damp-heat: avoid alcohol and meat" and the modern purine / alcohol mechanisms arrive at the same point by different roads (traditional).

## Chapter 10. Bridging Chinese and Western medicine: the four adjudicating divergence points

**Learning objectives**: restate each of the four divergence points in one sentence. [L3:分歧点1-4]

1. **Post-surgery / eczema "fa wu"**: the tradition restricts seafood, eggs, and poultry vs the modern side, for which the first choice in early post-surgery feeding is precisely these foods. Conclusion: unless an allergy is confirmed, restriction must not create a protein gap [L3:#44/#35].
2. **Red dates and longan for "enriching the blood"**: the traditional dietary-therapy comfort side vs the modern side's measured iron content (low iron, poorly absorbed). Conclusion: evidence-based iron repletion goes the heme-iron + vitamin-C route [L3:#52].
3. **Spinach and tofu "mutual restraint"**: a myth corrected — eaten together, oxalate absorption actually falls (calcium binds oxalate in the gut); for stone-formers this is a "favor", not an "avoid" [L3:#47].
4. **He Shou Wu for "darkening the hair"**: a famous traditional dietary item vs the modern hard warning of oral liver injury (confirmed by research). Conclusion: list it in the avoid column; self-directed oral use is forbidden [L3:#39].

**Summary**: bridging = write separately, then align; the drug conflict always outranks traditional dietary therapy.

## Chapter 11. FAQ (10 questions)

1. **Can this book replace seeing a doctor?** No. L3 states plainly that this book covers the daily and recovery side and does not replace medical care; for a red line, the only correct action is to seek care [L3:红线总纲].
2. **What is "fa wu"?** A folk / traditional surgery-domain concept. Post-surgery "fa wu" restriction has no universal basis (not known), and the protein gap created by restriction has real harm (confirmed by research); the single exception = strict avoidance for those with confirmed allergy [L3:#44/#35].
3. **When TCM dietary restriction and modern nutrition science clash, whom to follow?** Execute the strongest consensus items with confidence (such as avoiding alcohol); treat purely traditional items as individual self-trials (such as avoiding cold foods for period pain); where medication is involved, the drug conflict comes first; where the symptom's mainline is behavioral or therapeutic (dry skin = bathing, dry eyes = eye-use rhythm), diet is not the main operator [L3:分歧点/横切面/#38/#49].
4. **How to use honey for cough?** From age ≥1, as a non-drug measure for night cough (fairly well established); under age 1, absolutely forbidden (confirmed by research) [L3:#4].
5. **Can I drink sports drinks or cola for diarrhea?** Wrong match. Hypertonic sugar drinks osmotically worsen diarrhea; only the ORS sugar-salt ratio is designed for rehydration (confirmed by research) [L3:#9].
6. **Do bananas help constipation?** L3 does not list a standalone "banana relieves constipation" assertion. What has trial backing is total fiber, prunes, and kiwifruit (what is not listed gets no claim) [L3:#11].
7. **Can I take ibuprofen for period pain?** The modern first line is precisely NSAIDs (confirmed by research), which harm the stomach on an empty stomach; ginger has small-trial support; progressively worsening pain calls for checking endometriosis (red line) [L3:#25].
8. **Is a longer restriction list more serious and therefore better?** Quite the opposite. Unconfirmed broad-spectrum restriction has the real harm of nutritional imbalance (confirmed by research); the right tool is a trigger diary to locate individual triggers [L3:#6/#20/#37].
9. **At what point does pregnancy vomiting require medical care?** Unable to eat, weight loss >5%, ketonuria = the hyperemesis gravidarum red line; ordinary pregnancy vomiting uses ginger + small frequent servings + dry crisp carbohydrates in the morning before rising (confirmed by research / fairly well established) [L3:#28].
10. **Must gout mean avoiding all seafood for life?** In the acute phase and on the three hard avoids, be strict (confirmed by research); in the intercritical period the leads are enough water + low-fat dairy + urate-lowering medication as the doctor prescribes; cherry and coffee are only observational adjuncts (still being studied) [L3:#31].

## Chapter 12. Self-test (8 questions · with answers)

1. Acute diarrhea — step one and step two? — Oral rehydration salts for rehydration (reduces severe-case death); keep eating (shortens the course); do not drink only congee. [#9]
2. Name three red-line signals that require immediate medical care with no dietary advice. — Vomiting blood / black stools, thunderclap headache, sudden facial droop / limb weakness / slurred speech (the rest are in Chapter 2).
3. The modern position and tier on post-surgery "fa wu" restriction? — No universal scientific basis (not known); unless an allergy is confirmed, restriction must not create a protein gap; after surgery the body wants protein at 1.2-1.6g/kg (confirmed by research). [#44]
4. The complete evidence-based iron-repletion route? — Heme iron + vitamin C in the same meal (absorption up 2-3 fold) + tea and coffee kept about 1 hour apart; check a blood count and ferritin first. [#27/#52]
5. The two hardest dietary measures for insomnia? — Cut caffeine after midday (half-life about 5h); zero alcohol in the evening (it wrecks the second half of the night's sleep structure). [#19]
6. Why a trigger diary for migraine rather than a restriction list? — There is no universal dietary trigger (not known), individuals differ widely; broad-spectrum restriction lacks evidence. [#20]
7. The risk of restricting many foods for a child's eczema? — The risk of malnutrition / growth faltering outweighs the benefit (confirmed by research); multi-food restriction requires dietitian supervision. [#35]
8. Why not just buy an iron supplement for anemia? — Check the cause first: blind iron supplementation carries an overload risk, and for people with thalassemia iron is harmful (confirmed by research). [#52]
