# Child & Adolescent Mental-Health Crisis Canon v1 (English · 2026-09-12)

> **The gap this volume fills**: the healing universe already has an adult mood volume (the mood canon), but adolescence (roughly ages 10–24) is a dedicated face where depression / self-harm / suicide crisis differ in **presentation, communication, and treatment boundaries** — and the parent's perspective has long been blank. This volume does only this piece and does not duplicate the adult volume: the adult volume covers general principles; this volume covers the adolescent-specific face + what parents can do.
> **Red-line statement**: this volume is a **public-education text** — zero case studies, zero individual information, zero identifiable details. It is not a diagnostic tool and cannot replace a clinical consultation — the judgment to seek care always belongs to a psychiatrist; what this volume answers is "how parents understand, how to open the conversation, and when action is mandatory."
> **Epistemic discipline**: every key claim is labeled FACT (citation-level source) / MODEL (review and guideline consensus) / INFERENCE (derived from evidence) / HYPOTHESIS (candidate awaiting verification) / UNKNOWN (explicitly admitted not known). All PMIDs come from this lane's Europe PMC citation-level retrieval (channel = TinyFish, see source receipt) — none from memory.

## Face 1 · The dividing line: adolescent emotional storms ≠ disorder

**The core distinction [MODEL]**: the adolescent brain and emotional systems are in violent reorganization; large mood swings are a developmental norm. The diagnostic line is not "how big the emotions are" but three **functional lines**:

1. **Persistence**: a depression-like state (loss of interest, low mood, irritability) lasting **≥2 weeks, nearly every day, most of the day** — feeling down after one bombed exam and recovering within a week or two is normal fluctuation [MODEL · based on general diagnostic-framework framing].
2. **Functional decline**: school attendance and grades falling off a cliff, abandoning former hobbies, social withdrawal, self-care sliding — "the person can no longer hold their previous functioning" is a harder signal than "is the mood good or bad" [MODEL].
3. **Cross-situational**: abnormal at home, at school, and with friends, not only in a single context (single-context abnormality is more likely a problem of that context than a disorder) [MODEL].

**Irritability is a common face of adolescent depression [FACT · citation]**: adult depression often presents as sadness; in children and adolescents it more often presents as **irritability, temper outbursts, oppositionality**. The classic review on the diagnostic boundary between severe mood dysregulation and bipolar disorder (Leibenluft 2011, PMID 21123313) points out that chronic irritability and episodic rage in adolescents are often misread as "rebellion" or "bipolar," when they are in fact depression-spectrum signals needing evaluation. Parent self-check framing: **"blows up more easily than before, feels emptier after blowing up, and it has lasted more than two weeks"** merits professional evaluation — rather than waiting for the child to "grow sensible."

**When it is only a storm**: the fluctuation has a clear trigger, can be soothed, functioning is preserved, sleep and appetite are basically normal, and the parent-child relationship has friction but no deep rupture — observation + communication suffice; no need to pathologize [MODEL]. Stigmatizing normal development, and dismissing a disorder as rebellion, are errors equally harmful in opposite directions.

## Face 2 · Epidemiology in real numbers (framings labeled separately)

**Global framing [FACT · WHO 2025 fact sheets · retrieved live via TinyFish]**:
- Suicide is the **third leading cause of death among 15–29-year-olds** (WHO suicide fact sheet and adolescent mental-health fact sheet, consistent framing).
- Depression, anxiety, and behavioral disorders are **among the leading causes of illness and disability in adolescents** (WHO adolescent mental-health fact sheet, original framing).
- More than 720,000 people die by suicide each year in the whole population; a previous suicide attempt is an important risk factor for future suicide (WHO suicide fact sheet).

**Global adolescent symptom prevalence [FACT · citation]**: Racine et al. 2021 (JAMA Pediatr, PMID 34369987) meta-analysis during COVID: pooled detection of clinically elevated depressive symptoms in adolescents **25.2%** (95%CI 21.2–29.7), anxiety symptoms **20.5%** (17.2–24.4) — roughly **double** pre-pandemic estimates. Note: this is a "symptom-screening positive" framing, not clinically confirmed prevalence.

**China framing [FACT · report framing · kept separate from clinically confirmed framing]**:
- "Report on National Mental Health Development in China (2019–2020)" (the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Psychology mental health blue book): adolescent depressive **symptom detection rate 24.6%** (mild 17.2% · severe 7.4%) — a screening-detection framing.
- The 2022 blue book: among over thirty thousand adolescents, 14.8% carried some degree of depression risk; the 2023–2024 edition: adolescent depressive symptom detection in the 15%–25% range. Within the same framing the numbers fluctuate across editions, attributable to sampling and instrument differences [FACT · report framing].
- A review of adolescent depression in China (Front Psychol 2026, PMID 42630177) systematically organizes local prevalence, risk factors, and intervention status — a usable entry point into the Chinese-language literature.

**Ranking of suicide among causes of death in Chinese adolescents [UNKNOWN · honest downgrade]**: this lane could not verify, within this round's channels, an authoritative public single number for the rank of suicide among causes of death for ages 10–24 in China (candidate sources: the China Health Statistics Yearbook, GBD China estimates) — **prefer an empty cell to an invented one**; this cell stays empty pending a dedicated lane. The global framing is above (WHO).

**NSSI (non-suicidal self-injury) special entry**:
- Definition [FACT]: NSSI = self-inflicted bodily damage without suicidal intent (cutting, scratching, burning, hitting etc.); in ICD-11 it is a **standalone entry** (MMS chapter, MB23.E), coded separately from suicidal behavior (WHO ICD-11 framing; the NSSI entry was verified by retrieval, while the coding number of the suicidal-behavior entry was not verified in this round → that half-sentence is labeled MODEL).
- Prevalence [FACT · citation]: NSSI is common in adolescents with systematic differences by age and sex (Wilkinson et al. 2022, J Affect Disord, PMID 35304237); for the short-term course of NSSI in psychiatric care populations see Kiekens et al. 2024 (JAMA Netw Open, PMID 39436647). **A precise lifetime-rate figure in community samples: UNKNOWN** — definitions and instruments vary widely across studies; this lane cites no single number.
- **The boundary between NSSI and suicide attempts [INFERENCE · synthesis in progress]**: the two are conceptually distinct (with or without suicidal intent), but NSSI is a **risk marker** for later suicide attempts — a forward synthesis (PLoS One 2026 systematic review protocol, PMID 42721133) is integrating the prospective evidence on "NSSI → later attempts and death"; existing cohort evidence (PMID 39436647, 40995649) supports high comorbidity but non-equivalence. Parent framing: **"it's just self-harm, not wanting to die" does not stand as a safety conclusion — NSSI always merits professional evaluation.**

## Face 3 · How parents talk about it (first, break three taboos)

**① Asking about suicide directly does not "plant the idea" [FACT · citation-level falsification]**: Dazzi et al. 2014 (Psychol Med, PMID 24998511, King's College London systematic review), original conclusion: "no study was found showing a statistically significant increase in suicidal ideation among participants asked about suicidal ideation"; moreover, "acknowledging and talking about suicide may in fact reduce rather than increase suicidal ideation, and improve mental health among help-seeking populations." **Asking closes the door on the danger; not asking leaves the child to carry it alone.** This is the evidence-based falsification of the fear that "asking = inducing"; it shares origins with the adult-volume anchor mood:suicide_asking_safe.

**How to ask [MODEL]**: specific, calm, not an interrogation — "Have you been feeling really exhausted lately, sometimes feeling life is pointless?" is ten times better than "You're not having dark thoughts, are you?" If the child denies it but you have doubts: no forcing confessions; keep the door open ("whenever you want to talk, I'm here").

**② The four don'ts of listening [MODEL · communication consensus]**:
- **Don't say "cheer up / you just have too much free time"** — negating feelings = closing the conversation;
- **Don't compare** ("others have it far worse") — pain is not a ranking tournament;
- **Don't accuse** ("haven't we done everything for you") — guilt is fuel for depression, not its antidote;
- **Don't rush to fix** (whoever offers solutions first usually shuts down the sharing first) — catch first, discuss later.
The goal of each conversation is not "correcting the child" but **making them willing to open up again next time** [INFERENCE].

**③ When a medical visit is mandatory [MODEL · red-line list]**: if any one of the following appears, go to psychiatry / mental health (child & adolescent direction) **this very week**:
- suicidal ideation, a suicide plan, or self-harm behavior, any of them exposed;
- abnormal mood / irritability ≥2 weeks with functional decline (Face 1's three lines);
- repeated expressions like "the pain doesn't fit inside," "life is pointless," "they'd be better off without me";
- markedly disordered sleep and appetite + social withdrawal + grades falling off a cliff, occurring together;
- situational signals such as substance use, running away from home, "farewell-style" distribution of belongings (see Face 4).
Seeking care is not a verdict of "having a disorder" — it is **giving the brain a check-up**, the same logic as a blood test for a fever [INFERENCE].

## Face 4 · Crisis intervention (three things in the acute phase + the family ledger afterwards)

> **Crisis lines printed in this volume**: 12356 — China's national psychological assistance hotline; Beijing Psychological Crisis Research and Intervention Center hotline — landline 800-810-1117, mobile/online 010-82951332. Readers elsewhere: use your local crisis line — international directory findahelpline.com; US 988; UK/Ireland Samaritans 116 123. *(translator calibration; source anchor = 12356)*

**The suicide-risk signal list [MODEL]**:
- **Verbal**: "life is pointless," "you'd all be lighter without me," "the pain will stop soon" — any direct or metaphorical expression about death, disappearing, or goodbye;
- **Behavioral**: giving away treasured belongings, writing farewell-style texts, searching for suicide methods, escalating self-harm or shifting from NSSI toward injurious intent, a sudden "calm" (the relief of a decision), alcohol or drug misuse;
- **Situational**: major loss (relationship breakup, bullying exposure, a humiliation event), collapse of key support, family history of suicide, previous attempt history (WHO: a previous attempt is among the strongest predictors [FACT]), and not seeking help because of stigma (WHO, same framing).

**What to do in the acute phase [MODEL · the order must not be shuffled]**:
1. **No one alone**: ensure the young person is not alone during the high-risk window (handoffs must overlap — someone is always present);
2. **Remove the means**: move medications, sharp objects, and cords/ropes out of reach — "means restriction" is one of WHO's four key suicide-prevention measures [FACT · WHO suicide fact sheet];
3. **Call a hotline / go to hospital**:
   - **12356**: China's unified national psychological assistance hotline [FACT · established by a National Health Commission document 2024-12, unified national launch from 2025-05-01 · verified against nhc.gov.cn original text];
   - **Beijing Psychological Crisis Research and Intervention Center hotline**: landline 800-810-1117 · mobile/online users 010-82951332 [FACT · verified against the center's official site crisis.org.cn];
   - Other regions: **the local mental-health center / general-hospital emergency department** — this lane does not fabricate numbers for local hotlines it has not individually verified, and honestly routes readers to emergency departments and 12356.
4. Do not promise to keep a suicide secret (safety outranks promises); do not argue or hold court; let professionals take over.

**After a suicide attempt: the family's response [MODEL]**:
- The 48 hours to one week after an attempt is the peak window of re-risk; discharge ≠ the crisis is over — follow-up visits, medication management, and means restriction at home proceed in parallel [INFERENCE · based on WHO's framing that prior attempt = strongest risk factor];
- Two forbidden parent questions: "how could you be so foolish" / "what more could you possibly want"; two usable questions: "how much did it hurt right then" / "let's plan the best possible day for next week together";
- The family is a place of recovery, not an interrogation room: reduce humiliating talk (relatives' cross-examinations are blocked at the door) [MODEL];
- The whole family (siblings included) can receive psychological support — an attempt is itself a traumatic event for family members [INFERENCE].

## Face 5 · The honest ledger of treatment (two pages, neither frightening away nor glossing)

**Psychotherapy first line [FACT · citation]**: Zhou et al. 2020 network meta-analysis (Lancet Psychiatry, PMID 32563306, comparative efficacy of acute-phase treatments for depression in children and adolescents), original key points:
- **Fluoxetine ± CBT** is the optimal choice in acute-phase moderate-to-severe depression (the only two options significantly superior to pill placebo / psychological controls) [FACT];
- **IPT (interpersonal psychotherapy)** was significantly superior to the psychological controls; the evidence standing of IPT-A (the adolescent version) is in Mootz/Mufson 2026 annual review (Annu Rev Clin Psychol, PMID 42097988) and school-setting prevention research (JCCAP 2021, PMID 31429601) [FACT/MODEL];
- The same paper's honesty clause: "high-quality evidence is scarce" is the authors' own framing — effective ≠ evidence perfect [FACT].
Mild-to-moderate first episodes: structured psychotherapy (CBT/IPT-A) usually goes first [MODEL · guideline-consensus framing].

**Medication boundaries — the two-page ledger of the black-box warning [FACT · citation]**:
- **One page (risk)**: Hammad et al. 2006 (Arch Gen Psychiatry, PMID 16520440, an FDA-commissioned meta-analysis of individual data from 24 pediatric and adolescent trials): antidepressants raise suicidality (ideation/behavior) risk **mild-to-moderately** in pediatric patients — in depression trials the SSRI group's RR 1.66 (95%CI 1.02–2.68), all indications pooled RR 1.95 (1.28–2.98), **absolute risk difference only 0.02 (0.01–0.03)**. This is the evidence base of the FDA's 2004 black-box warning.
- **The other page (the risk of not treating)**: adolescent depression is itself among the strongest proximal risk domains for suicide (WHO framing; see Face 4); the self-risk, functional, and developmental costs of untreated moderate-to-severe depression are real [INFERENCE]. The correct reading of the risk comparison: **the black box describes a reason for monitoring, not a prohibition on medication** — the medication decision is made by three informed parties (psychiatrist + parents + adolescent), and the early weeks on medication need closer observation [MODEL].
- **The black box's downstream cost (page three of the honest ledger)**: after the warning, adolescent antidepressant use fell significantly alongside a widening treatment gap (Busch 2009, Health Aff, PMID 19414881; Kafali 2018, Psychiatr Serv, PMID 29241433); the net effect of the warning itself is contested in the literature (Fornaro 2019, Front Psychiatry, PMID 31130881) [FACT · use fell + the controversy exists].
- **Indication framing [FACT · retrieval-verified]**: fluoxetine is approved for depression in children ≥8 years, escitalopram ≥12 years (FDA / Mayo clinical-information framing) — "children can't take antidepressants" is an outdated claim, and "any antidepressant, used casually" is worse.

**The evidence tier of lithium for recurrence in adolescents [INFERENCE+UNKNOWN · honestly tiered]**:
- Adult face: lithium's anti-suicide effect has a decade-spanning body of systematic reviews (Waszak 2025, PMID 40430560; an updated meta of lithium for suicidal ideation/behavior/suicide, J Affect Disord 2025, PMID 40441661) [FACT · adult evidence exists];
- Adolescent face: systematic reviews of pharmacologic treatment for suicidality in children and adolescents explicitly state **high-quality evidence is scarce** (EClinicalMedicine 2026 structured review, PMID 41561405) [FACT · the scarcity itself has a citation];
- Conclusion: lithium for recurrence prevention and suicide protection on the adolescent bipolar spectrum belongs to the tier of **"adult-evidence extrapolation + limited direct adolescent evidence"** — extrapolation reasonable but limited in strength [INFERENCE]; adolescent-specific RCT-grade recurrence-prevention data: **UNKNOWN** (not retrieved this lane), honestly left blank.

**An honest footnote on a landmark trial [FACT · citation]**: TADS (the classic fluoxetine trial for adolescent depression) was re-analyzed in 2025 by a RIAT (restoring invisible and abandoned trials) project (Int J Risk Saf Med, PMID 40421808) — landmark studies too get re-examined and corrected; this is precisely the isomorphic reason that science is falsifiable and "a canon must be correctable."

## Face 6 · School and the internet

**School bullying: both the bullied and the bullies are at high risk [FACT · citation]**:
- The bullied face: van Geel et al. 2014 (JAMA Pediatr, PMID 24615300) meta-analysis: peer bullying / cyberbullying victimization is significantly associated with suicidal ideation and attempts [FACT]; for the association of traditional + cyber bullying with suicidal ideation in Chinese adolescents see BMC Psychiatry 2019 (PMID 31660917) [FACT];
- The bully face: Sourander et al. 2007 (Pediatrics, PMID 17671067, the Finnish From Boy to Man cohort): childhood bullies' early-adulthood outcomes (antisocial behavior, psychological problems etc.) were also significantly worse — **bullying is not "being strong"; it is itself a high-risk role** [FACT];
- Parent framing: the holes in "just hit back" are in falsification-face item 6; the correct actions = preserve evidence + contact the school + assess the child's state, three lines in parallel [MODEL].

**School refusal [MODEL]**: school refusal is usually a **symptom outlet** of anxiety / depression / bullying / learning difficulty, not laziness; the handling principles = early identification (at the first attendance wobbles) + neither dragging hard nor indulging + examine the upstream cause + school-family-medicine tripartite collaboration. Long-term school refusal is associated with poor adult functioning [INFERENCE]. This lane performed no citation-level verification for a school-refusal topic; this passage gives the consensus framing at MODEL epistemic state and leaves the citation slot as UNKNOWN — a dedicated lane will add anchors.

**Internet addiction: the disagreement written honestly [FACT/MODEL]**:
- **ICD-11 has adopted "gaming disorder" as a formal diagnosis** (entered by WHO 2019) [FACT];
- **DSM-5 has not** (listing "internet gaming disorder" only as a condition for further study) [FACT · classification status];
- The two editions measured in the same frame diverge: in the same youth sample, ICD-11-framed gaming disorder detection was 2.48% (95%CI 1.92–3.18) vs DSM-5-framed 3.83% (3.13–4.69) — ICD-11 more conservative (Addict Behav Rep 2026, PMID 42099438) [FACT];
- Judgment framing: diagnosis looks at **loss of control + priority takeover + continuing despite consequences + functional impairment + duration**, not at "playing long"; "a child who loves games = internet addiction" pathologizes a hobby, and "games can never be addictive" is the equally wrong pole [MODEL].

**Electric-shock / coercive "internet-addiction treatment" institutions [FACT · official disposition verified]**: the General Office of the Ministry of Health once issued a dedicated document **stopping the clinical application of electric-stimulation (or electroconvulsive) techniques to treat "internet addiction"** (nhc.gov.cn original text); contemporaneous reporting framed its safety and effectiveness as unestablished — such institutions are operations **already officially halted and without evidence-based support**; before seeking help, parents must check credentials (a proper psychiatric institution); any "forced correction where consent sits outside the child" is itself a source of harm [FACT · the halt document exists; INFERENCE · forced correction = source of harm].

## Face 7 · Falsification face (each item with its falsifier)

1. **"Asking about suicide induces suicide"** — falsified by the Dazzi 2014 systematic review (PMID 24998511: no consistent evidence that asking increases ideation; if anything it may reduce it). Falsifier: if a future high-quality prospective RCT shows significantly and reproducibly higher ideation rates in the asked group → this item is downgraded with an erratum.
2. **"It's just teenage rebellion; they'll grow out of it"** — grounds: persistent functional decline + symptoms ≥2 weeks is the evaluation line (Face 1); untreated adolescent depression carries real developmental and safety costs. Falsifier: if long-term cohorts show all those with persistent functional decline fully self-recover without intervention and with no safety events → the judgment line is revised.
3. **"Self-harm = attention-seeking, not serious"** — grounds: NSSI is highly associated with later suicide attempts (PMID 39436647/40995649/42721133, synthesis in progress); even where "attention-seeking" motivation exists, **needing attention is itself needing intervention**. Falsifier: if the prospective synthesis (PMID 42721133) shows NSSI has no independent predictive power for later attempts → "risk marker" is downgraded to "comorbidity marker" with an erratum.
4. **"Counseling is for the dramatic / only for the disordered"** — grounds: structured psychotherapies such as CBT/IPT-A work for adolescent depression (PMID 32563306/42097988); counseling is capability-building, not a stigma label. Falsifier: if the network meta-analysis is retracted or overturned by larger samples showing psychotherapy ineffective → Face 5 is rewritten.
5. **"Antidepressants make you dull / they're addictive"** — grounds: SSRIs have no evidence of addiction (discontinuation reactions ≠ addiction; see the adult volume's mood:discontinuation sister anchor); "makes you dull" has no consistent cognitive-impairment evidence. The real risk lies elsewhere (a mild early rise in suicidality + the black box, the two-page ledger written honestly, Face 5). Falsifier: if reproducible evidence of cognitive impairment or a dependence syndrome appears → this item is reversed.
6. **"If your child is bullied, just hit back"** — grounds: the bully role itself is associated with poor adult outcomes (PMID 17671067); pure violent retaliation trains the victim into the bully role and does not solve systemic bullying; the evidence-supported path = adult intervention + school procedures + assessment of the child's state (Face 6). Falsifier: if cohorts show retaliation strategies long-term outperform adult intervention without raising bully-role risk → the advice is revised.
7. **"Send internet addiction off for electric shock / forced withdrawal"** — grounds: electric stimulation for "internet addiction" was halted by the General Office of the Ministry of Health (safety and effectiveness unestablished, nhc.gov.cn original text); forced correction damages attachment and felt safety — precisely high-risk factors for depression. Falsifier: if a peer-reviewed RCT proves such techniques safe and effective AND officials lift the ban → this item is reversed (currently zero on both counts).
