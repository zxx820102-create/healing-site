# Vaccine Canon v1 (2026-09-12)

Vaccines are among the highest-return interventions in medical history: they erased from the earth a disease that had raged for at least three thousand years (smallpox), brought a disease that could paralyze a child for life (poliomyelitis) to the brink of eradication, and turned cervical cancer into the first cancer humanity can plausibly eliminate by vaccine. Yet vaccines are also the densest rumor terrain — because the vaccinated are healthy people, any coincidence of timing is mistaken for cause.

This canon has seven faces: **principles → evidence → safety → rumor falsification → China's immunization program → the doctor conversation → red flags**. Every key claim carries an epistemic label (FACT = data frozen to authoritative sources; MODEL = model estimate; INFERENCE = judgment derived from evidence; CANDIDATE = pending re-check).

## 1. The principles face: how vaccines train the immune system

### 1.1 The core principle: show the immune system the "wanted poster," without releasing the actual criminal

The immune system's most powerful weapon is **adaptive immunity**: on meeting a pathogen, antigen-presenting cells display the pathogen's signature fragments (antigens) to lymphocytes, which produce antibodies and T cells against it. The first response is slow and weak (1–2 weeks) — that is the disease happening. But the response leaves behind **memory B cells and memory T cells** (FACT · immunology foundational consensus); the next time the same pathogen arrives, the memory cells mount an attack far stronger than the first, within hours to days — the virus is cleared before it gains a foothold.

**A vaccine = a safe "rehearsal"**: remove the pathogen's disease-causing power (inactivation), weaken it (attenuation), or take only its signature fragments (subunit / mRNA / vector delivery), letting the body complete a full-dress rehearsal without falling ill. The memory the rehearsal leaves is the protection (FACT).

### 1.2 The platform table (plain-language version)

| Platform | Plain-language explanation | Representatives | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inactivated vaccine | The virus is "killed outright" and injected; the corpse is the textbook | Inactivated polio (IPV) · inactivated hepatitis A · inactivated influenza | Stable, mature safety record; usually needs multiple doses + boosters; generally cannot cause a real infection |
| Live attenuated vaccine | The virus is alive but its "kung fu is crippled"; mimics natural infection for the most complete training | MMR · varicella · oral polio (OPV) · BCG | Often one or two doses give lasting protection; generally avoided in severe immunosuppression (needs physician assessment) |
| Subunit / toxoid vaccine | Takes only one "coat" of the pathogen, or the detoxified toxin | DTaP (toxoid) · hepatitis B (surface antigen) · HPV (capsid protein) | Minimal ingredients, low reactogenicity; usually needs an adjuvant to "wake" the immune system |
| Conjugate vaccine | Hangs a protein "handle" on a polysaccharide antigen so even an infant's immune system can recognize it | Meningococcal conjugate · Hib | Solved the classic problem of infants responding weakly to pure polysaccharides |
| Viral vector vaccine | A harmless virus serves as the "delivery van" carrying the antigen blueprint | Ebola vaccine · some COVID-19 vaccines | Single-dose regimens have the advantage; effectiveness may be blunted in those with immunity to the vector itself |
| mRNA vaccine | Delivers the "antigen blueprint" (messenger RNA) directly into cells; the body prints the antigen itself | COVID-19 mRNA vaccines | Extremely fast development; the mRNA itself degrades within days and never enters the genome (FACT · mRNA mechanism consensus) |

(Each platform's detailed mechanism is textbook consensus · FACT; each product's specific process defers to its label · stays unfrozen.)

### 1.3 Herd immunity: vaccination is not one person's business

Transmission has a basic quantity, **R0**: how many people one infected person infects on average in a population with no immunity. To block transmission, the immune share must exceed **1 − 1/R0** (FACT · epidemiological formula).

| Disease | R0 (approximate range) | Herd-immunity threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Measles | 12–18 | **about 92–95%** |
| Pertussis | 12–17 | about 92–94% |
| Poliomyelitis | 5–7 | about 80–86% |
| Influenza (varies widely by pandemic strain) | 1.3–3 | about 20–60% |

(R0 is a **scenario-dependent model quantity** · MODEL — it shifts with population density and contact patterns and serves only to grasp magnitudes.)

### 1.4 "We skipped it and nothing happened" — the classic free-rider fallacy

Some say: all the other children are vaccinated, so mine is fine without. That sentence **is precisely proof that herd immunity is working** — you rode on everyone else's vaccination (INFERENCE). The problems:

1. This layer of protection is **borrowed**: once coverage falls, the protection fails immediately — measles's resurgence in many countries in recent years began almost every time in unvaccinated clusters (FACT · WHO measles-resurgence reporting).
2. Some people **cannot ride**: infants too young to be vaccinated, the immunocompromised (chemotherapy / transplant / HIV), the severely allergic to vaccine components — their only protection is high enough coverage around them.
3. Measles's 92–95% threshold means: 1 unvaccinated child in 20 may be enough for the virus to regain its footing.

## 2. The evidence face: the historical ledger numbers don't lie about

### 2.1 Smallpox: the only human disease humanity has eradicated

- In the pre-vaccine era, smallpox caused an estimated 300–500 million deaths in the 20th century (MODEL · historical estimates vary in framing).
- When WHO's intensified eradication program launched in 1967, there were still roughly 10–15 million cases a year worldwide (FACT · WHO smallpox history page).
- **The last natural case: Somalia, 1977**; **on 8 May 1980 the World Health Assembly formally declared smallpox eradicated** (FACT · WHO).
- The smallpox vaccine (cowpox/vaccinia) was a live attenuated/related-virus route — with the "show the immune system a wanted poster" idea, humanity crossed a disease off the death list.

### 2.2 Poliomyelitis: from 350,000 cases to two endemic countries

- When the global eradication initiative (GPEI) launched in 1988, an estimated **350,000 paralytic polio cases** occurred worldwide each year.
- Today global cases are down **more than 99%** (FACT · GPEI official page); wild polio now circulates endemically only in Afghanistan and Pakistan (FACT · GPEI; latest announcements prevail).
- Its price was lifelong irreversible paralysis — the vaccine turned the "iron-lung generation" into a historical term.

### 2.3 Measles: the true face of the "ordinary minor illness" that killed hundreds of thousands a year

- In the pre-vaccine era, measles killed an estimated 2.6 million people globally each year (MODEL · WHO pre-vaccine-era estimate).
- **2000–2024: measles vaccination is estimated to have prevented about 59 million deaths** (FACT · WHO official page 2026-07; CDC/MMWR's estimate for 2000–2023 is about 60.3 million · slightly different framing, same order of magnitude).
- On the long scale, over the past 50 years the measles vaccine is estimated to have prevented more than 90 million deaths (MODEL · Our World in Data model-based estimate).
- Measles still has no specific treatment — supportive care + vitamin A; prevention rests almost entirely on the vaccine.

### 2.4 HPV vaccine and cervical cancer: real cohorts, from "preventing a virus" to "preventing cancer"

This is the vaccine value proof at **cancer grade** — two large national registry cohorts:

- **Sweden (Lei et al., NEJM 2020)**: a national registry cohort of 1.67 million women aged 10–30. Among quadrivalent HPV-vaccine recipients there were 19 cases of invasive cervical cancer vs 538 among the unvaccinated; **those vaccinated before age 17 had roughly an 88% risk reduction** (FACT · NEJM 2020;383:1340-1348).
- **England (Falcaro et al., Lancet 2021)**: among women born after 1995-09-01 and offered vaccination at 12–13, cervical-cancer incidence **fell 87% (95% CI 72–94%)** and CIN3 precancerous lesions fell 97% — the researchers described cervical cancer as "almost eliminated" in these birth cohorts (FACT · Lancet 2021 publication page).

### 2.5 Why the influenza vaccine is needed every year

The influenza virus escapes immunity with two tricks: **antigenic drift** (small-step mutations in the HA/NA surface proteins, accumulating yearly) and **antigenic shift** (rare large-scale recombination, capable of causing pandemics). Drift leaves last year's immune memory incompletely matched to this year's strains, so WHO twice a year (February for the northern hemisphere, September for the southern) updates its recommended strains on the basis of the global surveillance network (FACT · WHO influenza surveillance mechanism). Getting a flu shot is like refreshing the wanted-poster photo each year — it cannot block every strain, but it markedly reduces severe illness and hospitalization (FACT · multi-season Cochrane/cohorts combined evidence; exact efficacy floats with strain match · MODEL).

## 3. The safety face: an honest ledger

**Principle: understating normal reactions forfeits trust; overstating vanishingly rare risks costs lives. Stratify by frequency and by certainty of attribution.**

### 3.1 Common and normal (FACT · each vaccine's label and monitoring consensus)

- Local: injection-site redness, swelling, pain, induration — self-resolving in 1–3 days.
- Systemic: low-to-moderate fever, fatigue, poor appetite, (in infants) transient fussiness — usually 1–2 days.
- These are the normal stirrings of the immune system's rehearsal. Note the mirror fallacy: **"no reaction = no effect" does not hold** — reaction size has no necessary relation to protection (FACT · immunology consensus).
- Live attenuated vaccines (e.g., MMR) can produce a transient low fever or sparse rash 5–12 days after vaccination — the vaccine virus being mildly managed in its rehearsal, not wild-virus infection.

### 3.2 True contraindications and deferrals (the boundary belongs to the doctor's judgment)

- **Contraindications (that vaccine generally not given)**: a **severe allergic reaction (anaphylactic shock)** to a component of the vaccine or to a previous dose of the same vaccine; **severe immunosuppression avoids live attenuated vaccines** (chemotherapy, post-transplant, certain immunodeficiencies — individualized by the physician; inactivated vaccines are usually fine).
- **Deferral (postpone, never permanently refuse)**: acute moderate-to-severe illness (including febrile illness) — vaccinate after recovery; a mere common cold without fever is usually no reason to defer.
- **Common misconceptions**: mild diarrhea, eczema, prematurity, breastfeeding, antibiotic use, family allergy history (without severe egg allergy) — **none of these** is a contraindication (FACT · WHO/CDC contraindication definitions). For egg allergy, the overall safety evidence for MMR and influenza vaccination is good, but proceed per the physician's on-site assessment (FACT → process: the doctor prevails).

### 3.3 Vanishingly rare adverse events and the monitoring systems (attribution graded honestly)

- **Anaphylaxis**: a vanishingly rare risk on the order of about one per million doses (MODEL · estimates across vaccines cluster around 1/million); this is why clinics **observe for 30 minutes** after vaccination — on-site treatment (epinephrine) almost always saves the person.
- **Febrile seizures**: post-vaccination fever in infants can occasionally trigger one; a single febrile seizure has an excellent prognosis in the great majority of cases (FACT · pediatrics consensus); those with a seizure history should tell the doctor and observe per guidance — **this is usually not a contraindication**.
- Other vanishingly rare events (the intussusception window risk with the rotavirus vaccine; the weak association signals between certain vaccines and Guillain-Barré syndrome) all went through the full "signal → verification → procedure adjustment" cycle, some leading to changed recommendations (FACT · e.g., the first-generation Rotashield market withdrawal).
- **How the monitoring systems work** (US example; China runs a corresponding AEFI monitoring system · stays unfrozen):
  - **VAERS (passive surveillance)**: anyone can report. The official statement of its limitation must be relayed honestly: "**report counts alone cannot serve as evidence of a causal link between a vaccine and an adverse event**" (FACT · VAERS/HHS official page). Its role is **radar** — it finds signals; it does not convict.
  - **VSD and the like (active surveillance)**: only controlled comparisons across databases covering millions can answer "do the vaccinated get X more often than the unvaccinated" (FACT · system design).
  - **What readers should know**: the social-media case report — "someone somewhere got vaccinated and then got X" — falls naturally into the VAERS-type trap of **report ≠ cause**; sequence in time is not causation (probability basics · FACT). Background rates never stop: in any vaccination season people have colds, strokes, and new autism diagnoses every day — it would happen without any vaccine.

## 4. The rumor-falsification main battlefield (8 rumors, full chains)

### R1 Vaccines → autism: one fraudster taxed a generation of children's immunity

The complete chain, every link checkable (all FACT):

1. **1998-02-28**: Wakefield and 12 co-authors published in The Lancet a case series of only **12 children** (no control group), insinuating a link between MMR and a new syndrome.
2. 2004: ten co-authors retracted the "causal interpretation"; The Times and others exposed Wakefield's litigation funding and ethics violations.
3. **February 2010**: The Lancet formally **retracted the paper** (recorded in CMAJ 2010).
4. **2010-05-24**: the UK General Medical Council ruled him dishonest and in breach of research ethics, **revoking his medical license** (Guardian 2010-05-24 record).
5. **January 2011**: BMJ published the investigation (Godlee editorial), characterizing it as **deliberate fraud** — the data had been altered to manufacture the association.
6. **The subsequent large-sample evidence (the real answer)**: two Danish national cohorts in a row — Madsen 2002 (NEJM, ~537,000 children) and Hviid 2019 (JAMA, **657,461 children**, including a high-risk susceptible subgroup): **MMR vaccination has no association with autism**, and does not trigger illness in susceptible children either (FACT · both papers' original conclusions).

**Falsifier**: any new study claiming a vaccine-autism association must first explain how it surpasses a 650,000-child national registry cohort; absent larger, cleaner evidence, the "zero association" verdict stands.**

### R2 "Vaccines contain poisons": the dose makes the poison — not the name making the fear

- **Aluminum adjuvants**: used for decades to boost responses, in minuscule doses. An infant's daily aluminum intake from breast milk / formula / solid foods is **higher** than the aluminum in one vaccine dose (FACT · the intake comparison ledger assembled by FDA/Offit); the real environmental-aluminum risk comes from large-dose occupational exposure, not vaccines.
- **"Mercury" = thimerosal (ethylmercury)**: metabolized differently from the genuinely harmful **methylmercury**; ethylmercury's half-life is short and it does not bioaccumulate (FACT · toxicology consensus). Routine childhood vaccines in the US removed thimerosal from 2001 — and autism diagnosis rates **did not fall** afterward; they kept rising (broadened criteria and recognition as the main drivers · INFERENCE) — this fact by itself falsifies the mercury hypothesis.
- **Formaldehyde**: used in producing some inactivated vaccines to inactivate/remove impurities, with minuscule residues in the final product; whereas the **formaldehyde naturally present in human blood** (a metabolic intermediate, on the order of ~1 mg in an infant's body) exceeds a dose's residue by more than an order of magnitude (FACT · toxicology comparison ledger). Eating a single pear delivers more formaldehyde than a vaccine's residue (MODEL · common science-communication conversion).

### R3 "Natural infection is better than the vaccine — every child gets the rash sooner or later"

"Natural immunity is real" — but its **price tag** is never shown by the rumor:

- Measles: wild-virus infection **wipes out large stocks of existing immune memory**, causing 2–3 years of immune suppression after infection (FACT · the Petrova et al., Science 2019 / Sci Immunol ledger), erasing old protection against other diseases besides; it can also kill and cause pneumonia and encephalitis.
- Varicella: not a "rash every child is bound to get"; it can cause skin infection, pneumonia, encephalitis, and returns in adulthood as shingles to collect interest.
- Polio: about 1 in 200 infections ends in permanent paralysis (FACT · WHO).
- Hepatitis B: chronicity rates in infants reach 90% (FACT · WHO), raising lifetime liver-cancer risk — which is exactly why the birth-dose is given within 24 hours of birth.
- **The risk-comparison ledger**: for vaccine-preventable diseases, natural infection's death/severe-illness risks exceed the vaccines' vanishingly rare risks by more than three orders of magnitude (INFERENCE · estimated from the case-fatality figures above vs million-dose-grade adverse events).

### R4 "Too many vaccines, too close together, will crush an infant's immune system"

- Offit et al.'s classic 2002 calculation in Pediatrics: an infant could theoretically **respond to about 10,000 vaccines at once** (FACT · original conclusion).
- The more counter-intuitive scientific accounting: thanks to protein purification and conjugation technology, **the total antigen count of the modern childhood schedule has fallen by more than an order of magnitude since the 1980s** (from ~3,000+ down to ~150–160 · FACT · Offit review framing; exact numbers float with schedule versions). "More injections" ≠ "more antigen load."
- The environmental antigens an infant meets daily through mouth and skin (bacteria, foods, dust mites) far exceed vaccine doses (FACT · immunology consensus). Vaccination occupies a small fraction of the immune system's "seats" and **does not crowd out** responses to other pathogens (R4's "uses up the immunity budget" model has been empirically refuted).

### R5 "The HPV vaccine causes infertility / premature ovarian failure"

- Large-sample evidence repeatedly shows no association: a US cohort (Schmuhl et al. 2020, populations seeking assisted reproduction / fertility treatment) and multinational registry studies all **found no decline in fertility among recipients** (FACT · study conclusions).
- Reports of the vanishingly rare POI (primary ovarian insufficiency) occur at rates comparable to background population incidence (FACT · monitoring review conclusions).
- The reverse accounting: it is treatment for cervical lesions caused by HPV infection itself (cone biopsy and the like) that is the established factor affecting fertility outcomes (FACT · obstetrics-gynecology consensus) — skipping the vaccine raises the risk along that road.
- After Japan suspended proactive HPV recommendation in 2013 (driven by anecdote, not new evidence), years of collapsed coverage and the disease's return made it a nation-scale natural experiment in the price of hesitancy (FACT · the Japan event history; multiple large Japanese domestic studies from 2015 found no causality · INFERENCE).

### R6 "People get the disease anyway after vaccination, so it's useless"

- **Individual level**: no vaccine claims 100% protection against infection; but the unvaccinated carry far higher risk than recipients of high-efficacy vaccines (measles, 2 doses, ~97%) (FACT · efficacy data).
- **Breakthrough infection ≠ vaccinated for nothing**: for fast-drifting viruses like influenza/COVID-19, the vaccine's core promise is **preventing severe illness, hospitalization and death** — breakthrough cases are milder and shorter (FACT · multi-season cohorts, consistent direction).
- **Population level**: even with breakthroughs, the vaccinated have shorter transmission windows and pass the infection to fewer people (INFERENCE · derived from viral-load and course data; magnitude varies by disease · MODEL).
- The seatbelt analogy: wearing a seatbelt, you can still be injured; no one concludes seatbelts are useless.

### R7 "Vaccines are a conspiracy / contain chips / the drug companies are hiding it"

- The vaccine-safety system is run crosswise by **independent institutions of many countries** (WHO/GACVS, national regulators, university hospitals); any systematic cover-up would require tens of thousands of professionals across the globe conspiring for decades — what actually happened in history is the opposite: an **open market withdrawal** (Rotashield) and an **open retraction** (the Wakefield case), proof the correction machinery runs (INFERENCE · inferring the system's character from case history).
- mRNA vaccines containing a "microchip/tracker": mRNA molecules are nanoscale and cannot carry any electronic device — physically impossible (FACT · material-scale common knowledge).

### R8 Vaccine hesitancy is not "ignorance"; it is a trust problem (the social face)

- In 2019 WHO named "vaccine hesitancy" one of the **ten threats to global health** (FACT · WHO official).
- The hesitancy triangle: **confidence** (do you trust the vaccine and the system), **complacency** (feeling the disease is long gone), **convenience** (can you actually get the shot easily) — most hesitators are not rejecting science; they have been hit by anecdote narratives + the timing-adjacency illusion + a trust gap (MODEL · the SAGE hesitancy decision matrix).
- **Communication that works**: no mockery, no jargon dumping; start by asking "what worries you most"; give concrete numbers in risk comparisons; telling the full falsification history of the Wakefield retraction works better than explaining mechanisms. A healthcare worker's one sentence remains the source parents trust most (FACT · multi-country surveys, consistent direction).

## 5. The China immunization-program face (policy information stays unfrozen)

> 🔒 **Freeze-boundary statement**: this section writes only **structure** and **stable facts**; all specific doses, ages, free/paid splits and age ranges **defer to the latest National Health Commission / China CDC immunization schedule, local policy and the vaccinating clinician**. Schedules get revised (HPV age expansion, version updates); this canon freezes no process numbers.

### 5.1 The children's free-vaccine spectrum (structural overview)

China's National Immunization Program vaccines are **free** for eligible children, covering 12 vaccine-preventable diseases (FACT · WHO China vaccines page framing): hepatitis B, tuberculosis (BCG), polio, pertussis, diphtheria, tetanus, measles-rubella-mumps (MMR), Japanese encephalitis, meningococcal disease, hepatitis A. Structural features:

- The first dose of hepatitis B vaccine is required **within 24 hours of birth** (the key window for blocking mother-to-child transmission).
- The same disease often has substitutable alternatives across processes ("inactivated / live attenuated" or "polysaccharide / conjugate") — whether a paid alternative is worth it is judged case by case by family budget and the doctor's advice.
- Nursery and school entry runs on a **vaccination-certificate verification** system; missed doses can be caught up with the certificate.

### 5.2 What adults should catch up on (mostly paid · per physician assessment)

- **HPV vaccine**: available to women aged 9–45 (domestic bivalent / imported bivalent / quadrivalent / 9-valent coexist; the 9-valent expanded to ages 9–45 · latest local supply and policy prevail); male vaccination is gradually opening in parts of the mainland (stays unfrozen).
- **Influenza vaccine**: one dose yearly (priority: ages 60+, pregnant women, chronic-disease patients, healthcare workers — the benefit of vaccination in pregnancy is well evidenced · FACT).
- **Pneumococcal vaccine**: older adults and high-risk groups (60/65+, per local policy).
- **Shingles vaccine**: ages 50+ (recombinant vaccine · paid · per local supply).
- **Hepatitis B**: check antibody (anti-HBs) first; boost if negative — especially with a carrier or high-exposure person in the household.

### 5.3 Where to get vaccinated, how to look it up

Community health centers / township health-station vaccination clinics are the main channel; provinces have vaccination-record lookup systems (regional apps such as Yuemiao, Jinmiaobao · names update by region, stays unfrozen). **One-sentence principle for any dose question: call the number on the vaccination certificate or ask the clinic's doctor; the current schedule table prevails.**

## 6. The doctor-conversation face: three sets of questions to ask well

### 6.1 Ask the doctor before vaccination

1. "My child has a runny nose / a slight fever today — can we still get this dose?" (acute illness with fever → generally defer; a common cold without fever → usually fine — the doctor judges on site)
2. "After the last dose he ran a high fever / had a seizure / broke out in a full-body rash — should we still get this one?" (report the prior reaction; the doctor decides continue / change process / refer for assessment)
3. "He was premature / has eczema / is on steroids / a family member is immunocompromised — is this dose live or inactivated?" (flag live-vaccine-sensitive scenarios in advance)
4. "Can this dose be given at the same time as other vaccines? How long a gap?" (co-administration of combination/multivalent vaccines has schedule rules; follow the doctor)

### 6.2 Confirm three things after vaccination

1. Stay for **30 minutes** of observation before leaving (against acute allergy — this is a required action, not a suggestion).
2. Ask clearly "what symptoms in the next 24–48 hours mean we come back / seek care."
3. Check the vaccination certificate on the spot for this record (vaccine name / batch number / date) — the batch number is the key to traceability and compensation.

### 6.3 Late or missed doses: how to catch up (principles; the doctor's plan prevails)

- **The great majority of vaccines can be caught up, and there is no need to "start over from scratch"** — assess the doses already given against the current schedule and rearrange the intervals (FACT · catch-up scheduling principles · specific intervals stay unfrozen).
- Hepatitis B dose 1 missed within the 24-hour window: **catch up as soon as possible**; blocking still works substantially (INFERENCE · derived from the blocking mechanism; the earlier the better).
- Adult catch-up works the same way: antibody status unknown → test first, then boost (e.g., hepatitis B), or boost straight per schedule (MMR, influenza need no such test).

### 6.4 Vaccination assessment for children with special health conditions (scenario list)

| Scenario | Broad direction (the physician's assessment prevails) |
|---|---|
| Preterm infant | Vaccinate by chronological age since birth; very low birth weight per the physician's individual plan |
| Stable primary immunodeficiency / HIV infection | Avoid live attenuated vaccines; inactivated generally usable — specialist assessment mandatory |
| Leukemia / chemotherapy / post-transplant | Avoid live vaccines during treatment; rebuild immunity per the specialist's window after completion / transplant |
| Febrile-seizure history / stable epilepsy | Not a contraindication; tell the doctor and observe per guidance (INFERENCE → process) |
| Eczema / broken skin | Just avoid the affected skin site; for severe eczema defer certain intradermal live vaccines per the doctor's judgment |
| Egg allergy | Under current consensus MMR/influenza are generally vaccinable; execute with the doctor's on-site observation |

## 7. The red-flag chapter: after vaccination, seek care immediately if any of these appear

> These are the signals needing **immediate medical care** (most appear within minutes to hours after vaccination — which is exactly why the 30-minute observation exists):

- 🔴 **Allergic reaction (most urgent)**: hives spreading rapidly over the body, swelling of face/lips/throat, difficulty breathing, wheezing, pallor, weak rapid pulse, confusion — **call emergency services immediately**.
- 🔴 **Seizure / convulsion** (especially first-ever, or not stopping after more than 5 minutes).
- 🔴 **Persistent high fever**: >39–40°C not responding to fever care, or fever lasting beyond 48 hours (low-to-moderate fever within 48h of vaccination is common; fever beyond that window needs another cause sought · INFERENCE).
- 🔴 **Abnormal crying**: inconsolable for >3 hours (infants), with high-pitched crying, a bulging fontanelle, drowsiness hard to rouse, refusing food and fluids.
- 🔴 **Abnormal injection site**: large-area redness and swelling (>5–10 cm and expanding), pus, continuous worsening (note: a small local abscess and slow scabbing at the BCG site is a **normal slow process**, but markedly swollen or discharging axillary/supraclavicular lymph nodes that fail to heal need care · INFERENCE → the doctor's judgment prevails).
- 🔴 **Anything that strikes you as "not right, unlike usual" in a serious way** — a parent's intuition deserves one in-person visit; better a wasted trip than gritting through it.

(The red-flag chapter's flip side: the common mild reactions listed in Face 3 are managed at home and need not crowd emergency resources.)

- Every FACT item traces to official records at WHO/CDC/GPEI/NEJM/Lancet/JAMA/BMJ/Pediatrics (per-anchor details in the receipt).
- MODEL items (R0, efficacy drift, historical death estimates) are scenario-dependent estimates; cite them with range and source year.
- INFERENCE items (the risk order-of-magnitude comparison, the system's correction character, the timeliness of the birth-dose block) are the sturdiest judgments under current evidence, overturnable by larger evidence.
- **This canon's falsifiers**: ① any new study claiming a vaccine-autism association → must surpass the 657,000-child Danish cohort's evidentiary strength; ② any "got vaccinated, then got X" case → must first pass controlled comparison (active surveillance), not report counting; ③ any process number → the latest official schedule prevails; this canon accepts no responsibility for frozen process numbers.
