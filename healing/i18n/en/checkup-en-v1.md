# Checkup-Report Interpretation Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: a checkup report is a probability map, not a verdict — an arrow says "this person differs from 95% of healthy people," not "this person has a disease"; turning single-point numbers into trend lines, turning the package-menu list into risk questions, and turning anxiety into one well-prepared doctor's visit is the entire content of this general canon.

Everyone reads dozens of checkup reports in a lifetime, yet almost no one is ever taught how to read them. A single arrow on a tumor marker can scare a healthy person into the oncology department; the six characters of "sinus arrhythmia" can make a young person believe they have heart disease; while the "fasting glucose 6.5" that truly requires action is often waved away with a light "it's nothing, check again next year." **The direction of misreading is often the opposite of the direction of risk: small false positives get magnified, big true signals get ignored.** This canon's seven faces: general rules, tumor markers, nodules and shadows, metabolic and cardiovascular, infection and immunity, doctor communication, falsification. Epistemic states labeled item by item; each organ's deep water belongs to its dedicated canon (interlinks in each face); this volume only ties the thread that strings them together.

## Face 1 The general-rules face: the four grammar rules of report language

**1. Reference interval = the range of the central 95% of healthy people — 5% of healthy people are naturally outside it** (FACT · definitional fact)

A lab report's reference interval is drawn as the **central 95%** of a healthy population's data. Two things are mathematically true at once: (1) any perfectly healthy person, on any single item, has about a 5% chance of landing outside it, flagged "↑" or "↓"; (2) a report reading 20–30 items together has **roughly a half-to-four-in-five chance of at least one arrow** (1−0.95²⁰≈64%, INFERENCE · magnitude estimated from item count and independence). The arrow's first meaning is "you are 5 of these 100 who don't take the ordinary road," not "you are ill." Outside the interval ≠ abnormal; inside the interval ≠ no disease (many early lesions show perfectly normal markers).

**2. Re-testing = reading the trend dynamically; trend > single-point number** (MODEL · methodology)

A single-point number has full meaning only when connected to "your own previous value." For a mildly out-of-range item, the clinically key question is not "what is it this time" but **direction and speed**: three consecutive slow climbs and years of stability at the same mild level mean completely different things. This is the real meaning of "come back for a re-check" — not the doctor brushing you off, but turning one snapshot into a stretch of film. Stable numbers + no symptoms + no imaging abnormality = most arrows' destination is "keep observing."

**3. Screening ≠ diagnosis: false positives and false negatives in plain words** (MODEL · epidemiological framework)

- High **sensitivity** = among people with the disease, a high share are caught (few escape).
- High **specificity** = among people without the disease, a high share are passed over (no false alarm) (few friendly-fire cases).
- **Positive predictive value (PPV)** = the share of alarmed people who are truly ill. **PPV depends on prevalence**: in a low-prevalence healthy checkup population, even a good tool with 95% specificity alarms mostly the innocent (verified numbers in this volume's Face 2).
- Conversely, a negative screen ≠ an insurance policy: every tool has a miss rate (false negatives); "checked this year, all clear" means clear "at this tool's resolution, on that day, over those items."

**4. "The more tests the better" is a fallacy: over-testing itself has a price** (FACT · multi-source consensus, direction consistent)

Every test carries a three-layer cost: **direct cost** (radiation, invasiveness); the **incidentaloma cascade** (finding a "what-if" little something, triggering a chain of punctures / surgeries and other invasive confirmations, most of which end benign — the systematic review cited in Face 2 measured complication rates of the false-positive chain as high as 15%); and **opportunity cost** (budget and attention spent on low-value items while the truly indicated age- and risk-based items go undone). **Whole-body PET-CT as screening for asymptomatic healthy people is a composite sample of all three costs** — high radiation dose, massive incidental findings, no guideline support for general-population screening; it is a diagnostic tool for patients with indications. See the care-access and overdiagnosis canon: `docs/healing/care-access-canon-v1-20260912.md`. The correct posture: **choose items by age, sex, family history, and exposure history — not by price and item count.**

## Face 2 The tumor-marker face: elevated ≠ cancer

**Core evidence (FACT)**: a 2026 systematic review in Asian Pacific Journal of Cancer Prevention (38 studies included · PMID 42522803 · DOI 10.31557/apjcp.2026.27.7.2425) concluded clearly: CA125, CA19-9, CEA, CA15-3, and AFP show **consistently low positive predictive values in asymptomatic populations**; false positives frequently trigger imaging re-checks, invasive procedures, even surgery, and **complication rates of these chained procedures run as high as 15%**; large RCTs showed no mortality benefit from population screening with tumor markers. These numbers demolish two illusions at once: "checked and high = found it early" and "checked and not high = safe."

**Why screening PPV in healthy people is naturally very low** (INFERENCE · Bayesian structure): PPV = sensitivity×prevalence ÷ (sensitivity×prevalence + false-positive rate×healthy rate). Most cancers have population prevalence on the order of a few per thousand; even at 95% specificity, 1,000 healthy people first yield about 50 false positives, drowning the single-digit true cases — **among the alarmed, the truly ill are often fewer than one in several dozen**.

**The correct uses of common markers** (FACT · use-positioning is guideline consensus; numeric details in the dedicated canons):

| Marker | Correct use | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| CEA | **Post-treatment** recurrence monitoring in colorectal cancer (combined with imaging and colonoscopy) | Not a bowel-cancer screening tool (screening relies on colonoscopy and stool occult blood); smokers may run mildly high |
| CA125 | Ovarian-cancer treatment follow-up; adjunctive assessment of pelvic masses | Not an ovarian-cancer screening tool; menstruation, pelvic inflammation, and benign cysts can all raise it |
| PSA | Prostate-cancer screening is a **contested shared decision** (individualized at 55–69) | Elevated ≠ cancer; prostatic hyperplasia, cycling, ejaculation, and inflammation all push it up; see the cardio/uro sub-volumes |
| AFP | Liver-cancer surveillance in **high-risk groups** (cirrhosis / hepatitis B) (combined with ultrasound) | Not a liver-cancer screening plan for ordinary healthy people; pregnancy and active hepatitis can also raise it |

**The placebo face of "tumor-marker four/five-item packages"** (MODEL): the package delivers the psychological settlement of "I did something," not risk information — low-PPV alarms manufacture anxiety, and the false reassurance of normal results conceals the age-appropriate screening that should be done (colonoscopy, breast, cervix). **The placebo effect has side effects too: it crowds out real screening.**

**When is it worth watching at all** (INFERENCE · clinical combined-judgment path): an isolated, mildly elevated marker with no imaging abnormality and no high-risk factors overwhelmingly ends in a re-check; the signals worth escalating are **combined** — (1) the same marker **rising progressively** (trend on re-check, see Face 1); (2) magnitude significant (several times the upper limit); (3) **an imaging-found lesion**; (4) the corresponding high-risk background (family history / cirrhosis / chronic infection). The (4)+(1)+(3) judgment must be made by a doctor; a tumor marker never convicts alone.

## Face 3 The nodules-and-shadows face: the grand settlement of "found it = have the disease"

**The master mechanism: detection rate ≠ prevalence (screening bias)** (FACT · multi-source consensus, direction level). High-resolution ultrasound/CT has photographed the share of structures that the previous generation "died peacefully with, nodules and all." The surge in detection is mainly the **surge in resolution**, secondarily a small real rise in incidence; conflating the two is the first source of nodule anxiety.

**Thyroid nodules**: ultrasound detects large numbers of asymptomatic nodules, of which only a minority are malignant (the side-by-side numbers for detection and malignancy are in the thyroid canon: `docs/healing/thyroid-canon-v1-20260912.md`). The pathway is standardized: ultrasound features (TI-RADS class grading) → biopsy only with high-risk features → even for thyroid cancer, most types progress slowly with excellent prognosis (MODEL · direction level; numbers and stratification in the thyroid canon). **Most thyroid nodules' final form is "a repeat ultrasound every 6–12 months."**

**Lung nodules**: the Fleischner Society's 2017 guideline stratification for incidentally found lung nodules is the quantitative template of "too small to bother" (FACT · guideline original numbers):
- Single solid nodule **<6 mm**: in low-risk patients, **no routine follow-up**; optional 12-month CT for high-risk.
- Single solid nodule **6–8 mm**: low-risk repeat CT at 6–12 months (a risk threshold of about 1% is what makes follow-up worthwhile — the guideline's own "worth managing" line; which inversely shows that managing something with <1% risk is waste).
- Part-solid nodule ≥6 mm with solid component <6 mm: CT at 3–6 months, then annual follow-up to 5 years.
(Source: Fleischner Society 2017 · Radiology 10.1148/radiol.2017161659; abstract verified via Radiopaedia / Radiology Assistant relay. The slower follow-up rhythm of ground-glass nodules is in the lung canon: `docs/healing/resp-canon-v1-20260912.md`.)

**Breast nodules**: the conceptual skeleton of BI-RADS classification (MODEL · concept level): category 1 normal, 2 benign, 3 probably benign (short-interval follow-up), 4 suspicious (biopsy), 5 highly suspicious. **Category 3's "follow-up" is not delay; it is a formal medical decision** — its basis is precisely "that category's malignancy probability is <2%, and biopsy harms more than waiting." Numbers and the full BI-RADS table are in the breast deep card (`docs/healing/deep-card-6th-breast-neoplasms-20260912.md`).

**The one-line settlement**: the shared script of the three nodule sisters (thyroid / lung / breast) — imaging progress made "detection" cheap, and medicine uses grading systems to split "detection" into "the overwhelming majority observe" and "the few act." The right response to a nodule report is not fear but **asking for the grade and the follow-up interval**.

## Face 4 The metabolic and cardiovascular face: the densest arrows, the biggest action levers

**Blood lipids: LDL before total cholesterol** (MODEL · framework level). "Normal total cholesterol = all fine" is a common misreading — total cholesterol is a grab-bag of HDL (good) + LDL (bad) + the rest; a high HDL can hoist the total into "normal" while the LDL item alone exceeds its line. Read lipids starting from **LDL-C**, and set the target together with **overall cardiovascular risk** (hypertension / diabetes / smoking / family history): the higher the risk, the lower LDL should be pressed. Numeric stratification in the cardiovascular canon: `docs/healing/cardio-canon-v1-20260912.md`.

**Blood glucose: the division of labor between fasting and HbA1c** (MODEL · division-of-labor level). Fasting glucose is "tonight's snapshot" (easily perturbed by late nights and stress); HbA1c is "the average frame of the past 2–3 months." The two do not replace each other; they are **the two gates of screening and diagnosis**: a checkup fasting glucose of 6.1–6.9 mmol/L (impaired fasting glucose) is the signal to "add an HbA1c / OGTT," not a diabetes verdict. The full spectrum is in the diabetes canon: `docs/healing/diab-canon-v1-20260912.md`.

**Uric acid: asymptomatic hyperuricemia ≠ gout, and "the lower the better" is falsified**:
- Asymptomatic hyperuricemia is the soil of gout, but most people never flare in a lifetime (MODEL · direction level). Treatment thresholds stratify by comorbidity: under the Chinese guideline framework, those without comorbidities start urate-lowering only at a higher threshold; with comorbidities (hypertension / diabetes / kidney disease etc.) the threshold is wider (the precise stratification of start lines 480/420 μmol/L · MODEL, see the guideline originals and the dedicated canon).
- **FACT**: the 2024 updated Chinese guideline for hyperuricemia and gout (Part I · PMID 40692263 · DOI 10.1111/1756-185x.70375; Part II · PMID 40851321) confirms: there is a formal urate-lowering treatment recommendation for asymptomatic hyperuricemia (not "no drug ever"); and the serum-urate management target in chronic gout is **maintenance within 180–300 μmol/L** — **"the lower the better" is falsified: below 180 is not a target; uric acid itself has antioxidant and neuroprotective physiological functions, and pressing it too low is equally harmful.**
- The first prescription in the asymptomatic phase is lifestyle (weight loss, alcohol limits, fructose limits); drug initiation is judged by the doctor at the thresholds.

**Mildly elevated transaminases: the most common cause is fatty liver, not hepatitis panic** (MODEL · direction level; numeric spectrum in the liver canon). The common benign spectrum of mild ALT/AST elevation (within 1–2× the upper limit), ranked: **fatty liver (metabolic-associated) heads the list year after year**, followed by recent effects of late nights / strenuous exercise / alcohol, and drugs (supplements included). The right rhythm is neither the panic of "abnormal liver function = liver disease" nor the neglect of "it's nothing," but **a re-check in 2–3 months + one liver ultrasound**; persistent elevation then enters systematic workup. The full spectrum is in the liver-metabolic canon: `docs/healing/liver-metabolic-canon-v1-20260912.md`.

**Sinus arrhythmia = in most cases a healthy person's normal phenomenon** (MODEL · textbook-grade direction). "Sinus" says the pacemaker site is correct (the sinoatrial node); "arrhythmia" says the heartbeat speeds and slows slightly with breathing (respiratory sinus arrhythmia) — **especially common in the young, a sign of a healthy autonomic nervous system, not a disease**. Between it and "heart disease" lies the entire diagnostic process.

**"ST changes on ECG" ≠ coronary heart disease** (MODEL · re-check path). ST-T changes are a large class of nonspecific ECG findings; body position, tension, early repolarization, and female menopause can all contribute. It is not a verdict but one of the signals **prompting integrated judgment with symptoms, risk factors, and where needed exercise stress / imaging studies**. Nonspecific ST-T changes without chest pain, without risk factors, and stable across repeated ECGs usually carry limited clinical significance. The re-check path is in the cardiovascular canon.

## Face 5 The infection and immunity face: antibodies, the hepatitis-B five-panel, and the "immunity package"

**Helicobacter pylori antibody ≠ current infection** (FACT · guideline-original grade):
- **A positive serum antibody only means "infected at some point in this life"** — antibodies persist for months (even longer) after the bacterium is cleared, so **serology cannot be used to confirm eradication after treatment**. The original position of the Saudi Helicobacter pylori Working Group 2022/2023 guideline (PMID 36204804 · DOI 10.4103/sjg.sjg_288_22): **the urea breath test (UBT) is the best choice for eradication confirmation, the stool antigen test (SAT) second as an alternative, and serology is not recommended for eradication confirmation, because antibodies persist for months after the infection clears.**
- The correct linkage: antibody positive at checkup + no prior eradication → the antibody may reflect current or past infection; **run one breath test (stopping PPIs ≥2 weeks and antibiotics ≥4 weeks before the test) to settle current infection**; only a positive leads to treatment discussion, and ≥4 weeks after treatment a repeat breath test confirms eradication.
- **Typed antibodies** (CagA/VacA etc.) attempt to distinguish strain virulence and infection stage (MODEL · direction level): valuable as epidemiological tools, but equally unable alone to confirm current infection; the clinical linkage remains the breath / stool antigen tests.
- See the GI canon: `docs/healing/gi-health-canon-v1-20260912.md`.

**The hepatitis-B five-panel quick read** (MODEL · serological framework grade; specific interpretation defers to infectious-disease physicians): "big three positives / small three positives" are folk names; the real language is the combination of five markers: **surface antigen (HBsAg) = virus resident now; surface antibody (anti-HBs) = immunity (vaccine success or recovered infection) — the only "positive is good news"; e antigen / e antibody = a rough pointer to viral replication activity; core antibody (anti-HBc) = the imprint of past infection, carried for life.** The three most common checkup outcomes: all negative (vaccination due), only surface antibody positive (the ideal state), and the "antigen + core antibody" combination (needs liver-function and viral-load evaluation — enter the dedicated-canon pathway, not panic).

**"Immunity indicator" packages (immunoglobulin five / lymphocyte subsets) have limited meaning for healthy people** (INFERENCE · multiple guidelines do not support routine use): these tests' home turf is the patient stratification of recurrent severe infections, suspected immunodeficiency, and hematologic disease; for ordinary checkup recipients the results neither predict "catching colds easily" nor guide any intervention — **there is currently no accepted normalized framing for an "immunity score," and inside a package it is a placebo slot** (interlinked with the immune-defense canon, see the canon index `docs/theories/CANON-INDEX.md`). The genuinely actionable "immunity" evidence is: sleep, exercise, vaccination records, and infection-frequency history.

## Face 6 The doctor-communication face: turning the report into one well-prepared visit

**Three things to do when bringing a report to the doctor** (MODEL · methodology):
1. **Rank the arrows by importance**, most worth discussing first (the ranking principle: bigger magnitude > smaller; with high-risk background > without; rising trend > stable; actionable > only observable — screening value and action lever point the same way).
2. **Ask three things**: (1) what does this arrow (/nodule/elevation) mean? (2) is a next test needed (which)? (3) when to re-check, and for what? — three questions trade vague anxiety for a concrete schedule.
3. **Bring historical reports**: one trend table beats ten verbal descriptions; the first visit's goal is often simply to establish "your own baseline."

**Follow-up adherence = the biggest action lever** (INFERENCE · structural level). Nodule surveillance, HbA1c re-tests, colonoscopy repeats… medicine schedules observation for the safe and action for the suspicious; **both roads collapse on "can't be bothered to re-check"**: those who should observe are lost to follow-up and miss the turn from benign to suspicious; those who should act delay past the window. A checkup report's endpoint is not "looked at it"; it is **the next date already written on the calendar**.

**The boundary of second-hand interpretation: AI / internet reading = information ≠ diagnosis** (MODEL · boundary level). AI and search engines can explain reference intervals, terminology, and disease associations clearly (real informational value), but they cannot do three things for you: **see your complete report and history, bear the judgment responsibility, perform a physical examination**. Their correct use is "preview and question generator" (understand the terms you don't, list the questions and bring them to the visit); the wrong use is "replacing the doctor's conclusion." Internet experience posts ("my marker was high and it turned out to be XX") are survivorship-bias concentrate with a sample size of one.

## Face 7 The falsification face: the six most widely circulated wrong equations

Each with its falsifier (what evidence would overturn the falsification):

1. **"An arrow = disease"** — CONTRADICTED. The reference interval is the central 95% of healthy people; 5% of healthy people are naturally outside; with many items read together, at least one arrow is a high-probability event (Face 1). *Falsifier: if an item's interval is not a statistical definition but a clinical-prognosis threshold, or if one person shows large, persistent, same-direction deviations across multiple items with symptoms — then that arrow upgrades to a true signal; this falsification does not exempt individuals.*
2. **"High tumor marker = cancer"** — CONTRADICTED. PPV in asymptomatic populations is consistently low; the false-positive invasive chain's complication rate runs to 15%; large RCTs show no mortality benefit (PMID 42522803). Smoking, inflammation, and benign disease all raise markers. *Falsifier: in the combination of high-risk population + progressive rise + an imaging lesion, the marker regains high PPV — what is falsified is the "isolated equation," not the marker itself.*
3. **"One normal checkup = a safe year"** — CONTRADICTED. Every tool has false negatives; a screening conclusion holds only within "this tool's resolution × that day × that package"; the age-appropriate dedicated items (colonoscopy / breast / cervix) is not covered by being "included" in a package. *Falsifier: only if some package were proven to have near-100% sensitivity across the full cancer spectrum for that population would this item loosen — no such tool exists.*
4. **"Whole-body PET-CT is the safest"** — CONTRADICTED. No guideline support in asymptomatic people; the triple cost of high radiation + the incidentaloma cascade + opportunity cost (Face 1 · interlinked with the care-access canon). "Safety" points the opposite way: the incidental findings manufactured by over-testing are one of the biggest pipelines pushing healthy people into the patient identity. *Falsifier: a new RCT showing PET-CT screening in asymptomatic people lowers all-cause mortality — currently nonexistent.*
5. **"Sinus arrhythmia = heart disease"** — CONTRADICTED. "Sinus" = the pacemaker site is correct; breath-linked speed variation in the young is normal physiology. *Falsifier: with palpitations / blackout / syncope symptoms, or combined with other ECG abnormalities, the same term enters another evaluation path — the noun unchanged, the context changed.*
6. **"A normal report = money wasted"** — CONTRADICTED. (1) Baseline value: a normal report is the origin of all future comparisons (trend medicine needs its first frame); (2) calibration value: a negative result keeps updating the posterior of "you belong to the low-risk group," the basis for lengthening screening intervals; (3) the opportunity-cost frame: the money for a checkup, set against one panic-driven self-paid test it displaces, is often a negative cost. *Falsifier: if a checkup has no baseline use for this person, changes no screening interval, and carries zero-risk information — then for this person, this time, it really is close to money wasted; what this falsification targets is "never wasted," while conceding "a wrongly chosen package can be wasted."*
