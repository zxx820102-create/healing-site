# The Supplement Truth Textbook v1 (2026-09-12)

> **Readers and how to use this book**: self-learners and health workers. Each chapter has learning objectives and a summary; the in-text `[L3:编号]` tokens lead back to the evidence-layer originals (claims + sources + falsifiers). This book never passes observational evidence off as causation, mechanism evidence off as hard outcomes, or regulatory ceilings off as safety ceilings — epistemic states are aligned word-for-word with the L3 canon, zero promotion. **There is exactly one main line: food first; supplements are a targeted tool for specific people with specific deficiencies.**

## Chapter 0. One-page version (L1 · readable with zero background)

> Abridged companion text: this chapter is a condensed transcription of `docs/healing/textbook/supplement-l1-80yi-20260912.md`, not word-identical; the companion is the fuller, supplemented version and can be distributed independently.

**The master line: food is the lead, supplements are the patch. Too few vitamins makes you sick, and too many makes you sick too — spend the money on eating well, and leave the supplements for the people who are truly deficient.**

Hold the four tiers: confirmed by research → fairly well established → still being studied → not known.

Seven action cards: ① food first, supplements after *(translator calibration: the source's compact motto 先饭后果, rendered per the vitamins canon's food-first principle)* (confirmed by research) ② the list of what almost everyone should take is short: 400 µg of folic acid daily for those who may become pregnant, B12 for strict vegetarians, vitamin D 400 IU from birth for infants (confirmed by research · guideline-level) ③ vitamin D depends on the population: for pregnant women, older adults, people with dark skin, and the housebound, supplementing is the reasonable tier (fairly well established); healthy adults need no routine testing (confirmed by research · the 2024 position) ④ don't use supplements to "prevent disease": high-dose vitamin E raises prostate cancer and hemorrhagic stroke, beta-carotene pills raise lung cancer in smokers, high-dose niacin for blood lipids left heart disease undiminished (confirmed by research) ⑤ don't take B6 long-term in large amounts: too little injures nerves and too much injures nerves too (confirmed by research) ⑥ vitamin A is a hard red line in pregnancy: excess in the first trimester is teratogenic (confirmed by research) ⑦ report your supplements before a blood draw: biotin scrambles heart-attack and thyroid labs, and B12/B6 inflate readings for months (confirmed by research).

Six myths, all answered no: supplements are harmless, the more the better; natural is better; multivitamins = insurance; vitamin C prevents colds; megadosing vitamin D; biotin against hair loss. Three not-knowns: hard outcomes for K2; whether sunshine can be replaced by a capsule; the best ratio for high-latitude winters. Pregnancy, chronic disease, long-term medication: before supplementing anything, ask your doctor.

## Chapter 1. Concepts and terms

**Learning objectives**: be able to define the clock law, the bidirectional law, and RDA/AI/UL, and understand why water-soluble/fat-soluble determines the toxicity profile.

### 1.1 Where supplements sit in nutrition engineering

The L3 food-first principle: the routine needs of all 13 vitamins can be met by food alone; a supplement is "a patch for deficiency risk", not "a lever for health gain" — this is the whole volume's verdict [L3:分级表]. How to read: at any supplement, first ask — who is truly deficient (Chapter 2), has it been proven useful (Chapter 3), and what happens when you overshoot (Chapter 4).

### 1.2 The clock law: who runs out fast, who runs out slow

Each vitamin's body store is different, and depletion speeds differ by orders of magnitude — this decides "how long after supply stops does trouble start" [L3:时钟律].

| Clock | Vitamins | Depletion time course | Typical scenario |
|---|---|---|---|
| Years | B12 | Years (liver stores on the 3–5-year scale · textbook value) | Vegetarians show signs only years after stopping animal sources |
| Months-to-quarters | A · D · E | Months to a year | Appears only when long-term low intake and malabsorption stack |
| Weeks-to-months | C · B9 · K1 | Weeks to months (C: nonspecific symptoms at 4–12 weeks) | Picky eaters show signs within weeks to months |
| Days-to-weeks | B1 and B2/B3/B5/B6/B7 | Days to weeks | For alcohol use, vomiting, and post-surgery, the count is in "days" |

Practical corollary: B1 has the thinnest reserve — with heavy alcohol use or severe postoperative vomiting, the danger zone arrives within days to weeks (the Wernicke risk); B12 is a "slow account" that can stay unsettled for years without showing — the urgency is entirely different [L3:B1/时钟律].

### 1.3 The bidirectional law: deficiency and excess, the same disease

"More is healthier" has never held universally in the vitamin domain: B6 deficiency **and** excess **both** cause peripheral neuropathy; A deficiency **and** excess **both** harm bone (excess adds a separate teratogenic window); D deficiency **and** excess **both** affect bone; K is the counterexample — too little causes bleeding, too much is nearly nontoxic [L3:双向律].

### 1.4 Dose vocabulary: what RDA, AI, and UL actually mean

RDA = the recommended intake that meets the needs of nearly all people; AI = the adequate intake observed when the evidence is insufficient to set an RDA (vitamin K uses an AI · the evidence baseline is weak); UL = the tolerable upper intake level. **Key warning (the B6 special case)**: the US UL is 100 mg, yet regulators have reported peripheral neuropathy cases at long-term intakes of ≥50 mg/day — **the UL is a regulatory ceiling, not "below it is safe"** [L3:B6].

### 1.5 Evidence grading and epistemic states

| L3 epistemic state | Plain-language tier | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| FACT | Confirmed by research | Confirmed by textbooks / authoritative guidelines / controlled trials, with sources verified verbatim |
| MODEL | Fairly well established | Holds at the level of mechanistic inference / single trials / surrogate markers |
| INFERENCE | Still being studied | Observational association; causation unproven |
| CONTESTED | Still contested in the field | High-quality evidence in mutual conflict |
| CANDIDATE/UNKNOWN | Still being studied / not known | A candidate short of evidence / honestly not known |

### Glossary (in order of appearance)

1. **Vitamin**: an organic substance the body requires, cannot synthesize (or cannot synthesize in sufficient amounts), and must take in from food in tiny amounts — 13 in total.
2. **Fat-soluble vitamins**: A/D/E/K — they dissolve in fat and accumulate in the liver and fat tissue; slow to be missed, slow to turn toxic.
3. **Water-soluble vitamins**: the B family + C — they dissolve in water, are hardly stored; fast to be missed, and mostly nearly nontoxic.
4. **The clock law**: each vitamin's store depletes at a different speed (B12 years → B1 days-to-weeks) [L3:时钟律].
5. **The bidirectional law**: the same vitamin causes disease when lacking and when in excess (B6/A/D) [L3:双向律].
6. **RDA**: recommended dietary allowance — meets the needs of nearly all healthy people.
7. **AI**: adequate intake — the observed value given when the evidence is insufficient to set an RDA.
8. **UL**: tolerable upper intake level; the B6 special case shows it is a regulatory ceiling ≠ a safety ceiling [L3:B6].
9. **RAE**: retinol activity equivalents — the unit for vitamin A; plant beta-carotene converts at 12:1.
10. **DFE**: dietary folate equivalents — synthetic folic acid's fasted absorption is about 1.7 times that of food folate.
11. **IU**: international units — the dosing unit for vitamin D/E supplements.
12. **The steady-state plateau**: serum markers are "held up" by homeostatic regulation, so readings don't change until stores run out — a normal serum value ≠ adequate stores [L3:检测1].
13. **Functional markers**: markers that check "is the enzyme turning" (ETK-AC, EGRAC, PLP, MMA, and so on) — closer to the truth than concentration markers [L3:检测2].
14. **Supplement-history confounding**: if no one asks what supplements you take before testing, the lab panel can be distorted wholesale [L3:检测3].
15. **Negative results**: conclusions from high-quality trials showing no benefit or even harm — Chapter 3 of this volume.
16. **Megaloblastic anemia**: in B12 or folate deficiency, the marrow builds abnormal red cells that are oversized and immature.
17. **Peripheral neuropathy**: nerve damage with numbness in the hands and feet, glove-and-stocking sensory abnormalities, and unsteady gait.
18. **Subacute combined degeneration**: B12-deficiency damage involving the spinal cord, which can leave irreversible injury.
19. **Neural tube defects (NTD)**: failure of the embryonic neural tube to close (anencephaly/spina bifida) — the most certain consequence of folate deficiency in pregnancy.
20. **Warfarin**: a vitamin-K-antagonist anticoagulant whose effect fluctuates with vitamin K intake (INR monitoring).

**Summary**: food first is the verdict; the clock law sets the urgency, the bidirectional law breaks the "harmless" default, and the UL carries regulatory meaning but is not a safety promise.

## Chapter 2. The thirteen at a glance: who lacks what, who truly should supplement

**Learning objectives**: for each vitamin, be able to state the typical deficiency picture, the populations that truly should supplement, and the excess risks.

### 2.1 The full-spectrum quick table

| Vitamin | Clock | Most typical when deficient | Who truly should supplement | The excess face |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Months | Night blindness → dry eye → blindness; repeated infections in children | Most people don't need it; retinol forms forbidden in pregnancy | First-trimester teratogenicity (hard red line); accumulation injures liver and bone [L3:A] |
| D | Months-to-quarters | Infantile rickets; adult osteomalacia | Infants 400 IU (mandatory); high-risk groups short of sunshine | UL 4000 IU; long-term megadoses raise calcium [L3:D] |
| E | Months | Healthy people almost never lack it; nerve damage in malabsorption | No "preventive" supplementation recommended | Hemorrhagic stroke +22%; prostate cancer +17% [L3:E] |
| K | Weeks | Hemorrhagic disease of the newborn; rare in adults | Newborn IM injection (done at the hospital); warfarin users keep a constant diet | Nearly nontoxic; the risk lies in interaction with warfarin [L3:K] |
| B1 | Days-to-weeks | Beriberi; Wernicke (eye-muscle paralysis + confusion) | Alcohol users, post-bariatric surgery, hyperemesis gravidarum (mandatory · as the doctor directs) | No poisoning reports by mouth; too-rapid IV injection carries risk [L3:B1] |
| B2 | Days-to-weeks | Angular cheilitis, glossitis, photophobia | Generally not needed | Nearly nontoxic; bright-yellow urine is not an efficacy marker [L3:B2] |
| B3 | Days-to-weeks | Pellagra's 3D (dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia) | Not needed (tryptophan converts) | Flushing threshold 35 mg; sustained-release high-dose forms are hepatotoxic [L3:B3] |
| B5 | — | Extremely rare (only producible as experimental deficiency) | No established supplementation indication | A recorded nontoxic domain [L3:B5] |
| B6 | Days-to-weeks | Peripheral neuropathy, sideroblastic anemia | Isoniazid users (mandatory); multivitamin-level amounts are safe | Neuropathy reported at long-term ≥50 mg [L3:B6] |
| B7 | — | Rare; hair loss and dermatitis in long-term raw-egg-white eaters | Not recommended for "hair" uses outside confirmed deficiency | Directly nontoxic; the indirect poison = scrambling lab tests [L3:B7] |
| B9 | Weeks-to-months | Megaloblastic anemia; neural tube defects in the embryo | **Everyone who may become pregnant: 400 µg daily (mandatory tier)** | Synthetic folic acid >1000 µg masks B12 deficiency [L3:B9] |
| B12 | Years | Megaloblastic anemia (the blood count can look normal); subacute combined degeneration | Strict vegetarians (mandatory); people over 50, on metformin, on PPIs, post-surgery | Oral forms basically nontoxic; the risk lies in missed diagnosis [L3:B12] |
| C | Weeks-to-months | 4–12 weeks of fatigue → scurvy (bleeding gums, wounds that won't heal) | Smokers' need +35 mg; people at zero fruit-and-vegetable intake | Diarrhea above 1000 mg per dose; the kidney-stone dispute has no settled verdict [L3:C] |

### 2.2 The three lines most worth remembering on their own

**Vitamin A's two tracks**: preformed animal-source vitamin A is directly usable; plant-source beta-carotene converts on demand (hence no UL · a built-in safety valve); but **beta-carotene in supplement form has an independent negative result in smokers (lung cancer up)** — "plant source means safe" does not hold for supplements taken by smokers [L3:A].

**Vitamin D's identity**: essentially a hormone precursor rather than an ordinary vitamin — synthesized in skin by sunshine, activated in two steps through liver and kidney; whether you "lack it" depends on sunshine and population, not one dose for everyone [L3:D].

**B12's missed-diagnosis face**: the neuropsychiatric form of pernicious anemia can have a completely normal blood count (nerve damage precedes the anemia) — at borderline values, stack MMA + holoTC to characterize [L3:B12].

**Summary**: there are only six mandatory-tier supplementation scenarios (preconception folate, vegetarian B12, infant vitamin D, post-surgery B1+B12, alcohol users' B1, newborn vitamin K — done at the hospital); everything else is either met by food or belongs in the negative-results territory.

## Chapter 3. The negative-results list: putting money and expectations in the right place

**Learning objectives**: be able to name at least 6 supplement interventions that were "studied, with negative or even harmful results".

These are not "not yet studied" — these are **studies completed, with negative or harmful conclusions** — and behind each stands a trial of several thousand to tens of thousands of people [L3:检测5].

| Intervention | Research result | Tier and source |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin E 400 IU/day "to prevent cancer" | Prostate cancer risk +17% — "antioxidant = cancer prevention" falsified | Confirmed by research (SELECT) [L3:E] |
| Vitamin E and stroke | Hemorrhagic stroke +22% (ischemic -10%; net effect uncertain) | Confirmed by research (BMJ 2010 meta-analysis) [L3:E] |
| Niacin at the 2 g level "to fix lipids and protect the heart" | HDL rose; cardiovascular events didn't fall; adverse events rose instead | Confirmed by research (AIM-HIGH/HPS2-THRIVE) [L3:B3] |
| Beta-carotene supplements (smokers) | Lung cancer risk rose instead | Confirmed by research (ATBC/CARET) [L3:A] |
| Vitamin C to prevent/treat colds | No prevention; regular takers shorten the course by about 8%; supplementing after infection doesn't shorten it | Confirmed by research (the Cochrane body) [L3:C] |
| Universal multivitamin supplementation | Insufficient evidence of benefit (the authoritative preventive task force's assessment) | Still being studied → cannot be sold as insurance (USPSTF 2022) [L3:分级表] |
| Universal vitamin D 2000 IU | In middle-aged and older adults with an adequate baseline: fractures, cardiovascular events, and cancer deaths all unmoved | Confirmed by research (VITAL) [L3:D2] |
| Biotin for "hair and nails" | Insufficient evidence outside confirmed deficiency | Insufficient evidence (honestly stated) [L3:B7] |
| Pantothenic acid "against stress" | The marketing claim is an analogy, with no controlled evidence | No supporting evidence [L3:B5/未知清单] |
| High-dose vitamin E, long-term ≥400 IU | Associated with raised all-cause mortality | Confirmed by research (the meta-analysis body) [L3:E] |

Three points for using this list: ① **negative results do not argue against "supplementing those who lack"** — what VITAL falsified is "benefit from supplementing further when already replete"; the benefit of vitamin D for people with rickets-grade deficiency has never been overturned [L3:D2]; ② **the two inference chains "antioxidant = cancer prevention" and "fixing lipids = protecting the heart" have been falsified end to end** — when a new advertisement of the same kind appears, apply this table directly; ③ **negative results update with new evidence** — the L3 registers a falsifiable clause for each line; verdicts change through new trials, not through sales patter [L3:证伪表].

**Summary**: supplement money belongs only in the intersection of "truly lacking + proven beneficial".

## Chapter 4. Safety boundaries: excess and interactions

**Learning objectives**: be able to state the fat-soluble accumulation logic, the vitamin A pregnancy red line, the B6 bidirectional-law details, where the iron-excess spectrum belongs, and the "ask your doctor first" boundary.

### 4.1 The four fat-soluble vitamins: accumulation is the root of toxicity

A/D/E/K are stored in the liver and fat tissue and cleared slowly; toxicity accumulates on a "months-to-years" clock [L3:时钟律].

- **Vitamin A**: acute megadoses raise intracranial pressure; chronic accumulation injures the liver, causes bone pain and hair loss; **exceeding 3000 µg RAE (=10000 IU) in the first trimester is the hardest teratogenic red line of any nutrient** — retinol supplements are forbidden in pregnancy, and animal liver stays under 100 g per week; high-dose vitamin E antagonizes vitamin A [L3:A].
- **Vitamin D**: UL 4000 IU; chronic megadoses cause hypercalcemia (kidney stones, nephrocalcinosis); the real accident history is **one-time accidental overdose from confusing IU and mL in infant drops**; combined with thiazide diuretics, the high-calcium risk rises [L3:D].
- **Vitamin E**: UL 1000 mg; bleeding tendency, interference with vitamin-K-dependent clotting, plus the two Chapter 3 negative results [L3:E].
- **Vitamin K**: intrinsically of very low toxicity, with no established UL; its real face is **interaction** — for people on warfarin, vitamin K intake must be **constant**, not restricted; swinging up and down makes the INR drift, with two-way risk of clots and bleeding [L3:K].

### 4.2 The B6 bidirectional law up close

Deficiency causes peripheral neuropathy, excess **also** causes it, and in some people recovery after stopping is incomplete; regulators have issued a safety warning on long-term ≥50 mg/day; blood PLP takes 1–3 months after stopping high doses to fall back. The medication red line: **for people with Parkinson's on non-combined levodopa preparations, B6 breaks levodopa down ahead of time**; the mandatory face in reverse: isoniazid users **must** co-supplement B6 [L3:B6].

### 4.3 The iron-excess spectrum: this volume's boundary statement

This vitamin volume covers only iron's three contact points: vitamin C raises plant-source iron absorption 2–3-fold; in hemochromatosis (the iron-overload disease), high-dose vitamin C promoting iron absorption is harmful (mechanism FACT); B6 deficiency causes sideroblastic anemia [L3:C/B6]. **The iron-excess spectrum itself is not separately itemized in the vitamin canon**; it belongs to the elements layer: UL 42–45 mg, **swallowed iron supplements are the leading cause of poisoning in children**, hereditary hemochromatosis (the HFE gene) — quoted from the elements canon's iron section with epistemic states as-is (FACT); this volume does not expand on it [元:Fe]. Principle: iron is not the myth of "no harm in topping up" — iron supplementation must first have laboratory grounds.

### 4.4 Interaction quick list

Vitamin K × warfarin (constant intake) · vitamin E × vitamin K (high doses worsen bleeding) · vitamin E × vitamin A (high-dose antagonism) · B6 × levodopa · vitamin D × thiazides (high calcium) · biotin × immunoassays (Chapter 5) [L3:A/D/E/K/B6/B7].

### 4.5 The medical boundary (this volume's heaviest sentence)

**Pregnancy, chronic disease, long-term medication — before supplementing anything, ask your doctor.** Triggers: pregnancy and breastfeeding (folate/vitamin A/D doses all require a doctor's order), Parkinson's medication (B6 antagonism), anticoagulants (K/E), anti-tuberculosis drugs (B6 mandatory), metformin/PPIs (B12 monitoring), bariatric surgery and heavy alcohol use (B1/B12 mandatory · doses as the doctor directs) [L3:分级表].

**Summary**: fat-soluble toxicity lies in accumulation, water-soluble toxicity in special cases; the biggest "toxicity" is often interactions and missed diagnoses, not the supplement itself.

## Chapter 5. Testing truth: which lab panel will deceive you

**Learning objectives**: be able to state the four testing general rules and the modern position on vitamin D screening.

1. **Serum "normal" ≠ tissue sufficiency**: serum vitamin A is held up by homeostasis until the liver stores are nearly exhausted (the steady-state plateau); the neurological form of B12 deficiency can show a normal blood count; serum folate reflects only recent intake — look instead at red-cell folate [L3:检测1].
2. **Functional markers > concentration markers**: checking "is the enzyme turning" is closer to the truth than checking "is the cargo there" — B1 uses ETK-AC, B2 uses EGRAC, B6 uses PLP, B12 uses MMA (high specificity; falsely high when kidney function is poor) + holoTC (more sensitive) [L3:检测2].
3. **Supplement history is testing's first confounder**: high-dose biotin makes troponin (the blood marker of heart-attack injury) falsely low and the thyroid function markers (TSH/FT4) falsely high — missed heart attacks, misdiagnosed thyroid function; the FDA has issued a safety communication with associated death reports; after high-dose B12, readings stay inflated long-term; B6 takes months after stopping to fall back — **a lab panel drawn without asking supplement history is half a lab panel** [L3:检测3].
4. **Hair testing is unreliable**: affected by dyeing, contamination, and keratin binding capacity, it correlates poorly with serum/functional markers — hair testing fails for trace elements and for vitamins alike [L3:检测4].
5. **The vitamin D screening position (the 2024 reversal)**: the authoritative endocrine society recommends **no routine 25(OH)D screening in healthy adults and most children** (the result doesn't change decisions); screening is recommended for osteoporosis/fragility fracture, malabsorption/post-surgery, chronic kidney disease, long-term vitamin D takers, older adults who fall, and pregnancy. "How low counts as deficient" has no settled verdict (the 20 vs 30 ng/mL camps · still contested in the field); **"you must be above 50"-style high targets are marketing noise** [L3:D3].

| What you want to check | What to check | The pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Erythrocyte transketolase activity coefficient | Clinically, empiric treatment often goes first (the treatment window is short) [L3:B1] |
| B12 | Serum B12 first pass + MMA/holoTC to characterize | True deficiency hides inside borderline "normal" [L3:B12] |
| Folate | Red-cell folate | Serum folate overstates status [L3:B9] |
| Vitamin D | 25(OH)D (restricted to the screening indications) | Routine screening in healthy people is not recommended; the target range is contested [L3:D3] |
| Vitamin K | Prothrombin time PT/INR (functional) | Serum vitamin K is not checked clinically [L3:K] |
| Anything | Report supplement history first | Biotin/B12/B6, the three big confounders [L3:检测3] |

**Summary**: first ask "does the result change any decision", then ask "which marker"; hair testing and "bundled full-spectrum panels" are not the entrance.

## Chapter 6. The "do I really need a supplement?" decision tree

**Learning objectives**: be able to place yourself by "food first → then testing/population → then supplements".

```
Starting point: I want to take a supplement
├─ First question: this nutrient — am I eating enough of it? — food first [L3:分级表]
│   ├─ Eating enough and no high risk → usually not needed; read the Chapter 3 negative-results list first
│   └─ Not eating enough → fix the diet structure first (the L3 gives TOP3 foods for every vitamin)
├─ Second question: am I on the "mandatory / strongly recommended" list? [L3:分级表]
│   ├─ Preconception / first trimester → folic acid 400–600 µg daily (mandatory · FACT)
│   ├─ Strict vegetarian → B12 (mandatory · zero plant sources)
│   ├─ Infants → vitamin D3 400 IU from birth (mandatory · guideline); newborn vitamin K IM injection is done at the hospital
│   ├─ Post-bariatric surgery / long-term alcohol → B1+B12 / B1 (mandatory · treatment doses as the doctor directs)
│   ├─ Over 50 / metformin / long-term PPI → B12 in crystalline form (strongly recommended)
│   ├─ Isoniazid users → B6 (strongly recommended · drug interaction)
│   ├─ Fat-malabsorption → A/D/E/K (strongly recommended · as the doctor directs)
│   └─ None of these → go to the third question
├─ Third question: any high-risk exposure or symptoms? [L3:D/C]
│   ├─ Smoking (vitamin C need +35 mg) · dark skin / housebound / wintering above 40°N (vitamin D high risk)
│   │  → see a doctor, check the right markers per Chapter 5 → supplement only once deficiency is confirmed, re-evaluate per course
│   └─ None → don't supplement; spend the money on food quality
└─ Universal to every branch:
   ①pregnancy / chronic disease / long-term medication → ask your doctor first [L3:分级表]
   ②report your supplement history before a blood draw (stopping biotin 2–3 days ahead is the common recommendation · fairly well established) [L3:检测3]
```

**Summary**: the order cannot be reversed — food first, then population and testing, and only then supplements; buying in the reversed order is exactly the consumption path the market designed.

## Chapter 7. Population quick reference

| Population | Key points | Evidence tier |

| Preconception / first trimester | Folic acid 400–600 µg/day, starting 3 months before conception; retinol-form vitamin A forbidden | Confirmed by research [L3:B9/A] |

## Chapter 8. FAQ (11 questions)

1. **Taking a multivitamin as insurance surely can't hurt?** It may not hurt, but the benefit is unproven (authoritative assessment: insufficient evidence for universal supplementation); it also occupies the psychological slot of "I'm managing my health", crowding out the actions that actually work [L3:分级表].
2. **Should vitamin D be tested at all? How low counts as deficient?** Routine screening is not recommended for healthy adults (confirmed by research · the authoritative 2024 position); "how low counts as deficient" is still contested in the field; "must be above 50" is marketing noise. People at high risk from little sunshine supplement by population and re-evaluate per course [L3:D3].
3. **VITAL says vitamin D doesn't work — was my supplementing all in vain?** What VITAL falsified is "benefit from supplementing further when already replete"; the benefit of supplementing D for those at high risk of deficiency was not overturned. Look at the population, not the headline [L3:D2].
4. **Does chugging vitamin C effervescent tablets help a cold?** No (supplementing after infection doesn't shorten the course); long-term regular takers shorten the course by about 8% — a statistic about "people who take it year-round", not an antidote for "take it once you're sick" [L3:C].
5. **Do B vitamins before drinking prevent drunkenness and protect the liver?** "Preventing drunkenness / protecting the liver" is not separately itemized in the L3 (the not-known tier); what is real: alcohol inhibits B1 absorption and doubles its consumption, and for long-term drinkers B1 supplementation is a treatment-grade necessity, as the doctor directs — but it "repairs damage", it is not a "permission slip" [L3:B1].
6. **Prenatal milk powder already has folic acid — do I still need the tablets? Could I overdose?** The UL for synthetic folic acid is 1000 µg/day — add up the total before stacking; the excess face is masking the hematological signs of B12 deficiency (the markers improve while the nerve damage continues). The concrete plan follows the prenatal checkups [L3:B9].
7. **My urine is bright yellow on a multivitamin — is that good absorption?** No. That is B2's color; solubility limits absorption and the surplus exits in urine — **urine color is not an efficacy marker** [L3:B2].
8. **Are the pricey "active forms" (P-5-P, methylcobalamin) worth buying?** For people whose conversion works, the form makes little difference (fairly well established); the exception is older adults' B12 — the crystalline form is not limited by stomach acid and is the better pick (confirmed by research). The form difference is felt in the wallet more than the body [L3:B6/B12].
9. **Is naturally extracted better than synthetic?** Both directions carry negative evidence: smokers taking "natural" beta-carotene supplements saw lung cancer rise instead; synthetic folic acid is absorbed at about 1.7 times the rate of food folate. Natural ≠ better; dose and population decide [L3:A/B9].
10. **Should children take vitamin supplements?** Infants: vitamin D 400 IU, mandatory; other healthy children: food first — children's ULs for each vitamin sit lower on the gradient (e.g., vitamin A UL 600 µg at ages 0–3), and handing a child an adult dose is risky; follow the pediatrician [L3:A/D].
11. **Should I stop supplements before a blood draw?** Biotin: stopping 2–3 days ahead is the common recommendation (fairly well established); high-dose B6: PLP takes 1–3 months after stopping to fall back; high-dose B12: readings stay inflated long-term — the most practical step is handing your supplement list to the doctor who orders the tests [L3:检测3].

## Chapter 9. Self-test (10 questions · with answers)

1. In the clock law, who runs out fastest and who slowest? — Fastest **B1** (days-to-weeks); slowest **B12** (years · liver stores of several years). [L3:时钟律]
2. Which vitamin's what evidence supports "the UL is a regulatory ceiling"? — **B6**: the US UL is 100 mg, yet peripheral neuropathy has been reported at long-term ≥50 mg/day (a regulatory safety warning). [L3:B6]
3. The hard red-line number for vitamin A in pregnancy? — exceeding **3000 µg RAE (=10000 IU) in the first trimester is teratogenic**; retinol supplements forbidden in pregnancy, animal liver under 100 g per week. [L3:A]
4. The result and meaning of the SELECT trial? — 400 IU/day of vitamin E raised **prostate cancer risk +17%** — "antioxidant = cancer prevention" falsified on vitamin E. [L3:E]
5. What did VITAL falsify, and what didn't it? — It falsified "the **replete** benefit from supplementing further" (fractures/cardiovascular/cancer all unmoved); it did not falsify "the **deficient** benefit from supplementing D". [L3:D2]
6. The authoritative 2024 position on vitamin D screening? — **no routine 25(OH)D screening in healthy adults and most children**; screening is reserved for osteoporosis, malabsorption, CKD, long-term D takers, older adults who fall, pregnancy, and similar populations. [L3:D3]
7. What is biotin's "indirect toxicity"? — **Test contamination**: falsely low troponin (missed heart attacks), falsely high TSH/FT4 (misdiagnosed thyroid function) — harmless to the body, harmful to the diagnosis. [L3:B7/检测3]
8. Which six are the mandatory-tier supplementation scenarios? — preconception folate, strict-vegetarian B12, infant vitamin D, post-bariatric B1+B12, alcohol users' B1, the newborn vitamin K IM injection. [L3:分级表]
9. The biggest risk of synthetic folic acid excess? — above 1000 µg/day it **masks the hematological signs of B12 deficiency**: the anemia is repaired while the nerve damage keeps progressing. [L3:B9]
10. The negative result of niacin "fixing lipids to protect the heart"? — at the 2 g level, HDL rose but **cardiovascular events didn't fall** and adverse events rose instead (AIM-HIGH/HPS2-THRIVE) — the "fixing lipids = protecting the heart" inference chain, falsified. [L3:B3]
