## Chapter 1. Concepts and terms

**Learning objectives**: be able to state how essentiality is decided, what each dose term covers, and which plain-talk tier answers to which epistemic tier.

### 1.1 What counts as an "element essential to the human body"

The bar is hard: **dietary insufficiency produces a reproducible functional impairment, and repletion reverses it** (the Hedgepath criteria) — meet the bar and you count as essential; miss it and you stay a candidate [L3:第一节总纲]. The list is therefore narrow: oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen make up about 96% of body mass; 7 major minerals (calcium, phosphorus, potassium, sulfur, sodium, chlorine, magnesium); 9 trace elements (iron, zinc, copper, selenium, iodine, manganese, molybdenum, cobalt, chromium); and a batch of ultratrace elements with contested essentiality, all held at candidate or unknown [L3:A1-A4].

### 1.2 Major versus trace: the difference is dose, not importance

Majors are counted in grams (calcium at kilogram scale inside bone), traces in milligrams/micrograms — "trace" does not mean unimportant, only that the required amount is small; the flip side of a small requirement is a **narrow safety window** — that is the double-window law.

### 1.3 The double-window law: deficiency and excess both cause disease

In the element domain there is almost no "the more, the healthier": iron — too little brings anemia, too much brings poisoning; selenium — too little brings Keshan disease, too much sheds hair; iodine — too little brings goiter, too much scrambles thyroid function; zinc chronically in excess backfires into copper deficiency [L3:Fe/Se/I/Zn] — read every element in two columns: "deficient · excess".

### 1.4 Dose vocabulary

RDA = Recommended Dietary Allowance, the intake meeting nearly everyone's need; AI = Adequate Intake, an observational value used where evidence falls short (potassium, chlorine, manganese, chromium run on AI); UL = Tolerable Upper Intake Level — a safety ceiling, not an efficacy threshold, and certainly not "free to add more as long as you stay below it".

### 1.5 The four-tier crosswalk of epistemic states

| L3 epistemic state | Plain-talk tier | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| FACT | confirmed by research | in textbooks/authoritative guidelines/PG rows · verifiable |
| MODEL | fairly well established | mechanism inference or cross-mapping · not directly measured |
| CANDIDATE | still being studied | evidence not yet sufficient · candidate |
| ANALOGY | analogy | a conceptual analogy · not to be used as fact |
| UNKNOWN | not known | we don't know · rather leave a blank than invent |
| TCM·traditional | traditional claim | never promoted · marked agree/differ item by item |

### Glossary

1. **Essential element**: an element that passes the essentiality criteria (deficiency produces reproducible impairment, repletion reverses it) [L3:总纲].
2. **Double-window law**: the same element causes disease in both deficiency and excess [L3:各元素节].
3. **RDA**: recommended dietary allowance; **AI**: adequate intake; **UL**: tolerable upper intake level.
4. **Hydroxyapatite**: the mineral crystal of bone and teeth; its fluoride variant fluorapatite resists cavities.
5. **Heme iron**: the iron in animal meat and blood (15-35% absorbed); **non-heme iron** = plant sources; paired with vitamin C, absorption rises 2-3 times [L3:Fe·interaction 31].
6. **Ferritin**: the iron-storage indicator; <50 is the intervention line (the restless-legs association) [L3:Fe].
7. **Ceruloplasmin**: the copper-transport protein synthesized by the liver, doubling as "iron's loader"; copper deficiency → iron stuck in storage → iron-refractory anemia [L3:Cu].
8. **Metallothionein**: the metal-binding protein inside gut cells, where zinc and copper compete [L3:D2-N1].
9. **DMT1**: the divalent metal channel in the gut, shared by iron, manganese, and lead [L3:D2-N6/N9].
10. **Deiodinase**: the selenium-bearing enzyme that converts thyroid hormone T4 into active T3 — the hinge of the selenium-iodine chain [L3:Se].
11. **Keshan disease**: endemic selenium-deficiency cardiomyopathy (named along the low-selenium belt from the Northeast to the Southwest) [L3:Se].
12. **Cretinism**: intellectual impairment from iodine deficiency in pregnancy — the world's most preventable intellectual impairment [L3:I].
13. **Wolff-Chaikoff effect**: the protective mechanism by which the thyroid temporarily "shuts down" under iodine excess; its failure triggers hyper- or hypothyroidism [L3:I].
14. **Hemochromatosis**: the hereditary disease (HFE gene, C282Y) in which iron absorption runs uncontrolled and deposits in liver and spleen [L3:Fe·SNP].
15. **Wilson disease**: the hereditary disease (ATP7B mutation) in which copper excretion fails and copper deposits in liver and brain [L3:Cu·SNP].
16. **Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)**: the vitamin needed for blood formation and myelin; cobalt acts only through it [L3:Co].

**Summary**: essentiality is judged by function; three dose terms; the double-window law runs through the whole volume; this text never steps outside its epistemic tiers.

## Chapter 2. Star dust and the list: where elements come from, whose body they enter

**Learning objectives**: be able to tell the scientific side and the boundary of the star-dust hook; explain the from-and-to path in one figure; produce the periodic table × human body crosswalk.

### 2.1 The star-dust hook (strictly anchored on its scientific side)

The Big History volume's temperature ladder, tier 8, carries a FACT-grade assertion: **stellar fusion forges the heavy elements (carbon, nitrogen, oxygen … iron), and supernova explosions sow them** — supported by helioseismology / nuclear physics / measured elemental abundances [E2E3:第 8 级]. The iron in your blood, the calcium in your bones, the iodine in your thyroid were all born inside stars. The hook ends here — in-body function follows this volume's L3.

One more observation: silicon and aluminum, extremely abundant in the crust, are not taken up by the body, while indispensable iron holds only a modest share of the crust — **entry on the list follows function, not abundance**, which is exactly what the Hedgepath criteria mean [L3:总纲]. The abundance-mismatch coefficients: not listed separately in the canon (common-knowledge layer of this textbook · no numbers cited).

### 2.2 Figure 2-1. Where elements come from → where they go

```

        ↓
```

The table's overall tier: MODEL/ANALOGY — the TCM five zang are functional system models, not one-to-one anatomical organs; row-by-row labeling guards against "impersonating FACT" [L3:B1].

### 5.2 Each zang's key element chains

- **Heart**: potassium, magnesium, calcium, and sodium jointly set the heart's electrical activity (the element side of palpitations); selenium deficiency = Keshan disease; vitamin B1 (thiamine) deficiency = beriberi heart disease [L3:B2].
- **Liver**: the storage-and-transport hub — the iron bank · the ceruloplasmin field · about 90% of vitamin A stored in the liver (liver disease + zinc shortfall → night blindness = the mechanistic footnote to "the liver opens into the eyes") · selenoprotein detox [L3:B2].
- **Spleen (the transformation-transport function cluster)**: stomach acid (chlorine itself) activates iron · zinc runs digestive enzymes and taste · sodium drives glucose co-absorption · vitamin B1 runs gut motility — the nutrition chain behind "the spleen governs muscles and limbs": malabsorption → muscle wasting [agree] (mechanism FACT · mapping MODEL) [L3:B2].
- **Lung**: oxygen itself; CFTR (the chloride-channel protein · its gene disease = cystic fibrosis); intravenous magnesium for severe acute asthma attacks is guideline-level treatment; the selenium-vitamin C-vitamin E antioxidant network [L3:B2].
- **Kidney**: the kidney-bone axis (active vitamin D → the gut absorbs calcium and phosphorus → mineralization · PTH mobilizes bone calcium · phosphorus excess → chronic PTH/FGF23 activation) is the modern kidney-bone mainline; sodium-potassium-chlorine regulation = "the kidney governs water" [agree]; 50%+ of fluoride leaves in urine [L3:B2].

### 5.3 Six-fu anchors and the verdicts on "five flavors" and "eat the shape"

Six fu at a glance: stomach = the precondition for stomach-acid iron activation; gallbladder = the precondition for emulsified fat-soluble vitamin absorption; small intestine = the main absorption battlefield where iron, calcium, zinc, and manganese compete; large intestine = water and potassium reabsorption; bladder = water excretion; sanjiao (triple burner) = no anatomical counterpart (ANALOGY/UNKNOWN) [L3:B3].

**The five flavors enter the five zang** [TCM·traditional · none promoted]: salty enters the kidney [partly agree] (sodium regulation is mainly renal); pungent enters the lung [partly agree · MODEL] (capsaicin receptors sit in the airways); sour enters the liver [differ/weak]; sweet enters the spleen [ANALOGY]; bitter enters the heart [UNKNOWN] [L3:B4].

**"Eat the shape", item by item**: blood nourishes blood = one of the foods densest in heme iron [agree]; liver nourishes the liver = top-configured density of vitamin A + iron, copper, zinc [agree], but in pregnancy observe the vitamin A ceiling; **bone broth builds bone [differ] — lab measurement puts each bowl at only a few milligrams to a few tens of milligrams of calcium, far below milk, a FACT-grade counterexample**; brain nourishes the brain [ANALOGY]; kidney nourishes the kidney = selenium really is high [partly agree], but a "tonifies the kidney" function has no evidence [differ] [L3:B4].

**Summary**: mappings are never promoted, verdicts labeled row by row; "opens into the eyes" and "governs bone" have mechanistic footnotes, "bone broth for calcium" has a FACT-grade counterexample — traditional claims get checked one by one: neither swallow whole nor throw away.

## Chapter 7. The element interaction network and supplement staggering

**Learning objectives**: be able to draw the iron-calcium-zinc triangle of competition; recite the staggering table.

- **The iron × zinc × calcium triangle** (FACT): divalent cations share the gut channel — don't pair zinc tablets with a fasting-morning iron dose · keep iron and calcium ≥2h apart [L3:D1·interaction 15/32].
- **Zinc × copper** (FACT): dose-dependent antagonism — zinc supplementation >50 mg/day long-term calls for copper evaluation · monitor ceruloplasmin [L3:D2-N1].
- **Selenium × iodine** (FACT): synergy — deiodinase carries selenium; test both elements together on the thyroid front [L3:D2-N2].
- **Copper × iron** (FACT): copper deficiency causes "iron-refractory" anemia; anemia that resists treatment — check copper [L3:D2-N3].
- **Iron × lead** (FACT): iron deficiency amplifies lead absorption (shared DMT1) — children's iron status is the defense line against lead toxicity [L3:D2-N6].
- **Calcium × fluoride** (MODEL): co-administration chelates and cuts absorption — stagger the doses. **Sodium × lithium (pharmacological · FACT)**: patients on lithium salts must never sharply restrict sodium — a psychiatric red line [L3:D2].

## Chapter 8. Testing and the medical boundary

**Learning objectives**: be able to state the three boundaries: hair test/blood test, supplements/doctor, exposure/medical care.

1. **Hair tests are inadmissible**: hair mineral analysis is not a gold standard (mainstream consensus · FACT); a "hair-test deficiency report" is no basis for supplements [L3:C 节证伪器].
2. **Blood tests decide, symptoms are clues**: the deficiency signals in the body-site table are mostly MODEL grade; diagnosis rests on ferritin, plasma ceruloplasmin, plasma zinc, 25(OH)D and other labs [L3:诚实边界附].
3. **Ask the doctor before supplements**: pregnancy, kidney disease, long-term medication — supplements pass the doctor first; in kidney disease, supplementing potassium or magnesium can be fatal (the hyperkalemic-arrest red line); those on lithium salts must never sharply restrict sodium [L3:K/D2-N7].
4. **Heavy-metal exposure = a seek-care event**: no safe threshold for lead or mercury; detox patches have no evidence (the "not known" tier); suspected exposure (occupation · old paint · contaminated water) → go directly for medical testing [L3:A4].
5. **Children's iron supplements are a household safety line**: accidental ingestion = the top cause of child poisoning — keep supplements where children cannot reach them [L3:Fe].

**Summary**: the judges of "deficient or not" are blood tests + the doctor; sales pitches that bypass these boundaries are treated as myths, every one.

## Chapter 9. FAQ (10 questions)

1. **Is a daily multimineral tablet good insurance?** The insurance story is not generally proven; majors come from food, traces are supplemented for deficiency not for idle insurance — first read the double-window columns in Chapters 3/4 [L3:A2/A3].
2. **Will calcium supplements give me kidney stones?** The high-dose supplement link to kidney stones is MODEL; "the more the better" additionally has a negative RCT — settle your calcium in dairy and soy [L3:Ca].
3. **How much iron in pregnancy?** The RDA jumps to 24-29 mg — the dose follows your prenatal blood count and ferritin; never self-escalate [L3:Fe].
4. **How do vegetarians protect iron and zinc?** Pair non-heme iron with vitamin C (a 2-3 times rise); get zinc from beans, nuts, and whole grains; on a long-term vegetarian diet, check ferritin and plasma zinc regularly [L3:Fe/Zn].
5. **Iron didn't fix the anemia — now what?** Check three things: copper (the loader missing from post), vitamin A, and the absorption side (post-GI-surgery) — "iron-refractory anemia" is a chain disease, not a dose disease [L3:D2-N3].
6. **Should I take iodine if my thyroid function is off?** Iodine's double window: deficient needs repletion, excess scrambles it (Wolff-Chaikoff failure · worsens Hashimoto's) — labs first, dosing second, follow your endocrinologist [L3:I].
7. **Is my picky-eating child zinc-deficient?** Dulled taste is one zinc-deficiency signal, but at MODEL grade — test blood first, decide after, and never self-diagnose from ads [L3:Zn/C].
8. **Can hard water or mineral water count as a mineral source?** What water carries is small change; minerals come almost entirely from food (fairly well established) [L3 未单列·由食物密度面推知].
9. **Do heavy sweaters need extra salt?** Sodium deficiency is rare and sits mostly in loss scenarios like heavy sweating; the everyday sodium problem is excess, not shortage [L3:Na].
10. **Is calcium alone enough for an older adult's osteoporosis?** No: calcium supplementation in non-deficient people has a negative fracture-rate RCT; the kidney-bone axis still has the active-vitamin-D link — check vitamin D and manage comprehensively under medical guidance [L3:Ca/B2].
