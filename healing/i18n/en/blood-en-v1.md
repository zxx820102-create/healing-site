# Blood and Anemia Canon v1 (2026-09-12)

> **In one sentence**: anemia is a **symptom**, not a disease name — the first step when a lab report arrives is classification, not immediately "nourishing the blood"; about one quarter of the world's population is anemic (FACT), while the biggest "blood-nourishing" market in Chinese-speaking communities (ejiao/dates/brown sugar) contains almost nothing that can build blood (FACT, stratified in Chapter 3); in South China one in six to ten people carries a thalassemia gene, and for the subgroup "mistaken for iron deficiency" iron is not a health measure but harm (FACT).

---

## Chapter 1 The master ledger: what anemia is and how to read the lab report

### 1.1 Anemia = a symptom, not a disease name

The definition of anemia is mechanical: **the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity is insufficient**, defined clinically as a hemoglobin (Hb) concentration below the fifth percentile of the same population. The WHO cutoffs updated in 2024 (FACT):

| Population | Anemia cutoff |
|---|---|
| Adult men | Hb < 130 g/L |
| Non-pregnant adult women | Hb < 120 g/L |
| Pregnant (traditional framing, whole pregnancy) | Hb < 110 g/L |
| Second trimester (2024 edition, fifth percentile) | Hb < 105 g/L |

Behind "anemia" lies an entire disease spectrum: **iron deficiency (most common) → anemia of chronic disease/inflammation → renal anemia (insufficient erythropoietin) → inherited hemoglobinopathies such as thalassemia → B12/folate deficiency → problems of the bone marrow itself (aplastic anemia, leukemia, myelodysplasia) → blood loss (gastrointestinal/menstrual/urinary tract)**. They all share the same exit — "low Hb" — but their treatment directions differ completely, sometimes oppositely. That is exactly why **the first step with an anemia lab report is classification, not buying a blood-nourishing product** (FACT, this canon's founding claim).

### 1.2 The three-step reading of a lab report

**Step 1: compare Hb against the cutoffs; confirm anemia and its degree.** Mild (Hb 110-119 g/L non-pregnant women) / moderate (80-109) / severe (<80) is the WHO's common grading (FACT). Note that 130 for men is a borderline value — a male Hb hovering around 130 is also worth investigating, because men lack the "natural explanation" of menstrual blood loss, and any anemia in a man defaults to hunting for a bleeding source (INFERENCE, from general clinical practice).

**Step 2: look at MCV (mean corpuscular volume); classify by "size" into three groups.** This is the first blade of the anemia differential (FACT):

- **Microcytic (MCV<80 fL)**: mainly iron-deficiency anemia, thalassemia, anemia of chronic disease. **Microcytic ≠ necessarily iron-deficient** — a thalassemia carrier's MCV can drop to 60-70 fL with iron stores completely replete; this is the core of Chapter 4.
- **Normocytic (MCV 80-100 fL)**: early iron deficiency, anemia of chronic disease, renal anemia, bone marrow problems, acute blood loss.
- **Macrocytic (MCV>100 fL)**: B12 deficiency, folate deficiency (megaloblastic anemia), hypothyroidism, alcohol/liver disease. The macrocytic direction points to supplementing B12/folate — **iron does nothing** (FACT).

**Step 3: look at reticulocytes — the bone marrow's "production report".** Reticulocytes are immature red cells just released from the marrow; their proportion tells you whether it is "production too low" (low reticulocytes → missing raw material / marrow problem / renal) or "loss too high but the marrow is compensating flat out" (high reticulocytes → hemolysis, recovery after hemorrhage) (FACT, hematology-textbook mechanism). Anemia + markedly raised reticulocytes = work up hemolysis and bleeding first; anemia + low reticulocytes = work up raw materials and marrow first. This one line item splits the reasoning in half — the most underrated cell on the lab report.

### 1.3 The correct handling pathway for "anemia" (master map)

```
Low Hb found
  → Classify: MCV size + reticulocytes + (when needed) ferritin/serum iron/B12/folate/hemoglobin electrophoresis
  → Find the cause: iron deficient? (menstruation/GI tract/diet) chronic disease? kidney? family history of thalassemia? macrocytic → B12/folate
  → Treat the cause: iron deficiency → iron (Chapter 2); thalassemia → iron forbidden (Chapter 4); B12 → B12 (Chapter 5)
  → Danger signals go through the red-flag channel (Chapter 6)
```

Skipping classification and going straight to "supplementing" at any step can supplement in the wrong direction — at best useless (treating thalassemia as iron deficiency and supplementing into iron overload), at worst a delayed diagnosis (managing GI-bleed anemia as "qi and blood deficiency" for half a year).

## Chapter 2 The iron-deficiency anemia face: the real science of "building blood"

### 2.1 The truth about iron absorption

**Heme iron vs non-heme iron** (FACT, nutrition-textbook framing): heme iron from meat, fish and poultry is absorbed at a high rate (about 15-35%); non-heme iron from plants and iron supplements is absorbed at a low rate (about 2-8%) and strongly modulated by the diet.

- **Vitamin C promotes absorption** (FACT): vitamin C in the same meal (citrus, bell peppers, etc.) can raise non-heme iron absorption several-fold — the mechanism is reducing Fe³⁺→Fe²⁺ and forming soluble chelates. Taking oral iron with orange juice is a pairing with a real physiological basis (MODEL, mechanism clear).
- **Tea and coffee inhibit** (FACT, magnitude has measured support): tea polyphenols/tannins significantly inhibit non-heme iron absorption; in the classic-study framing, strong tea with a meal can suppress more than half (magnitude MODEL — exact percentages vary widely by tea type and strength; the actionable conclusion is "clearly inhibitory, avoid with meals"). Spacing iron 1-2 hours from tea and coffee is the prudent practice.
- **"Spinach builds blood" is a half-truth** (FACT, stratified): spinach's iron content is not low among vegetables, but (a) oxalate binds the iron and suppresses absorption, so the real absorption rate is very low; (b) by absolute iron per 100 g times absorption rate, the iron obtained from eating spinach is nowhere near enough to correct established iron deficiency. Spinach is a good vegetable; it is not a treatment for anemia. Likewise, **expecting any single "high-iron vegetable" food remedy to correct iron-deficiency anemia is unrealistic** — established iron-deficiency anemia needs medicinal iron (see 2.3); food's role is prevention and maintenance (INFERENCE, from treatment-guideline framing).

### 2.2 Menstrual blood loss: the number-one cause of iron deficiency in reproductive-age women

The leading cause of iron deficiency in reproductive-age women is **menstrual blood loss** — not a "weak constitution", but an iron ledger that runs every cycle (FACT). Operative criteria for heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) (verified: NICE NG88 2018 / ACOG):

1. **Objective line**: regularly more than 80 mL of menstrual blood per cycle (the criterion carried by NICE and multiple guidelines) — but nobody measures with a graduated cup in real life, so the next two lines are more practical;
2. **Quality-of-life line** (NICE NG88's current primary definition): blood loss heavy enough to **interfere with physical, emotional, social and daily quality of life** counts as HMB — e.g., soaking through a pad/tampon hourly for several hours, needing to get up at night to change, side-leaking despite a pad plus tampon;
3. **Duration line** (ACOG): periods lasting more than 7 days;
4. **Anemia corroboration line**: iron deficiency in a reproductive-age woman (especially low ferritin) is itself grounds for asking back about menstrual volume — about one third of reproductive-age women meet HMB criteria (FACT), and HMB can empty iron stores first and hemoglobin second, so **low ferritin with Hb still normal is already "iron deficiency", not "nothing wrong"** (FACT).

**Falsification of "a heavy flow expels stasis/toxins"**: menstrual discharge is endometrial tissue plus blood; the body has no "stasis-expelling" mechanism that needs heavier bleeding to do its work. An excessive flow only builds a negative ledger of "blood loss → iron deficiency → fatigue → feeling worse". Treating HMB as "expelling stasis" and delaying care is the cultural root of reproductive-age women's anemia being long dismissed (falsification face in Chapter 7).

### 2.3 The correct posture of iron therapy

**(1) Alternate-day dosing — new evidence supported by isotope studies** (FACT, verified: Stoffel et al., Lancet Haematology 2017, 600+ citations; von Siebenthal et al., Lancet Haematology 2023):

- Oral iron induces a **hepcidin rise lasting about 24 hours**; dosing again inside that window clearly reduces absorption efficiency (FACT). Mechanism: hepcidin is the "master gate" of iron absorption; the gut responds to the iron dose by closing the gate to protect itself (MODEL).
- Therefore **once daily with spacing, or once every other day**, yields a higher total absorbed amount than multiple daily doses; in isotope-labeling measurements, 100 mg every other day delivered on the order of twice the absorbed amount of 100 mg daily in divided doses (FACT; NHS and other guidelines have adopted this evidence).
- Practice: pick one medicinal iron product, **once daily or every other day, on an empty stomach (1 hour before or 2 hours after a meal), with vitamin C, away from tea, coffee, calcium and milk**; if side effects appear (nausea/black stools/constipation), reduce the dose + go alternate-day + take with a small amount of food — do not abandon treatment.

**(2) The full course — "stop once the numbers are normal" is the classic mistake** (FACT): Hb usually returns to normal in 4-8 weeks, but that only refills the "transport layer"; **refilling the iron stores (ferritin) requires about 2-3 more months of treatment**, until ferritin reaches target. Stop early, and the next period/pregnancy/blood donation knocks you back to where you started.

**(3) Recheck-driven**: after 2-4 weeks of treatment the Hb should rise (reticulocytes rise first); **an Hb that does not rise at all = go back and re-investigate** (absorption problem? ongoing blood loss? not iron deficiency at all?) — this is also the evidence logic behind "when iron doesn't work, don't double the dose; find the cause first" (FACT, general guideline practice).

## Chapter 3 A special chapter on "blood-nourishing" scams — honest stratification, no disparagement of food traditions

> Position statement: this chapter mocks no food tradition. Ejiao, jujube dates and brown sugar hold places in culture and cuisine that deserve respect; this chapter does one thing only — **separate "cultural foods" from "medical means of correcting anemia"**, because confusing them costs real money and delays real anemia.

### 3.1 Ejiao: donkey-hide gelatin collagen — not iron, not hemoglobin

- **Composition facts** (FACT, verified: US Congressional Research Service (CRS) reports / multi-source consistent): ejiao is a gelatin made by boiling donkey hide. Collagen is an **incomplete protein** — lacking tryptophan and other essential amino acids, ranking low on the protein-quality scale; **its iron content is negligible**.
- **Evidence face** (FACT, stratified): high-quality clinical evidence for ejiao "building blood" (large randomized controlled trials with Hb/ferritin endpoints) is lacking; existing studies are mostly in-vitro/animal-level explorations of "ejiao-peptide–iron complexing" (the 2025 PMC/MDPI entries are of this kind) — **iron complexing in a test tube ≠ correcting a person's anemia after eating it**; a full stretch of clinical validation sits in the middle of that evidence chain (INFERENCE, no unilateral promotion of epistemic state).
- **Fair positioning** ([TCM·Traditional] stratification): in traditional Chinese medicine ejiao is a blood-tonifying medicinal with a thousand-year history of use; TCM use is prescribing inside a pattern-differentiation system. But **at the levels of nutrition science and evidence-based medicine it cannot replace medicinal iron in correcting iron-deficiency anemia**; using ejiao as "anemia treatment" and delaying the classification workup is where its real risk lies.

### 3.2 Jujube dates and brown sugar: big sugar, small iron

- **Jujube dates**: dried jujube carries iron on the order of ~2 mg per 100 g, at the low end of non-heme absorption, and the everyday serving (a few dates a day ≈ 20-30 g) contributes a fraction of a fraction of daily need (about 20 mg/day for adult women) (FACT, food-composition-table framing). What it mainly contributes is sugar.
- **Brown sugar**: about 96-97% sucrose, iron about 2-3 mg/100 g; a daily spoonful (≈10 g) contributes ≈0.3 mg of iron while delivering about 4 g of free sugar (FACT). **The comfort of "brown-sugar water for period cramps" comes mainly from the placebo effect of hot water and the short-lived fuel of sugar, and has nothing to do with "building blood"** (MODEL, physiological-mechanism level; the evidence-based treatment of dysmenorrhea is NSAIDs and hormonal regimens — not expanded here).
- The true ledger of "brown-sugar water after childbirth to replenish qi and blood": sugar intake up, iron accounting ≈0 — exactly when postpartum is the window that needs iron to rebuild stores (FACT).

### 3.3 The master answer to the "like nourishes like" frame

"Eat what ails you / like nourishes like" is analogical thinking, not a nutritional mechanism. Nutrition's real ledger has only three channels: **eat enough iron/B12/folate/protein + an intact absorption pathway + a non-leaking blood-loss pathway**. Any "blood-nourishing" claim should be pressed with three questions: ① how much effective nutrient does it contain (content)? ② what is the absorption rate (form)? ③ which type is your anemia (classification)? A product that cannot answer these three questions cannot build blood no matter how ancient its label (INFERENCE, methodological level).

### 3.4 Supplement-iron vs medicinal iron

| Dimension | Supplement iron (e.g., certain compound "blood-nourishing oral liquids") | Medicinal iron (ferrous sulfate/ferrous succinate/iron polysaccharide complex, etc.) |
|---|---|---|
| Dose labeling | Often vague or counted as "extract" | Elemental iron precisely stated (e.g., 100 mg/tablet) |
| Treating anemia | Dose usually far too small to correct iron-deficiency anemia | Guideline first line; course and rechecks computable |
| Regulation | Food grade; no efficacy claims allowed | Drug grade; efficacy and safety with approval data |
| Positioning | Optional edge nutrition supplement | The treatment for diagnosed iron deficiency |

**Conclusion** (FACT/INFERENCE): diagnosed iron-deficiency anemia → medicinal iron + full course + rechecks (Chapter 2); not diagnosed → classify first, then decide whether to supplement (Chapters 1 and 4). "Supplements are gentle and better for long-term use" is a pseudo-question in the treatment setting — an insufficient dose is the greatest un-gentleness of all.

## Chapter 4 The thalassemia face: South China's genetic background, and the harm of supplementing in reverse

### 4.1 The South China genetic background (numbers verified)

Thalassemia is an inherited disorder of hemoglobin-chain synthesis; carriers themselves are mostly asymptomatic or only mildly anemic. **Southern China is a global high-frequency belt** (FACT):

- Population carrier rates of roughly **11%-24%** across Guangdong, Guangxi and Hainan (FACT, verified: BGI citing Xiong F et al., Clin Genet 2010, and the 2012 Guangdong thalassemia-control baseline survey);
- Carrier rate about **16.8%** among Guangdong registered residents of reproductive age (FACT, verified: Shenzhen municipal government portal's public-education framing, 2022) — "about 1 in 6 Guangdong residents carries" comes from the same source (same-source conversion);
- Some areas of Guangxi reach 24%; the northern Guangdong mountains are similarly high (e.g., Shaoguan weighted average about 20%, individual counties 25.22%) (FACT, verified: Guangdong government web 2024 report).

### 4.2 Thalassemia trait = carrier: mild anemia is common, but **iron must not be the default**

This is the most important sentence of the chapter (FACT, verified: Haematologica 2025 / UCSF / Medscape, multi-source):

- The carrier's genetic defect is in the **globin chains**, not the iron. Iron does not improve their Hb or MCV (Medscape's explicit framing: in α-thalassemia iron improves no hematologic parameter);
- Some non-transfusion-dependent thalassemia patients develop **spontaneous iron overload** through abnormal regulation of intestinal iron absorption (hepcidin suppression); Haematologica 2025 reported case series of β-thalassemia trait with iron overload (sometimes even needing therapeutic phlebotomy) — UCSF's characterization: iron overload is one of the main pathogenic factors in thalassemia populations (FACT);
- The real harm chain: **thalassemia carrier (very low MCV) → mistaken for iron deficiency → long-term iron supplements/blood-nourishing products → iron deposits in liver and heart → organ damage**. "Thalassemia treated as iron deficiency with iron" is not a theoretical risk; it is a concrete clinical error in high-carrier-rate regions (FACT+INFERENCE);
- Balancing clause (honest stratification): a thalassemia carrier can also have true iron deficiency at the same time (in reproductive-age women both routes can stack; iron-deficiency prevalence among carriers is not low — 2024 PMC cohort framing). So the rule is not "thalassemia forbids iron across the board" but **"very low MCV must check ferritin first; a thalassemia picture with normal ferritin forbids iron; only low ferritin opens the supplement question"** (FACT, general clinical practice).

### 4.3 Premarital/prenatal screening = what should actually be done

- **Like-type carrier couples are the source of major thalassemia**: when both partners carry α or β thalassemia, each pregnancy has a theoretical 1/4 chance of a child with major thalassemia — β-major needs lifelong transfusion plus iron chelation; α-major (Hb Bart's hydrops fetalis) is mostly stillbirth or perinatal death (FACT, Mendelian genetics + clinical outcomes).
- **The screening pathway is mature and cheap** (FACT, verified: the national health commission's prevention manual, consistent with Guangxi/Guangdong hospital public education): complete blood count (MCV<80 as the red-line hint) → hemoglobin electrophoresis (raised HbA2 hints β-carrier) → genetic testing to confirm when needed. One screening round for both partners settles the mating risk; high-risk pregnancies can have prenatal diagnosis (chorionic villus/amniotic-fluid genetic testing).
- In a province with 16.8% carrier rates, **a premarital/preconception screen costing tens to a few hundred yuan prevents decades of transfusion dependence for one family** — an action item ten thousand times more important than "nourishing the blood" in South China (INFERENCE, cost-effectiveness judgment).

### 4.4 Thalassemia vs iron deficiency: one small table for the first-pass differential

| Clue | Iron-deficiency anemia | Thalassemia carrier |
|---|---|---|
| MCV | Mildly-moderately low (70-80 fL common) | Can be extremely low (60-70 fL not rare) |
| Ferritin | Low | Normal or high |
| Red cell count (RDW/Mentzer index) | RDW often raised; MCV/RBC>13 hints iron deficiency | RBC often normal or high; MCV/RBC<13 hints thalassemia (MODEL, screening heuristic) |
| Hemoglobin electrophoresis | Normal | Raised HbA2 in β-carriers |
| Family history | No pattern | Generations of "mild anemia" in southern families |
| Response to iron | Hb rises | No response (in ferritin-normal carriers) |

The Mentzer index and RDW are first-pass screening heuristics (MODEL, not diagnostic); **the diagnostic dividing line only comes from ferritin + electrophoresis ± genetics** (FACT).

## Chapter 5 The B12/folate face: the macrocytic half of the sky

### 5.1 Vegan B12: a must-supplement, not an option

- **Plant-based diets contain no active B12** (FACT, nutrition consensus): B12 is synthesized by microbes and exists in animal foods (meat, eggs, dairy, fish). A pure vegan who does not take fortified foods or supplements faces **deficiency as a matter of time** (body stores can last years, masking the causality). This is the exception chapter that any "vegan is naturally healthy" claim must write in — veganism can be a healthy dietary pattern, provided B12 is actively supplemented (FACT).
- B12 deficiency in a vegan mother during pregnancy or lactation can cause **irreversible neurodevelopmental injury in the infant** (FACT, pediatric consensus) — this is not a matter of personal choice; it is a nutritional bottom line requiring intervention.

### 5.2 Atrophic gastritis in the elderly: eating enough ≠ absorbing it

The most common mechanism of B12 deficiency in the elderly is not eating too little but **broken absorption**: atrophic gastritis → insufficient gastric acid and intrinsic factor → both food-B12 liberation and ileal absorption impaired (FACT, mechanism clear; interlinks with the gi: gastrointestinal volume's atrophic-gastritis node). So an older person who "eats plenty of meat yet is anemic plus has numb hands and feet" should trigger this line directly — check B12 + gastric function, not buy a blood-nourishing product first. Long-term metformin and acid-suppressing drug (PPI) users are in the same column (FACT, drug-induced malabsorption).

### 5.3 Pregnancy folate, interlinking with the women's volume

Folate demand rises markedly in pregnancy; deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia and **fetal neural tube defects**; 0.4 mg daily from 3 months before conception is a public-health iron law (FACT). See the fem: (women's health volume) pregnancy face — this canon only sets the blood-side coordinate: **in the differential of pregnancy anemia, iron deficiency and macrocytic (folate/B12) deficiency often coexist**; if anemia persists after iron, remember to check the other half (FACT, general clinical practice).

### 5.4 The neurological warning of megaloblastic anemia: the irreversible window

B12 deficiency is more than anemia: it participates in myelin synthesis, and deficiency can cause **subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord** (impaired proprioception, unsteady gait, paresthesias) and cognitive change (FACT). Two hard rules:

1. **Neurological symptoms of B12 deficiency may never fully recover if treatment is delayed** — one of the few "the longer you wait the worse it gets" alarms in hematology (FACT);
2. **High-dose folate can correct the anemia while masking the B12 deficiency**, letting neurological damage progress under the false comfort of "blood counts improving" (FACT, classic clinical trap). So in macrocytic anemia, check B12 before supplementing folate, or supplement both and give B12 first (FACT, guideline general practice).

## Chapter 6 The red-flag chapter: anemias that must not be "slowly managed"

**Red-flag list** (any one item → seek care promptly; do not take the "food-remedy observation" route):

1. **Anemia + black/tarry stools, blood in urine, or blood in stool**: hints a gastrointestinal or urinary bleeding source — iron deficiency in men and postmenopausal women defaults to working up this route (FACT). Black tarry stools are the classic signal of upper GI bleeding — an emergency-grade clue.
2. **Progressive fatigue + skin petechiae/ecchymoses, or unstoppable gum/nose bleeding**: the platelet or bone marrow red line — needs peripheral blood and, when needed, bone marrow examination to exclude hematologic disease (FACT). The triad of anemia + bleeding tendency + recurrent infections = seek care immediately.
3. **Anemia + unexplained weight loss/night sweats**: the tumor and systemic-disease screening line (FACT).
4. **Childhood anemia + developmental delay/pica (eating dirt, chewing ice)**: a window-period problem affecting neurological development, and needs differentiating thalassemia (especially in South China), lead exposure and chronic disease (FACT).
5. **Transfusion decisions belong to the doctor**: "anemic → transfuse, it rises fast" is a misconception — transfusion is a **prescription decision by Hb threshold, symptoms and cardiopulmonary status** (for stable mild-to-moderate anemia transfusion brings no benefit and carries overload/allergy/circulatory burden), and transfusion thresholds in chronic anemia are more conservative than intuition (FACT; the restrictive transfusion strategy is the guideline mainstream). Treating transfusion as "fast nourishment" both distorts medical decisions and books future liabilities of iron overload and alloimmunization (FACT).

## Chapter 7 The falsification face (seven items, each independently shareable)

1. **"Ejiao builds blood" — falsified**: ejiao = donkey-hide gelatin collagen (incomplete protein, negligible iron); high-quality clinical evidence absent; in-vitro peptide–iron complexing studies cannot be upgraded into efficacy claims. A cultural food can be eaten; used as treatment it misleads. (FACT, stratified)
2. **"Jujube dates and brown sugar build blood" — falsified**: iron contribution ≈ a fraction of daily need, sugar contribution considerable; the comfort of "warm womb, replenished qi and blood" comes from hot water and sugar, not hematopoietic raw materials. (FACT/MODEL)
3. **"Anemia = qi deficiency; manage it, don't test it" — falsified**: anemia is a multi-spectrum symptom; classification workup (complete blood count + ferritin ± electrophoresis/B12) is the premise of all handling; skipping classification and going straight to "management" = supplementing thalassemia into iron overload, and managing a GI bleed for half a year. (FACT, this canon's Chapters 1 and 4 linked)
4. **"Iron hurts the stomach, so it can't be taken" — falsified**: GI side effects are indeed common, but **alternate-day dosing + empty stomach with vitamin C + taking with a little food when needed + switching formulation** resolves most tolerance problems; the real cost of abandoning treatment over side effects (long-term iron deficiency harming heart, lungs, cognition, pregnancy outcomes) far exceeds the nuisance of adjusting the posture. (FACT, Stoffel 2017/2023 evidence chain)
5. **"Thalassemia needs iron" — falsified (harm in exactly the opposite direction)**: thalassemia lacks globin, not iron; giving iron to a ferritin-normal thalassemia picture = storing waste in liver and heart. Against South China's high carrier background, **check ferritin before iron** should become common sense. (FACT)
6. **"Blood donation drains your vital essence" — falsified**: one whole-blood donation is 450 mL (about 200-250 mg of iron) — plasma volume recovers in 24-48 hours, red cells in 4-8 weeks (NHS framing: hemoglobin back to baseline in 6-12 weeks, whole-blood donation interval 12 weeks). The "vital essence" narrative has no corresponding physiological entity; the real caution is **monitoring ferritin in frequent donors** (especially menstruating female donors) — that is fine management, not a prohibition. (FACT)
7. **"A heavy flow expels stasis" — falsified**: see 2.2 — menstrual discharge is endometrium and blood, not "stasis" needing expulsion; romanticizing HMB as stasis-expelling = romanticizing the largest treatable cause of iron deficiency. (FACT/INFERENCE)
