## 1. The concept-science face

### 1.1 Self-efficacy: not "feeling great", but "feeling able to get it done"

- **Definition**: Bandura's self-efficacy = a person's belief about "whether I can organize and execute the actions needed to reach a specific goal". **Domain specificity** is its key difference from global self-esteem: one can hold "I'm not bad" (high self-esteem) while simultaneously holding "I can't handle public speaking" (low efficacy). Classic sources: Bandura 1977 in *Psychological Review* and the 1997 monograph [bibliographic level · no PubMed hit this round, unverified · MODEL].
- **The four source channels** (MODEL · labeled as above):
  1. **Mastery experiences** — completing something hard with your own hands; the strongest channel;
  2. **Vicarious experiences** — seeing someone similar to yourself succeed (model similarity > model halo);
  3. **Verbal persuasion** — being credibly and specifically affirmed; the weakest, and it decays easily;
  4. **Physiological and affective states** — reading a racing heart and sweaty palms as "I'm excited" versus "I can't do this".
  → **Action implication (INFERENCE)**: the four channels' strength ordering directly sets intervention priority — **the number-one move is always to arrange one real success experience**, not to say nice things. Nice words are only channel 3, and channel 1 (getting something done) cannot be substituted by words.
- **Why behavioral medicine cares**: self-efficacy is widely treated as one of the core mediating variables of health behavior change (exercise adherence, chronic-disease self-management, cessation; review-level consensus · MODEL). Wiring to the burnout volume: one of ICD-11 burnout's three dimensions is precisely "decreased occupational efficacy" (wrk:职业效能感下降 · FACT); wiring to the flow-and-creation volume: the flow channel is essentially a mastery-experience stream of "skill-challenge balance", a renewable source of efficacy (INFERENCE).

### 1.2 The self-esteem correction face: high self-esteem is not a panacea, and "high" has a dark side

- **The Baumeister 2003 review (PSPI · PMID 26151640 · FACT)**: "Does High Self-Esteem Cause Better Performance, Interpersonal Success, Happiness, or Healthier Lifestyles?" — the conclusion face: high self-esteem's subjective well-being benefit is clear, but its **causal gains for performance / relationships are far weaker than the folk narrative**; and high self-esteem is not a one-way good — **narcissistic high self-esteem** turns toward aggression and derogation when threatened. This is the representative framing of the "dark side of high self-esteem" research face.
- **Narcissism ≠ high self-esteem (FACT+MODEL)**: narcissism is a structure of self-aggrandizement + entitlement + need for admiration, **psychometrically separable** from genuine high self-esteem (Neff & Vonk 2009 · PMID 19076996 is one of the separation evidences). The cross-cultural meta (Sedikides 2026 · PSPB · PMID 39921533) shows the narcissism–well-being association is **far weaker and more mixed than the folk narrative "narcissists do better"** — buying stability by "putting others down to lift yourself up" does not work (for the behavioral evidence see also Zhang L 2006 · PMID 16738022: the threatened egoist pays a higher price entangled in failing ventures).
- **The threatened ego (MODEL)**: the risk point for violence and aggression is not "high self-esteem" itself, but the combination "high yet unstable, and dependent on external validation" (the Baumeister-line framing · wiring to §7's violence line).

### 1.3 Stability matters more than height: contingent self-esteem is the core variable

- **Sowislo 2014 (J Abnorm Psychol · PMID 25222046 · FACT)**: the same prospective paper compares how **low self-esteem, unstable self-esteem, and contingent self-esteem** predict depressive symptoms — within the makeup of "fragile self-esteem", **the unstable and contingent components are the real predictive sources**; it is not only "low" that matters.
- **Contingencies of self-worth**: the Crocker-line research face — when self-worth is staked on a domain (grades / looks / others' approval), failure in that domain becomes the failure of **the whole person**. Crocker 2006 (J Pers · PMID 17083665): pursuing self-esteem (proving oneself) conflicts with self-regulatory resources; Park 2007 (PSPB · PMID 17933747): contingent-self-esteem individuals show impaired goal pursuit after academic failure.
- **Roth 2020 (Front Psychol · PMID 32849049 · FACT)**: self-esteem stability's direct predictive power for psychological outcomes can be compared separately from self-esteem level — "high but roller-coaster" and "moderate but stable" are not the same kind of asset.
- **One-line conclusion (INFERENCE)**: **the goal is not to raise the self-esteem number, but to convert self-esteem from a "performance-linked floating asset" into a "stable interest-bearing asset".**

### 1.4 Body image: the evidence face in the filter era

- **Social media × body dissatisfaction (FACT)**: Bonfanti 2025 (Body Image · PMID 39721448) systematic review + meta: the association between **upward social comparison** on social media and body dissatisfaction and eating-disorder symptoms holds; Zhang 2025 (Body Image · PMID 41161231) experimental-study meta: **video platforms (short video) deserve more vigilance than image platforms** for negative body-image effects — motion plus looks-based algorithms amplify comparison exposure.
- **The filter mechanism (MODEL/INFERENCE)**: filters turn "internalization of ideals" into a daily input — what you see every day is your own face algorithmically smoothed and others' bodies curated; the comparison baseline is systematically raised. **This is an environment-design problem, not merely a psychological weakness** — the intervention priority should be changing the environment (§4 card C), not scolding yourself for "caring too much".
- **Wiring to the exercise line (FACT)**: Wang 2026 (Front Psychol · PMID 42325342) Chinese college students' exercise intervention × **physical self-worth (body-esteem)** systematic review + meta; Zhou 2025 (BMC Public Health · PMID 41174613) children/adolescents exercise × depression/anxiety/self-esteem network meta — **exercise is one of the few hard channels that improves body image and self-esteem simultaneously** (connects to move-canon/exercise-canon).

## 2. The evidence face: what actually works

### 2.1 The CBT line for low self-esteem: a model and a path exist

- The Fennell line (Oxford) treats low self-esteem as an intervenable cognitive construct: the core is "bottom-layer negative self-beliefs + deflecting rules (effort / perfectionism as compensation) + a post-trigger self-criticism loop". Fennell 2004 (Behav Res Ther · PMID 15325901) gives the integrated framing of depression, low self-esteem, and (mindfulness-assisted) CBT [FACT · title-level].
- **Evidence strength honestly stated**: low-self-esteem CBT's RCT base is thinner than depression/anxiety CBT's (this volume found no large-sample low-self-esteem-specific meta · UNKNOWN); but as a component of depression/anxiety programs it has a mature clinical tradition (INFERENCE). **Operational implication**: CBT's core move — **test thoughts rather than suppress them** — is the best-evidenced skeleton in low-self-esteem self-help (§4 cards A/B are its operationalization).

### 2.2 Self-compassion: the steadier road than "raising self-esteem"

- **Neff & Vonk 2009 (J Pers · PMID 19076996 · FACT)**: self-compassion and global self-esteem are **two different ways of relating to the self**; what self-compassion supplies is **a stability that does not depend on positive self-evaluation** — no need to mobilize evidence after every failure to prove "I'm still great".
- **Self-compassion ≠ self-indulgence (MODEL+FACT face)**: conceptually self-compassion = self-kindness + common humanity (suffering is part of being human) + mindfulness (neither exaggerating nor suppressing), three elements (the Neff line · bibliographic level); behavioral evidence: self-compassionate people's **self-improvement motivation rises rather than falls** (Breines & Chen 2012 · bibliographic level · no PubMed hit verified this round · honestly labeled); the adolescent self-compassion × psychological distress negative-association meta (Marsh 2018 · Mindfulness · PMID 30100930 · FACT).
- **Why it takes priority over raising self-esteem (INFERENCE)**: the self-esteem-raising path requires "winning", and fails or backfires when you cannot win (§1.3's contingency); the self-compassion path remains usable at the failure scene itself — **it is the only tool that does not pick its situation**.

### 2.3 Behavioral experiments and small-wins strategy: the experiential source of efficacy

- **Principle (MODEL)**: per §1.1's four-channel ordering, mastery > vicarious > verbal persuasion > physiological reappraisal. Behavioral experiments (a CBT technique) and the "small-wins strategy" both **manufacture mastery experiences on purpose**.
- **Wiring to the flow-and-creation volume (INFERENCE)**: flow = the mastery-experience stream of dynamic skill-challenge balance — pick a difficulty "reachable with a jump"; winning raises the tier, losing lowers it: that is the efficacy gauge's self-calibration process.
- **Wiring to nof1 self-experiments (INFERENCE)**: the PG `healing_nof1_trial_v1` self-experiment face is essentially an efficacy-training tool — designing small experiments yourself, running them, looking at the data: each round is a "I can study and change myself" mastery experience.

### 2.4 Exercise × self-esteem: the hard-evidence channel

- Wang 2026 (PMID 42325342 · FACT · Chinese college students' body-esteem meta) + Zhou 2025 (PMID 41174613 · FACT · children/adolescents three-outcome network meta): exercise interventions' improvement of self-esteem / body-esteem has meta-level support in both age bands.
- **Low self-esteem × depression directionality (FACT)**: Sowislo 2014 (PMID 25222046) prospective comparison confirms fragile-self-esteem components predict depressive symptoms — together with §1.3's Crocker/Park contingency evidence face, "low self-esteem is the underlying amplifier of anxiety/depression" has substantive support in the **prospective direction**; the full causal test of the "low self-esteem ↔ domestic-violence two-way" was not verified this round (UNKNOWN · wiring at §7).

## 3. The culture face: Chinese-context modesty and measurement

- **The East-West self-enhancement dispute (CONTESTED · both cases honestly)**: the Heine line argues East Asian samples show **little self-enhancement** under classic self-report paradigms (Heine 2007 PSPR · PMID 18453453, "finding the East Asian self-enhancement"; Heine 2005 JPSP · PMID 16287416, a point-by-point reply on pan-cultural self-enhancement evidence); the Sedikides line argues that with implicit / indirect measures the East Asian self-enhancement evidence remains. **This is a measurement-and-method dispute, not a "who is higher" dispute** — the canon issues no verdict.
- **Implication for measurement (MODEL)**: East Asian participants' moderate responding on self-report scales like the Rosenberg (avoiding extreme scores) is a composite of **response style + modesty norms**; **"modesty" does not equal low self-esteem** — reading East Asian groups' self-report means directly as "collectively low self-esteem" is a measurement artifact (INFERENCE). **Implication for this library's readers**: do not stick a "low self-esteem" label on yourself from an online self-esteem scale score — the score mixes modesty norms, response style, and actual state, three layers; and a single score ≠ stability (§1.3).
- **The real risk of the modesty culture (INFERENCE)**: a norm of modesty is not the same as inner stability; when modesty is internalized from courtesy into an automatic self-criticism channel, it turns from a cultural asset into a burden. The distinguishing marker: **object-specific modesty ("this time the luck was good / the team carried") is courtesy; global self-negation ("I as a person am no good") is the repair target**.
- **Identity in the era of social comparison**: the comparison objects the algorithm feeds everyone are the same track's frontrunners (§1.4) — comparison-baseline distortion is an environment property. The identity boundary (need:身份认同 · incl. post-illness reconstruction) gets an anchor only in this volume, no face — left for later lanes.

## 4. Action cards (dose-level operations)

> Universal discipline: the following are all everyday self-help level; when the §5 care-seeking line is triggered, seek care first — the action cards do not replace treatment.

### Card A: The efficacy small-win card (self-efficacy · three steps starting today)

1. **Pick one real thing completable in 5-15 minutes** (not a pseudo-goal like "become confident" — send the email you've put off for three days / do 10 minutes of stretching / cook a meal). Tick it on completion, **record it somewhere visible** (this library's health diary works). Frequency: 1-3 items a day.
2. **Difficulty gradient +10%**: complete the same tier three days in a row → move up a tier (the skill-challenge balance of flow); stuck → drop half a tier; **dropping a tier is not failure, it is calibration**.
3. **Attribute failure to method, not to person**: fix the review sentence as "this time ____ (method / timing / difficulty) didn't cooperate; next time change to ____"; "I'm just incapable" is banned (that sentence directly severs the mastery-experience attribution inside the four channels).

### Card B: The self-compassion card (usage at the failure scene)

1. **The three-step sentence pattern** (the Neff three elements operationalized): ① acknowledge "this genuinely hurts" (mindfulness · neither exaggerating nor denying); ② common humanity — "anyone hit by this would hurt" (severing "I alone am this bad"); ③ self-kindness — "if a friend hit this, I would say ____ to them; now I say that sentence to myself".
2. **Dose**: use on the spot at failure / shame, 1-2 minutes; in heavy self-criticism rumination, write it down (3-5 sentences).
3. **Division of labor with card A**: card B handles the failure scene (so one failure does not become an identity verdict); card A handles daily accumulation (manufacturing success evidence) — **B first, then A**; do not open new tasks before the emotion is caught.

### Card C: The body-image card (the filter era)

1. **Social media dieting**: unfollow / mute every account that makes you want to compare bodies and faces the moment you see it (the experimental meta confirms comparison exposure is an intervenable input end · §1.4); follow 2-3 function-oriented body accounts (athletic performance / rehab / food and energy).
2. **The filter-informed disclaimer**: for your own retouched works and others' polished content alike, attach one mental footnote: "this is a finished product, not a baseline"; in family / friend group sharing you can deliberately keep the original photo, **practice showing the original once** (a behavioral experiment · channel-1 mastery experience).
3. **Functional exercise reframing**: change the exercise goal from "what shape to build" to "what capability to reach" (2-3 sessions a week of brisk walking / strength / any sustainable form — what the meta-level evidence supports is exercise itself improving self-esteem and body-esteem, not body renovation).

### Card D: The parenting praise card (process praise instead of trait praise)

- **Evidence (FACT · PMID 9686450)**: Mueller & Dweck 1998 (JPSP) experiment series: children praised as "smart" (trait praise) tended more to give up after setbacks, showed sliding performance, and misreported their scores; children praised for "effort / strategy / process" (process praise) were more resilient.
- **Operation**: point praise at **the concrete process** ("you tried three different methods", "you checked that step carefully") rather than **trait labels** ("so smart", "so pretty"); the same for criticism — point at behavior, not personality ("this problem's steps were missed" ≠ "how are you so stupid").
- **The dissection of "praise only, never criticize" is at §6 claim 5** — half true, half false; taken apart below.

## 5. The care-seeking line: when self-esteem issues need professional help

> The triage line is for triage only; diagnosis belongs to the clinician. Any line triggered → book psychiatry / clinical psychology or a proper psychotherapy institution.

1. **The depression line**: low mood / loss of interest nearly every day for ≥2 weeks + functional impairment (work / school / self-care affected) → depression screening (low self-esteem is often depression's precursor and residual face · §2.4).
2. **The eating-disorder line**: food restriction, bingeing, vomiting / laxative misuse, or compensatory over-exercise over weight / body shape; or rapid weight loss → eating-disorder specialty evaluation (the heaviest complication of body-image issues · §1.4).
3. **The body-dysmorphia line**: worry over a perceived appearance "flaw" consuming large amounts of time daily, repeated mirror-checking / covering / seeking cosmetic procedures with undiminished distress → the body dysmorphic disorder evaluation direction.
4. **The crisis line**: self-harm / suicidal ideation at any intensity → immediate crisis intervention (local psychological assistance hotline / emergency department). This line always outranks every self-help card.
5. **The relationship-violence line**: systematic belittling and humiliation within an intimate relationship ("you're worthless"-style sustained grinding down) is the core form of psychological abuse (connects to the violence map `safe:degradation`) — **this is not you having low self-esteem; this is harm**; red-flag recognition and help channels follow the violence map (12338 / 110 / 12348). *(Translator calibration: mainland China anchors; readers elsewhere use local domestic-violence and emergency services.)*
6. **The medication face, honestly**: no medication is specifically registered for low self-esteem; medication decisions happen inside the treatment of comorbidities (depression / anxiety), made by a doctor (INFERENCE).

## 6. The falsification face: five viral self-help chicken soups

> Verdict format: claim → evidence → epistemic state.

1. **"Say 'I'm the best' to the mirror daily; say it enough and you'll believe it"** → **CONTRADICTED (for low-self-esteem people)**. Wood 2009 (Psychol Sci · PMID 19493324): after repeating positive self-statements, low-self-esteem participants' mood got **worse**, and state self-esteem fell; only high-self-esteem participants benefited slightly. For the self-doubting, the brain auto-refutes the statement, and the refuting process itself manufactures more counter-evidence. **Replacement**: card B's "friend's perspective" + card A's real small wins.
2. **"The law of attraction, self-esteem edition: think success, visualize the win, and you win"** → **CONTRADICTED (as a self-help proposition) + honest boundary**. The Baumeister 2003 (PMID 26151640) review face: feeling high self-esteem does not auto-convert into performance gains — "visualizing the win" manufactures only a feeling; it produces none of the four channels' mastery experiences; the positive-fantasy-lowers-action-tendency research face (the Oettingen line · bibliographic level · not verified online this round) runs the same way. **Boundary**: imagining the process and planning (mental contrasting) works; imagining the outcome itself does not. **Replacement**: card A's difficulty gradient.
3. **"Put others down / step on people, and you stand taller"** → **CONTRADICTED**. Narcissism and stable high self-esteem are psychometrically separable (Neff & Vonk 2009 · PMID 19076996); the narcissism × well-being cross-cultural meta shows the payoff is weak and mixed (Sedikides 2026 · PMID 39921533); the threatened egoist stays entangled in failure at a higher price (Zhang L 2006 · PMID 16738022); high-yet-unstable self-esteem turns toward aggression when threatened (Baumeister 2003 dark side · PMID 26151640). What stepping on people buys is unstable debt, not self-esteem.
4. **"Be harder on yourself — drill-sergeant discipline; discipline IS self-esteem"** → **CONTRADICTED (as a self-esteem-building means)**. Staking self-worth on external performance = the standard recipe for contingent self-esteem (Crocker 2006 · PMID 17083665; Park 2007 · PMID 17933747); contingent-self-esteem people's goal pursuit after failure is **impaired**, not enhanced (same anchors); fragile-self-esteem components prospectively predict depressive symptoms (Sowislo 2014 · PMID 25222046). Drill-sergeant discipline is an accelerator of "high but unstable": on stilts when winning, falling harder when losing. **Replacement**: card B catches the emotion first; card A calibrates difficulty.
5. **"Children may only be praised, never criticized — criticism destroys self-esteem"** → **half true, half false · judged apart**. **The true half**: crude, person-targeting demeaning criticism does harm. **The false half**: Mueller & Dweck 1998 (PMID 9686450) shows **praise style matters more than praise quantity** — praising "smart" (trait praise) makes children more fragile in the face of setbacks; per the Dweck-line follow-ups (bibliographic level), growth-framed feedback (process + strategy + concrete improvement points) beats indiscriminate puffing, and also beats empty criticism. **Verdict: INFERENCE → operationalized = card D** (concrete-process praise + behavior-pointing-not-personality feedback — both are needed).
