# Relationship Violence Recognition & Help-Seeking Canon v1 (English)

> **This canon is protective content — not an academic review, and still less a case-handling manual.** It answers three questions: **how to recognize signals of violence in a relationship; which signals mean real danger; where to seek help, and what friends can do.** The protective tone holds throughout: no rendering of case details, no judgment of victims, no teaching of any "how to win the divorce lawsuit" style operations.

---

## 0. Positioning and boundaries (read first)

**What this canon is**:
- An extension of the healing universe's "red-flag recognition" capability — it connects to the layered design of the `fn_healing_life_report` red-flag emergency line and the 54-red-flag recognizer (recognition ≠ disposition · routing to professional channels).
- Written for three kinds of readers: people who may currently be inside relationship violence; friends and family around a suspected victim; and the system capability itself, as it is later wired into the healing universe's Q&A face.

**What this canon is not (hard boundaries)**:
1. **It does not teach specific legal operations.** Evidence preservation is covered only as principles and destinations (e.g., "medical records and police-report receipts are worth keeping"), never "how to gather evidence for divorce" or "how to fight for custody" — those must be taken up by lawyers and legal aid (see the §4 channel card).
2. **It does not gender-presuppose victims.** A victim can be of any gender, in any relationship form (married / cohabiting / dating / after divorce); so can the person using violence. The statistical fact that female victims hold a high share in the literature (the §1.4 both-sides discussion) does not constitute a gender presumption about any individual case.
3. **Discovering a child victim = mandatory reporting duty.** China's Anti-Domestic Violence Law establishes a mandatory reporting clause: schools, kindergartens, medical institutions, residents' (villagers') committees, social work services, assistance management, welfare institutions and their staff who discover persons without civil capacity or with limited civil capacity suffering domestic violence **shall** report promptly to the public security organs (article number per the statute text). Any system handling signals of child victimization must carry this duty face.
4. **Zero case-detail rendering.** No plots, no dialogue, no "storytelling" — detail rendering is a re-traumatization risk for victims and a privacy risk for the system.
5. **All channel numbers defer to the latest official publication** (checked at this volume's writing time; see §4 and the receipt).

## 1. Definition and scale face

### 1.1 The intimate partner violence spectrum (FACT · definition face)

Intimate partner violence (IPV) is not one thing called "hitting"; it is a spectrum. Control is the core feature running through the spectrum — a single incident is not necessarily the most dangerous; **the persistent structure of control is what defines violence's relationship form** (FACT · conceptual definition, from the coercive control theoretical framework; see 1.3).

| Dimension | Content | Common presentations (non-exhaustive) |
|---|---|---|
| Physical violence | Injury to the body, and threats of it | Beating, shoving, restricting movement, destroying property as deterrence |
| Psychological / emotional abuse | Systematic belittling, intimidation, manipulation | Insults and humiliation, tracking and surveillance, threats (against the person / against children / against pets / of suicide), long-term silent-treatment punishment |
| Sexual violence | Sexual demands and sexual control against one's will | Coerced sexual acts, sexual degradation, sex used as punishment or barter |
| Economic control | Control of money and survival resources | Controlling all income, forbidding work, every expense requiring an accounting, manufacturing debt dependence |

### 1.2 "Cold violence": the informal term and its academic counterpart (FACT · concept mapping)

"Cold violence" (leng baoli) is a Chinese everyday expression, not a strict academic term; its academic counterparts are mainly **psychological abuse / emotional abuse**, often taking the form of long-term neglect, refusal to communicate, and systematic cold-shouldering and belittling used as punishment. Key points:
- Psychological abuse is as much a constituent part of IPV as physical violence, and it often **precedes** and **accompanies** physical violence.
- China's Anti-Domestic Violence Law defines domestic violence to explicitly include **psychological harm** ("bodily, psychological and other harm inflicted by such means as habitual verbal abuse and intimidation") — in the legal context, "cold violence" is not "not domestic violence".
- Boundary note: occasional silent treatment and quiet spells are not equivalent to systematic psychological abuse; the distinguishing features are **persistence, controlling intent, and fear of consequences** (MODEL · conceptual operationalization).

### 1.3 Coercive control: why "control" is the core feature (FACT · concept source)

Evan Stark, in *Coercive Control: How Men Entrap Women in Personal Life* (2007, Oxford University Press), proposed that the essence of intimate relationship violence is often not (or not only) one assault after another, but a **structure that strips freedom and autonomy** — through surveillance, isolation, economic control, rules and punishments, the victim's living space keeps shrinking. Stark called it a "liberty crime".
- This canon cites the book title as published (the title reads as it reads), but **this canon does not gender-presuppose**: the dynamic structure of coercive control can appear in relationships of any gender combination (INFERENCE · framework extrapolation; the framework's gender boundaries are themselves a live discussion in the literature).

### 1.4 Prevalence and scale (FACT · every figure with its framing)

**Global framing (WHO)**:
- WHO's 2021 release *Violence against women prevalence estimates, 2018* (WHO/UN interagency working group): worldwide, **nearly 1 in 3 (30%)** of ever-partnered women aged 15 and older have experienced physical and/or sexual intimate partner violence in their lifetime; the regional range runs from about 20% (Western Pacific) to 33% (African Region).
- Globally, about **6%** of women report non-partner sexual assault (lifetime).
- Globally, **as many as 38% of murders of women are committed by an intimate partner** (WHO fact sheet framing).
- Among women who have experienced IPV, about **42%** report being injured as a result (WHO framing).
- The WHO collaborative re-analysis published in *The Lancet* (Sardinha et al., 2022) gives a narrower-framing estimate: **27% (uncertainty interval 23–31%)** of ever-partnered women aged 15–49 have experienced physical and/or sexual IPV (lifetime).
- **Framing warning**: 27% and 30% coexist because of different age framings (15–49 vs 15+) and model-version differences — not a contradiction. Any citation of a prevalence figure must carry four elements — "what ages, ever-partnered or not, physical/sexual/psychological, lifetime or past year"; **a single number without framing is refused citation outright**.

**China framing**: this canon v1 has not locked a nationally sampled, repeatedly verified IPV prevalence figure (the literature exists, but title-level verification was not completed this round) — recorded honestly as UNKNOWN; prefer a blank to an unverified number (UNKNOWN is a first-class citizen). Verifiable next round: the ACWF sampling surveys and the three-wave Women's Social Status survey framings.

### 1.5 The gender-symmetry controversy: both cases presented honestly (FACT · controversy face)

This is the most famous persistent controversy in IPV research; this canon does not adjudicate — it only presents both sides as they stand:

**The symmetry side**:
- Archer (2000, *Psychological Bulletin*), in a meta-analysis of aggression between opposite-sex partners, found that women's frequency of committing physical aggression is not lower than men's (higher in some analyses).
- Straus (2011), building on 200+ studies, argued that "gender symmetry" holds at the behavior-count level.

**The asymmetry side (rebuttals tied to injury and control contexts)**:
- Critics note that the symmetry results mostly come from **behavior-checklist counting** (e.g., the CTS scale counting "the act occurred"), which does not distinguish severity, consequences, initiation context, or the possibility of self-defense (Fanslow et al. 2023 et al.).
- Injury and lethality outcomes are markedly asymmetric: under the WHO framing, **38% of murders of women are committed by an intimate partner**; female victims report heavier injury rates, fear, and long-term consequences.
- Control and subsequent intimidation (stalking, post-separation violence) hold a higher share in female-victim cases (coercive-control literature face).

**This canon's operational conclusion (INFERENCE)**: at the **behavior-count** level the literature holds near-symmetric evidence; at the **lethality, injury-consequence, and control-structure** level the evidence is markedly asymmetric. Therefore: (a) a person of any gender can be a victim — the help channels are open to everyone; (b) danger assessment (§2) centers on "lethality escalation signals", not on gender.

## 2. Recognition face: the red-flag lists (this canon's core asset)

> **How to use**: a red flag is "a signal worth stopping for and taking seriously" — not a diagnosis, and still less a legal conclusion. Yellow flag = worth noticing and checking; red flag = take seriously and consider seeking help; **extreme red flag = a lethal-risk signal — contact professional channels immediately for a danger assessment**. The list is ordered "strongest lethality-escalation predictors first".

### 2.1 Extreme red flags: lethality escalation signals (any one of these is worth seeking help immediately)

| # | Signal | Evidence face |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **History of strangulation** (being choked / strangled / having mouth and nose covered, whether or not marks were left) | **One of the strongest single predictors of later death risk**: Glass et al. 2008 (J Emerg Med 35(3):329-335) found that women non-fatally strangled by a partner had subsequent risk of being killed rise to on the order of **7-fold** (exact OR and CI per the original paper · title-level verification). **Having been strangled ≠ "the vital spots were missed"** — neck compression can cause internal injuries that deteriorate with delay (MODEL · mechanism face), so a strangulation history is simultaneously an **indication for medical evaluation** (the emergency line), not only an indication for help-seeking |
| P2 | Holding / having access to a weapon, or threats made with a weapon | Campbell et al. 2003 (Am J Public Health 93(7):1089-97, a controlled study of 220 intimate-partner homicide cases): prior threats with a weapon and similar factors are independent risk factors for femicide (the killing of women) |
| P3 | Threats to kill you, the children, or "we die together"; the violent partner threatens suicide and uses it as leverage | A threat is an expression of intent; Campbell 2003 as above (threat-type factors remain significant in multivariable models) |
| P4 | Violence **escalating persistently** in frequency and severity | Escalation is the pathway signature of lethal risk (literature-consensus face · MODEL); this also corrects the old "cycle" picture — see §3.2 |
| P5 | Stalking, surveillance, and harassment piling on top of a violence history after a separation / after a breakup is proposed | The separation period is a high-risk window for violence and lethality (MODEL · risk-timing face; high consensus in the literature) |
| P6 | Your own strong fear that "he will kill me / I could really die" | The victim's own danger intuition is itself a valid predictor — the Campbell Danger Assessment formally includes a victim self-assessment dimension (Campbell 2009 validation study, J Interpers Violence 24(4):653-674) |

### 2.2 Red flags: serious signals (several at once = rising danger)

| # | Signal | Note |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Financial control**: taking away income, forbidding work, full accounting of every expense, manufacturing economic dependence | Economic control = the structural lock of "unable to leave" (connects to §3.3) |
| R2 | **Social isolation**: cutting your contact with family and friends, monitoring communications, demanding location reports at all times | A core tactic of coercive control (Stark 2007 framework face) |
| R3 | **Property-destruction threats**: smashing objects, slamming doors, harming pets as deterrence | "Violence against things" is the rehearsal and the threat-expression of violence against people (MODEL · behavior face) |
| R4 | Sexual control and sexual degradation | A constituent dimension of the IPV spectrum (§1.1) |
| R5 | Violence begins or escalates during pregnancy and the perinatal period | WHO fact sheet: IPV is associated with elevated risks of miscarriage, stillbirth, preterm birth, and low birth weight (FACT · association face) |
| R6 | The violent partner's unemployment stacked with alcohol / substance misuse | Campbell 2003 risk-factor face (unemployment is one of the independent risk factors) |
| R7 | Long-term humiliation and belittling until you believe you are "worthless" | The core form of psychological abuse; systematic degradation is the preparatory engineering of control |

### 2.3 Yellow flags: signals to keep an eye on (may be early, or may stop here)

- The "love = possession" narrative: relationships that begin with intense possessiveness, with location check-ins packaged as "caring".
- Checking phones and social accounts; "transparency demands" escalating one-way onto you.
- Emotional extremity: rage at any frustration, and the blame always assigned to you or to the outside.
- Using children, pets, or money as bargaining chips.
- Threats of "I can't live without you", with the responsibility for that hung on you.

### 2.4 Structural notes (why the tiers are ordered this way)

- Following the healing universe's 54-red-flag recognizer layering: **recognition ≠ disposition** — every line of the red-flag lists points to "have a professional do a professional assessment", not to the system or friends assigning labels on their own.
- The Campbell Danger Assessment (DA) and its 5-item short form **DA-5** (Messing, Campbell & Snider 2017, J Adv Nurs 73(12):3220-3230, DOI 10.1111/jan.13459) are among the best-validated IPV lethality risk assessment tools in the literature. **This canon does not self-assess and does not score**: danger assessment should be carried out by trained professionals within the full context — the lists' value is letting you know which signals are worth putting a professional assessment on the schedule.
- Strangulation history is listed explicitly and on its own line (P1): it is the strongest-evidence signal on this list, and the one most often underestimated by victims and bystanders alike.

## 3. Evidence face

### 3.1 Effects on children: witnessing domestic violence is a core adverse childhood experience (ACE) item (FACT · research face)

- The CDC's official definition of ACEs explicitly includes "experiencing or witnessing violence" — witnessing intimate partner violence is one of the core ACE items (CDC VetoViolence official framing).
- The original ACE study (Felitti et al. 1998, Am J Prev Med 14(4):245-258) established the dose-response association framework between adverse childhood experiences and adult health risks (including leading causes of death) (title-level anchor · this canon did not independently re-verify the original text; epistemic state FACT · classic citation).
- Intergenerational effect: Forke et al. 2019 found that children who witnessed domestic violence carry an elevated risk of their own IPV involvement in adulthood (intergenerational transmission).
- Meta-level evidence: Zhu et al. 2024 (Cambridge) meta-analysis shows ACE is positively associated with both IPV perpetration and IPV victimization.
- **Wiring note**: this face connects to the E8 volume and the ACE research face, and to the diary-side observation of the `v_diary_to_symptoms` symptom reverse-lookup; signals of child victimization trigger the §0.3 mandatory-reporting duty face.
- Protective restatement: ACE is not a verdict, it is a risk map — the risks are intervenable and repairable (the healing universe's existing trauma-informed framing).

### 3.2 The "cycle of violence" theory has been corrected: not a cycle — an escalation (FACT→MODEL · the theory's history presented honestly)

- Lenore Walker proposed the "cycle of violence" in 1979: tension building → acute battering → honeymoon phase (apology and warmth), three stages. This was a historic conceptual milestone — it was the first time "why victims stay" was placed inside a discussable framework.
- **Revision one (shape)**: subsequent research and reviews note that not all relationships fit the cycle; in many relationships there is no "honeymoon phase", and the stage division cannot describe most actual trajectories (review and clinical framings, e.g., the corrected statements of Cleveland Clinic and DVSN).
- **Revision two (direction)**: the long-term trajectory is more often **escalation** than cyclical return — higher frequency, greater severity, with "calm periods" shortening over time and even disappearing. This is also the basis for §2.1-P4 listing "persistent escalation" as an extreme red flag.
- **Operational implication**: treat "they apologized this time, things will get better" as an **unproven assumption**, and treat "violence escalates" as the **working hypothesis** — this is the safer default where life is concerned (INFERENCE · protective default).

### 3.3 "Why can't they leave": traumatic bonding is a neural mechanism, not a character flaw (MODEL · science face + de-shaming)

This is this canon's most important **de-shaming** passage: a victim stays, usually, not because of being "stupid", "weak", or "in it for the money", but because multiple mechanisms stack:

1. **Traumatic bonding**: Dutton & Painter (proposed 1981; empirically tested 1993, PMID 8193053) found that the **intermittency** of abuse (abuse alternating with kindness) produces **stronger** emotional attachment than continuous abuse — the intermittent-reinforcement mechanism is isomorphic with the learning mechanism of addiction (MODEL · mechanism analogy). The unpredictable alternation of cruelty and warmth is precisely what locks the victim's attachment system onto the expectation that "next time will be better".
2. **Economic and survival lock-in**: economic control (R1) and isolation (R2) systematically dismantle "leaving" at the material and social levels — leaving is not a single decision; it is an engineering project requiring housing, income, physical safety, and social support.
3. **Real fear**: the moment of leaving and the period when a breakup is proposed are peak-risk windows (§2.1-P5) — "staying" is sometimes unconscious risk management.
4. **The backlash of social reactions**: meeting doubt and blame after disclosure ("why didn't you leave sooner") deepens the trauma and suppresses later help-seeking (social-reactions research face; see §4.4).

**Protective conclusion**: for any victim, "why haven't you left yet" is never the right question; the right question is "how do we make leaving possible, and safe".

## 4. The help-seeking action card (this canon's soul)

> **Master principle: safety > evidence > relationship conclusions.** Every action first passes the question "will this step make the risk bigger"; the timing and manner of major moves (leaving, calling the police, suing) should, as far as possible, be planned with professional support (shelter institutions, social workers, lawyers, the Women's Federation).

### 4.1 Evidence preservation (principles face · specific legal operations are routed to professional channels)

Things worth keeping (the keeping itself must be covert, and personal safety comes first):
- **Medical records**: after an injury, seek care and ask the doctor to record the injuries and the cause of injury truthfully in the medical record — the medical record is a record formed independently within the medical system.
- **Injury photographs**: photos that carry time information, from multiple angles, backed up to the cloud.
- **Police-report receipts and written warnings**: after reporting, ask for the receipt; the public security organ may issue a **written warning (gaojieshu)** for less serious cases (an institution of the Anti-Domestic Violence Law); the written warning is an important evidence form in later proceedings.
- **Original chat / call records**: keep the originals (do not merely screenshot and then delete the originals).
- **A preparedness bag** (kept with a trusted third party): documents, a small amount of cash, regularly taken medications, a spare key, key contacts written on paper.

Boundary restated: this canon does **not teach** "how to use evidence in a lawsuit" — evidence strategy, admissibility judgment, and divorce and custody operations are taken up by lawyers / legal aid (§4.3).

### 4.2 Safety plan elements (outline)

1. **Danger self-check trigger line**: any extreme red flag of §2.1 appears → put "contact a professional danger assessment" first.
2. **Executable destinations**: confirm in advance 2 places you could turn to in an emergency (a family or friend's home / a shelter), and agree on a code word.
3. **Emergency bag and documents**: the §4.1 preparedness bag.
4. **Electronic safety**: check whether monitoring software has been installed on the phone; clear call logs after calling the police / seeking help; be cautious with shared accounts and family location sharing.
5. **Plans for children and pets**: who leaves with whom, who receives them — agreed in advance.
6. **The high-risk period after leaving**: a stretch of time after separation is a risk window (P5) — change daily routes, tell trusted people, keep channels open.

### 4.3 The help-channel card (China context · numbers defer to the latest official publication)

| Channel | Number / how | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency police | **110** | Stopping violence on scene, dispatch records, the entry point for written-warning applications; first choice in emergencies |
| Women's Federation rights-protection hotline | **12338** | Domestic-violence complaint intake, consultation, coordination (women's federation organizations took in 252,000+ domestic-violence complaint instances in 2016–2022 via 12338 and similar channels — Beijing People's Congress published framing) |
| Public legal services hotline | **12348** | The legal aid entry point: application conditions, lawyer matching (people in financial hardship may apply for free legal aid) |
| Personal safety protection order | Apply to the **people's court** | Anti-Domestic Violence Law Article 23: an application is available upon suffering domestic violence **or facing real and present danger**, and is **not conditional on having filed for divorce**; it may prohibit violence, prohibit harassment / stalking / contact, and order the violent party to vacate the residence (Article 29 measures face); violating the protection order can bring fines, detention, up to criminal liability |
| Temporary shelter | **Temporary shelter sites** set up by county-level / municipal-level governments or based on assistance-management institutions; women's federation organizations | Temporary living assistance for emergency refuge |
| Grassroots organizations | Residents' / villagers' committees, one's employer | The duty-bearers for dissuasion and mediation under the Anti-Domestic Violence Law |
| Medical care and injury documentation | Seek care + tell the doctor the cause of injury | Injury records (connects to the red-flag emergency line) |

> **"Facing real danger" is enough to apply for a protection order** — no threshold of "already beaten to some degree"; this is the door the law leaves open for seeking help early.
> Channel numbers and service hours may change: **defer to the latest official publication** (checked at this canon's writing time: 110 / 12338 / 12348 are all publicly standard numbers).

### 4.4 What friends can do: "I'm here" works better than "you should leave" (FACT→INFERENCE · research face)

Social-reactions research (e.g., studies of IPV survivors' disclosure reactions, PMC8378660 et al.): listening, soothing, empathic **positive social reactions** are associated with better psychological outcomes and with willingness to seek help later; doubting, blaming, pressuring reactions run the other way.

The friend's support ladder (INFERENCE · operationalized from the research face):
1. **Catch first, don't judge**: "I believe you" · "this is not your fault" · "thank you for telling me" — these three sentences are the best-supported openers on the research face.
2. **Do not order them to leave**: "leave now / if you don't, it's on you" = countering control with control, and when the other person is not ready it cuts the only channel of trust. **Staying connected protects a person better than demanding action** — a disconnected victim stands closer to danger.
3. **Offer concrete options, not decisions**: hand over the §4.3 channel card; ask "would you like me to sit with you while you make this call?"
4. **Help lower the engineering cost of leaving**: keep a preparedness bag on their behalf, offer an emergency place to stay, give a ride once.
5. **Be present on a schedule**: agree on a fixed check-in (even an emoji as a code word); raise the check-in frequency in high-risk periods (after arguments, after a breakup is proposed).
6. **Recognize and escalate**: if an extreme red flag appears (especially strangulation history, weapons, threats to kill), directly encourage and assist contacting 110 / a professional danger assessment; upon discovering child victimization, note that although the §0.3 mandatory-reporting duty-bearers are institution staff, an ordinary citizen reporting to the police is equally lawful and proper.
7. **Take care of yourself too**: a supporter can also carry vicarious stress; seek professional support for yourself when needed.
