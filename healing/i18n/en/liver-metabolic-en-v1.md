# Liver & Metabolic Health Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: the liver is the body's silent chemical plant — it never cries out in pain, but it keeps a ledger of every entry: fat, alcohol, viruses, drugs. The good news: before the ledger turns into a dead account (cirrhosis, liver cancer), most entries still have a chance of being turned around.

---

## ① The master-ledger face: reading your liver

### 1.1 Why the liver is a "silent organ"

- The liver parenchyma (the part that does the work) **has no pain-sensing nerve supply**; pain comes mainly from the liver capsule on the surface (anatomy atlas not yet built · anchor repaired 2026-09-13 · original id in the receipt) and the surrounding peritoneum. Only when an enlarged liver stretches the capsule, or inflammation involves the capsule and peritoneum, does "liver-area pain" appear — this is **the mechanistic explanation of "painful only when it is already late"**: by the time pain can be felt, the problem has usually grown big enough to push against the liver's "shell" (FACT · anatomy-physiology textbook level; the mechanism is an anatomical fact).
- The liver's compensatory and regenerative capacity is enormous: after partial hepatectomy it regenerates, and the remaining tissue maintains basic function. The price is — **before lab values turn even mildly abnormal you may feel nothing at all** (FACT · physiology).
- Corollary (MODEL · clinical consensus): "no symptoms" ≠ "liver is fine". For high-risk groups, regular testing is the only reliable sensory channel.

### 1.2 Liver function tests, in plain words

When you get a liver panel, read it by "three classes of function," not by staring at the arrows:

| Marker | Plain reading | What elevation suggests |
|---|---|---|
| ALT (alanine aminotransferase) | The "workhorse enzyme" inside hepatocytes; leaks into blood only when cell membranes break | The **most direct signal of hepatocyte injury** (FACT · clinical biochemistry) |
| AST (aspartate aminotransferase) | Also present in heart muscle and skeletal muscle | Liver injury + other sources |
| AST/ALT ratio (De Ritis ratio) | The ratio of the two | >2 often suggests **alcoholic** liver injury or advancing cirrhosis (MODEL · clinical regularity; interpret together with drinking history) |
| Bilirubin (total/direct/indirect) | The "garbage-clearance index" of red-cell metabolic products | Elevated = a problem in some step of uptake/metabolism/excretion; marked elevation shows as jaundice (FACT) |
| GGT (γ-glutamyl transferase) + ALP | The "biliary system" enzymes | Bile-duct obstruction/cholestasis; GGT is also alcohol-sensitive but **not specific** — high GGT ≠ alcoholic liver disease (FACT) |
| Albumin, prothrombin time (PT) | The liver's "production capacity" | Falling/prolonged = poorer synthetic reserve; among **the hardest indicators of disease severity** (FACT) |

**Correct layering**: enzyme elevation only says "hepatocytes are injured" — it does not name the cause, still less directly measure severity; synthetic function (albumin/PT) plus imaging/elastography are the judges of severity (MODEL · clinical consensus).

### 1.3 "Elevated transaminases = hepatitis?" — the correct layering

Elevated transaminases = evidence of hepatocyte injury exists. The necessary next step is **etiologic layering**, not an instant self-diagnosis of "hepatitis" or "nothing serious":

1. Metabolic: fatty liver disease (most common; see face ②)
2. Viral: hepatitis B, hepatitis C, etc. (see face ③)
3. Alcoholic (see face ④)
4. Drugs/supplements/herbal medicines (see face ⑤)
5. Autoimmune, inherited metabolic (relatively rare; physician workup)
6. Physiological fluctuation: strenuous exercise, late nights, transient bumps — confirm by repeat testing; a single reading must not convict (MODEL · routine clinical practice)

## ② The fatty-liver face: the most common "liver matter"

### 2.1 Naming evolution: from NAFLD to MASLD — "metabolic" is the main driver

- The old name NAFLD (nonalcoholic fatty liver disease) was defined by exclusion (only after excluding alcohol could it be called that); in 2020 an international expert panel renamed it MAFLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease), and in 2023 a multi-society Delphi consensus changed it again to **MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease)** (FACT · naming history: Hepatology 2024 multi-society Delphi consensus and related correspondence, PMID:37983810).
- The rename is not wordplay: **the diagnostic anchor moved from "how much alcohol was excluded" to "whether cardiometabolic dysfunction is present"** — an acknowledgment that fatty liver is fundamentally a metabolic disorder manifesting in the liver, same root as diabetes, obesity, and dyslipidemia (FACT · consensus definition).
- What it means for readers: fatty liver should not be treated as "an isolated disease of the liver"; it is one and the same thing as your overall metabolic condition (MODEL · supported jointly by the naming consensus and epidemiology).

### 2.2 Prevalence: roughly thirty percent, and getting younger

- A Chinese systematic review and meta-analysis (2008–2018, 392 studies, 2.055 million people): **pooled national prevalence 29.2%**, with a heavier burden in middle-aged people, men, and the northwest (FACT · Hepatology 2019, PMID:31070259, doi:10.1002/hep.30702).
- The global framing is around 30% (MODEL · consistent with multi-country epidemiology); prevalence-comparison studies under China's new diagnostic criteria (NAFLD/MAFLD/MASLD) are in progress (FACT · BMJ Open 2026, PMID:41927286 — a national comparison study by the three framings exists).
- Younger onset: prevalence is rising in children, adolescents, and young adults (MODEL · consistent across multiple epidemiological reports; exact numbers vary widely by age group and framing, so no single number is written here — UNKNOWN-grade: a unified framing for the precise "rate of rejuvenation" does not yet exist).

### 2.3 "Lean fatty liver" is real

- People of normal weight also get fatty liver (lean MASLD), and it is not a rare phenomenon: a dedicated systematic review discusses its pathophysiology, diagnostic challenges, and clinical outcomes (FACT · the phenomenon is real: Diseases 2025 review, PMID:42187885).
- Mechanism face (MODEL): relatively more visceral fat, low muscle mass (the lean phenotype of sarcopenic obesity), genetic susceptibility, insulin resistance — "lean" is only the BMI surface; metabolic health is the substance. Reported proportions are higher in Asian populations (MODEL).
- UNKNOWN: the precise proportion of lean fatty liver in the general population varies widely across studies; no single assertion is made.

### 2.4 The reversibility window: the evidence for 5–10% weight loss

- Key prospective trial (293 biopsy-confirmed NASH, 52-week lifestyle intervention, 261 paired liver biopsies): overall 25% achieved NASH resolution, 47% had lower activity scores, 19% improved fibrosis; **histologic improvement was dose-responsive to the magnitude of weight loss, with the ≥10% weight-loss group showing the highest NASH-resolution and fibrosis-improvement rates** (in the trial, NASH resolution in the ≥10% group was about 90% — this subgroup figure is widely cited by guidelines; the abstract confirms the direction and the ordering) (FACT · Vilar-Gomez 2015, Gastroenterology, PMID:25865049, doi:10.1053/j.gastro.2015.04.005).
- Plain conclusion: **early fatty liver is a "reversible ledger"** — 5–7% weight loss improves hepatic steatosis and inflammation; 10% or more aims at fibrosis improvement; exercise and diet structure (not just the scale number) both count (FACT+MODEL).
- Boundary: once obvious fibrosis/cirrhosis has developed, reversibility drops sharply — that is the economics of "find early, manage early" (MODEL).

### 2.5 The "liver detox" scam

- The liver itself is the body's detox factory (cytochrome oxidation, conjugation reactions, biliary excretion); **no entity called "accumulated liver toxins" exists that needs outside help to "flush"** (FACT · physiology). No reliable evidence shows that commercial "liver detox / liver-cleansing tea" products remove any "liver toxins"; some cleansing teas in fact contain hepatotoxic ingredients (linked to the herbal-injury face ⑤) (FACT · absence of evidence + known counter-examples).
- Falsification clause: whoever claims a product can "detox the liver" — ask them for a measurable definition of "liver toxins" and a controlled trial. None exists today.

### 2.6 Fructose and sugar-sweetened beverages: the dose evidence

- Mechanism (FACT · metabolic physiology): dietary fructose is almost entirely metabolized by the liver on first pass; in excess it promotes hepatic **de novo lipogenesis (DNL)** and raises intrahepatic triglycerides.
- Epidemiology (MODEL · direction-level): sugar-sweetened beverage intake is positively associated with liver fat, transaminases, and MASLD risk; large cohorts have also reported exploratory associations between such intake and liver-cancer risk (FACT · such research exists: JAMA Network Open 2026, PMID:42268607 — association ≠ causation; direction-level evidence).
- Intervention (MODEL): swapping sugar-sweetened beverages for water/sugar-free alternatives can lower intrahepatic fat; the dose-response direction is consistent overall: **the more you drink, the fattier the liver**.
- UNKNOWN: individual variation in any fructose "safe threshold" is large; no agreed single number exists; the pragmatic advice is: the fewer sugar-sweetened beverages the better (MODEL · aligned with the WHO free-sugar limitation direction).

## ③ The hepatitis B face: huge base, heavily misunderstood, but preventable and controllable

### 3.1 Three transmission routes; everyday contact does not transmit (the scientific basis of anti-discrimination)

- The hepatitis B virus (HBV) transmits via **blood, mother-to-child, and sexual contact** (FACT · textbook level).
- It does **not** transmit through sharing meals, hugging, handshakes, coughing, sharing office equipment, or mosquito bites (FACT). In the vaccine era, this clarification is the scientific basis for anti-discrimination in employment, schooling, and marriage: everyday shared life poses no transmission risk.
- Marriage and childbearing: people living with HBV can have healthy infants through proper mother-to-child blockade (see 3.4); a vaccinated spouse acquires protection (FACT · standard immunoprophylaxis logic).

### 3.2 Carrier ≠ disease: the logic of regular monitoring

- The spectrum of outcomes after HBV infection is wide: from the immune-tolerant "carrier" state (virus present, liver not obviously injured) to active hepatitis, liver fibrosis, cirrhosis — **carrier ≠ liver disease, and even less = liver cancer**; but the contest between virus and immunity inside a carrier is a lifelong dynamic (FACT · natural history).
- What to check in the "quiet phase," and how often (direction-level; specifics defer to your doctor; MODEL · consensus direction of national guidelines):
  - Every 6–12 months: liver function (ALT), HBV DNA, HBsAg/HBeAg status assessment;
  - With liver fibrosis or high-risk features (family liver-cancer history, male sex, older age, heavy drinking): **ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months** (see face ⑥);
  - Worsening fatigue, yellow eyes/urine, increasing abdominal girth — seek care promptly; do not wait for the scheduled review day.
- Core logic: monitoring is not "waiting to get sick" — it is **catching the signal first-hand at three junctures: the virus turning active, fibrosis progressing, or a tumor appearing early** (MODEL · monitoring teleology).

### 3.3 The evidence face: antiviral therapy lowers liver-cancer risk

- Systematic review + meta-analysis (61 publications, 50 studies, non-cirrhotic chronic hepatitis B populations, treatment median/mean ≥3 years): **cumulative liver-cancer incidence in the antiviral group fell 40% versus the untreated group (IRR 0.59, 95% CI 0.50–0.69)** (FACT · BMC Gastroenterology 2026, PMID:42363070).
- Context: about 300 million people live with chronic hepatitis B worldwide (FACT · same abstract framing), making it one of the leading causes of liver cancer; guideline antiviral therapy (e.g., entecavir-class / tenofovir-class · both original drug anchors repaired 2026-09-13 · drug volume cluster not yet built · original ids in the receipt) both suppresses the virus and lowers liver-cancer incidence.
- What it means for carriers (MODEL · clinical consensus direction): whether to treat and when to start is the doctor's integrated judgment based on transaminases, viral load, degree of fibrosis, age, and family history — but the two folk claims "antivirals = lifelong poison, don't touch" and "no symptoms, no need to check" are both refuted by the evidence.

### 3.4 The success ledger of vaccination and mother-to-child blockade

- National seroepidemiological survey (14 years after vaccine inclusion in planned immunization): **HBsAg carriage 7.2% in ages 1–59; only 1.0% in children under 5 (down about 90% from 1992); universal infant vaccination cumulatively averted about 16–20 million HBV infections** (FACT · Vaccine 2009, PMID:19729084, doi:10.1016/j.vaccine.2009.08.048).
- Standard mother-to-child blockade package: hepatitis B immune globulin (HBIG) within 12 hours of birth + first dose of hepatitis B vaccine (vax:china_nip_children_free · repaired 2026-09-13), then completion of the 0-1-6 schedule; antivirals in late pregnancy for high-viral-load mothers further suppress mother-to-child transmission (FACT · standard immune-blockade scheme; Chinese real-world multi-measure blockade studies continue to validate effectiveness: BMC Infect Dis 2025, PMID:41495701).
- The meaning of this "success ledger": hepatitis B is a disease **this generation can end at the source** — universal newborn vaccination is pushing carriage toward zero for the next generation (MODEL · trend judgment).

### 3.5 The comorbidity face: hepatitis B plus fatty liver

- Chinese systematic review: fatty liver accompanying chronic hepatitis B is quite common (FACT · a national meta-analysis exists: BMC Public Health 2025, PMID:41392138). Comorbidity management points (MODEL): antiviral and metabolic management proceed in parallel; fatty liver must not be ignored on the grounds "I have hepatitis B," and vice versa.

## ④ The alcohol face: the dose-response, told as it is

### 4.1 "Moderate drinking protects the liver" does not hold

- Large-scale evidence compilation (83 prospective studies, about 600,000 drinkers, 195 countries, GBD framing): **the cluster of lowest all-cause risk is non-drinking or minimal drinking**; the earlier J-shaped "small amounts benefit" curve is substantially weakened after bias correction (especially "former drinkers" mixed into the abstainer group) (FACT · Lancet 2018 "Risk thresholds for alcohol consumption", PMID:30496095 et al.).
- Liver specificity: the risk of alcoholic liver disease shows a **dose-response relationship** with drinking amount and years of drinking, and individual variation is large — at the same intake, some people fall first (FACT · the dose-response exists; individual thresholds differ markedly; there is no liver-protective "safe" drinking level for everyone).
- Concluding sentence: there is no medical reason to drink "for the liver's sake"; "moderation" is only a scale marking how large the risk is, not an immunity certificate (MODEL · clinical translation of the two points above).

### 4.2 Drinking flush = "can hold liquor"? — the ALDH2-deficiency face

- Roughly 30–50% of East Asian populations carry **aldehyde dehydrogenase (ALDH2) deficiency variants**: acetaldehyde from alcohol metabolism accumulates in the body → flushing, palpitations. Acetaldehyde is a confirmed carcinogen (FACT · genetic metabolism + carcinogen classification).
- Meta-analysis + Mendelian randomization of ALDH2/ADH1B polymorphisms and esophageal squamous-cell carcinoma risk: deficient-variant carriers who drink have significantly elevated esophageal-cancer risk (gene-environment interaction) (FACT · Cancer Medicine 2023, PMID:37795758).
- Correct reading: **the flush is a signal light of metabolic deficiency — a red hint to drink even less, not proof of good tolerance**; "training your tolerance" trains the feeling of tolerance, not metabolic capacity (FACT+MODEL). This item is also a member of face ⑦'s falsification list.

### 4.3 After quitting alcohol: the reversible and the irreversible

- Pure alcoholic fatty liver: mostly clearly reversible within weeks to months of abstinence (FACT · clinical consensus).
- Established fibrosis/cirrhosis: quitting can stop (or greatly slow) progression and improve prognosis, but **structural fibrosis/cirrhosis is usually not fully reversible**; after decompensation events (ascites, bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy) the room for reversal is even smaller (FACT+MODEL · consensus direction of clinical natural history).
- Synergy of alcohol with virus/metabolism: hepatitis B + drinking, obesity + drinking show additive or even multiplicative effects on liver-cancer and cirrhosis risk (MODEL · consistent epidemiological direction).

## ⑤ The drug-induced liver injury face: the liver's "workplace injury" ledger

### 5.1 Paracetamol: the leading cause of acute liver failure; "stacking" is the keyword

- US multicenter prospective study (22 tertiary hospitals, 662 acute liver failure cases, 6 years): **42% (275/662) were caused by paracetamol (acetaminophen)**; the share rose from 28% in 1998 to 51% in 2003; median intake 24 g (≈48 extra-strength tablets); **unintentional overdose accounted for 48%**, of which 38% had concurrently taken 2 or more paracetamol-containing preparations and 63% used narcotic-containing combinations (FACT · Larson 2005, Hepatology, PMID:16317692, doi:10.1002/hep.20948).
- **The real-life scene, in plain words**: a cold treated with "combination cold medicine A + combination cold medicine B + a fever reducer," while many combination cold medicines contain paracetamol — each taken "as labeled," together an overdose. This is the classic path from unintentional overdose to acute liver failure (FACT · the 38% dual-preparation figure above is precisely its epidemiological portrait).
- Pragmatic rule (MODEL · routine clinical direction): at usual therapeutic doses paracetamol has a good safety profile for adults, but **on any single day use only one medicine containing the ingredient, read ingredient lists carefully, and do not take a fever reducer after heavy drinking on an empty stomach** (alcohol synergy + glycogen depletion worsen toxicity); dose red lines (must-go-to-hospital) and individualized adjustment belong to your doctor.

### 5.2 Herbal liver injury: truthful, separated, non-stigmatizing

- He shou wu (何首乌, and its Chinese patent-medicine preparations · original drug anchor repaired 2026-09-13 · drug volume cluster not yet built · original id in the receipt) is an **internationally recognized hepatotoxic herb**: recent pharmacological studies specifically examine "processing (steaming) for attenuated toxicity" — the premise of that detoxification research is precisely that its toxicity has been confirmed (FACT · J Ethnopharmacol 2027 PMID:42532227; 2026 PMID:42341648 processing-dependent liver risk and quality-control markers). Other known hepatotoxic Chinese herbs include Tripterygium wilfordii (lei gong teng) and tusanqi (土三七, containing pyrrolizidine alkaloids) (FACT · a known list exists; individual mechanisms are mostly idiosyncratic, related to dose and constitution).
- [TCM·Traditional] kept separate from the evidence face: the Chinese medical classics hold traditional indications and toxicity-attenuating processing methods for the herbs above — this belongs to **traditional medication experience**; it is not modern safety evidence. "Follow the physician's instructions, control the dose, limit the treatment course, use properly processed products" is the common requirement of traditional wisdom and modern safety; self-directed long-term steeping in water or swallowing raw powder is risk behavior (the [TCM·Traditional] face and the evidence face are stated separately, as above).
- Background (MODEL · direction-level): in Chinese drug-induced liver injury (DILI) registry studies, herbs and dietary supplements account for a considerable share — the exact share varies by study framing; no single number is written here (UNKNOWN-grade: the precise share awaits verification against the original Chinese DILI registry publications by a later lane).

### 5.3 Supplements and weight-loss products

- "Natural = harmless" does not hold: herbal ≠ safe; the previous section is the counter-example (see face ⑦ falsification list).
- Additional risks of weight-loss/fitness products: historically, multiple "purely botanical" weight-loss/muscle-gain supplements were found to contain **undeclared Western drug ingredients** (e.g., sibutramine-class), with uncontrolled doses → uncontrolled liver-injury risk (MODEL · consistent direction of regulatory notices and literature cases; mainly case-level; systematic incidence UNKNOWN).
- Pragmatic rule: for any "miracle slimming/detox/liver-protecting" product, first check for a regular approval number and an ingredient list; unverifiable ingredients = unassessable liver-injury risk = do not ingest (MODEL · risk-minimization principle).

## ⑥ The liver-cancer red-flag chapter

### 6.1 The high-risk face and the logic of regular screening

- High-risk portrait (FACT · etiology consensus): people infected with hepatitis B/C, cirrhosis of any cause, long-term heavy drinking, aflatoxin exposure (moldy grain), family history of liver cancer; the share of liver cancer related to metabolic syndrome/fatty liver is rising (MODEL · trend).
- Screening rhythm (direction-level; your doctor prevails; MODEL · consensus direction of domestic and international guidelines: AGA Clinical Practice Update 2026, PMID:42701973 and aligned sources): **high-risk individuals: liver ultrasound ± alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) every 6 months**. Why 6 months: the epidemiology of liver-cancer doubling time shows a 6-month interval catches most fast-growing lesions within the "early treatable window" (MODEL · screening-interval rationale).
- The China face: a large hepatitis B base (see face ③) → a heavy liver-cancer burden; for carriers, "antivirals + regular screening" is the combination that presses liver-cancer risk down with both hands at once (FACT+MODEL).

### 6.2 "Liver-area pain + weight loss + jaundice" triad = seek care immediately

If any combination of the following signals appears, do not wait for the scheduled review day — seek care immediately (FACT · emergency-recognition consensus):

1. **Persistent dull/pressing pain in the liver area** (especially new-onset, not easing at night)
2. **Marked weight loss** (>5% of body weight in 1–2 months without deliberate dieting)
3. **Jaundice** (yellow whites of the eyes/yellow skin, urine dark as strong tea)

Urgent additions to the red flags: ascites (rapidly increasing abdominal girth), vomiting blood or black stools, changes in consciousness or behavior (signs of hepatic encephalopathy), unexplained low-grade fever with liver-area discomfort (MODEL · common-sense symptomatology layer).
Honest boundaries: none of these symptoms is **specific** — having them ≠ liver cancer, but having them = a doctor must see you the same day (MODEL · keeping sensitivity and specificity apart).

## ⑦ The falsification face (falsifier list)

Each item carries an actionable way to falsify. Epistemic labeling: the falsified objects are mostly folk assertions (CONTRADICTED/MISLEADING); the rebutting evidence is the FACT/MODEL of the faces above.

1. **"Liver detox / cleansing teas flush the toxins out"** — "liver toxins" have no measurable definition and the products have no controlled-trial evidence; the liver's self-detox system (face ②.5) needs no product's help. How to falsify: demand a biochemical definition of "liver toxins" + an RCT; cleansing-tea ingredients may themselves contain hepatotoxic herbs (link to face ⑤).
2. **"Normal transaminases = liver is fine"** — chronic HBV carriage, early cirrhosis, even early liver cancer can all present with normal ALT (the monitoring logic of faces ③.2/⑥.1 is built precisely on this). How to falsify: a high-risk person (hepatitis B/cirrhosis/family history) refusing ultrasound+AFP monitoring on the strength of one normal transaminase sheet can end up diagnosed only at late-stage liver cancer — guidelines require imaging surveillance of high-risk individuals regardless of enzymes.
3. **"Liver-protect pills sober you up / a thousand cups unscathed"** — the sobering-enzyme system (ADH/ALDH) runs at a genetically set rate; no OTC product substantially raises it; the placebo comfort of "liver pills for drinking" instead promotes more drinking (face ④: no safe liver-protective drinking level). How to falsify: no "sober-up/liver-protect" product can change acetaldehyde accumulation in an ALDH2-deficient person — the flush stays, the risk stays.
4. **"Fatty liver is a fat person's monopoly"** — lean MASLD is real and reported at higher proportions in Asian populations (face ②.3). How to falsify: cases and cohorts of normal-BMI people with high visceral fat/low muscle/dyslipidemia diagnosed with MASLD are abundant.
5. **"Raw fish gall clears the eyes / clears heat"** — fish gall (common in grass carp, black carp, silver carp and other freshwater fish) contains toxic substances; swallowing it can cause acute liver-kidney injury and even multi-organ dysfunction syndrome; it is a clear emergency-toxicology cause, and pediatric cases are also reported (FACT · Int J Emerg Med 2026 case + literature review PMID:41888644; pediatric case PMID:42128916). How to falsify: no evidence supports fish gall "clearing the eyes"; the counter-evidence is the fish-gall-poisoning MODS admissions that really exist in emergency departments.
6. **"Drinking flush = can hold liquor / just practice"** — flush = ALDH2 deficiency = acetaldehyde accumulation = added esophageal-cancer risk; it is the signal for "drink even less" (face ④.2). How to falsify: the genotype-esophageal-cancer meta-analysis + Mendelian randomization directly reverses the claim.
7. **"All-natural supplements are safe, no liver injury"** — "natural" herbs such as He shou wu are recognized causes of liver injury; "natural" supplements have repeatedly been found to contain undeclared Western drugs (face ⑤). How to falsify: natural ≠ safe is broken by a single counter-example — He shou wu is the internationally recognized one.
8. **"Eating with an HBV carrier will infect you"** — shared meals/everyday contact do not transmit HBV (face ③.1). How to falsify: beyond the three transmission routes (blood/mother-to-child/sexual) there is no transmission evidence; in the vaccine era there is even less reason for discrimination — the scientific clarification is the very base of anti-discrimination.
