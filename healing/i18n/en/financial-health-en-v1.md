# Financial Health Canon v1 (2026-09-12)

> **⚠️ Header declaration**: this volume is **educational information**, studying the relationship between "money and the body" from a health-medicine perspective. **It is not investment advice.** The whole volume contains zero specific investment products, zero individual stocks, zero currencies, zero yield recommendations. All amounts involved are the source literature's own US-dollar purchasing-power framing; convert to local purchasing power when reading.
> **Topic source**: entry #2 of the human-needs full-spectrum mapping compass (`docs/healing/human-needs-map-v1-20260912.md` §3-2) — financial security is a thin slot at the L2 layer, and financial stress is the number-one crowding-out source for health behaviors: the underlying amplifier of anxiety, insomnia and family conflict. That the healing universe had no face for this = one of the biggest holes in "actually solving problems".
> **Sibling alignment**: docs/healing/human-needs-map-v1-20260912.md compass entry #2 · diet/mindbody-factors M1 stress / M4 cognitive load · diet/sleep-canon · PG behavioral_economics_health (10 rows) · u26-finance (system scale).
> **One-line stance**: money is not a money matter; it is a health matter. What this volume studies is **how financial stress enters the body, how behavioral science catches it, and when to seek care** — not how to get rich.

## 1. The financial-stress → health evidence chain

### 1.1 Debt × mental health (the chain's main trunk)

- **Richardson, Elliott & Roberts 2013**, "The relationship between personal unsecured debt and mental and physical health: A systematic review and meta-analysis", *Clinical Psychology Review* 33(8):1148-1162 (PMID 24121465 · DOI 10.1016/j.cpr.2013.08.009 · Europe PMC verified cited 237+ · Google Scholar framing 800+) — **the association between unsecured debt (credit cards / consumer loans / payday loans) and mental-health outcomes is especially strong**, stronger than the association with physical health (FACT · the original conclusion sentence verified). Effect magnitude: the risk of psychological disorder in the unsecured-debt population is on the order of **2–3 times** that of the debt-free population (INFERENCE · the original's precise OR marked anchor-pending).
- **Why "unsecured"**: no collateral = no fallback; collection pressure lands directly on the person. Secured debt (mortgages) has a much weaker association with health (MODEL · directional conclusion within the same meta).
- **The dose relationship (MODEL · multi-cohort direction consistent)**: debt amount, number of debts, and count of default/collection experiences all **gradient upward** with psychological symptoms — the number of debts predicts psychological distress better than the total amount (fragmented repayment across multiple creditors = cognitive load + a wider collection front).

### 1.2 Economic strain × depression/anxiety

- Cross-sections and prospective cohorts agree: perceived financial strain's association with depressive and anxiety symptoms is robust, and **perceived strain predicts mental-health outcomes better than objective income** (MODEL · the relative-deprivation path of "earning less than the neighbors" runs parallel to the absolute-poverty path).
- **The interface meaning for the healing universe (INFERENCE)**: in the mindbody M1 stress cell's list of emotional sources, economic strain should rank first as a **chronic low-grade pressure source** — it never erupts but is online all day, precisely the typical input for HPA-axis chronicization.

### 1.3 Financial insecurity × sleep (the sleep interface)

- **Chessa et al. 2025** (SLEEP family · bibliographic-level verification): in the association between perceived financial difficulty and insomnia / poor sleep quality, **psychosocial factors (worry rumination / emotion regulation) mediate roughly 40% (insomnia) / 35% (sleep quality) of the share** — i.e., "money → can't sleep" runs mainly through the cognitive pathway of "lying in bed thinking about money" (FACT association + mediation-decomposition grade).
- Kim et al. 2025 (a Nature-family journal · bibliographic-level verification): severe financial stress and reduced sleep satisfaction and shortened sleep duration damage each other bidirectionally.
- **Action meaning (INFERENCE)**: the remedy ordering for financial insomnia is ① write the worries into a paper list (externalization · cuts rumination) ② automated rules (see §3 card 2) ③ sleep-hygiene routine (`diet/sleep-canon`). A direct "cheer up" does nothing — in the tunneling state the bandwidth is already occupied (§2.1).

### 1.4 The savings buffer × health outcomes (the buffer effect)

- **The liquidity hypothesis (MODEL)**: the buffer you can actually use on hand (checking / instantly available) predicts financial stress and mental health better than total assets (including property and pensions) — **the pressure comes from "what if something happens next week", not from net worth**.
- "Could you come up with a small sum of cash for an emergency" (the literature's classic question: could you assemble $2,000 for an emergency) is a stronger predictor of psychological distress than income (INFERENCE · magnitude framing · the original's precise effect anchor-pending).
- **This is why the action card puts the buffer first (§3 card 1)**: the buffer directly cuts the stress sources of 1.1–1.3, while investment returns contribute almost nothing to the stress pathway.

### 1.5 The pay-cycle physiology face (the austere period and the arrival period)

- **Evans & Moore 2011**, "The Short-Term Mortality Consequences of Income Receipt", *Quarterly Journal of Economics* — **short-term mortality rises just after income arrives** (death peaks near benefit/wage payment days: acute effects of vigorous activity, consumption behavior, and changed medication patterns; bibliographic record and conclusion sentence verified).
- Health-behavior translation (INFERENCE): at the pinched end of the month (the austere period) diet / sleep / medication adherence slide, and on payment-arrival day behavior rebounds (binge drinking / binge eating / spending impulses) — **the cash-flow cycle is itself a physiological cycle**. Action meaning: schedule chronic-disease medication refills and follow-up visits within 3 days after payday where possible (aligning adherence with cash flow · INFERENCE-grade micro-intervention).

## 2. The decision-science face (behavioral economics · FACT/MODEL layered)

### 2.1 The scarcity mindset's tunneling (scarcity captures attention)

- Mullainathan & Shafir, *Scarcity: Why Having Too Little Means So Much* (2013, Times Books): scarcity (money / time / social connection) captures attention and produces **tunneling** — seeing only the most urgent thing in front of you, systematically neglecting the important-but-not-urgent (checkups / repayment dates / insurance), and levying a **bandwidth tax** (cognitive bandwidth declines).
- Epistemic state marked honestly: scarcity changes behavior = **MODEL grade** (multiple experiments in the book + field studies consistent); precise quantifications of the form "lacking money lowers IQ by X points" (Mani et al. 2013 Science sugarcane-farmer study) **carry a replication controversy** — the quantification face is CONTESTED, the direction face robust (INFERENCE).
- **Health meaning**: in the tunneling state, reasoning is useless — **explaining long-term planning to someone inside the tunnel = teaching swimming strokes to someone drowning**. The correct order: first hold them up with structural design (automation / checklists / default options), then talk planning (the theoretical basis of §3 card 2).

### 2.2 Loss aversion's distortion of financial decisions

- The pain of a loss is on the order of 2 times the pleasure of an equal gain (Kahneman-Tversky prospect theory · MODEL).
- Three health-relevant distortions (MODEL): ① the **disposition effect** — unable to hold winning positions, clutching losing ones (selling at a loss = admitting the loss = pain); ② **loss-chasing (chasing)** — immediately betting more to win it back after a loss, isomorphic with the core behavior of gambling disorder (§4.1); ③ **mental accounting** — year-end bonuses / red envelopes go into the "can be splurged" account — automated saving's true opponent is exactly this.

### 2.3 Anchoring

- Entry-price anchoring ("I'll sell once it falls back to my cost basis" — the market does not know your cost basis); installment plans split a large sum into small anchors of "just one coffee a day" (payment pain split apart · INFERENCE).
- **The anti-scam interface**: scams use high anchors ("the neighbor doubled his money") to reset your reference point of expectation (§4.3).

### 2.4 The "money-and-happiness curve" — a textbook-grade case of evidence evolution

Three acts of evidence evolution (this section doubles as a teaching case of "how science self-corrects"):

1. **Act one · Kahneman & Deaton 2010** (PNAS 107:16489-16493 · title/volume/page verified): a cross-section of about 450,000 Americans — **life evaluation keeps rising with income; emotional well-being flattens after annual income reaches about $75,000**. The origin of the pop slogan "money can't buy happiness" (the original conclusion is more precise than the slogan).
2. **Act two · Killingsworth 2021** (PNAS · experience sampling · 30,000+ sample · real-time smartphone sampling): well-being **keeps rising with income, with no plateau at $75k** — in direct conflict with 2010 (recorded honestly: a CONTRADICTED state appeared; both entries retained).
3. **Act three · the adversarial collaboration · Killingsworth, Kahneman & Mellers 2023** (PNAS · "Income and emotional well-being: A conflict resolved" · cited 329 · conclusion sentence verified): the two parties + an arbiter re-analyzed Killingsworth's data — **the merged conclusion: for the least happy minority (roughly 15–20% of people), well-being rises with income up to about $100k and then flattens; for the remaining majority it keeps rising**. Kahneman conceded that the correct 2010 statement was "**unhappiness** stops decreasing beyond an income point", not "happiness stops increasing".
4. **The health-medicine translation (MODEL)**: money's effect on emotion is **asymmetric** — for the distressed / high-pressure population (the least happy minority), rising income is a potent "painkiller" up to a threshold; past the threshold, earning more contributes almost nothing to emotion. **The marginal happiness return of escaping poverty is the largest; the marginal happiness return of sudden wealth approaches zero.** For the healing universe: the priority audience for financial-face work is the stressed-end population, not the incremental-end population.

## 3. The action-card face (this canon's soul · evidence-based "how to do it")

> Each card has four cells: evidence tier / how / dose / boundary and counter-evidence. All amounts are literature framing or proportion framing — **not investment advice, no product recommendations of any kind**.

### Card 1. The emergency buffer takes priority over investing

- **Evidence tier**: liquidity predicts financial stress better than net worth (MODEL · §1.4); the buffer directly lowers the trigger probability of the debt-to-mind chain (INFERENCE).
- **How**: first goal a **starter buffer** (1 month of fixed expenses or a minimum viable amount); second goal **3–6 months of fixed expenses**. The 3–6 months figure is consumer-finance practice convention (INFERENCE · convention tier · not medical evidence); the starter buffer is the hardest-evidence step (even a small buffer significantly cuts the small-emergency → high-interest-debt conversion chain · INFERENCE).
- **Dose**: automated monthly transfers feed the buffer first (on payday · card 2); only after the starter buffer is full do debt repayment and long-term saving run in parallel.
- **Boundary**: a buffer that is too thick (>12 months) is the scarcity mindset run in reverse (loss aversion spilling over · INFERENCE) — the buffer is a pressure valve, not the goal itself; inflation erodes idle cash, so stop once full and plan separately for the excess (not specifying any destination for it = within this canon's boundary).

### Card 2. Automated saving (willpower outsourcing)

- **Evidence tier**: **one of behavioral science's most robust conclusion faces** — default options + commitment mechanisms > willpower. Thaler & Benartzi 2004, "Save More Tomorrow", JPE 112(S1):S164-S187 (cited 4400+ · 78% participation rate · the four-segment verification sentences checked): employees **commit a portion of future raises to savings**; average savings rates rose from 3.5% to 13.6% after participation (FACT · original numbers framing).
- **How**: on payday **itself**, transfer out automatically (to buffer / debt / fixed savings) — move first, spend after; start from an amount "too small to hurt" (e.g., 5% of income), and auto-escalate half a step with each raise (a personalized transplant of the SMaT mechanism · INFERENCE).
- **Dose**: set once, review every 6 months — automation is itself the counter to tunneling (§2.1: the person in the tunnel gets caught by structure whether or not they rely on willpower).
- **Boundary**: automation does not handle the structural deficit of income < expenses (that is an income/debt-restructuring question · UNKNOWN, beyond this volume); it does not substitute for medical care or psychotherapy.

### Card 3. The high-interest debt snowball priority

- **Evidence tier**: both strategies carry evidence. **Mathematically optimal = the avalanche method** (repay the highest interest rate first · maximum interest saved · arithmetic FACT); **behaviorally optimal = the snowball method** (repay the smallest first · rapidly clear single debts): Gal & McShane (Journal of Marketing Research · debt-management field data) empirically showed that the "small victory" of **closing a single account significantly raises subsequent repayment persistence** (bibliographic record and conclusion verified) — the self-efficacy mechanism (the mirror of §1.1's debt-count × psychological distress: one fewer debt = one smaller collection front + one notch more efficacy).
- **How**: list everything (creditor / rate / minimum payment); if the rate gap is wide (e.g., >10 percentage points) choose avalanche; if rates are similar or you have repeatedly given up, choose snowball — **a sustainable second-best beats an unsustainable best** (MODEL · this card's verdict sentence).
- **Dose**: pay every account's minimum first (prevent default), then put all attack ammunition on the target debt; clear one, and roll its monthly payment into the next (the snowball rolls).
- **Boundary**: verify the credentials of any third-party "debt optimization" service first (§4.3 scam hooks); being harassed by collectors / sued / borrowing from one lender to pay another = beyond self-help scope, go through legal and professional counseling channels (red-line list §4.2).

### Card 4. Money communication and reducing family conflict

- **Evidence tier**: Dew, Britt & Huston 2012 (*Journal of Family Issues* · cited 430+ · conclusion sentence verified): **financial disagreements are one of the strongest predictors of divorce, stronger than other common marital disagreements** (FACT association grade).
- **How**: the **money meeting** — once a month, fixed, 30 minutes, three agenda items (reconcile last month's out-of-budget spending / plan next month's big expenses / each states one money-stress point); the rule = **reconcile the books, never assign blame** (blame activates loss aversion and defense · INFERENCE); each person's "stress point" must be heard, not adjudicated.
- **Dose**: monthly once beats a single marathon deep-talk (conflict intensity correlates with accumulated un-talked-about time · INFERENCE); amount transparency starts from "big items mutually disclosed"; one-step full transparency is not required.
- **Boundary**: controlling finance showing up in the meeting (one party in total control / signs of economic abuse) = a relationship-violence red flag, beyond this card (see human-needs L3-36 relationship-violence blank slot · UNKNOWN, face not yet built).

## 4. The risk red-line face

### 4.1 Gambling / speculation addiction (the behavioral-addiction ICD-11 face)

- ICD-11 has the category "disorders due to addictive behaviors", with **gambling disorder** as one of them (the other being gaming disorder) — behavioral addictions are classified alongside substance addictions (FACT · WHO ICD-11 · Brand et al. 2020/2023 review bibliographic records verified).
- **The health boundary with investing (MODEL · mechanistic-structure criterion)**: leveraged trading / contracts / high-frequency short-term trading share gambling's behavioral structure — **betting more after losses to win it back (chasing) + a fast payout rhythm + tunnel-visioned screen-watching**. Once that structure appears, it enters addictive-spectrum evaluation, regardless of what the underlying asset is.
- **The 4-question self-screen (a CAGE-style variant · INFERENCE-grade screening heuristic · not a diagnosis)**: ① Do you trade/bet hiding it from your family? ② Do you trade with borrowed money or money meant for living? ③ Do you immediately bet more to win it back after a loss? ④ Do you get restless and irritable when not watching the market? — **≥2 items = stop immediately and seek help** (specialty clinic / crisis line / mutual-aid group channels); this is a care-seeking signal, not a willpower problem.
- **Red line**: borrowing to gamble / diverting the family emergency fund = past the line; follow §4.2 for help; no self-help.

### 4.2 The boundary of "medicalizing financial anxiety" (the care-seeking / help-seeking red-line list)

Financial stress is a **trigger**, not a **diagnosis**; the money problem and the mood problem must be handled in parallel — solving the money does not dissolve a depression that has already formed (INFERENCE). Any of the following = a care-seeking / help-seeking signal, **no longer in the realm of "cheer up" or "go make money first"**:

| # | Red line | Action |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | low mood / loss of interest lasting ≥2 weeks and affecting work and life | psychiatric / psychological outpatient evaluation (the depression-screening line) |
| R2 | insomnia >3 weeks or frequent all-night rumination | seek care + run this volume's §1.3 externalization in parallel |
| R3 | recurrent palpitations / chest tightness / panic attacks | rule out physical illness first (cardiology), then attribute psychologically |
| R4 | thoughts like "if I were dead I wouldn't have to repay", or any self-harm ideation | **immediate** help (crisis hotline / emergency department) — the highest red line; this thought is state-dependent and will be relieved by treatment |
| R5 | gambling/trading behavior out of control (§4.1 ≥2 items) or borrowing from one lender to pay another | behavioral-addiction specialty / debt legal counseling in parallel |
| R6 | avoiding all calls and email because of collections, social-function withdrawal | legal counseling + psychological support in parallel (collection has legal boundaries) |

### 4.3 The investment-scam psychological-hook checklist (anti-scam = protecting health)

A scam injures more than money — post-fraud shame, self-blame and family conflict are severe psychological events (INFERENCE · the mechanism behind the commonly delayed crime reporting). **The eight-hook list** (FACT at the structure layer · each a concretization of a known behavioral-science effect):

1. **Guaranteed principal with high returns** — promising "sure profit, no loss" + abnormal returns = red flag number one (risk and return are inseparable · arithmetic grade).
2. **Scarcity urgency** — "last spots / limited window / closes tonight": urgency drives even tunneling-free normal people into the tunnel (§2.1).
3. **Social proof** — screenshots of wins in the group, friends recruiting friends, shills testifying (loss aversion's mirror: others are winning = my loss).
4. **Authority disguise** — fake licenses / impersonated institutions / fake expert backgrounds (verify only on the regulator's own site · never via links the other party provides).
5. **Sunk-cost averaging-down** — "already down, add more to break even": the self-trap shared by scams and losing positions.
6. **The FOMO structure** — "miss it now and it's gone": manufacturing reference-point deprivation (§2.3 anchoring).
7. **Inside information** — real inside information is a crime; the legal world has no free inside information.
8. **Unverifiable complexity** — a mechanism you can't understand = a money trail you can't see ("I don't understand it but it's amazing" is precisely the evacuation signal).

**The verdict rule (protective heuristic · INFERENCE)**: any opportunity hitting ≥2 hooks simultaneously = abandon directly — no research, no small test, no "just play with a little" (the small test is precisely the entry point of hooks 3–5).

## 5. The falsification face (five pieces of internet money-chicken soup sampled)

> Falsification standard: give an applicable boundary or a counter-example and the claim is "downgraded to a conditional statement"; anything without an operable definition and a falsifier = does not enter the evidence face.

1. **"The compound-interest myth: save X a month, and in 30 years you're a multimillionaire"** — downgraded. Applicable boundaries: ① nominal return ≠ real return (inflation erosion · over a 30-year horizon the difference is enormous); ② returns are not guaranteed (sequence risk · the market's long-run return equals no individual's smoothed 30-year return); ③ the model builds in no income interruption (unemployment / illness — precisely the normal state of the §1 population); ④ no fees or taxes deducted. **The long-termism direction is FACT; the precise numbers are mostly an illusion for any individual (MODEL→CONTESTED)**.
2. **"Consumption downgrading is universal medicine"** — downgraded. Counter-example: extreme cuts → life satisfaction plummets → revenge rebound (isomorphic with the dieting–bingeing cycle · INFERENCE); social spending zeroed out → loneliness rises → health wear (the mindbody M3 backlash). A sustainable cash-flow improvement beats one-off extreme austerity; what should be cut is "unfelt spending", not "nourishing spending".
3. **"Fix your mindset before you fix your money"-class empty talk** — does not enter the evidence face. No operable definition, no dose, no falsifier — unfalsifiable = unusable. The falsifiable replacement: "build the automation and buffer structures first (§3 cards 1/2); only then does cognitive upgrading have an execution vehicle" — a testable action sequence, not a slogan.
4. **"Money is earned, not saved"** — downgraded to a conditional statement. Partially true for high-marginal-income people (time value > the savings); for most households, **losing control of cash flow is the entrance to high-interest debt** (§1.1, the starting point of the unsecured-debt × mental-health evidence chain) — saving and earning are not a binary opposition; flow conservation needs both ends (CONTESTED → conditionalized).
5. **"The law of attraction / manifesting wealth"** — does not enter the evidence face and does active harm: it suppresses anxiety as "negative energy", when anxiety is the action trigger (§1.3: externalizing the rumination is the real answer). The correct path: convert anxiety into automated rules and a paper list (tunneling's remedy is structure and externalization, not calmly waiting for money · INFERENCE).

1. This volume is educational information, not investment advice; no product / stock / currency / yield recommendations of any kind (a synonymous restatement of the header · against quote-mining).
2. Precise effect sizes are mostly magnitude-framed (INFERENCE): Richardson 2013's precise OR, the $2,000 emergency-question effect size — marked **anchor-pending verification**; verified against the original text they are promoted, falsified they are changed.
3. Dollar amounts are literature framing; purchasing power must be converted to the local era and place; the $75k/$100k thresholds are 2010–2023 US purchasing power.
4. **Cultural boundary (UNKNOWN)**: the evidence body comes from the anglophone West; the financial-stress structure of intra-family transfers, intergenerational mutual aid and mortgage culture in a Chinese context is not adequately covered — downgrade one grade when citing.
5. Falsifier list: ① any high-quality meta showing "debt × mental health: no association or negative" → §1.1 collapses; ② tunneling's direction face falsified (scarcity does not change decision structure) → §2.1 / card 2's theoretical basis collapses; ③ structured money-communication formats of the money-meeting type increasing conflict in RCTs → card 4 withdrawn. Record on discovery and issue a v2.
6. This volume does not cover: insurance allocation, pension systems, tax, inheritance — all adjacent faces (human-needs L2-24 insurance blank slot, not yet built).
