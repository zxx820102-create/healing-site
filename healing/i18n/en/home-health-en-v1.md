## 1. The air face (highest leverage)

### 1.1 Indoor PM2.5: sources, and the purification-vs-ventilation trade-off

**The sources face (MODEL · source composition varies household to household · prefer an empty cell to an invented one)**:
- Cooking (especially pan-frying and deep-frying — a single cooking session can spike indoor PM2.5 by 1–2 orders of magnitude short-term · many measurement papers; the magnitude = INFERENCE);
- Gas-stove combustion by-products (NO2 / particulates — see §1.4);
- Outdoor infiltration (seeping through building gaps in smog season · outdoor concentration is a main driver of indoor · MODEL);
- Smoking (the strongest single point source of indoor PM2.5 · quitting = the first action · the direction is undisputed);
- Human-raised dust / bedding / pets (mostly coarse particles · the PM10 face).

**The purification face: HEPA is the most validated technology (FACT · definition grade)**:
- **HEPA definition (EPA official page · updated 2026-01 · title-level verified)**: HEPA = High Efficiency Particulate Air filter, **≥99.97% removal of the hardest-to-catch 0.3 μm particle size (the most-penetrating particle size, MPPS)** — a framing trap: 0.3 μm is not the floor of "bigger particles it blocks"; it is the filter's **weakest** point; smaller (<0.1 μm · diffusion mechanism) and larger (>1 μm · interception/inertial mechanisms) particles are captured with **higher** actual efficiency (FACT · filtration physics). So the popular claim "HEPA can't stop fine particles / viral aerosols" runs opposite to filtration physics (CONTRADICTED · as to filtration efficiency itself).
- **Measured-effect face**: portable air purifiers lowering indoor PM2.5 have measured evidence (the RCT/pilot-type hits found: McIntyre 2024, E Environ Health Insights, an environmental-justice community pilot with reduced PM exposure — PMID 38863688 · title-level). **Cardiovascular/respiratory endpoints** (blood pressure / inflammatory-marker improvements) are reported in multi-site Chinese RCTs but with small, heterogeneous effects — this lane did not verify each paper's conclusions (MODEL · not expanded). **Honest framing: the purifier's hard evidence = lowering particulates themselves (FACT); improved downstream health endpoints = MODEL/INFERENCE.**
- **Selection action card (INFERENCE · guideline-grade common knowledge)**: ① insist on a HEPA type (not "negative-ion / electrostatic precipitation / ozone" types — see §6.1/§6.5); ② match CADR to room area (US AHAM convention: CADR(cfm) ≈ room area (sq ft) × 2/3 · guideline-grade) — too small = running it for nothing; ③ replace filters on schedule (a saturated filter = rising resistance + falling efficiency).

**The ventilation face: opening windows is a double-edged sword (trade-off card · INFERENCE)**:
- Window ventilation simultaneously clears CO2 / formaldehyde / VOC accumulation (§1.2/§1.3) — **this is what a purifier cannot do** (purifiers do not remove gaseous pollutants · HEPA handles particles only · FACT · principle grade);
- Opening windows at outdoor-pollution peaks (morning/evening traffic · smog days · pollen season) invites the pollution indoors; when outdoor air is good (after rain in the early morning · midday) = the golden free-ventilation window;
- **Strategy (INFERENCE)**: run the range hood while cooking + a delay window after stir-frying; smog days = windows shut + purifier; day to day, open windows by outdoor AQI time slots — "purifier vs windows" is not either/or; it is the division of labor: **particles to the filter, gases to ventilation**.

### 1.2 CO2 and cognition: the 1000 ppm threshold, framed honestly

**Verified anchors (title-level)**:
- **Satish et al. 2012, Environ Health Perspect (PMID 23008272)**, "Is CO2 an indoor pollutant? Direct effects of low-to-moderate CO2 concentrations on human decision-making performance": test subjects took decision-making tests at 600/1000/2500 ppm — **at 1000 ppm, moderate declines in decision performance; at 2500 ppm, clear impairment** (FACT · controlled experiment exists · effects as observed under test conditions).
- **Allen et al. 2016, Environ Health Perspect (PMC4892924 · cited ~1,500)**, "Associations of Cognitive Function Scores with Carbon Dioxide, Ventilation, and Green Building...": under green-building conditions (lower CO2 / higher ventilation), composite cognitive-test scores were significantly higher (abstract-level: green conditions +61%, green+ conditions +101% · specific multiples cited at abstract level; check the original when quoting). The Allen team's later double-blind replication extension (the "COGnition" series · Buildings 2019) points the same way (FACE face · this lane did not independently open the original · labeled UNKNOWN); the Harvard school of public health ran a dedicated feature on this research line (title-level verified).
- **The counter-example, presented honestly (CONTESTED · this canon's single most important opposing piece of evidence)**: Scully et al. 2019 (Nature family · astronaut-surrogate population) found **no clear effect of 5000-ppm-grade CO2 exposure on the relevant cognitive tasks** — verified at search-snippet grade. That is: **the independent causality of "CO2 itself impairing cognition" remains disputed in the low-concentration range**; the sturdier framing is that **CO2 is a high-fidelity proxy for under-ventilation** (exhaled CO2 accumulating = insufficient air exchange = a whole suite of VOCs and biological pollutants accumulating in step).

**Action card H1 (CO2 ventilation · INFERENCE)**:
- The bedroom is CO2's disaster zone (doors and windows shut + a night of breathing): under-ventilated sleep environments commonly reach the 1,500–3,000 ppm range (common range in measurement literature · INFERENCE);
- A CO2 meter (NDIR-sensor type, tens to a hundred-odd yuan) beats any "smart" gadget: **>1000 ppm signals under-ventilation — crack a window / run the fresh-air system**;
- Same for the study room and meeting rooms: when attention drops, check the CO2 before blaming yourself;
- Stop condition: don't chase <500 ppm (the outdoor background is ~420 ppm and rising yearly; urban indoors can rarely reach it); holding under 1,000–1,200 is enough (INFERENCE · framing).

### 1.3 Radon: the second cause of lung cancer — and the cheapness of testing is itself the action card

**Verified anchors (title-level)**:
- The WHO radon fact sheet (updated 2023-01): **radon causes 3%–14% of lung cancers** across countries (depending on average radon levels and smoking rates);
- **US EPA, "Health Risk of Radon": radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer, with about 21,000 lung-cancer deaths a year in the US** (FACT · official framing);
- The WHO Handbook on Indoor Radon (Zeeb et al. 2009 · cited 780+): gives the national reference-level framework for indoor radon (≤300 Bq/m³ · lower, toward 100, suggested where conditions allow · guideline grade).

**The mechanism face (MODEL)**: radon is a radioactive gas from uranium decay · it seeps indoors from soil/rock through foundation cracks and accumulates in poorly ventilated lower floors/basements; inhaled radon progeny α-decay and damage bronchial epithelium. **Radon × smoking has a super-multiplicative risk structure (MODEL · literature framing) — smokers' radon risk is amplified.**

**Action card H2 (radon testing · INFERENCE · the best value-per-yuan card in this canon)**:
- A long-term radon test kit (activated-charcoal / electret / continuous monitor) costs on the order of a hundred yuan — **this is the one-time checkup "too cheap to skip"**;
- Priorities: basements / first floors / high-radon geology / newly built tightly sealed homes;
- Above the reference level → commission professional remediation (slab sealing / sub-slab depressurization) — **buy no "anti-radon coating" before you test**.

### 1.4 Gas safety: NO2 × childhood asthma, and the CO alarm

**Verified anchors (title-level)**:
- **Lin W, Brunekreef B, Gehring U. 2013, Int J Epidemiol (PMID 23962958)**, "Meta-analysis of the effects of indoor nitrogen dioxide and gas cooking on asthma and wheeze in children": gas cooking / indoor NO2 is associated with raised childhood asthma and wheeze risk (meta · effect sizes per the original paper · FACT meta-exists grade · association not causation);
- The WHO Guidelines for Indoor Air Quality: Selected Pollutants (2010 · official page title-level verified): gives indoor limits for CO, NO2, formaldehyde (30-minute mean 0.1 mg/m³), benzene, naphthalene and others (guideline grade · values per the original).

**Action card H3 (the gas trio · INFERENCE)**:
1. **A CO alarm** (carbon monoxide is colorless and odorless · a nighttime leak = losing the ability to call for help in your sleep — the alarm is the only reliable line of defense); install one wherever the dwelling has any gas appliance or an adjoining garage;
2. **No gas water heater in an enclosed bathroom space** (a direct-vent unit in the bathroom = the classic CO-poisoning scene; winter-north high incidence);
3. **Gas-leak red flag**: smell gas → **do not turn on lights, do not touch any electrical switch, do not run the exhaust fan** → close the valve → open windows → go outside to call for repair/report (an electric spark igniting the gas = taboo number one · emergency-commons grade).

**The CO-poisoning red line R1**: headache + dizziness + nausea + weakness **and several people in the home with the same symptoms** (no fever, no cough) → open windows at once, everyone leave the scene, emergency department / call 120 (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number — *(translator calibration)*) — "if the whole family feels unwell at once, think CO first" is this canon's most life-saving sentence (INFERENCE · red flag).

## 2. The light face

### 2.1 Daylight × circadian rhythm: the two orders of magnitude indoor lighting cannot cover

**The order-of-magnitude facts (FACT · multi-source illuminance tables agree · title-level verified)**:

| Scene | Illuminance |
|---|---|
| Noon direct sunlight | 32,000–100,000+ lux |
| Overcast outdoors | 1,000–25,000 lux |
| Indoor office lighting | 300–500 lux |
| Living room at night | 100–300 lux |

**The meaning (INFERENCE · connects to the sleep-canon morning-light anchoring)**: outdoor morning light beats indoor lighting by **2–3 orders of magnitude** — "making up the morning light" with indoor lamps is physically impossible; the circadian system's strong-morning-light signal (the suprachiasmatic nucleus's time-setting) is essentially only obtainable outdoors/at a window. The sleep canon's "morning light anchoring, dark nights" cell (N5 B3/M5 · guideline grade) is the upstream anchor of the sleep face; this canon unfolds its home-execution face:
- **The H4 morning-light card**: 10–30 minutes outdoors or at a window within an hour of waking — even an overcast day runs thousands to tens of thousands of lux, which is enough (overcast 1,000–25,000 lux, per the table above · FACT);
- Older adults / home-office workers / those with winter-low-mood tendencies are the groups that most need to execute this (bright-light therapy for seasonal mood has a mature literature face · not expanded here · points to the sleep/mood canons downstream).

### 2.2 The blue-light truth: intensity > color temperature — one quantitative framework

**Verified anchor (title-level)**: **Brown TM et al. 2022, PLoS Biology (cited 780+)**, "Recommendations for daytime, evening, and nighttime indoor light exposure to best support physiology, sleep and wakefulness in humans" — the international consensus recommendation, expressed in **melanopic EDI** (melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance · the effective stimulus to the melanopsin-containing ipRGC pathway): **daytime minimum ≥250 melanopic EDI (vertical illuminance at the eye)**; sharply narrowed in the evening, with the sleep environment as close to dark as possible (evening ≤10, sleep environment ≤1 order of magnitude · values per the original paper · guideline grade).

**The correction to the popular narrative (this canon's second falsification card, placed in the body)**:
- "Blue-light protection = color-temperature protection" is a simplified story. **The dose is set mainly by intensity and duration; spectral position is a second-order term** (MODEL · a direct inference of the melanopic framework);
- Randomized evidence that "blue-blocking glasses improve sleep" is thin and inconsistent (the field has a 2023 Cochrane systematic review and a 2026 updated review (PMID 41602785 · bibliographic grade) · this lane did not open and verify their conclusions · UNKNOWN);
- **The executable alternative (INFERENCE)**: at night turn **brightness** down, keep lights farther from the eyes, and switch on warm night modes as an aid — all cheaper than buying blue-light films/glasses and aimed at the main lever.

### 2.3 Recommended illuminances by fixture (action card H5 · guideline-grade general values)

| Scene | Recommended illuminance |
|---|---|
| Reading / desk work | 300–500 lux (more for fine work) |
| Living-room general activity | 100–300 lux |
| Corridors / stairs / bathroom transitions | 100–150 lux (the key = no shadow cliffs) |
| Reading at 65+ | the table ×2–3 (the aging lens transmits less and the pupil admits less · MODEL · guideline-grade compensation convention) |
| Night bathroom path | motion-sensing night light · low mounted · warm color (also serves §5 fall prevention) |

## 3. The sound-environment face

### 3.1 The WHO noise framing (this canon's anchor · guideline grade)

- **WHO Night Noise Guidelines for Europe (2009 · official page title-level verified)**: the night-noise recommendation is **Lnight,outside 40 dB**; countries unable to reach it get an interim target (55 dB order · mentioned on the official page as interim target). Sleep's physiological effects (awakenings / altered sleep architecture) are measurable from lower levels (the 30–40 dB band · MODEL).
- **WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines for the European Region (2018 · official page title-level verified)**: systematic-review-based recommendations and strength-of-recommendation gradings for road traffic (Lden 53 dB order), rail, aircraft and other sources — **noise's disease consequences (sleep disorder, annoyance, children's cognition, metabolic and cardiovascular indicators) are supported by systematic reviews** (guideline grade).
- The earlier guideline (1999) gave bedroom recommendations on the order of 30 dB LAeq (later literature citing "daytime 35 dB / night 30 dB" visible in search snippets · guideline grade).
- **Framing discipline**: all of the above are **population-level guideline values for environmental noise exposure**, not "your bedroom must be an anechoic chamber"; when citing any specific dB value, note the Lden/Lnight/distance/outdoor-vs-facade framing (INFERENCE · misuse prevention).

### 3.2 Low-frequency noise (MODEL/INFERENCE)

- Characteristics: penetrates ordinary sound-insulating construction (walls/windows insulate low frequencies far less than mid-highs) · diffracts strongly · and annoyance is disproportionate (equal-loudness low-frequency rumble annoys more · literature framing MODEL);
- Common sources: fan-coil units / fresh-air main units / elevator shafts / pump rooms / restaurant exhaust downstairs / aging refrigerator compressors;
- The remediation order (INFERENCE): **locate the source (room-by-room switch-off testing) → treat at the source (vibration isolation / repair / negotiating with building management) → treat the receiving face (sound-insulating windows do limited work on low frequencies; don't count on curtains)** — for low-frequency noise, find the pump and the fan first; don't buy acoustic foam first.

### 3.3 White noise: the evidence, graded (INFERENCE · misuse prevention)

- **Adults**: evidence of benefit for falling/staying asleep is limited and study quality uneven — "if it helps, continue; if not, don't force it";
- **Infants**: the commonest use is masking environmental sounds for sleep; **machine volume and distance must be controlled** (research suggests maximum-volume settings can exceed recommended exposure limits at crib distance · bibliographic-memory grade · this lane did not verify the original — execute as "lowest sufficient volume + far from the head of the bed" · INFERENCE); AAP guidance leans toward limiting infant environmental sound (guideline grade · values to be verified);
- **The master principle**: the first solution to a noise problem is **reducing it (remove/block the source)**; white noise is **masking (the second solution)** — using masking to cover a persistent noise that needs remediation (the fan downstairs, say) treats the symptom as if it were the cause (INFERENCE).

## 4. The water and contact face

### 4.1 Drinking water: interlinking the beverages canon (interlink · no duplicate verdicts)

The water face (quantity / boiling / microplastics / lead) is settled in **`docs/healing/diet/beverages-canon-20260912.md` §A3** (that canon has verified: no safe lead threshold + run old pipes 30s–2min before filling; boiling = microbial safety + fewer microplastics · PMID 41850144). This canon adds only two cells, **pipes and equipment** (the interlink relationship · wiring):
- **Lead pipes / PFAS overview (FACT hazard grade + UNKNOWN for this domain)**: no safe lead threshold (WHO drinking-water guideline framing · cited in the beverages canon); PFAS = the extremely persistent "forever chemicals" class of industrial/consumer chemicals, with drinking water a main exposure route — **anchor (title-level verified)**: the EPA final rule of 2024-04-10, "PFAS National Primary Drinking Water Regulation" (Federal Register 2024-26): PFOA/PFOS MCLG=0, enforceable limit MCL=**4.0 ng/L (ppt)**; on 2026-05-18 EPA announced a follow-up proposed rule (the keep-the-MCL framing per official headlines). The PFAS background of Chinese urban water sources = **UNKNOWN (an empty cell · not invented)**;
- **Action card H6 (water · INFERENCE)**: worried about PFAS/lead → choose **reverse osmosis (RO) or filters certified for PFAS claims** (activated carbon handles some PFAS · RO is most thorough · equipment-commons grade); changing filters on schedule is the precondition of everything.

### 4.2 Indoor humidity: the 40–60% three-way trade-off (this canon's key balance face)

**Why an "interval" and not a single point (FACT sub-arguments + INFERENCE synthesis)**:

| Relative humidity | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **<30%** | — | Dry, irritated respiratory mucosa; static; wood cracking |
| **30–50%** | Dust-mite and mold breeding suppressed (**Arlian 1999, JACI PMID 10518832**: lowering relative humidity effectively controls dust-mite populations — FACT experiment-exists grade); enveloped viruses like influenza survive and transmit less at mid humidity (**Reiman 2018, PLoS One PMID 30252890**, "Humidity as a non-pharmaceutical intervention for influenza A" · FACT experiment-exists grade) | Dry-winter regions need humidification |
| **>60%** | — | Mites/mold breed faster (damp-returning walls/carpets); some building materials off-gas faster |

**The synthesized recommended interval = 40–60% (prevailing guideline convention · guideline grade; this canon gives the action value 40–55% by "the mite line 50% first" · INFERENCE)**.

**Action card H7 (humidity · INFERENCE)**:
- A thermo-hygrometer (tens of yuan) before any humidifier/dehumidifier — without measurement there is no humidity management;
- Heated bedrooms in winter: humidify to 40–55%, **change humidifier water daily + clean on schedule** (a dirty humidifier = spraying microbial aerosol into the air · risk-commons grade; ultrasonic types are water-quality sensitive · prefer purified/distilled water or evaporative/condensing types);
- The south's damp-returning "return-of-the-south" spells and the plum-rain season: dehumidify to <60% (mold and mites prevented together);
- Mite-allergic households: sunning/drying bedding often (the heat + dryness double kill) comes before dehumidifying (linked to §6.2).

## 5. Fall prevention and aging-in-place (all ages)

### 5.1 The epidemiological hard face (FACT · official/literature title-level verified)

- **The WHO falls fact sheet**: about **684,000 people die from falls globally each year** — **falls are the second leading cause of unintentional injury death after road traffic**; older adults bear the brunt;
- **The gravity of hip-fracture prognosis (multi-source consistent)**: on the fall's side it says "fracture"; on the outcome side it says "life or death" — **1-year mortality after hip fracture is about 15–30%** (Downey 2019 PMC · cited 520+: "traditionally reported as 30%"; Adeyemi 2026 medrxiv systematic review: "most studies report 15–30%"; Guo 2025 Frontiers: "commonly >20% even in high-income countries"). **The expected benefit of preventing one older-adult fall far exceeds the sum of most supplements' promises** (INFERENCE · comparative statement);
- **The bathroom = the home's number-one injury scene (US framing · FACT)**: CDC MMWR (2011 · 2008 data): about **234,000 US emergency visits a year for bathroom injuries in people 15+**, of which **81.1% were caused by falls**, with the high-risk acts = getting in/out of the tub/shower. **The popular-narrative inversion trap**: it is often quoted as "80% of older adults' falls happen in the bathroom" — wrong. The correct framing is "81% of bathroom injuries are caused by falls" (the direction cannot be inverted · this canon preserves the evidence).

### 5.2 Home modifications and exercise for older adults: two levers backed by systematic reviews

- **Home modification / assistive equipment**: Crosby et al. 2023, Syst Rev (PMID 37936167) + a 2026 update (Assist Technol) — systematic-review support for home modification and assistive equipment reducing falls among community-dwelling older adults (FACT review-exists grade · effect sizes per the original papers);
- **Exercise**: the ESSA (Exercise & Sports Science Australia) 2025 updated position statement (Tiedemann et al. · PMID 39341781) + a systematic review of community group exercise (Zhou 2024 · PMID 39167923) — **balance + strength training** are the core ingredients of fall-prevention exercise (guideline grade).

**Action card H8 (bathroom anti-slip · all ages · the best value-per-yuan modification card)**:
1. Non-slip mat/strips in the shower zone (or a non-slip floor finish);
2. **Load-bearing grab bars** beside the toilet + in the shower zone (suction-cup bars count only as temporary — if you install, install drilled load-bearing);
3. In older-adult households: switch to a **seated shower** + shower stool (eliminating the high-risk act of standing flamingo-style on one leg to pull on trousers);
4. Motion-sensing night lights along the night path (falls peak at night toileting · light + slippers, a two-factor pair);
5. Remove loose rugs / curled doormats / raised door-sill transitions (or fix with tape);
6. Household slippers = well-fitting non-slip pairs; retire the foam clogs that are "off with one kick" (INFERENCE · commons grade);
7. Medication review: sedatives/hypnotics/antihypertensives/antidiabetics cross with fall risk — hand to the physician for assessment (interlink with the food-drug-interaction canon · never stop medications on your own).

### 5.3 Child home safety (linked to the diet-drug booklet · interlink)

- **Window-fall prevention**: window limiters (restricting opening width) + beds/stools away from windowsills — the first-priority modification for high-floor households (INFERENCE · the injury mechanism is clear);
- **Ingestion prevention**: medicines/cleaners/pesticides **high up + locked** (child ingestion is a regular of the pediatric ER; the management anchor = the food-drug-interaction canon and its red-flag face · bring the packaging to care · no inducing vomiting unless professionally directed);
- **Scalds**: set the water-heater outlet temperature to ≤49°C order (anti-scald guideline convention · guideline grade) + test the water before a bath;
- Anti-tip anchoring of furniture (dressers/TVs — climbing crush injuries peak in children · commons-grade INFERENCE).

## 6. The falsification face: five home-wellness wastes of money, dissected

> Verdict-label framing: CONTRADICTED = evidence/official framing directly opposes the claim; not admitted to the evidence face = no falsifiable mechanism and no reproducible effect. Each item gets a "replacement action" — the point of falsification is moving the money to real leverage.

| # | Claim | Verdict | Basis and replacement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Negative-ion generators purify the air** | Downgraded to "a physical effect ≠ purification" + **a companion ozone risk** | Negative ions charge particles so they **settle onto walls and furniture** (including the breath you inhale just before that); air readings can drop while the pollution never left the room; and the discharge process can produce ozone (itself a lung irritant). **CARB (California Air Resources Board) official guidance explicitly advises consumers against using such ozone-releasing "air cleaner" devices in the home** (title-level verified · "strongly advises against the in-home use"). Replacement: a HEPA purifier (§1.1) |
| 2 | **Mite-killer gadgets (beating/UV/small-suction machines) remove mites** | Partly effective but **aimed at the wrong main target** | The core variables of mite control are **humidity and temperature** (Arlian 1999: lowering RH controls dust mites · PMID 10518832), then heat treatment of fabrics; a small gadget's UV dose and beating depth do limited work against mites and allergens deep in fabric (MODEL). **Sunning/drying bedding = the heat + dryness double mechanism · the mechanism is right and near zero cost**; the gadget only supplements at the fabric surface. Replacement: sun/dry bedding · hot wash ≥55°C · humidity <50% (§4.2) |
| 3 | **Houseplants remove formaldehyde** | **CONTRADICTED (at room scale)** | The source = the 1989 NASA sealed-chamber experiments (plants do absorb small amounts of VOCs · the lineage visible on the Iowa State Extension page · title-level); extrapolated to a real room it is **a drop in the bucket** — hundreds of pots per room would be needed for a noticeable effect on a new home's formaldehyde load, and at night, without light, plants reverse to consuming oxygen and emitting CO2 (negligible, but absorption also stops). **The American Lung Association's official blog ran the title "Actually, Houseplants Don't Clean the Air"** (title-level verified). Replacement: source control (compliant materials/furniture) + **long-term ventilation** (formaldehyde off-gassing runs on a years-long clock) + CMA-accredited testing before move-in (the H9 checklist) |
| 4 | **Magnetized water / small-cluster water / quantum water cups** | **Not admitted to the evidence face** | No falsifiable mechanism claim, no reproducible controlled effect (the category's core claims drift with marketing scripts — not even a stable falsifier exists — and by XYZ's epistemic constitution it does not even rate HYPOTHESIS). Replacement: spend that money on a radon test kit + a CO alarm + non-slip mats (this canon's three cheapest life-saving cards) |
| 5 | **Home ozone machines for "disinfection and purification"** | **CONTRADICTED + active harm** | Ozone is a fully confirmed respiratory irritant (a main component of the pollution face); the concentration that "kills everything" also injures human lungs, and reaction by-products (aldehydes / ultrafine particles) can be newly generated. CARB's official framing, as in item 1, advises against home use (title-level verified). Replacement: windows (for gases) + HEPA (for particles); if disinfection is truly needed, hand it to professional ozone treatment (unoccupied + thorough airing afterward · beyond "home daily use") |

## 7. Action-card summary and the "new home / old home" checklists

### 7.1 Action-card index (H1–H9 · by value per yuan)

### 7.2 What a new home should be checked for (pre-move-in checklist)

1. **Before moving in**: CMA-accredited formaldehyde/VOC testing (ventilate only, no occupancy until within limits · formaldehyde off-gassing runs on a years-long clock → keep ventilating long after move-in); radon testing (new homes are tightly sealed · may accumulate more); gas-pipeline pressure test confirmed;
2. **Installation positions**: gas water-heater location (not an enclosed bathroom space) + CO alarm positions (bedrooms/corridors) + smoke alarm; window limiters (children/high floors); pre-embedded backing for toilet/shower grab bars (installing now is cheaper than in ten years — **renovate for your own 80-year-old self** · INFERENCE);
3. **Equipment selection**: a HEPA-type purifier (CADR matched to area) · fresh-air system (when budget allows = the double answer to CO2 + particles) · thermo-hygrometer · NDIR CO2 meter;
4. **The light face**: night-light circuits / motion-sensing lights; older-adult zones pre-provisioned at ×2–3 illuminance.

### 7.3 What an old home should be checked for (existing-housing checklist)

1. **Pipe vintage**: galvanized pipes / lead solder / brass-fixture era assessment (any doubt → run the water 30s–2min before filling · interlink with beverages-canon §A3); aging wiring / overloaded circuits (the fire face · commons grade);
2. **Radon**: old homes are not exempt (geology decides) · test lower floors/basements first;
3. **Gas**: aging hoses replaced (metal corrugated) · stove age · CO alarm (old homes often lack one);
4. **Bathroom modification**: the full H8 set (the single highest-priority renovation spend in older-adult households);
5. **Sound insulation and low frequency**: locate the downstairs/neighbor fan-pump low-frequency sources (the §3.2 order);
6. **Windows**: limiters + street-facing sound insulation (mid-high-frequency sealing improves markedly · keep low-frequency expectations low · INFERENCE).

### 7.4 Red lines for seeking care (this canon's R1–R3 · linked to the red-flag face)

- **R1 suspected CO**: multiple people with the same symptoms (headache, dizziness, nausea) without fever → open windows + leave the scene + emergency department / call 120;
- **R2 after a fall**: severe hip/groin pain, unable to stand → emergency department (possible hip fracture · never self-massage it); after a head impact, confusion/vomiting/drowsiness → emergency department (linked to the healing_red_flags face);
- **R3 gas leak**: smell it → no electric sparks → close the valve → open windows → call for repair/report from outdoors.
