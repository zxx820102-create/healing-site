# Cardiovascular Health Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease is the number-one cause of death worldwide and in China — but it is not "a sudden misfortune." It is the story of one blood vessel slowly aging over decades and, one day, finally overloading — **and the very chronicity of the process is exactly what means the window for intervention is long.**
>
> **How to read this volume**: ① start with the master ledger (how big the burden · how slow the process) → ② hypertension → ③ blood lipids → ④ coronary disease and stroke → ⑤ the pieces of "maintenance common sense" overturned by modern research → ⑥ life after diagnosis → ⑦ the red-flag list and master answers to eight common rumors.

---

## ① Master ledger face: the weight of the number-one cause of death, and the age of a blood vessel

**How big is the burden (FACT · verified numbers)**

- **Global**: cardiovascular disease is the world's number-one cause of death. The WHO fact sheet (2022 data) estimates about **19.8 million** deaths from cardiovascular disease, roughly 32% of all global deaths; of these, about 85% die of heart attack and stroke. An earlier framing puts 2019 at about 17.9 million (the old WHO page still circulates; the two framings coexisting is a data-year difference, not a contradiction).
- **China**: the *China Cardiovascular Health and Disease Report 2023* projects **330 million** people currently living with the disease (of whom about 13 million stroke, about 11.39 million coronary heart disease, about 8.9 million heart failure). Cardiovascular disease ranks **first** in the cause-of-death structure of both rural and urban residents: in 2021 it accounted for **48.98% and 47.35%** of rural and urban deaths respectively — **2 in every 5 deaths** are from cardiovascular disease.
- These are not distant statistics: this is the disease "every family has." And precisely because of that, it is one of the fields with the **highest return per unit of health investment** among interventionable areas.

**Atherosclerosis: not an event, a process (FACT · pathology consensus)**

Many people imagine a heart attack as the accident of "a vessel suddenly blocking." The real sequence:

1. From the **teenage years**, fatty streaks can appear beneath the arterial intima — everyone has started this process; the only difference is speed;
2. Over **decades**, LDL particles keep burrowing into the vessel wall → oxidation → engulfed by immune cells to form foam cells → plaques slowly grow;
3. **A plaque can reach moderate size with zero symptoms** — this is the slyest part of "chronic";
4. One day the plaque ruptures (or erodes) → platelets rush in to plug the gap → a thrombus forms → **within minutes**, a chronic course that took decades to grow becomes an acute event.

**So the correct mental model is**: the acute event (heart attack / stroke) is "sudden," but its foundation (atherosclerosis) is a **chronic process**. What is sudden is the moment the answer is revealed; what is chronic is the years spent taking the exam.

**"The age of a blood vessel" (running metaphor · METAPHOR)**

Picture your coronary artery as a water pipe decades into service: the "sand grains" (LDL) in the water burrow day and night into the gaps in the pipe wall, and rust spots (plaques) slowly bloom on the inner surface. A pipe does not suddenly grow old one morning — it **grows a little older every day**; you just keep hearing the water flow as normal. The day a rust spot flakes off and jams the valve, the repairman can only deal with "the jammed moment" — the rust took decades to form.

A rough mental scale for vascular age (MODEL · not a lab measurement): hypertension is extra pressure applied daily, smoking is an extra knife-cut daily, high blood sugar is the acid that makes the wall more brittle, LDL is the dominant sand grain, and exercise is a good filter cartridge. **You cannot return the pipe to factory condition, but you can absolutely decide how fast it ages.**

## ② Hypertension face: the silent killer, and the small matter of "measuring blood pressure"

**Why it is called the silent killer (FACT · mechanism consensus)**

Hypertension itself has almost **no symptoms** — no dizziness ≠ normal blood pressure; most people with hypertension feel nothing. Its damage takes the "quietly demolishing the wall" route: persistently elevated pressure → arterial walls bearing excess load for years → endothelial injury accelerating atherosclerosis → small arteries hardening and turning brittle → the four target organs damaged in sequence: **brain (stroke), heart (infarction / heart failure), kidneys (renal failure), eyes (retinopathy)**. The day you "feel it" is usually the day a target organ has already failed. Within cardiovascular disease — China's top cause of death — one of the most common modifiable drivers behind stroke and infarction is uncontrolled hypertension.

**The home-measurement standard (FACT · framing of the Chinese Hypertension Guidelines 2024 Revision / the Chinese Blood Pressure Measurement Guide / the 2019 Chinese Home Blood Pressure Monitoring Guideline)**

- **Before measuring**: in the 30 minutes before, no smoking, no coffee / strong tea / alcohol, no vigorous exercise; empty the bladder; **sit and rest quietly at least 5 minutes** in a quiet environment before measuring.
- **Posture**: seated, back supported, feet flat on the floor, legs not crossed; cuff center at **heart level** (upper-arm electronic monitor; lower edge of the cuff 2–3 cm above the elbow crease; tightness such that 1–2 fingers fit underneath); no talking during the measurement.
- **Which arm**: **measure both arms the first time**, then record **the side with the higher value** (a large inter-arm difference — persistently >10 mmHg — should be reported to your doctor at the visit).
- **How many days**: for diagnostic evaluation, **measure on 5–7 consecutive days**, **once in the morning and once in the evening each day**, 2–3 readings per session, 1 minute apart, averaged; the first day's data may optionally be discarded, the rest averaged.
- **Which device**: a certified **upper-arm electronic monitor** first (wrist / finger devices not recommended); mercury columns have largely exited the home setting.
- **Write it down**: date, time, value, heart rate — bring the whole logbook to the doctor; far more useful than "I feel it's high / not high."

**Diagnostic framing: repeated measurements; never a single-reading verdict (FACT · guideline)**

- Office blood pressure: **3 measurements on different days** ≥140/90 mmHg before diagnosing;
- Home blood pressure: the mean over **5–7 consecutive days** ≥**135/85** mmHg (the home diagnostic threshold, 5 lower than office);
- Conversely: **a single high reading (office or home) ≠ hypertension** — the white-coat effect, a night of poor rest, either can push up one reading. Standardized repeat measurement first; talk diagnosis after.

**The real magnitude and potency of salt reduction (FACT)**

- WHO recommendation: adults, **<5 g of salt per day** (sodium <2 g) — **about one flat beer-bottle cap**. Actual average intake among Chinese residents is commonly in the 9–11 g range, nearly double the recommendation — which means salt reduction has an especially large room for improvement in Chinese populations.
- The efficacy evidence is not merely "theoretically useful": the 5-year large-scale **salt substitution trial in rural China (SSaSS, NEJM 2021)** — a low-sodium, potassium-enriched salt substitute replacing regular salt — **stroke risk fell 14%**, and major cardiovascular events fell 13%. A cheap intervention charged against the number-one cause of death.
- **Safety boundary**: potassium-enriched (low-sodium) salt is not good for everyone — **renal insufficiency, current use of potassium-sparing diuretics / certain antihypertensives, elevated hyperkalemia risk** — these people must ask a doctor before switching salt (logic stated explicitly: potassium that cannot be excreted accumulates in blood and can cause arrhythmia).
- The biggest share of salt is not the cooking spoon; it is **processed foods, takeout, pickled items, sauces** — "invisible salt" often makes up more than half.

**Falsification: "no symptoms, nothing to manage" (CONTRADICTED)**

→ Hypertension was never defined as "symptomatic high blood pressure" but as blood pressure high enough to **quietly smash the target organs**. Having no symptoms only means the nerves have not sounded the alarm; it does not mean the wall is intact. Guideline treatment logic keys on blood pressure values and risk assessment, not symptoms. Memorize this one in reverse: **precisely because there are no symptoms, measure; measure and you will know; manage and it will not blow.**

## ③ Blood lipid face: LDL, statins, and the master answer to the "soften the blood vessels" scam

**LDL is the hardest-established causal risk factor (FACT · double evidence)**

- **Genetic-side evidence (Mendelian randomization)**: Ference et al., *European Heart Journal* 2017 (EAS consensus statement) — pooling Mendelian randomization studies with statin RCTs, multiple evidence lines covering **more than 2 million participants** consistently indicate: **LDL causes atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD), and the causal effect is independent of the means used to lower LDL**. The "correlation or causation" question that entangles nutrition science has, for LDL, a rare clean-cut answer: **causation**.
- **Intervention-side evidence (statin RCTs)**: CTT Collaboration, *Lancet* 2012 meta-analysis (170,000+ participants): each **1.0 mmol/L** reduction in LDL cuts the risk of major vascular events **proportionally by about 21%** (RR 0.79, 95%CI 0.77–0.81), with consistent effects across broad populations and a good safety profile.
- **In one sentence**: LDL is not "a reference item on a lab report"; it is the **raw material of atherosclerosis itself**. Lowering LDL is one of the hardest-evidenced interventions in all of medicine.

**The real benefit rate of statins — NNT in plain words (FACT + INFERENCE · transparent derivation)**

"Number needed to treat (NNT)": how many people must be treated, for how long, to prevent 1 event on average. It is the ruler that translates "efficacy" into plain words.

- **Primary prevention** (people who have never had a heart attack / stroke): USPSTF systematic-review framing; the NNT of 5 years of statins to avoid 1 major cardiovascular event is about **94** (<70 years old) to **62** (≥70 years old).
- **Plain words**: **100 people without heart disease taking a statin for 5 years together avoid roughly 1–2 heart attacks / strokes.** For any one person the benefit looks "tiny"; set against 330 million patients, it is millions of events avoided. The drug is not an amulet; it is a **probability shifter** — it shifts your entire 5-year risk curve down by about one fifth.
- **Secondary prevention** (a heart attack / stroke has already happened): baseline 5-year risk about 20%; applying CTT's 21% relative reduction per mmol/L, the absolute benefit is about 4 percentage points, **NNT≈25** (INFERENCE · transparently derived from the verified RR) — the higher the baseline risk, the more people the same pill saves. This is why guidelines are most aggressive about lipid lowering in secondary prevention.
- **Honest boundary**: NNT depends on baseline risk. For very-low-risk people the NNT is larger, closer to the "is it worth it" range that needs individualized discussion — exactly the account to work out with your doctor after a checkup, rather than scaring yourself or fooling yourself.

**Side effects, told honestly (FACT + MODEL)**

- **Muscle symptoms really exist**: some people on statins do get muscle aches / weakness; reported incidence in RCTs is markedly lower than in clinical interviewing (the finer you ask, the more gets reported), but it **really exists** — it is not imagination. New and persistent muscle symptoms → **test muscle enzymes first and inform your doctor; let the doctor judge any adjustment**, rather than silently quitting the drug (in secondary prevention, quitting on your own trades events that have already happened for "comfort").
- **The nocebo effect has experimental proof**: the **SAMSON trial** (Wood et al., *New England Journal of Medicine* 2020, N-of-1 design): patients who had stopped because of side effects alternated statin, placebo, and no tablet — **about 90% of the "side effects" appeared just the same during the periods when patients did not know they were taking placebo / nothing**. The symptoms are **real** (not faked), but triggered mainly by the act of "swallowing a tablet," not by the tablet's ingredients. Similar designs (e.g., StatinWise) reached the same conclusion. Knowing this is itself treatment — many people successfully restart statins once they understand nocebo.
- **Liver-enzyme monitoring boundary (MODEL · ask-your-doctor item)**: statins can raise transaminases (mostly mild and transient); check baseline liver function once before starting; whether to recheck and how often afterward is decided by the doctor based on the specific drug and any underlying liver disease — **no need for long-term repeated self-ordered blood draws**. For a significant liver history, the prescribing plan is the doctor's call.

**Master answer to the "soften the blood vessels" food scam (CONTRADICTED)**

"Eat XX to soften blood vessels / clear plaques" (vinegar, onion, wood-ear fungus, red wine, deep-sea fish oil, the various "vascular street-sweepers") — **no food has ever been shown to soften vessels that have already hardened**. Vascular stiffening = structural change made of collagen deposition + calcification + a lipid core; it is not limescale in a pipe that a vinegar soak can dissolve. Real "plaque intervention" looks like this (MODEL): guideline-concordant LDL lowering (drugs bringing LDL to target) can **slow plaque progression, stabilize vulnerable plaques, and in some studies even shrink plaque volume** — but that is **drug engineering measured in years**, not dietary supplementation. What food can correctly do: help cut salt, cut saturated fat, control total calories — these **lower the rate of new damage**, they do not reverse existing rust.

## ④ Coronary disease and stroke face: red-flag features, and the atypical trap

**Chest pain differential: which are emergency red flags (FACT · consensus; this section is recognition logic, not diagnosis)**

**Red-flag features (several at once → call 120 immediately (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number — *(translator calibration)*), rest where you are, and do not drive yourself to hospital)**:

- **Location and character**: retrosternal / precordial **crushing, tightening, pressing, heavy sensation** (the classic description: "like an elephant sitting on the chest"), not a pinpoint, flickering stab;
- **Radiation**: toward the **inner left arm, both arms, jaw, neck, shoulder / back**;
- **Accompaniments**: **cold sweat, nausea and vomiting, difficulty breathing, sense of impending doom**;
- **Trigger pattern**: **provoked by exertion / emotion / heavy meals, relieved within minutes of rest** (the rhythm of typical angina); **still persisting >15 minutes without relief at rest** = suspected acute coronary syndrome, handle as an emergency.

**Not-a-red-flag ≠ safe (honest reminder)**: pain that changes with breathing or posture, a stab one fingertip can point to precisely, pain reproducible by pressing — these better support chest-wall / costochondral / nerve origins, but still cannot 100% exclude the heart. **Chest pain you cannot judge = chest pain that needs a doctor.** This volume gives only the "features that mean go immediately," and deliberately provides no "features that mean you may skip."

**The atypical-population trap (FACT · CDC / AHA + literature)**

- **Women**: more often present with **shortness of breath, nausea / vomiting, fatigue, jaw / neck-and-back aching, cold sweat**; chest pain may be unremarkable;
- **The elderly**: symptoms are blander — fatigue, poor appetite, confusion;
- **People with diabetes**: **the most dangerous "atypical" is the painless one** — autonomic neuropathy can make an infarction **entirely free of chest pain** (the literature is explicit: diabetes and advanced age are associated with "painless presentation"). It may show up as unexplained breathlessness, heavy sweating, collapse.
- **In one sentence**: for these three groups, when "upset stomach, tiredness, breathlessness" persists without relief, think of the heart. Missing the atypical = missing the very people who most need rescue.

**Stroke recognition: BEFAST (interlink; not repeated)**

The full treatment of the stroke-recognition mnemonic (B Balance / E Eyes / F Face / A Arms / S Speech / T Time), the golden time window, and the "never feed water or medicine" rule is **not repeated in this volume** — see the first-aid canon §④ directly: `docs/healing/first-aid-canon-v1-20260912.md` (weave anchor `dis:stroke`). This volume adds only one causal connection: **stroke and heart attack share the same foundation** — hypertension, atrial fibrillation, LDL, smoking, diabetes. Guard these five, and you guard both number-one killers at once.

**Carotid plaque: the tiered response when a checkup finds one (FACT · tiered by evidence)**

"Carotid plaque" on checkup reports is increasingly common; the correct tiered response:

1. **Asymptomatic + stenosis <50%**: not an emergency. It is a "window warning" of whole-body atherosclerosis; the response = manage LDL / blood pressure / blood sugar, quit smoking + recheck as directed — **not panic, and not "plaque-removing" supplements**.
2. **Asymptomatic + moderate-to-severe stenosis**: under modern intensive medical therapy, **the annual ipsilateral stroke risk of asymptomatic carotid stenosis has fallen to about 1%** — medication is the base layer; whether adding surgery (endarterectomy / stenting) helps is limited and accumulates over time and must be weighed individually (the large ACST trial's 5-year absolute benefit about 5.3%); have it assessed by vascular surgery / neurology specialists — most people need not go looking for the knife.
3. **Symptomatic stenosis** (ipsilateral TIA / stroke within the past six months) + stenosis ≥50%: the group with the clearest intervention benefit (CEA / CAS) — **this is the tier that genuinely needs active surgical evaluation**.
- Epistemic note: the tiering logic = a direction consistent across multiple guidelines (FACT); specific values update with trials and guideline versions; defer to specialist assessment.

## ⑤ Primary prevention's modern reversals: three pieces of "common sense" rewritten by evidence

**Reversal one: aspirin for primary prevention — USPSTF 2022 formally withdrew the initiation recommendation (FACT)**

- **Past**: low-dose aspirin was once standard advice — "a daily pill to protect the heart" for middle-aged and older adults (some old recommendations even attached colorectal-cancer prevention as a bundled benefit).
- **Now**: the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) **April 2022** final statement (*JAMA*): in adults **≥60, do not initiate** low-dose aspirin for primary prevention of cardiovascular disease (**Grade D**); ages 40–59 with higher 10-year cardiovascular risk = **individualized, shared decision-making** (Grade C).
- **Logic**: aspirin = antiplatelet = lowers heart-attack risk and **bleeding risk** at the same time (gastrointestinal bleeding, intracranial bleeding). Pooled across multiple large trials, the expected heart-attack benefit in primary-prevention populations proved **insufficient to cover the bleeding harm**; the older the age, the more the bleeding harm tips the scale — a recommendation direction flipping with evidence is what a scientific system **working normally** looks like, not a self-own.
- **Boundary (important)**: the above addresses whether someone who has **not yet** had a heart attack / stroke should **start** taking it. **For people already diagnosed with coronary disease or given a stent (secondary prevention), the antiplatelet plan is entirely different, and they must never stop it on their own (self-stopping carries thrombosis risk)** — whether aspirin is a life-saving drug or an unnecessary source of bleeding for you is a judgment your doctor makes from your history.

**Reversal two: "a daily glass of red wine protects the heart" — evidence downgraded (FACT)**

- The J-shaped curve (light drinkers healthier than abstainers) comes mainly from **observational studies**, contaminated by two confounders: **reverse causation** (sick people are advised to quit, piling patients into the "non-drinker" group) and "sick-quitter bias." More rigorous prospective designs and Mendelian randomization studies keep eroding the protective effect (MODEL).
- **The ballast fact**: IARC has listed alcoholic beverages as a **Group 1 carcinogen since 1987** — the same tier as tobacco, with causal links to at least 7 cancers; WHO's 2023 statement: **no level of alcohol consumption is safe for health**.
- **The net ledger**: the polyphenols in red wine can be had just as well from grapes / grape juice, without the Group 1 carcinogen attached. "Heart protection" cannot protect against "cancer causation"; the ledger is negative. **If you don't drink, don't start for health's sake; if you drink, less is better, and stopping is best.**

**Reversal three: fish-oil preparations — LDL-negative, and the difference from "prescription grade" (FACT)**

- **Ordinary fish-oil supplements**: no RCT evidence of cardiovascular benefit. Large trials (e.g., STRENGTH, an omega-3 carboxylic acid preparation, in people with high TG) were **negative on the primary endpoint**.
- **A mechanism cold fact**: omega-3 does **not lower LDL**; high-dose (4 g/day) EPA+DHA does lower triglycerides ≥30%, but often **comes with a mild rise in LDL** (AHA 2019 science advisory). So for the LDL that matters most, "take fish oil to lower blood lipids" points in exactly the wrong direction.
- **The real fork**: **prescription-grade high-purity EPA (icosapent ethyl)** delivered event benefit in the population whose TG remained high on statins (REDUCE-IT trial) — that is a **drug**: prescription-only, with indications, with monitoring — **not the same thing** as the fish-oil capsules on the shelf. Crediting the prescription drug's success to the supplement is the supplement industry's favorite sleight of hand.

## ⑥ Recovery and life face: after the disease, how to live

**Cardiac rehabilitation: the underrated Class I recommendation (FACT)**

- Cardiac rehabilitation (exercise training + risk-factor management + psychological support + medication optimization + follow-up education) is a **guideline Class I recommendation**: the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA heart-failure guideline and subsequent reviews state it plainly — **exercise training is recommended for heart-failure patients able to participate (regardless of ejection fraction)**; the benefit evidence for post-coronary / post-infarction rehabilitation is equally solid (exercise capacity, symptoms, quality of life, readmission).
- **Where the underestimation lies**: Class I benefit evidence, yet actual participation has stayed chronically low — many believe "rest quietly after a heart attack," which is exactly backwards. **What "quiet rest" wastes away is muscle, cardiopulmonary reserve, and confidence**; progressive exercise under evaluation and safeguards is the road that makes the heart trustworthy again.
- **Safe start (MODEL · principle level)**: doctor evaluation first (any arrhythmia / ischemia / decompensation needing treatment); start at low intensity (e.g., brisk walking on flat ground); use "**can talk, cannot sing**" as the intensity gauge (full sentences possible = intensity appropriate); increase duration step by step, then speed. For known coronary disease, run high-intensity plans past a cardiac rehabilitation clinic. Chest pain, near-fainting, unusual breathlessness = stop and seek care.

**Two home gauges for heart-failure patients (FACT)**

- **Daily weight — the most sensitive fluid scale**: every day **on waking, after urinating, before breakfast**, in the same clothing, on the same scale — weigh and record. Logic: heart failure = a pump that cannot keep up → the kidneys cut output → sodium and water retained → **weight raises the alarm days before edema shows**. Alert line: **weight gain >2 kg within 3 days** (European patient-education framing), or the US framing **2–3 lb in one day / 5 lb in one week** — cross the line and contact your doctor; do not wait until you are gasping to act.
- **Fluid-restriction logic**: in severe heart failure / fluid-retention episodes, guideline practice suggests limiting fluids to **1.5–2 L/day** (counting soup and porridge; fruit counted by actual water content); not every heart-failure patient needs strict restriction — **mild cases are not heavily restricted; tightening comes with decompensation, and the specific amount is set by the doctor based on blood sodium and degree of retention**. Salt restriction as in ② (usually stricter for heart failure).
- The essence of these two gauges: **turn "discovered at the follow-up visit" into "seen at home first"** — the bulk of heart-failure readmissions is fluid retention, and retention is visible on the home scale.

## ⑦ Red-flag chapter + master falsification face (eight items)

**The call-120-now red-flag collection (FACT · grouped by system)**

1. Acute persistent chest pain (>15 minutes), or severe chest pain with cold sweat / radiation / sense of impending doom — suspect heart attack;
2. BEFAST positive (Balance / Eyes / Face / Arms / Speech, any one) — suspect stroke; write down the onset time;
3. Blood pressure **≥180/120** accompanied by: chest pain, shortness of breath, blurred vision, slurred speech, severe headache — hypertensive emergency;
4. Clearly worsening breathlessness at rest, or waking at night unable to breathe unless sitting up (heart-failure decompensation signal);
5. Heart-failure alert line: gain of more than 5 lb (about 2.3 kg) in a week, or 2 kg in 3 days;
6. Fainting / blackout on exertion, or resting heart rate persistently <40 with dizziness and fatigue — prompt specialist evaluation (rule out conduction block).

**Falsification face (CONTRADICTED · each: claim → evidence → correct posture)**

1. **"IV drips to flush the vessels; change-of-season clot prevention"** → No reliable evidence supports "periodic IV flushes" preventing heart attack or stroke; an atherosclerotic plaque is structure inside the vessel wall, not sediment in the lumen that can be "flushed away"; infusions themselves carry allergy / infection / volume-load risks (worst in elderly heart-failure patients). → Correct posture: **year-in, year-out regular** lipid / pressure / glucose management and quitting smoking — that is the true way to "open the vessels"; slow medicine taken every year beats fast water flushed every season.
2. **"Supplements can replace statins"** → Statin benefit rests on RCTs of 170,000+ people (CTT) plus genetics — double evidence; no supplement has comparable evidence; "natural" does not mean effective, and red-yeast-rice products are in fact statin-like substances of unknown dose (MODEL · ask-your-doctor item). → Stopping the drug for supplements = dismantling your own probability shifter by hand.
3. **"Blood-pressure drugs are addictive / wreck the liver and kidneys; you can't take them long-term"** → Antihypertensives contain no addictive substances; "it shoots back up the moment I stop" is because hypertension itself is mostly lifelong, not because the drug creates dependence; uncontrolled hypertension is the real main force **damaging kidneys, brain, and heart** — most modern antihypertensives in fact carry organ-protection evidence; that individual drugs require liver/kidney monitoring is part of follow-up, not evidence of "damage." → Long-term regular medication + regular follow-up + lifestyle dose reduction (less salt and less weight can genuinely reduce drugs, adjusted by the doctor).
4. **The "slower heartbeat, longer life" misreading** → This extrapolates the "athlete's heart" to everyone: sinus bradycardia in long-term endurance trainers is a training adaptation, a physiological phenomenon; whereas **symptomatic** bradycardia in ordinary people (dizziness, fatigue, blackouts, fainting) can signal conduction-system disease and needs evaluation, even a pacemaker. "A slightly low resting rate" and "pathological bradycardia" are two different things; **slow rate + symptoms = see a doctor**; slow rate + no symptoms + trained = usually harmless.
5. **"Vessel blocked? Put in a stent and it is settled forever"** → The ISCHEMIA trial (*NEJM* 2020, n=5179, stable coronary disease with moderate-to-severe ischemia): **initial invasive strategy + stent vs conservative medical therapy first — no significant difference in death or heart attack** — stents are life-saving for **acute occlusion** (a heart attack happening right now), but not superior to drugs for **stable plaques**; a stent does not change the diffuse course of the body's vessels, and after the procedure lifelong secondary prevention continues (statin / antiplatelet / pressure and glucose control / no smoking). → Stent = emergency engineering at the blockage point + a propped-open span, not a completion certificate; the blood vessels' account is settled across the whole body.
6. **"Deep-sea fish oil as medicine: lowers lipids, protects the heart"** → See ⑤ reversal three: supplement-grade fish oil has no cardiovascular-benefit evidence and does not lower LDL; long-term high-dose use carries bleeding tendency and other safety uncertainties (MODEL). → To protect the heart, lower LDL first (per your doctor's plan); a fish-oil capsule cannot replace a single prescription drug.
7. **"Asymptomatic hypertension needs no attention"** → See the ② falsification. One ledger line added: in China, 2 of every 5 deaths are cardiovascular, and a large share of that is the direct consequence of "no symptoms, so no management."
8. **"Food can soften vessels / clear plaques"** → See the ③ master answer. No food has been shown to reverse vascular stiffening; what can reverse the plaque trend is disciplined LDL management, on the scale of years.

**The master when-to-see-a-doctor outline**: this volume is a map for understanding, not a prescription. Every **starting / stopping / adjusting of a medication**, every **abnormal test result**, every "suspected" at a red-flag node has the same endpoint — hand it to the doctor. The entire meaning of home measurement and the home gauges is that you arrive before the doctor **carrying your logbook**, not that you replace seeing the doctor.
