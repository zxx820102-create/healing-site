# Skin Health Canon v1 (English) (2026-09-12)

> **In one sentence**: skin is the body's largest organ (about 2 square meters, about 4 kilograms in adults); it is not "an object to be made whiter and smoother" but a wall that renews itself — **most skincare problems are, in essence, wall-maintenance problems; most skincare rumors are, in essence, mistaking the "wall" for a "storefront."**

---

## ① The basics face: skin structure and the barrier — the first principles of skincare

### 1.1 The three-layer structure, plain-words version [FACT]

- **Epidermis (outermost, about 0.05–0.1 mm)**: an avascular layer of "living brick wall." The outermost **stratum corneum** is corneocytes that are already dead yet still functional (bricks), embedded in intercellular lipids (the mortar: ceramides, cholesterol, free fatty acids arranged in roughly equimolar ratio). The whole epidermis completes one renewal from basal layer to shedding about every 28 days.
- **Dermis (middle layer, about 2 mm)**: the skin's "rebar and springs" — collagen provides tensile pull, elastic fibers provide rebound; blood vessels, nerves, sweat glands, and hair follicles all live here. Photoaging, wrinkles, and acne scars are fought mainly in the dermis.
- **Subcutaneous tissue (deepest)**: the fat-dominated "cushion and insulation layer," storing energy and buffering impacts.

[METAPHOR] Think of the skin as a wall: the epidermis is the brick wall (bricks = corneocytes · mortar = lipids), the dermis is the rebar mesh (collagen · elastic fibers), the subcutis is the foam infill (fat). Paint brushed onto the wall (skincare products) mostly affects only the outermost bricks and the paint itself — very little ever drills into the rebar mesh.

### 1.2 Barrier function: the first principle [FACT]

The foundation of all skin function is the **barrier**: keeping out what is outside (microbes, allergens, chemical irritants, part of ultraviolet light) and keeping in what is inside (water, electrolytes). Three measurable pillars:

1. **Stratum-corneum water content** (10%–30% healthy range);
2. **Intercellular lipids** (the mortar between bricks · ceramides are the lead);
3. **Transepidermal water loss (TEWL)** — the lower, the better the "water lock."

Once the barrier is damaged (over-cleansing, over-exfoliating, an eczema flare, alkaline irritation), TEWL rises → water is lost → dryness and scaling → irritants and allergens enter more easily → inflammation and sensitivity. **Much "sensitive skin" is not born; it is built by repeatedly breaking the barrier** [MODEL·mechanistic explanation, direction clear].

### 1.3 What "moisturizing" actually preserves [FACT]

A moisturizer is not "pressing water into the skin" (the skin's water comes from internal circulation, not the outside); it does three jobs [FACT·pharmaceutics classification]:

- **Occlusive**: petrolatum (vaseline), mineral oils — laying a film on the surface, cutting transepidermal water loss by 40%–99% (petrolatum is the upper-bound reference);
- **Humectant**: glycerin, hyaluronic acid — grabbing water from the environment and the deeper layers and holding it in the stratum corneum (in a dry environment an occlusive must accompany it, otherwise the humectant "back-sucks");
- **Emollient**: replenishing the "mortar" components and smoothing the scaling (the ceramide family belongs here).

So "moisturizing" preserves the **stratum corneum's water content and lipid structure**, not "irrigating" the dermis. The causal chain of "drink enough water and your skin is dewy" holds only in severe dehydration; the evidence that everyday extra water changes skin water content is weak [INFERENCE·based on the consistent direction of physiology and clinical evidence].

## ② The acne face: the most common inflammatory skin disease

### 2.1 The four factors of disease [FACT]

Acne is the superposition of four things: ① **abnormal keratinization of the follicular-pilosebaceous duct** (keratin plugging the pore opening); ② **increased sebum production** (androgen-driven); ③ **proliferation of microbes such as Cutibacterium acnes** (the plugged oily environment); ④ **the inflammatory response** (an immune cascade, increasingly seen in recent years as running throughout and even preceding the visible first three). The four factors map onto the targets of every treatment — no single drug hits all four, so moderate-to-severe acne requires combination.

### 2.2 The real positioning of the OTC three-piece set [FACT·onset counted in weeks]

| Ingredient | Which factor it hits | Real positioning | Onset time (weeks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Benzoyl peroxide (BPO)** | Kills bacteria + mild keratolysis | First-line topical for inflammatory papules/pustules; low resistance risk | Weeks to take effect; assess at 4–8 weeks |
| **Salicylic acid (BHA)** | Keratolytic, unclogs pores | A supporting role for comedone-dominant mild cases; concentration 0.5%–2% | Weeks; irritation risk rises with concentration and frequency |
| **Adapalene (a retinoid)** | Corrects keratinization + anti-inflammatory | The core for comedones and non-inflammatory lesions, prevents new acne and acne marks — a first-line choice | **Slow: 8–12 weeks for visible improvement; the first weeks may bring an "acne-flare-like" worsening** |

Three usage logics [FACT·from topical-retinoid reviews and clinical-update guideline direction]: topical retinoids are used at night, require tolerance-building (every other day / pea-sized amount), and are forbidden in pregnancy or when planning pregnancy; BPO bleaches fabrics and needs compatibility care when used with adapalene (fixed combination products have solved the stability problem); **all topical acne medicines are to be applied "over the whole face," not "spot-applied"** — they prevent acne that has not yet appeared. Do not stop for "not working" before onset — **to judge whether an acne product works, give it at least 8–12 weeks** [FACT].

### 2.3 Popping pimples and acne marks: why the hand leaves permanent evidence [FACT→MODEL]

The cost of popping has three layers [FACT]: ① what is not fully extracted is instead pressed deeper toward the dermis; ② the flora of hands and nails introduces infection; ③ mechanical destruction injures the dermis — the direct source of acne scars (true scars). Acne marks come in two kinds, handled completely differently: **red marks (PIE, residual capillary dilation)** fade on their own with time; **brown marks (PIH, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation)** fade with strict sun protection + time + (optionally) brightening actives. **The first means of preventing marks and scars is not a mark-removing product, but: treat early, don't squeeze, protect from the sun strictly** [INFERENCE·the consistent evidence direction of the three factors together].

### 2.4 The see-a-doctor face for moderate-to-severe acne [FACT]

With any of the following, see a dermatologist (stop the self-experimenting): nodules, cysts (deep, hard, tender lumps) — they almost inevitably scar; moderate-to-severe acne unresponsive after 8–12 weeks of topicals; acne with menstrual irregularity, hirsutism, or hair loss (pointing to endocrine problems such as polycystic ovary — the cause needs workup). The dermatologist's effective weapons (short-course oral antibiotics, oral isotretinoin, hormonal-regulation regimens for female patients) all have clear indications and monitoring requirements; this volume only points the way and does not expand prescriptions [scope_note].

### 2.5 The dietary evidence: observational tier, labeled honestly [INFERENCE]

- **High glycemic-load diets**: multiple observational studies (cohorts/case-control) suggest a positive association with acne severity; the mechanism candidate is the insulin/IGF-1/mTOR pathway [INFERENCE·epidemiologically consistent but few intervention trials]. A 2026 review lists ultra-processed diets → insulin-mTOR → lesion inflammation as a mechanism-candidate chain (PMID 42653128).
- **Dairy (with the signal reported more consistently for skim milk)**: an observational association exists, with mechanism candidates (IGF-1, milk-derived hormones), **but intervention evidence is insufficient and the association strength is moderate-to-weak** [INFERENCE·not enough to guide "quit milk to cure acne"; enough to support a low-risk self-trial of "heavy milk drinkers with active acne may try reducing and observe 8–12 weeks"].
- The elimination principle: change one variable at a time, give it a full cycle, keep records — this is the only honest way to turn observational evidence into personal evidence [MODEL].

## ③ The eczema/atopic-dermatitis face: basic therapy is moisturizing, not an accessory

### 3.1 Barrier repair is the "basic therapy" itself [FACT]

One core pathology of atopic dermatitis (AD) is a **barrier-gene defect** (filaggrin mutation is a strong genetic factor) + immune imbalance. Therefore **high-quantity, high-frequency, long-term use of moisturizers (emollients) is guideline-grade basic therapy** — not a "skincare add-on" but a treatment at the same rank as drugs [FACT·consistent direction of European and national guidelines]. Practice logic: do not stop during flares or remissions; apply within 3 minutes of every bath; the amount must be large (for adults, hundreds of grams per week over trunk and limbs is attainable — "being stingy with the amount" is the top cause of moisturizer failure); regular application prolongs remissions and reduces steroid use [INFERENCE·multiple clinical studies in consistent direction]. The 2026 CTMP pathway (Cleanse–Treat–Moisturize–Protect, PMID 42046609) likewise places moisturizing as a formal link in the treatment chain, not the closing touch.

### 3.2 The other side of steroid phobia: the dose-and-frequency logic of proper use [FACT]

"Corticosteroid phobia" is widespread among AD patients and parents of affected children, and is the number-one behavioral factor of poor adherence and treatment failure — fear-driven "apply too little, stop too early, refuse when indicated" produces repeated flares, which instead create the vicious cycle of "needing more steroid" [FACT·consistent multi-country findings with instruments such as TOPICOP]. The logical framework of proper use (specific prescriptions follow the insert and your doctor):

- **Potency tier matched to site**: weak steroids for face, folds, and thin delicate sites; medium-to-strong only for palms, soles, and thick lichenified plaques — "steroid potency" is not "the weaker the safer the better"; a weak steroid that cannot hold thick plaques' inflammation is the true waste;
- **Frequency**: once to twice daily during flares, thin application, usually for no longer than the treatment-course window given by the insert and the doctor; after remission, step down frequency actively rather than stopping abruptly;
- **The core evidence on the worries**: local side effects such as skin atrophy are mainly associated with "long-term, high-potency, wrong-site" misuse — a different thing from "using as prescribed for the course." **Conflating misuse with proper use is the root of steroid phobia's spread** [FACT·review (PMID 42181656) conclusion direction].
- Calcineurin inhibitors and other non-steroid topicals are supplementary options for face/neck and long-term maintenance, weighed by the doctor [scope_note].

### 3.3 The boundary of trigger hunting [MODEL]

Common triggers: dryness with low temperature and humidity, sweat irritation, contact irritants (detergents, wool, fragrance), dust mites/pet dander (some patients), infection (staphylococcal colonization worsens), emotional stress. **The boundaries of hunting**: ① a positive allergen test ≠ that substance is the trigger (many positives are only "sensitization," not "disease-causing"); ② the evidence for extreme dietary elimination is weak — consider targeted avoidance only with a clearly observed, stably repeating "food intake → worsening within 24–48 hours" relationship; ③ large-area flares, oozing, fever — see a doctor first to exclude infection (emergencies such as eczema herpeticum), before lifestyle attribution [FACT+MODEL].

## ④ The sun-protection face: what is prevented is not "tanning," but photoaging and skin cancer

### 4.1 UVA/UVB division of labor, plain-words version [FACT]

- **UVB (medium wave)**: high energy, shallow penetration, strikes mainly the epidermis — **the main culprit of sunburn (erythema)** and the trigger band for vitamin D synthesis; strongest in summer and at midday; ordinary glass blocks it almost entirely.
- **UVA (long wave)**: lower energy, deep penetration (reaches the dermis), **constant all year, passes through ordinary glass** — **the main force behind photoaging ("sun aging") and part of skin cancer**, and it can amplify UVB's damage.
- In one sentence: UVB handles the "pain," UVA handles the "aging"; for UVB look at SPF, for UVA look at the broad-spectrum label / PA or the UVA-in-a-circle logo [FACT].

### 4.2 What the SPF number really means: not doubling logic [FACT]

SPF is defined as "the ratio of the UVB dose needed to produce sunburn with, versus without, the sunscreen." Converted to interception proportions (AAD official framing, verified 2026-09):

- **SPF 15 ≈ intercepts 93% of UVB; SPF 30 ≈ 97%; SPF 50 ≈ 98%; SPF 100 ≈ 99%.**

**From 30 to 100, interception gains only 2 percentage points** — the number's growth is a **log curve of sharply diminishing returns, not doubling** [FACT·AAD sunscreen FAQ + StatPearls NBK537164 cross-consistent]. What truly separates outcomes is not buying a higher number but **applying enough (about 2 mg per square centimeter — roughly a one-coin-sized blob, one pump, for the whole face), reapplying every 2 hours, and missing no spot** — real-world application is commonly less than half the standard amount, and SPF 50 at half dose actually delivers about SPF 7–15 [INFERENCE·dose-protection is nonlinear; laboratory and field studies in consistent direction].

### 4.3 Hard protection first: clothing, hats, umbrellas are the first line [FACT]

Clothing, wide-brimmed hats, sunglasses, parasols — physical shielding's protection "number" far exceeds sunscreen's and does not depend on dose or reapplication: tightly woven dark long-sleeve fabric commonly reaches UPF 30–50+, while sunscreen in real use almost never reaches its labeled value. The guideline-grade ordering is: **dodge (midday shade) → cover (clothing, hat, umbrella, glasses) → coat (sunscreen as the backstop)**. Sunscreen's role is to back up "the face, neck, and hands that cannot be covered," not to back up deliberate sunbathing [FACT·AAD three-step recommendation].

### 4.4 The vitamin D versus sun-protection dilemma: handled honestly [FACT+INFERENCE]

Reports of a higher share of vitamin D deficiency among strict sun-avoiders do exist (the 2026 study of sun-protection culture among Chinese university students, PMID 42582924, is one case). The mainstream dermatology consensus on handling it (Skin Cancer Foundation/AAD framing, verified): **"with sun protection and vitamin D, you can have both"** — deliberately unprotected sun exposure for vitamin D production is not recommended; vitamin D is safely obtained through food (fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified milk) and supplements [FACT·framing]; the efficiency of vitamin-D production by sun varies enormously with latitude, skin tone, season, and age, and generic advice like "sun for 15 minutes" is not generic [INFERENCE]. The honest solution to the dilemma: **keep protecting from the sun, get vitamin D by mouth, let a serum 25(OH)D test tell the truth** [MODEL].

### 4.5 The sunscreen-ingredient safety controversy: evidence tier labeled [FACT (the controversy itself) + INFERENCE (the conclusion)]

- **Physical (inorganic) sunscreens**: zinc oxide, titanium dioxide — act by reflection/scattering + absorption; broad-spectrum, stable, low irritation; drawbacks are whitening and heavy texture. The controversy point: **nano-particle inhalation risk attaches only to spray formulations** (for the face prefer non-spray, or spray into the hand first then apply) [FACT·regulator assessment framing].
- **Chemical (organic) sunscreens**: absorb UV and convert it to heat. Controversy focus: ① some ingredients (oxybenzone et al.) are absorbed through skin to plasma-detectable levels — **"detectable" ≠ "harmful"; the health-outcome evidence is unsettled** [FACT·FDA 2019–2020 systemic-absorption studies + subsequent positions]; ② coral-reef impact is an ecological issue (Hawaii et al. have legislated bans on some ingredients); human safety is a separate matter [FACT].
- **Conclusion tier**: on current evidence, **sunscreen's skin-cancer/photoaging benefits far outweigh the potential risks of the ingredient controversy**; for sensitive skin and infants/toddlers (under 6 months of age: no sunscreen, shielding first), physical sunscreen is the prudent choice [INFERENCE·consistent direction of risk-benefit weighing]. Spreading "chemical sunscreen causes cancer" as settled exceeds current evidence [CONTRADICTED for the strong claim].

## ⑤ The hair-loss face: chronic-disease management, not a miracle race

### 5.1 Androgenetic alopecia: the two-drug evidence and honest expectations [FACT]

Mechanism: follicles sensitive to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) → follicular miniaturization → shorter growth phase, thinner hairs. The two-drug evidence status (verified 2026-09):

- **Minoxidil (mainly topical; oral is an off-label extension)**: multiple candidate mechanisms (vessels / potassium channels / growth-phase extension); **both men and women can use it topically**. The 2025 network meta-analysis (33 studies · Gupta et al. · PMID 40586152) placed minoxidil formulations and finasteride/dutasteride formulations in one comparative framework — **oral finasteride/dutasteride classes rank high overall on hair-density outcomes; topical minoxidil works but with a smaller magnitude** [FACT·the NMA exists and the ordering direction; specific ranking values vary by outcome — see the original text].
- **Finasteride (oral 1 mg, for men)**: inhibits type-II 5-alpha reductase, lowering DHT. Long-term (years) randomized and open-extension data support maintenance and partial regrowth; **sexual-function-related adverse events are reported in systematic reviews, at low incidence and mostly reversible on stopping, but individual risk belongs in a discussion with your doctor** [FACT·2026 systematic review PMID 42396141 exists + conclusion direction; prescription per doctor].
- **Expectation management (the single most critical item)** [FACT·consistent for both drugs]: **stopping loss (stabilizing the shedding process) is the primary outcome; "regrowth (density recovery)" is a secondary outcome some people get**; onset is slow (slowing visible at 3–6 months, assessed at 6–12 months); **gains recede to the natural course within months to a year after stopping**; the earlier the intervention, the less the follicles have been miniaturized past saving, the higher the odds — **a fully smooth, shiny bald scalp region has no follicles left to rescue**.
- Topical finasteride and other new formulations are expanding (PMID 41695992 review); evidence is accumulating [a candidate face in transition from HYPOTHESIS→FACT].

### 5.2 Alopecia areata: the self-limiting face and the see-a-doctor line [FACT+UNKNOWN]

Alopecia areata is immune-mediated patchy hair loss (smooth, round bald patches with sharp borders); **limited patches have a considerable rate of spontaneous regrowth** — a 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of placebo-controlled studies quantified spontaneous regrowth specifically (PMID 41147234), which is also the main source of the placebo arms' "efficacy" [FACT·the meta-analysis exists]. Therefore: a single small patch can take "observe 3–6 months + see a doctor to discuss topical/intralesional options"; **alopecia totalis/universalis, rapid progression, eyebrow or eyelash involvement — go straight to the doctor** (in recent years JAK inhibitors have changed the moderate-to-severe treatment landscape, PMID 41024743 expert consensus). The specific spontaneous-remission proportion varies with study definitions; this canon gives no single number [UNKNOWN·awaiting the meta-analysis's original figures].

### 5.3 Postpartum hair loss: physiological [FACT]

Pregnancy's high estrogen prolongs the growth phase → hormones plunge after delivery → large numbers of follicles enter the resting phase synchronously → diffuse shedding appears 2–4 months after childbirth (telogen effluvium), **usually self-recovering within 6–12 months, no hair-growth treatment needed** [FACT·textbook-grade telogen-effluvium mechanism]. The exceptions worth watching: accompanied by fatigue, cold intolerance, retained weight (check thyroid), patchy shedding (alopecia areata), or no recovery at 12 months — see a doctor for cause-finding [INFERENCE].

### 5.4 The "hair-growth shampoo" scam [FACT]

A shampoo stays on the scalp tens of seconds to a few minutes and **cannot change DHT or follicular miniaturization** (both drugs' pharmacology requires sustained action); an "anti-shedding shampoo" can at most control oil and reduce secondary irritation. Products claiming to be a "hair-growth shampoo" with before/after photos attached have an evidence tier of = marketing [CONTRADICTED for the regrowth claim]. Nationally approved "hair-nourishing" special cosmetics also cover only "adjunctive anti-shedding," a different species from drug efficacy [FACT·regulatory classification framing].

## ⑥ The red-flag chapter: skin is the mirror of the viscera

Any one of the following = see a dermatologist promptly; these are not skincare problems:

1. **The ABCDE melanoma rule** [FACT·international standard]: **A**symmetry, **B**order irregular/blurred, **C**olor non-uniform (brown-black-red-white mixed), **D**iameter >6 mm, **E**volving — changing in size/shape/color/height/symptoms over the short term. Of these, **E (evolving) carries the highest weight** — "a years-old mole suddenly looks different" is far more dangerous than "a big mole from birth" [FACT·review (PMID 41614452) stresses dermoscopy and change history beyond ABCDE].
2. **Non-healing ulcers/wounds**: a break in the skin not healing beyond 4 weeks (especially lower limbs, oral cavity, external genitalia) — skin cancer and systemic disease must be excluded.
3. **Rapidly enlarging "moles" or subcutaneous nodules**: clearly growing, rising, bleeding, and crusting repeatedly within weeks to months — beware melanoma, squamous-cell carcinoma, and skin metastases.
4. **New, persistent, non-fading redness / large-area erythema**: whole-body or extensive diffuse redness with scaling (erythroderma) can secondarily follow psoriasis, eczema, drug eruptions, and lymphoma — emergency-grade, not "an allergy to endure."
5. **Annular erythema + metabolic-abnormality direction**: migratory/persistent ring-shaped erythematous lesions accompanied by abnormal blood sugar or weight change deserve workup toward diabetes and internal malignancy (necrolytic migratory erythema → glucagonoma is the classic but rare "skin-viscera" mirror case) [INFERENCE·the association direction of skin lesions and internal disease, not one-to-one].
6. Additional lines: purpura-like non-blanching petechiae, rash with fever, blistering drug-eruption-like reactions involving eyes/mouth/genitals — the emergency line [FACT].

## ⑦ The falsification face: the six most widespread skincare rumors [epistemic state labeled one by one]

1. **"Drinking collagen replenishes collagen"** — ingested collagen is digested into amino acids and small peptides and **is not shipped preferentially to the face to be rebuilt as skin collagen** [FACT·protein digestion physiology]. Candidate studies of "small peptides have signaling action" exist but are mostly manufacturer-funded with small effects [HYPOTHESIS]. The real protection of skin collagen: sun protection (prevents photoaging breakdown) + balanced protein intake + not smoking [FACT].
2. **"Detox masks / whitening pills"** — "skin detox" has no medical definition: sweat glands' main function is temperature regulation; "detox" is not a measurable skin function [CONTRADICTED]. The status of oral whitening pills: individual ingredients (some polyphenols, cysteine peptides) have small trials; evidence strength cannot support "become whiter" promises, and regulatory notices of illegal prohibited additives detected in some products really exist [INFERENCE·insufficient evidence + safety risk].
3. **"All-natural = gentle"** — "natural" ingredients of unknown concentration, such as fruit acids/lemon essential oil, are precisely major offenders in contact dermatitis; "natural" is a marketing attribute, not a safety attribute [CONTRADICTED for the equivalence claim]. Toxicology's first maxim: the dose makes the poison — natural and synthetic are entirely equal before this [FACT].
4. **"Steroid face": misuse and proper use must be separated** — "steroid-dependent dermatitis" stems from **long-term rule-breaking abuse of potent steroids** (common in no-name "instant whitening/anti-acne creams" with illegal additives, or self-applied potent steroids on the face long-term), and deserves criticism [FACT]; but refusing a doctor-prescribed short course of weak-to-moderate steroids on those grounds conflates prescription drugs with illegal additives — **the object of fear should be "the hidden steroid in illegal additives," not "the regular prescription steroid"** [FACT+INFERENCE·see face ③].
5. **"Homemade masks are safer"** — fruit mash (unknown acid concentration + sugar feeding microbes), egg white (salmonella + allergenic protein), honey-lemon (acid erosion + photosensitization) — homemade mixes cannot be sterilized, cannot hold a stable concentration, and have no preservative system; microbial and allergen risk exceeds industrial products [FACT·microbiology and contact-dermatitis principles]. "Edible = wearable" is a false inference: the skin barrier is not the digestive tract [CONTRADICTED].
6. **"Alkaline water / baking soda to degrease the skin"** — healthy skin's surface is weakly acidic (pH≈4.5–6.5); alkalis (pH 9+) dissolve the barrier's lipid structure and destroy the acid mantle; the short-term "squeaky clean" cost is a damaged barrier and rebound oiliness [FACT·skin pH physiology + MODEL·rebound mechanism]. The principles of cleansing: mild surfactants, lukewarm water, about twice a day — **"washing until tight" = the signal of over-cleansing** [FACT].
