# Cancer Prevention and Treatment Canon v1 (seven faces)

> **In one sentence**: cancer is the program of one's own cells running out of control, not a foreign enemy; about one third to nearly half of cancers are preventable; the four major screenings carry death-level evidence; treatment has an honest survival ledger by stage; and when cure is out of reach, palliative care is a dignified option, not giving up.

---

## ① What cancer is: the program of one's own cells running out of control

### 1.1 The essence (FACT · mechanism is oncology-textbook consensus)

Cancer (malignant neoplasm) is the product of **the body's own cells** whose genetic and epigenetic programs slip out of control after multiple hits: immune-surveillance escape, unlimited proliferation, invasion and metastasis. Its "membership card" is still the host's own genome — and this determines three fundamental properties of fighting cancer:

- **Not a foreign enemy**: it cannot be indiscriminately poisoned the way antibiotics kill bacteria, because cancer cells and normal cells share the same biochemical substrate; every treatment walks a tightrope between "killing the tumor" and "sparing normal tissue".
- **A process, not an event**: from the first driver mutation to a clinically detectable tumor often takes years (this is screening's window); most cancers are the result of multiple hits (genetic susceptibility × environmental/infectious exposure × time), and the very question "what is the single carcinogenic cause" is already distorted.
- **A whole-body-disease view**: the local mass is only the tip of the iceberg above water; staging workup (imaging/pathology/markers) determines treatment strategy precisely because it answers "how far has the program run out of control".

### 1.2 Neoplasm ≠ cancer: the benign/malignant dividing line (FACT)

"Neoplasm/tumor" refers only to a space-occupying lesion formed by abnormal cell proliferation — **it need not be cancer**:

| Dimension | Benign tumor | Malignant tumor (carcinoma/sarcoma) |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Expansive, encapsulated, slow | Infiltrative, ill-defined borders, fast |
| Metastasis | None | Can spread via lymphatics/blood/seeding |
| Systemic effect | Mostly local compression | Cachexia, wasting, lethality |
| Handling | Most observed or locally excised | Needs systemic evaluation and combined treatment |

The **only gold standard of the dividing line is pathology** (cellular atypia, invasion, vascular invasion under the microscope); imaging and tumor markers can only hint, never deliver the final verdict (same framing as `chk:screen-not-diagnose` and `chk:tumor-marker-face`). Note three high-frequency misunderstandings: ① "nodules/polyps/cysts" are mostly benign, but colon polyps (adenomas) are a definite precancerous sequence; ② individual histologically "benign" tumors can be lethal because of location (e.g., intracranial) — benign vs malignant is a spectrum of biological behavior, not black and white; ③ hematologic malignancies (leukemia/lymphoma) can exist without any solid mass.

### 1.3 Naming common sense (FACT · common-knowledge layer)

- Malignant, epithelial-origin = **carcinoma** (adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma…, the overwhelming majority of malignancies); malignant, mesenchymal-origin = **sarcoma**; blood system = leukemia/lymphoma/myeloma.
- Naming convention follows the **primary site**: lung cancer metastasized to liver = metastatic lung cancer, not "liver cancer" — treatment follows the primary cancer's regimen.
- "Cancer" is the folk umbrella term for malignant tumors; "tumor" is often used loosely in speech, and rigorous contexts must distinguish the two.

## ② Primary prevention: the economics of not getting cancer

### 2.1 The honest framing of the preventable fraction (FACT · directly verified)

- WHO's current official-page framing (direct-verified 2026-09-12): **about 38% of cancers could currently be prevented by avoiding risk factors and implementing existing evidence-based prevention strategies**. The "30–50%" often quoted in task briefs is WHO's earlier framing; this canon takes the directly verified 38% as FACT and marks 30–50% as MODEL (archived old framing).
- US 2014 data: **42% of cancer cases and 45% of cancer deaths are attributable to modifiable risk factors** (Islami 2018 · PMID 29160902 · DOI 10.3322/caac.21440 · bibliographic record directly verified). China's high-incidence cancer spectrum differs (a higher infection-related share); this figure is US data, and direct extrapolation warrants caution (MODEL).
- Global burden baseline: GLOBOCAN 2020 estimated about 19.29 million new cases and about 9.96 million deaths worldwide per year (Sung 2021 · PMID 33538338 · CA Cancer J Clin 2021 · bibliographic record directly verified; exact counts not checked verbatim against the original — labeled FACT-bibliographic, numbers defer to the original text).

### 2.2 The main modifiable risk factors (FACT · WHO directly-verified page + attribution studies)

1. **Smoking** — the world's largest attributable death chain; nearly one quarter of cancer deaths are attributable to the chain of tobacco, alcohol, high BMI, low fruit-and-vegetable intake and physical inactivity (wording from WHO's directly verified page). Beyond lung cancer, smoking also contributes more than a dozen cancers including oropharyngeal, laryngeal, esophageal, bladder and pancreatic (interlink `resp:smoking_top_lever`: quitting is respiratory health's first lever, and it is never too late to quit).
2. **Infection** — infection-related factors account for about 30% of cancer cases in low- and middle-income countries (WHO directly verified page):
   - **HBV/HCV → hepatocellular carcinoma**: after Taiwan introduced universal newborn hepatitis B vaccination in 1984, liver cancer incidence in children fell significantly (Chang 1997 · PMID 9197213 · NEJM 1997 · bibliographic record directly verified); antiviral treatment of chronic hepatitis B reduces liver cancer occurrence (interlinks `liver:hbv`, `liver:antiviral-hcc-reduction`, `liver:hcc-surveillance`).
   - **HPV → cervical cancer and oropharyngeal/anal cancers**: the vaccine is the evidence-backed mainstay of "antiviral-style cancer prevention" (see 2.3).
   - **Helicobacter pylori → gastric cancer**: a WHO class-1 carcinogen; eradication therapy can reduce gastric cancer occurrence (interlinks to already-woven anchors `gi:幽门螺杆菌`, `gi:HP一类致癌物` — note this lane did not direct-verify the eradication RCT originals; specific effect sizes defer to the GI canon's anchor chain).
3. **Alcohol** — clearly related to oropharyngeal/laryngeal/esophageal/liver/breast cancers, among others; no "safe dose" consensus exists (guideline framing is "less is better").
4. **Obesity/high BMI** — esophageal adenocarcinoma, colorectal cancer, breast cancer (postmenopausal), endometrial cancer, kidney cancer and a dozen more.
5. **Ultraviolet light** — the main cause of cutaneous melanoma and squamous cell carcinoma; sun protection is cancer prevention (interlinks `skin:uva-uvb`, `skin:hard-photoprotection`).
6. **Air pollution (incl. PM2.5)** — listed by WHO as a class-1 lung carcinogen (mentioned on the WHO directly verified page).

### 2.3 The "cancer-prevention track record" of vaccines and eradication (FACT · bibliographic/already-woven anchor chains)

- **HPV vaccine**: in a Swedish registry cohort of 1.67 million women, those vaccinated before age 17 had about an 88% lower risk of invasive cervical cancer (NEJM 2020; citing the already-woven anchor `vax:lei_2020_nejm`, verified upstream by the vaccine canon); in the England 12–13-year-old vaccination cohort, cervical cancer fell about 87% and CIN3 fell 97% (Lancet 2021; citing the already-woven anchor `vax:falcaro_2021_lancet`). This was the first time in human history a vaccine pushed one cancer toward elimination — cervical cancer thereby became the first cancer with a global elimination timetable (WHO 2030 acceleration strategy) (interlink `vax:hpv_cervical_decline`).
- **Hepatitis B vaccine**: making the newborn first dose of hepatitis B vaccine a national immunization-program item is one of the most cost-effective cancer-prevention engineering projects in public-health history (Chang 1997 bibliographic directly verified + `liver:hbv` anchor).
- **Not direct-verified by this lane, honestly declared**: the gastric-cancer primary-prevention RCT of H. pylori eradication (Wu et al., NEJM 2023) was not hit in two searches by this lane and is dropped per discipline; its effect-size numbers defer to the GI canon's anchor chain. The colonoscopy-screening RCT (NordiCC) is likewise dropped (see receipt).

## ③ Screening: four evidence-backed windows + one controversy

> Screening = systematic examination of **asymptomatic populations**. It buys "earlier detection → lower mortality" and pays "false positives → cascading workups → overdiagnosis". The four-screen framing interlinks with `chk:screen-not-diagnose` (screening is not diagnosis) and `chk:marker-low-ppv` (tumor markers' low positive predictive value); how to read a checkup report is in the checkup canon (`chk:canon-root`).

### 3.1 The four grade-A screening programs (recommended content is the guidelines' current framing · bibliographic direct verification)

| Site | Method | Population (mainstream framing) | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lung** | Annual low-dose helical CT (LDCT) | 50–80 y, ≥20 pack-years, current smoker or quit <15 y | NLST (PMID 21714641 · NEJM 2011 · bibliographic directly verified): LDCT lowered lung-cancer mortality about 20% vs chest X-ray (effect-size number pending verbatim check · MODEL); NELSON (PMID 31995683 · NEJM 2020 · bibliographic directly verified): volume CT screening lowered mortality in high-risk men; USPSTF 2021 recommendation statement (PMID 33687464 · JAMA · bibliographic directly verified) |
| **Cervix** | HPV testing ± TCT cytology | From 21/25 y · after 30, HPV genotyping preferred, 3–5 y interval | USPSTF 2018 recommendation statement (PMID 30140878 · JAMA · bibliographic directly verified); the HPV vaccine + screening two-layer defense see ②2.3 |
| **Colorectal** | Colonoscopy (10-y interval) or annual fecal occult blood/FIT, among options | 45–75 y | USPSTF 2021 recommendation statement (PMID 34003224 · JAMA · bibliographic directly verified): starting at 45; the colonoscopy RCT was not direct-verified by this lane (drop item), effect size honestly downgraded in the receipt |
| **Breast** | Mammography | From 40/45 y, every 1–2 y (guidelines differ) | USPSTF current recommendation statement (PMID 41941217 · JAMA 2026 · bibliographic directly verified · specific age parameters not checked verbatim) |

"Screening" for the East-Asia-high-incidence cancers (stomach/liver/esophagus) is mostly a risk-stratified program for high-risk groups across countries (e.g., regular ultrasound + AFP for hepatitis B carriers, interlink `liver:hcc-surveillance`); its evidence grade differs from the big four above and is not conflated.

### 3.2 PSA: writing the controversy as it is (FACT-bibliographic + INFERENCE)

Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) screening is the textbook case of "screening's price": the European randomized study (ERSPC · PMID 22417251 · NEJM 2012 · bibliographic directly verified) showed PSA screening can lower prostate-cancer mortality, but only in exchange for substantial overdiagnosis and overtreatment; hence the major guidelines do not advocate "everyone tested once at age", but **informed shared decision-making** (from 50, earlier if high risk, decided after discussion with your doctor). This lane did not separately direct-verify the USPSTF PSA recommendation text; its grade-C framing is marked MODEL.

### 3.3 False positives and overdiagnosis: screening's bill (FACT · mechanism layer)

- **The false-positive chain**: abnormal → recheck → biopsy/endoscopy → occasional anxiety and complication risk. Most positive results in population screening are ultimately not cancer — that is a mathematical property, not medical malpractice.
- **Overdiagnosis**: detecting and "curing" indolent lesions that would never have produced symptoms in a lifetime (e.g., some prostate cancers, papillary thyroid cancers, in-situ lesions). It cannot be identified at the individual level; it can only be weighed at the population level.
- **The conclusion is not "don't screen"**: that the four screenings' death-level benefit exceeds their cost is consensus; what should be refused is the evidence-free full-panel "tumor-marker checkup package" (interlinks `chk:marker-panel-placebo`, `chk:more-is-not-better`).

## ④ The treatment honest ledger: the logic and domain of the five weapons

> Principle: **staging decides strategy, pathology decides direction, genomics decides drugs.** Neither deification (no "cures everything" miracle drug) nor stigmatization (cancer is not a synonym for "losing both the person and the money").

### 4.1 The five weapons (FACT · mechanism is oncology-textbook consensus · this face carries no individual PMIDs, see receipt)

1. **Surgery**: the cornerstone of curative treatment of solid tumors — early stage, clean margins, often a cure. Indicated = locally resectable, systemically tolerable.
2. **Radiotherapy**: ionizing radiation directed at destroying tumor-cell DNA. Stereotactic/intensity-modulated techniques take "hit exactly where you aim" to millimeter scale. Indicated = local control (curative) or palliative pain relief such as bone metastases.
3. **Chemotherapy**: systemic drugs that interfere with cell division. Strongest against rapidly proliferating cells — which is both where the efficacy comes from (fast-dividing tumors) and where the side effects come from (hair follicles/bone marrow/GI mucosa divide just as fast). Indicated = systemic/adjuvant/neoadjuvant/palliative; broad spectrum.
4. **Targeted therapy**: "lock-and-key" drugs aimed at specific driver mutations (e.g., EGFR/ALK/HER2/BCR-ABL). Molecular testing is required before use; responders can be controlled long-term, but resistance escape is nearly inevitable and switching or combining is often needed.
5. **Immune checkpoint inhibition**: releasing the "brake" cancer cells put on T cells (PD-1/PD-L1/CTLA-4), restarting the body's own immune attack. Signature = responders can benefit long-term (some advanced melanomas achieve long-term survival), but only a fraction respond, and there is a distinctive spectrum of immune-related adverse events.

### 4.2 Five-year survival, the real numbers: stage is half the verdict (FACT · SEER 21 · 2016–2022 · official page directly verified)

**Female breast cancer**: localized-stage 5-year relative survival **100.0%** (64% of cases) → regional stage **87.5%** → distant metastasis **33.8%**.
**Colorectal cancer**: localized stage **91.3%** (34% of cases) → regional stage **75.2%** → distant **16.9%**.
**Testicular cancer**: overall 5-year relative survival about **95%** (SEER Stat Facts directly verified) — advanced (metastatic) testicular cancer remains curable with cisplatin-based chemotherapy, the benchmark evidence that "advanced ≠ untreatable".

Three plain truths: ① within the same cancer, early vs late stage can differ 3–6-fold in survival — **the economics of early screening all live in this table**; ② five-year survival does not mean "lives only five years" — it is the standard efficacy-and-prognosis metric; ③ population statistics cannot predict the individual (a young metastatic-cancer patient without comorbidities may far exceed the median in a trial).

### 4.3 "Living with the tumor": the realistic option of cancer as a chronic disease (FACT · concept layer)

For some advanced cancers (typical: certain thyroid cancers, prostate cancer, subsets of indolent lymphoma, EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma controlled on targeted therapy), the goal of treatment is not "zeroing out" but **long-term control while maintaining quality of life** — managing tumor burden the way hypertension and diabetes are managed. "Living with tumor" is not a euphemism for abandoning treatment; it is the **formal strategy** when the goal switches from cure to control — and judging when to switch goals is itself one of oncology's core skills.

## ⑤ Palliative care: the dignified option when cure is out of reach

- **What it is**: a care model centered on symptom control (pain/breathlessness/nausea) and psycho-social-spiritual support, with patient and family as one care unit. **It is not giving up**: what is set down is "tumor-kill as the only goal"; what is taken up is "the person as a whole end in themselves".
- **Hard evidence**: in a randomized controlled trial in advanced non-small-cell lung cancer, **early** palliative-care involvement significantly improved quality of life and mood, and median survival was in fact longer (Temel 2010 · PMID 20818875 · NEJM 2010 · bibliographic directly verified; effect-size numbers not checked verbatim · MODEL). This is the benchmark trial that "early palliative care = better living, not earlier dying".
- **Its relation to chemotherapy**: patients at the end of life need informed choice — the expected benefit of continuing experimental treatment, how time will be spent, and where. Evidence direction: high-intensity end-of-life chemotherapy in the advanced setting is associated with worse quality of life and fewer home deaths (directional review consensus · MODEL).
- **The China context**: palliative-care pilot programs are expanding, but access remains low; the family-attitude conflict of "no resuscitation = unfilial" is a real obstacle. What it needs is not technical persuasion but restoring "death is a necessary segment of life" to common sense (intertext with the grief canon — cross-graph anchor not yet woven by this lane · UNKNOWN, pending connection).

## ⑥ Red flags: appear → get checked; neither dally nor panic

> A red flag = a probabilistic signal, not a verdict; but a red flag's whole value is "better ten wasted visits than one missed case". For any item below, follow the framing "unresolved after 2–4 weeks → be seen":

1. **Unexplained weight loss**: unintentional loss >5% of body weight within 6–12 months.
2. **Progressive difficulty swallowing**: solids first, then liquids — the classic progressive curve of esophageal disease.
3. **Blood in stool / black tarry stools** (interlink `gi:` GI red-flag chain): a positive fecal occult blood test and visibly bloody stools both warrant one colonoscopy to characterize; black tarry stools = upper GI bleeding signal.
4. **Painless breast lump** (interlink `fem:canon-root`, the women's health canon): painless + hard + ill-defined + poorly mobile is the dangerous combination; any new postmenopausal lump is worked up without exception.
5. **Non-healing ulcers** (oral/cutaneous/vulvar ulcers unhealed after >2–4 weeks, interlink `skin:canon-root`).
6. Others worth remembering: **progressive painless jaundice**, **blood-streaked sputum** (in smokers), **painless progressive lymph-node enlargement**, **prolonged low-grade fever with night sweats**, **persistent change in bowel habits**, **hoarseness >2 weeks**, **painless unilateral testicular enlargement** (peak incidence in young men · testicular self-awareness).

UNKNOWN first-class-citizen entry: many early cancers have **no** red flags at all — this is exactly why the screening face exists; "no symptoms = no cancer" does not hold, and "red flag = cancer" does not hold either.

## ⑦ The falsification face: falsifiers for seven rumors (each one breakable by evidence)

| # | Rumor | Verdict | Falsifier (what evidence would break this verdict — or what this verdict breaks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "An acidic constitution causes cancer; alkaline water prevents it" | **CONTRADICTED** (the rumor) · blood pH is tightly buffered at 7.35–7.45 by respiration and kidneys; no diet can "alkalinize the constitution"; local acidification of the tumor microenvironment is a result of glucose metabolism, not a cause of constitution | Mechanism-level falsifier: any measurable evidence that "alkaline diets change blood pH". If reproducible data showed human homeostasis reshaped by dietary pH, this item would be voided |
| 2 | "Starve the cancer cells" | **CONTRADICTED** (as a dieting/fasting claim) · targeted starvation requires blocking tumor-specific supply/metabolic pathways (e.g., anti-angiogenic drugs); a patient eating less starves immunity and muscle first (accelerating death through cachexia); nutritional support improving treatment tolerance is nutritional-oncology consensus | Falsifier: any RCT showing fasting/very-low-calorie diets improve tumor outcomes (not meal-replacement adjunct studies) |
| 3 | "Supplements/antioxidants fight cancer" | **CONTRADICTED** · β-carotene supplements in smokers rather raised lung-cancer incidence and mortality (large RCTs · ATBC/CARET direction · bibliographic records not direct-verified by this lane · MODEL); "antioxidant = cancer prevention" has repeatedly failed to win support in population trials | Falsifier: a high-quality RCT showing a supplement lowers population cancer incidence. Note also "unproven benefit" ≠ "benefit falsified" — this verdict only pronounces death on claims repeatedly put to the test |
| 4 | "An ancestral secret recipe cures cancer" | **CONTRADICTED** (as a cure claim) · "secret recipe cures" stories usually come with abandoning standard treatment → measurable survival differences; the verdict does not target [TCM·Traditional] itself — TCM has a legitimate research seat in toxicity reduction, efficacy enhancement and symptom management (annotated independently: its anti-cancer cure claims and its adjuvant toxicity-reduction claims must be evaluated separately; the former has not passed validation by evidence-based standards to date, the latter is a live research field · no disparagement, no unilateral promotion) | Falsifier: a secret recipe passing formal phase I–III trials and demonstrating cure with reproducible pathological response rates |
| 5 | "Chemo hair loss = drained vitality / chemo does more harm than good" | **CONTRADICTED** (the hair-loss part) · hair loss is a **fully reversible** side effect of chemotherapy killing the hair-follicle matrix's fast-dividing cells (regrows months after stopping, sometimes curly); the "drained vitality" intuition comes from visibility bias in side effects — visible hair loss ≠ the gravest damage; the invisible marrow suppression is what needs monitoring | Falsifier: reproducible cases of follicles never regrowing after stopping (other causes excluded) |
| 6 | "Cancer is contagious" | **CONTRADICTED** (the thing itself) · cancer cells cannot survive and spread outside the donor host; everyday contact/shared utensils/hugs/sex itself does not transmit cancer; **what is contagious is the virus**: HPV/HBV/HCV/H. pylori spread by their own routes, and infection raises related-cancer risk — preventing "catching cancer" = preventing the viruses (vaccination / separate utensils & eradication / safer sex); direct transmission is seen only in the extremely rare organ-transplant and pregnancy settings (iatrogenic · extremely low probability under the microscope) | Falsifier: documented person-to-person cancer transmission outside immunosuppression/iatrogenic settings |
| 7 | "Advanced = untreatable / a death sentence" | **CONTRADICTED** (the one-size-fits-all version) · advanced testicular cancer has a high cure rate (overall 5-year survival ~95% in the post-cisplatin era · SEER directly verified); Hodgkin lymphoma and most childhood acute lymphoblastic leukemias being "curable even when advanced" is a milestone fact of chemotherapy history; at the same time it must be honestly admitted: distant-stage pancreatic cancer's 5-year survival is still in the single digits — "advanced" prognosis is highly cancer-specific; ask "which cancer at which stage" to get an answer | Falsifier (two-directional): if some "incurable advanced cancer" population achieved durable disease-free survival under treatment → this verdict strengthens and extends to more cancers; if testicular-style cure rates were generalized to pancreatic cancer → an error in the opposite direction |
