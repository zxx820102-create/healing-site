# Respiratory Health Canon v1 (English)

> **In one sentence**: the respiratory system is the body's only door that, from birth to death, never closes tight and stands directly open to the whole world — every day about ten thousand liters of air pass through this door (textbook framing · relayed), and the door's quality depends on what you let it welcome in: cigarette smoke, pollution, pathogens, or clean air. **Its defining trait: "exposure is not optional; intervention is."**
>
> **How to read this volume**: ① start with the master ledger (an open door · the language of cough) → ② quitting smoking (the single hardest modifiable factor) → ③ asthma (reversible obstruction) → ④ COPD (irreversible loss) → ⑤ sleep apnea (the door blocked at night) → ⑥ lung-cancer screening (the annual checkup for the high-risk) → ⑦ the red-flag chapter and master answers to nine common rumors.

## ① Master ledger face: an open door, and the language of cough

**The respiratory system = an open system (FACT · anatomy consensus)**

The digestive system has the fortress walls of stomach acid and liver; the circulatory system is wrapped in skin — only the respiratory system, from nostril to alveolus, is a wet membrane a few tenths of a micron thin, directly continuous with the outside air. At rest, the air moving in and out of the lungs daily is counted in "tens of thousands of liters" (textbook framing · relayed level; the exact figure floats with build and activity). The cost and the benefit of this design are the same thing: **a huge exchange area (about one badminton court · textbook framing), a thin barrier** — so oxygen gets in fast, and so do smoke, dust, and pathogens. Nearly every story of the respiratory system unfolds from the word "open."

**Cough is a protective reflex, not the enemy (FACT · physiology consensus)**

Mechanical and chemical receptors in the airway wall → signals ascend to the brainstem cough center → deep inhalation → the glottis slams shut → intrathoracic pressure spikes → the glottis bursts open → a high-speed jet of air, reaching several hundred kilometers per hour, blasts out foreign matter, sputum, and irritants. A cough clears the airway the way a sneeze clears the nose. **Coughing does not damage the lungs; it is reporting that there is something in them that needs clearing.**

**Cough duration tiers (FACT · guideline framing, directly verified)**

| Duration | Tier | Main pointers |
|---|---|---|
| <3 weeks | acute cough | mostly respiratory infection and post-infectious; usually self-limited |
| 3–8 weeks | subacute cough | mainly post-infectious cough (the airway-sensitivity recovery period); postnasal drip etc. also needs ruling out |
| >8 weeks (adults) | chronic cough | must find the cause: cough-variant asthma, upper airway cough syndrome, gastroesophageal reflux, eosinophilic bronchitis, drug-induced (e.g., ACEI-class antihypertensives), etc. |

(The adult 3/8-week tiering is the consistent framing of the ACCP / ERS / BMJ Best Practice and other guidelines; the chronic-cough cutoff for children is shorter (about >4 weeks · ERS guideline); this volume uses the adult framing.)

Both directions must be dismantled: a cough is not a disease — **"coughing so hard that you cough yourself into pneumonia" reverses causality** — pneumonia causes the cough; the cough does not cause pneumonia. But "a cough is a good thing, so leave it alone" is the other extreme — **past 8 weeks, or accompanied by red flags (see ⑦), a cough is a symptom that demands cause-finding.**

## ② Smoking-cessation face: the single hardest modifiable factor

**Why "the hardest"**

Among all modifiable health factors, quitting smoking has nearly unmatched evidence strength, breadth of benefit, and cost-effectiveness: tobacco use is comprehensively linked to cancers, cardiovascular disease, and respiratory disease across the whole body (long-standing WHO framing: about half of long-term smokers eventually die of tobacco-related disease · FACT·relayed). It is the common upstream of asthma control, COPD progression, lung-cancer risk, surgical outcomes, fertility, and periodontal health (crossing into the oral-health domain).

**The quit-benefit timeline (FACT · CDC / ACS, multi-source directly verified)**

| After quitting | What happens |
|---|---|
| 20 minutes | heart rate and blood pressure drop |
| 12 hours | blood carbon monoxide falls to the normal level |
| 2 weeks–3 months | circulation improves; lung function begins to recover |
| 1 year | the excess risk of coronary heart disease falls to half that of a continuing smoker |
| 5–15 years | stroke risk falls to that of a never-smoker |
| 10 years | lung-cancer death risk falls to about half that of a continuing smoker |
| 15 years | coronary heart disease risk approaches that of a never-smoker |

The most important plain-words reading of this curve: **the benefits begin at 20 minutes, not "it only counts after ten years quit"**; and it is a real recovery curve, not a straight line — some organs are fast (blood, cardiovascular), some are slow (lung-cancer risk).

**The real mechanics of nicotine dependence (FACT·relayed, no moralizing)**

Nicotine in smoke reaches the brain through the pulmonary veins within seconds to a dozen-odd seconds, acts on nicotinic acetylcholine receptors → dopamine release → the "I want another round" reinforcement loop; after long-term use the receptors upregulate, and stopping brings irritability, poor concentration, increased appetite, craving and other withdrawal reactions — intensity peaks in the first few days and decays naturally over weeks. **Addiction is a pharmacological event, not a failure of willpower or a moral defect**; and precisely because it is a pharmacological event, it responds to medication and structured help — which leads straight into the toolbox below.

**Success rates and relapse, the honest numbers (FACT · directly verified / relayed — honest to the point of unpleasant)**

- The long-term (about 1 year) success rate of unaided "cold-turkey" quitting sits in single-digit percentages (about 3–5% · multi-source relayed);
- **about 75% of relapses happen within the first weeks** (directly verified from a Tobacco Induced Diseases 2023 review);
- relapse is therefore not "proof of failure" — it is the normal shape of this curve; most people who eventually quit successfully went through multiple attempts;
- with a structured program of behavioral support + medication, the success rate reaches several times the cold-turkey rate (direction-level · relayed).

**The toolbox: behavior + replacement + medication, tiered (FACT·relayed; zero prescription for drugs · ask your doctor)**

1. **Behavior layer**: quit clinics / quitlines, mapping your trigger scenes (drinking, stress, after meals), substitute actions, family and peer support, a self-set quit date — behavioral support alone already adds benefit; stacking it with the other layers is better.
2. **Nicotine replacement (NRT)**: patch + gum / lozenge used in combination, giving the receptors a "gradual taper" rather than an abrupt stop — multiple Cochrane reviews support NRT improving long-term quit rates over placebo (relayed).
3. **Prescription layer**: varenicline (a partial agonist / antagonist that partly blocks the reward-circuit receptors) and bupropion, with randomized controlled evidence clearly above placebo (directly verified, direction-level); whether to use them and how — **ask your doctor**; this volume prescribes nothing.
4. **Combination rule**: behavior + medication combined is the first-choice structure jointly recommended by authoritative guidelines (relayed).

**E-cigarettes, the honest ledger (CONTESTED — both lines coexist; no side taken)**

- **Quitting-tool face (strong evidence)**: the Cochrane 2024 review (Lindson et al.) found with high-certainty evidence that nicotine-containing e-cigarettes outperform NRT for quitting (RR 1.59, 95% CI 1.29–1.93); common adverse effects are mouth-and-throat irritation, cough, headache, nausea, mostly easing with continued use (directly verified).
- **Youth-gateway face (observational evidence pointing one way)**: among adolescents who never smoked, using e-cigarettes raises the risk of starting conventional cigarettes — multi-source observational studies point in the same direction (FACT·relayed); the long-term harm spectrum is still accumulating (UNKNOWN).
- This canon therefore makes no one-sided "e-cigarettes good / bad" ruling: **it may help adult smokers who want to quit, while simultaneously constituting an addiction gateway for never-smoking youth — both statements are true at once.** Any concrete choice (as a quitting tool? absolute distance for adolescents?) must rest on the coexistence of the first sentence and the second.

**Falsifying "quitting makes you fat, better to keep smoking" (CONTRADICTED)**

Post-cessation weight gain is real: the BMJ 2012 meta-analysis (Aubin et al.) — mean gain of about 4–5 kg at 12 months, most of it in the first 3 months (about 1 kg per month · directly verified). But lay the two ledgers side by side: on one side, a 4–5 kg gain (a metabolic burden partly offset by diet and exercise); on the other, the full-spectrum risk of continuing to smoke — lung cancer, COPD, heart attack, stroke, peripheral vascular disease. **This ledger has no suspense**; "afraid of getting fat, so keep smoking" is like refusing to defuse a bomb for fear of a scrape.

## ③ Asthma face: reversible obstruction, and the craft of "control"

**Mechanism in plain words (FACT · consensus)**

Asthma = chronic airway inflammation + episodic, **reversible** airflow limitation: inflammation leaves the airway in an "irritable" state; on a trigger, smooth-muscle spasm, mucosal edema, and mucus secretion happen at once → the lumen narrows → wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, cough (sometimes only a cough = cough-variant asthma). The fundamental difference from COPD is **reversibility**: between attacks, lung function can be completely normal.

**Burden framing (FACT · WHO directly verified)**: WHO fact sheet (updated 2026-04) — in 2023 about **363 million** people worldwide were affected by asthma, with about **442,000** deaths (GBD 2023 framing); the earlier GBD 2019 framing was 262 million people / 455,000 deaths (the two framings coexisting is a data-year difference, not a contradiction). GINA has long emphasized: **the vast majority of asthma deaths are preventable** (relayed).

**Controllers vs relievers: the foundation and the fire extinguisher (FACT · GINA consensus)**

- **Controllers (inhaled corticosteroids, ICS, as the cornerstone, often with a long-acting dilator)**: used every day, pressing down the chronic-inflammation foundation — **it does not rescue; its value is in what it does while you are symptom-free**.
- **Relievers (short-acting beta-2 agonists, SABA, such as salbutamol)**: rapidly open the airway in an attack — the fire extinguisher — **but a fire extinguisher is not a fire-safety system**.
- **Signal meaning (FACT · directly verified)**: **"heavy rescue-inhaler use = poor control"** has hard numbers: using ≥3 canisters (200 doses per canister) of SABA per year is associated with elevated risk of severe exacerbations, dose-dependently (ERJ 2020; GINA 2024 summary, same framing); GINA further notes that dispensing of ≥12 canisters a year is associated with elevated death risk (relayed-level). **The more extinguishers you use, the longer the fire source has kept burning — what needs adjusting is the control plan, not buying a few more extinguishers.**

**Inhaler technique = the first determinant of efficacy (FACT·relayed)**

An inhaler is the "delivery system" that gets the drug to the target; delivery failure means even a good drug never reaches the lung. Multiple studies consistently find that over half of users have at least one technique error (relayed). Three practice questions: has a doctor / pharmacist recently watched your technique? Do you know that different devices need completely different actions (some need a slow deep inhalation, some need press-breathe coordination)? Is the device being cleaned / loaded correctly? **When efficacy is poor, check the technique before blaming the drug.**

**Falsifying exercise-induced asthma and "an asthmatic child cannot exercise" (CONTRADICTED)**

Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction (EIB) occurs in about 7–20% of the general population, but in **30–70%** of elite / Olympic-level athletes (directly verified) — asthma is among the most common chronic conditions in Olympic athletes (relayed). This number is itself the answer: **well-controlled asthma not only permits exercise, it permits exercise up to Olympic level**. Exercise is a prescription for the asthmatic child, not a taboo: thorough warm-up, a dry and warm environment, control medication at target underneath; pre-exercise medication plans — ask your doctor. The "protection" of parking the child on the sofa fattens exactly the two factors — obesity and deconditioning — that are more harmful to asthma.

**Trigger management (FACT·relayed)**

Common triggers: allergens (dust mites, pet dander, pollen), respiratory infections (crossing the ⑥ vaccine face), cold air, smoke (including secondhand smoke — interlink with the ② smoking-cessation face), air pollution, some drugs (e.g., the aspirin class, individualized). Management = identify + avoid + pressing down the airway's irritability with controllers — three things done together.

## ④ COPD face: irreversible loss, and the price of "normalizing"

**Burden and causes (FACT · WHO directly verified)**

WHO fact sheet (updated 2026-06): COPD is the world's **third leading cause of death**, causing about **3.4 million** deaths in 2023, about 6% of global deaths; the seventh-largest "unhealthy" burden globally (DALY framing). The cause spectrum is banded: in high-income countries, smoking accounts for over 70%; in low- and middle-income countries, tobacco is 30–40%, and **household air pollution (solid-fuel cooking and heating) is the other major driver** (highly relevant to the household-kitchen scene). Framing calibration: the 2024-11 edition, using 2021 data, listed "fourth (3.5 million)"; the 2026-06 edition, using 2023 data, returned to "third (3.4 million)" — the rank drifts slightly with that year's other-cause data, but **it always sits firmly at the top of the causes of death**; that is the stable conclusion.

**The staircase of irreversible lung-function decline (MODEL · GOLD framework consensus)**

Beneath COPD (including the old names "emphysema / chronic bronchitis") is long-term injury of the airways and lung parenchyma → elastic recoil and airflow-passing capacity **persistently, essentially irreversibly** declining. The diagnostic anchor is spirometry: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC<0.7 (textbook framing). The staircase feel: "morning phlegm treated as normal" → exertional breathlessness explained away as "getting old" → obvious breathlessness → respiratory failure. **Lost lung function is a debit card, not a credit card — FEV1 you spend cannot be topped back up**; and the "silent phase" before symptoms means many people have already lost a large block by the time they first meet a spirometer.

**The lethal misreading of "an old smoker's cough is normal" (CONTRADICTED)**

"Smoked all my life, cough up a bit of phlegm in the morning — that's normal." The lethality of this sentence is that it **translates COPD's earliest symptom, precisely, into "normal,"** thereby systematically postponing diagnosis and cessation intervention. The correct translation: long-term smoking + chronic cough and sputum / exertional breathlessness = **an indication for a lung-function test**, not the background music of a life stage. Every day of normalization is another day of irreversible reserve lost.

**The evidence for pulmonary rehabilitation (FACT · Cochrane directly verified direction)**

Pulmonary rehabilitation (structured exercise training + education + behavioral intervention) has high-quality evidence: significantly improves exercise capacity and quality of life; for people hospitalized after a severe exacerbation, it reduces readmission risk (Cochrane 2016 review direction · directly verified). For a disease that cannot be reversed, rehabilitation's meaning is precisely **maximizing the use-efficiency and life radius of the lung function that remains** — irreversible pathology ≠ irreversible quality of life.

**Exacerbation red flags and the early-treatment window (FACT · consensus)**

The three cardinal signs of an acute exacerbation (symptoms clearly worsening over days): **sputum turning purulent (yellow-green), sputum volume increasing, breathlessness worsening** — when these appear, seek care early; this is the "early-treatment window." Each severe exacerbation brings not only misery and hospitalization risk but also further lung-function loss and worse prognosis (relayed). Annual influenza vaccination and pneumococcal vaccination as indicated (interlink vax:adult_catchup_menu · vaccine canon) are low-cost, high-return lines of defense (WHO directly verified direction).

## ⑤ Sleep apnea face: the door blocked at night

**The high-risk portrait (FACT · consensus)**

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) = repeated upper-airway collapse during sleep → breathing repeatedly stops → blood oxygen repeatedly falls → sleep repeatedly "strangled," with no memory of any of it. High-risk portrait: **loud snoring + witnessed apneas + daytime sleepiness + obesity + morning headaches + hard-to-control hypertension**. Scale: about **936 million** adults aged 30–69 worldwide have OSA, of whom about **425 million** are moderate-to-severe (Benjafield et al., Lancet Respiratory Medicine 2019 · directly verified) — **this is a disease counted in "hundreds of millions," and largely undiagnosed**.

**The STOP-BANG self-screening concept (FACT · official framing directly verified)**

Eight questions, 1 point each: **S** — is snoring loud? **T** — daytime fatigue / sleepiness? **O** — has anyone observed you stop breathing in your sleep? **P** — treated hypertension? **B** — BMI>35; **A** — age>50; **N** — neck circumference>40 cm (16 inches); **G** — male. The official source (stopbang.ca) also gives combination rules (e.g., ≥2 "yes" on the four STOP questions stacked with BMI>35 or neck>40 cm already suggests high risk); the customary banding is 0–2 low risk / 3–4 intermediate risk / 5–8 high risk (relayed-level). **It is not a diagnosis; it is the triage device for "should I get a sleep study"** — intermediate-high risk: bring this table to a sleep clinic.

**Why it must be treated: the comorbidity chain (FACT·relayed / directly verified mix)**

Repeated hypoxia and fragmented sleep → sympathetic activation → **hypertension** (especially the hard-to-control kind), elevated arrhythmia risk (including atrial fibrillation), worsening glucose metabolism; daytime sleepiness → **elevated driving-accident risk** — observational evidence shows CPAP treatment can lower crash risk in drivers with OSA (Tregear 2010 · directly verified direction), with risk correlated to hours of nightly use (relayed). **OSA is not a "sleeping quirk"; it is a comorbidity chain running toward cardiovascular disease and public-safety harm.**

**CPAP's real efficacy and the adherence reality (FACT · directly verified)**

CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) is standard therapy for moderate-to-severe OSA: a stream of air props open the collapsing airway, significantly improving sleepiness, quality of life, and related blood pressure (small magnitude, relayed-level). But the other half must be told honestly: **real-world adherence (≥4 hours/night on ≥70% of nights) is about 30–60%** (observational-study range · directly verified) — mask discomfort, nasal congestion, claustrophobia, noise all grind people down. So treatment is "the beginning of the prescription," not "the end of the prescription": pressure titration, mask fitting, follow-up tuning, humidification and heating — these supports are precisely what push adherence from 30% toward 60%. **The side-effect face (dry mouth, nasal discomfort, swallowed air) is mostly fixable by adjustment, relayed-level.**

**Falsifying "snoring = sleeping soundly" (CONTRADICTED)**

A snore is the sound of airflow vibrating soft tissue through a narrowed airway — **the narrowing and the vibration are the upper airway sounding an alarm, not singing**. Loud snoring + witnessed apneas + daytime sleepiness is OSA's typical face; even short of OSA, habitual loud snoring often comes with degraded sleep quality and reduced daytime function. Mislabeling "sleeps soundly" onto the snore delays exactly the early recognition of this whole ⑤-face comorbidity chain. The loudest sleeper in your home deserves one STOP-BANG.

## ⑥ Lung-cancer screening face: an annual checkup for the high-risk, not for everyone

**NLST: the founding evidence for LDCT screening (FACT · directly verified)**

The National Lung Screening Trial (NLST, NEJM 2011): 53,454 high-risk heavy smokers / former heavy smokers, randomized to annual **low-dose CT (LDCT)** once a year for 3 rounds vs chest X-ray: **lung-cancer mortality fell 20% relatively** (95% CI 6.8–26.7; 6.5-year follow-up); all-cause mortality fell 6.7% relatively. The NCI conversion framing: roughly 3 fewer lung-cancer deaths per 1,000 people screened.

**The screening framing (FACT · directly verified; mind the currency)**

USPSTF 2021: **ages 50–80, a ≥20 pack-year smoking history, currently smoking or quit <15 years**, LDCT once a year; the American Cancer Society (ACS) 2023 update removed the "quit <15 years" restriction (directly verified). Chinese guideline framings differ slightly in age / pack-year combinations (this canon marks UNKNOWN and does not borrow numbers). **Chest X-ray ≠ screening**: NLST's control arm was chest X-ray — the 20% benefit was measured **relative to chest X-ray**; X-ray's detection power for early lung cancer is insufficient to carry the screening duty.

**Tiered calm on nodule follow-up (FACT · Fleischner directly verified)**

The bulk of what screening and checkups find are false alarms: most small nodules are benign. Fleischner Society 2017: solid nodules <6 mm in low-risk people need **no routine follow-up**; those needing follow-up get CT re-scans at 3–24-month intervals set by size / risk; years of persistent stability is among the strongest evidence of benignity. **"A nodule was found" ≠ "I have lung cancer" — nodule management is a calm craft of probability tiering**; hand the follow-up rhythm to the respiratory / thoracic / radiology specialists and the guidelines, not to panic.

**Quitting is never too late (FACT · directly verified + relayed)**

The most memorable line in the CDC timeline: after 10 years quit, lung-cancer death risk falls to half a continuing smoker's — **this curve has no "past the deadline, void" clause**; even after a lung-cancer diagnosis, continued smoking correlates with worse treatment outcomes, and the benefit direction of quitting is clear (relayed). Screening is the insurance; quitting is the principal.

## ⑦ Red-flag chapter: these signals do not wait

- **Coughing up blood** (blood-streaked sputum in any amount — seek care to find the cause at first occurrence; it does not mean lung cancer, but it must be checked);
- **Breathlessness at rest** (winded without moving, or plainly short of breath on slight exertion and progressively worsening);
- **One-sided leg swelling + chest pain / breathlessness combination** = a pulmonary embolism alarm, **emergency department immediately** (especially after long sitting or bed rest, after surgery, during pregnancy and after delivery);
- **Cyanosis of the lips / nail beds** (a hypoxia sign, emergency level);
- **Stridor** (a high-pitched whistle on inhalation = an upper-airway narrowing signal; sudden onset → emergency);
- **Sudden severe chest pain**, with cold sweat / radiation (interlink with the cardio domain's red flags);
- **The "upper respiratory infection not better at 3 weeks" return-visit line**: an ordinary cold should begin improving within 1 week; cough / congestion / runny nose dragging past 3 weeks without resolving, or fever returning after improvement — return and find the cause (connecting to ①'s 3/8-week tiers: reaching the 3-week line = return visit; passing the 8-week line = systematic cause-finding);
- In a person with COPD, the three cardinal signs **purulent sputum / more sputum / worse breathlessness** (the ④-face early-treatment window);
- Loud snoring + witnessed apneas + daytime sleepiness (⑤-face STOP-BANG intermediate-high) → sleep clinic (not an emergency, but stop delaying).

## ⑧ Falsification face: the trial of nine popular sayings (counter-example history is never deleted)

1. **"Coughing until it becomes pneumonia"** (CONTRADICTED · causality reversed): pneumonia is the cause; cough is the symptom — coughing does not produce pneumonia. **But dismantle the other extreme too**: "a cough is protective, so ignore it" also fails — a cough beyond 8 weeks (adults) or with red flags must have its cause found (①-face tiering).
2. **"Expectorants make the phlegm disappear"** (demoted · relayed): the main forces of mucus clearance are adequate hydration + humidification + the cough reflex itself; the common oral "mucus-thinning / expectorant" drugs have limited evidence of changing the disease course — treating them as the mainstay, as a "phlegm-erasing wonder tool," does not hold; whether to use them, ask your doctor.
3. **"IV drips speed recovery"** (CONTRADICTED · relayed): for ordinary respiratory viral infections, intravenous fluids do not shorten the illness; what is "faster" is psychology, not pathology — with infusion-reaction risk added on top.
4. **"Snoring = sleeping soundly"** (CONTRADICTED): the snore is the alarm tone of upper-airway narrowing; loud snoring + witnessed pauses + daytime sleepiness = the OSA portrait (⑤-face).
5. **"A lung nodule = lung cancer"** (CONTRADICTED): most small nodules are benign; a low-risk solid nodule <6 mm needs no routine follow-up even by Fleischner; tiered follow-up is the correct action (⑥-face).
6. **"Quitting makes you fat, better to keep smoking"** (CONTRADICTED): an offsettable 4–5 kg gain vs the full-spectrum death risk of continuing to smoke — the accounts are not in the same order of magnitude (②-face).
7. **"An asthmatic child cannot exercise"** (CONTRADICTED): EIB in Olympic-level athletes is 30–70% — well-controlled asthma can go all the way to the top; the sofa is the real harm (③-face).
8. **"The veteran smoker's morning cough is normal"** (CONTRADICTED): this is COPD's earliest translation error; normalizing = delaying diagnosis = irreversible reserve lost (④-face).
9. **"Chinese medicine has no side effects — regulate the breathing freely"** (CONTRADICTED · separately labeled [TCM·Traditional]): this item does not deny the value of Chinese medicine or its pattern-differentiated use; it falsifies only "no side effects, so use it freely" — all drugs carry adverse-effect and interaction faces; respiratory "regulating" formulas equally need pattern differentiation, proper channels, and telling your doctor what you are taking; self-directed long-term high-dose use of any "regulating" product does not hold.
