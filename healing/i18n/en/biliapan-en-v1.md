# Gallbladder & Pancreas Canon v1 (English · the 2026-09-12 volume)

The gallbladder and pancreas are the two most easily misunderstood organs of the digestive system: the gallbladder gets treated as "a bag for flushing out stones" and "a useless pocket," while the pancreas — deep-seated and ferocious at onset — is often dismissed as "stomachache" until it turns critical. This canon compresses the specialty face into seven faces: **gallstones (stratified) → cholecystitis & bile ducts (recognizing emergencies) → pancreatitis (the two big causes) → pancreatic-cancer warnings (combined signals) → post-op life (adaptation realities) → red flags (hard lines) → falsification face (anti-scam firewall)**. Intended readers: people whose checkup report says "gallstones/gallbladder polyps/pancreatic duct dilation," families where someone has had pancreatitis, and everyone who wants to finally settle "can the gallbladder really be removed or not."

Epistemic labels (five states): FACT = authoritative-source data verified via search this round; MODEL = mechanism/framing estimate; INFERENCE = action judgment derived from evidence; UNKNOWN = not yet verified, not claimed; CONTRADICTED = claims already shattered by evidence (each myth carries its falsifier). **This canon does not replace a doctor**: all diagnosis, surgery, and medication decisions follow the physician and the latest guidelines.

---

## ⓪ Master line: the biliary-pancreatic unit, emergencies first

Bile is generated by the liver (upstream in the liver-metabolic canon, `liv:胆汁生成`); the gallbladder is merely the storage-and-concentration bag; the bile duct and pancreatic duct converge at the duodenal papilla — **so a biliary problem can detonate the pancreas (biliary pancreatitis), and a swollen pancreas can crush the bile duct shut (obstructive jaundice)**. The two organs must be understood together. The second law: this field has an extremely high density of emergencies (acute cholecystitis, cholangitis, and necrotizing pancreatitis can all become lethal within hours to days), and **any "regulation/stone-flushing/diet-therapy" advice yields immediately to seeking care once a red flag appears** (same law as the first-aid canon `fa:急症识别`).

## Face 1 Gallstone face: prevalence is the "norm"; management is "stratification"

### 1.1 The real prevalence numbers: one or two in ten adults carry stones

- Adult gallstone prevalence is about **10–15%** (FACT · EASL 2016 clinical practice guideline, J Hepatol 2016;65:146-181, PMID 27085810, European framing; most are asymptomatic). Female sex, increasing age, obesity, and family history raise risk; men and the very old also carry substantial prevalence (MODEL · composition framings vary by population, not frozen).
- Most stone carriers remain **asymptomatic for life** — an ultrasound report of "gallbladder stones" without any symptoms is a "norm," not a "verdict" (INFERENCE · jointly derived from prevalence and natural-history data).

### 1.2 Should asymptomatic stones be removed: stratified logic, not one-size-fits-all

**Natural history is the stratification's evidence base**:

- The Gracie & Ransohoff classic cohort (NEJM 1982;307:798-800, PMID 7110244): over follow-up of asymptomatic stones, symptom emergence was low — **about 10% at 5 years, about 15% at 10 years, about 18% at 20 years** (APPROX · abstract-level citation), and most first symptoms were not complications but manageable biliary colic — the conclusion that "the innocent gallstone is not a myth" (FACT · the title is literally "the innocent gallstone is not a myth").
- The Italian population cohort (Festi 2010, J Gastroenterol Hepatol 2010;25:719-724, PMID 20492328): over follow-up most stone carriers stayed asymptomatic; expectant management (watchful waiting) is feasible in those without high-risk factors (FACT · conclusion-level).

**Stratified action (INFERENCE, synthesizing EASL 2016 and the natural history above)**:

1. **Repeated typical symptoms (biliary colic)** → laparoscopic cholecystectomy is standard management (FACT · EASL guideline direction-level).
2. **Asymptomatic but with high-risk morphological indications** → discuss prophylactic removal; indications include **porcelain gallbladder (wall calcification), stones ≥3 cm, gallbladder polyps with adverse features, localized adenomyomatosis, and similar** (FACT · indication directions enumerated by EASL 2016; single-item evidence strength in §1.3/§1.4); **those with coexisting diabetes** face higher proportions of severe presentations at acute flare, so the surgical threshold is lowered (INFERENCE · guideline-hint level; the surgeon evaluates specifics; diabetes management in `diab:血糖管理总纲`).
3. **Asymptomatic and without high-risk indications** → observation (expectant management) is the safe option jointly supported by guidelines and natural history (FACT); no anxiety-driven gallbladder removal for "fearing cancer while keeping it"; periodic follow-up ultrasound suffices (interval as the doctor directs; the checkup face interlinks `chk:超声检查`).

### 1.3 Porcelain gallbladder: state the risk honestly — the historical numbers have been revised down

The porcelain gallbladder (extensively calcified wall) was once written into textbooks as high cancer risk (historical estimates in double-digit percentages) — **modern literature has revised this risk sharply downward**: Schnelldorfer's re-examination found its association with gallbladder cancer far weaker than the historical framing (J Gastrointest Surg 2013, PMID 23423431); Brown & Geller argued in the same period for re-evaluation on modern evidence (Arch Surg 2011, PMID 22121531). The current framing: porcelain gallbladder remains on the prophylactic-removal indication list, but the risk magnitude is low single-digit percentages, not "teens to twenties" (MODEL · modern-review framing, varying across studies, no single number frozen). **Admitting the historical estimates overshot ≠ denying the indication** — a calcified gallbladder blinds ultrasound surveillance, which is a retained risk (INFERENCE).

### 1.4 "Eat blandly and the stones will pass": falsified

- Cholesterol-stone mechanism is bile's three components (cholesterol/phospholipids/bile acids) falling out of balance into supersaturation + nucleation (MODEL · classic physicochemical mechanism); once a stone has formed, **diet cannot "flush" a formed stone out** (CONTRADICTED · for formed gallbladder stones, "eat blandly to pass stones" has no evidence; falsifier: if stones really could be passed, EASL and national guidelines would not restrict dissolution drugs to extremely narrow indications with month-scale courses — and dietary adjustment has never been among the treatment options).
- Diet's real relationship is at the **prevention level**: regular meals (prolonged fasting favors bile stasis), weight control but **avoiding rapid weight loss** (rapid loss actually promotes stones), moderate dietary fiber and coffee intake associated with lower stone-formation risk (MODEL · epidemiological association level, Diehl review Gastroenterol Clin North Am 1991, PMID 2022415; effect sizes not frozen). **Eating blandly matters for "growing fewer new stones and triggering fewer flares," not for "passing the ones already there"** (INFERENCE).

## Face 2 Cholecystitis & biliary face: the golden window for recognizing emergencies

### 2.1 Acute cholecystitis: right-upper-quadrant pain + fever; ultrasound is first-line

- Typical onset: persistent right-upper-quadrant/upper-abdominal pain (often escalating from biliary colic), fever, nausea and vomiting. **Murphy's sign**: press deeply below the right costal margin and have the person inhale deeply — the inflamed gallbladder meeting the pressing hand arrests the breath — a classic physical-exam sign, included in the TG18 diagnostic criteria (FACT · Tokyo Guidelines 2018 diagnostic criteria, Yokoe et al., J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2018;25, PMID 29032636).
- **Ultrasound is the first-line imaging** (FACT · the TG18 criteria include wall thickening, ultrasonographic Murphy sign, etc.; the checkup face interlinks `chk:超声检查`).
- Management at direction level: antibiotics + early (or delayed) laparoscopic cholecystectomy, stratified by severity by the surgeon (FACT · TG18 treatment-strategy direction-level; specific surgical timing = physician decision).

### 2.2 Acute cholangitis: Charcot's triad = the emergency within emergencies

- **Charcot's triad = abdominal pain + fever with chills + jaundice** — suggesting bile-duct obstruction with infection (FACT · the classic definition retained by TG18, Kiriyama et al., J Hepatobiliary Pancreat Sci 2018;25, PMID 29032610).
- Two honest notes: ① the triad is **highly specific but insensitive** — only a subset of patients shows all three; missing one or two does not exclude cholangitis (FACT · the TG18 criteria explicitly distinguish "definite/suspected" tiers); ② the triad plus shock + altered consciousness = Reynolds' pentad, signaling acute obstructive suppurative cholangitis, **lethal within hours** (FACT · textbook-level consensus; this volume lists UNKNOWN for the specific historical provenance, not separately verified — handled by the emergency protocol without waiting).
- Action card (INFERENCE): **cholangitis = emergency department the same day**, not "take some anti-inflammatories and watch."

### 2.3 ERCP stone extraction: the modern standard answer for bile-duct stones

- For stones in the bile duct (not the gallbladder), standard management is **ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography) stone extraction + sphincterotomy** — minimal trauma, and able to drain suppurative cholangitis in the same session (FACT · ESGE 2019 clinical guideline, Manes et al., Endoscopy, PMID 30943551).
- Three common-sense points: ERCP is a "through-the-mouth endoscopic procedure," no laparotomy; its main risk is post-ERCP pancreatitis (below double-digit percentage, procedure-related, FACT: within ESGE guideline discussion scope); **ERCP removes the bile-duct stones — the gallbladder stones remain, so elective cholecystectomy usually follows** (INFERENCE · mainstream guideline strategy direction; the surgeon decides specifics).

## Face 3 Pancreatitis face: the two big causes and the honest ledger of severe disease

### 3.1 The two big causes: biliary vs alcoholic

- The two big causes of acute pancreatitis: **gallstone obstruction (biliary, first in China and most populations) and heavy drinking (alcoholic)**; the rest are hyperlipidemia, drugs, post-ERCP, etc. (FACT · ACG 2024 guideline, Tenner et al., Am J Gastroenterol 2024, PMID 38857482; composition varies by population as a framing matter).
- The direct mechanism of biliary pancreatitis = a small stone lodging at the ampulla (MODEL · classic mechanism); **managing biliary pancreatitis = clearing the bile-duct stones and the gallbladder in the same admission or electively**, otherwise recurrence is high (FACT · guideline direction-level; recurrence prevention in the recurrent-pancreatitis consensus, Pancreas 2018, PMID 29894415).
- "Pancreatitis recovered, so drinking is fine again" is a high-risk rumor — continued drinking in alcoholic pancreatitis markedly increases recurrence and chronicization (CONTRADICTED → see Face 7; abstinence is the only repeatedly validated core intervention, FACT: guideline direction-level).

### 3.2 Recognition: a feast + persistent upper-abdominal pain

- Recognition card (INFERENCE): **after binge eating/heavy drinking, severe persistent upper/upper-central abdominal pain radiating in a band to the back, slightly eased by sitting up and leaning forward, with vomiting that does not relieve the pain** — "fine once vomited" is the stomach; "still painful after vomiting, worse with every hour" should summon the pancreas (INFERENCE · derived from clinical features and gallstone prevalence). The severe-disease differential of postprandial upper-abdominal pain interlinks the GI canon `gi:红旗分流优先`.
- Blood lipase/amylase + ultrasound to find stones is the standard starting point (FACT · ACG 2024 direction-level; lipase more specific than amylase is consensus framing).

### 3.3 The severity ledger: stating necrotizing pancreatitis mortality honestly

- Most acute pancreatitis is mild and self-limited; but about one to two in ten progress to **severe/necrotizing pancreatitis** — pancreatic tissue necrosis, infection, organ failure (FACT · review framing, Vege et al., Gastroenterology 2026 "The Many Challenges of Necrotizing Pancreatitis", PMID 42462828; proportion APPROX).
- **Mortality stated honestly**: necrotizing pancreatitis mortality is double-digit (previously reported on the order of 15–30%, higher in infected necrosis; declining in recent years with minimally invasive approaches but still heavy) (APPROX · review-level citation, no specific number frozen; absolute figures belong to the treating physician's assessment). This is not a disease of "a few IV days and done" — **recognition, speed of seeking care, and swallowing no folk remedies before admission decide the trajectory** (INFERENCE).
- Direction-level treatment common sense: early generous fluid resuscitation (volume and rate titrated by the physician), early enteral nutrition, ICU for the severe; the old idea of "starve until the pain fades" has been replaced by "early oral/tube enteral nutrition" (FACT · ACG 2024 direction-level).

### 3.4 The recovery diet staircase and pseudocyst follow-up

- Recovery diet staircase (INFERENCE, guideline direction-level): after mild disease settles, **return early to oral low-fat soft food** (no prolonged fasting required); low-fat, small frequent meals, no alcohol in the weeks after discharge, gradually transitioning back to normal eating — both "rice porridge and pickles for a month" and "feast the day of discharge" are the two wrong extremes (boundary: specifics set by the nutrition and treating team).
- **Pseudocyst (pancreatic pseudocyst) follow-up**: after severe/necrotizing pancreatitis, fluid collections/walled-off necrosis can form, most of which resolve spontaneously — **asymptomatic and stable ones need only imaging follow-up; intervention only for compression, infection, continued growth, or persistent symptoms** (FACT · direction-level consensus within ACG 2024 and Vege 2026 discussion scope; follow-up intervals = physician-set; `chk:影像随访` interlink). The core of follow-up discipline is "recheck on schedule + never mask symptoms": abdominal pain re-worsening, fever, early satiety with vomiting = return to hospital (INFERENCE).

## Face 4 Pancreatic-cancer warning face: silent progression and the combined signals

### 4.1 The reality of silent progression

Pancreatic cancer grows retroperitoneally, early on painless and non-jaundiced, and is usually already late when typical symptoms appear — **it is one of the very few cancers essentially invisible to routine checkup packages**: routine chest X-ray and ordinary blood chemistry do not target it; abdominal ultrasound can miss pancreatic-tail lesions behind bowel gas (MODEL · test-capability-boundary framing). This is not an invitation to panic but to **knowing the combined signals + high-risk people screening proactively** (screening protocols stay unfrozen: follow the physician and the latest guidelines).

### 4.2 CA19-9: a follow-up and treatment-monitoring tool, not a screening tool

- CA19-9's real role: an auxiliary marker for **treatment monitoring / recurrence follow-up** in diagnosed pancreatic cancer patients (FACT · clinical-use consensus).
- **CA19-9 cannot be used for population screening**: neither sensitivity nor specificity suffices — cholangitis, cholecystitis, cirrhosis, indeed other digestive diseases all raise it; benign false positives coexist with early-cancer false negatives (FACT · Barugola et al. "Is CA 19-9 a screening marker?" Dig Liver Dis 2009, PMID 19297258); its value is likewise limited in screening studies of new-onset diabetes populations (FACT · Choe et al., Hepatobiliary Pancreat Dis Int 2018, PMID 29752133).
- Falsifier (same source as Face 7): if CA19-9 could screen, guidelines would long since have put it into routine checkups — no mainstream guideline has ever done so (CONTRADICTED · "one CA19-9 test can detect pancreatic cancer").

### 4.3 The combined signals: new-onset diabetes + jaundice + wasting

Each signal alone can be common; **in combination they are a yellow alert** (INFERENCE):

1. **New-onset diabetes**: pancreatic cancer can be diagnosed shortly after diabetes onset — the pancreatic cancer risk of new-onset diabetes populations is significantly higher than ordinary people's (FACT · Scherübl, Gastroenterology 2026, PMID 41740862; external validation of the ENDPAC model in a UK family-practice cohort, Br J Gen Pract 2026, PMID 42392832). **New-onset diabetes after 50, without obvious common drivers such as obesity, and accompanied by wasting** = worth discussing with a doctor "whether to do one targeted examination for the pancreas" (INFERENCE; the diabetes master face `diab:新发糖尿病` interlink).
2. **Painless jaundice (yellowing skin and eye whites + dark urine)**: the first presentation of a pancreatic-head lesion crushing the bile duct — **painless does not mean unimportant; quite the opposite** (INFERENCE · derived from anatomy and clinical framing).
3. **Unexplained wasting + persistent upper-abdominal/back pain**: marked weight loss within months with no dietary or exercise explanation (INFERENCE).

### 4.4 The family-history face

First-degree relatives with pancreatic cancer, or families carrying clear predisposition syndromes (such as hereditary pancreatitis or specific gene-mutation lineages), belong to the high-risk group — **entitled to enter periodic surveillance programs** (FACT · high-risk surveillance is consensus direction; specific protocols and starting age stay unfrozen, follow the genetics clinic and latest guidelines). Smoking is a modifiable risk factor; quit and it falls (FACT · direction-level).

## Face 5 Post-operative life face: how to eat after gallbladder removal

### 5.1 Post-cholecystectomy diarrhea: common, mechanism clear, relievable

- A considerable share of people experience **more frequent/looser stools** after gallbladder removal — the literature ranges from about ten to twenty percent (APPROX · Mao et al. BMC Gastroenterol 2025 reported incidence and influencing factors, PMID 40217192; large variation across studies, not frozen).
- Mechanism = **bile-acid related**: with the "bile warehouse" gone, the liver's continuously secreted bile acids enter the intestine "open-loop" after meals; excess bile acids reaching the colon stimulate motility and secretion (MODEL · bile-acid diarrhea mechanism, Thirkettle et al. Br J Gen Pract 2026, PMID 41478638; Maqsood et al. review Cureus 2026, PMID 42291991). Post-operative gut microbiota composition also shows measurable changes (FACT · Ye et al. BMC Microbiol 2026, PMID 42374196 — association level; mechanistic attribution UNKNOWN, no overreach).
- Action card (INFERENCE): most people **adapt on their own within months**; meanwhile low-fat small frequent meals and cutting caffeine and alcohol irritation relieve most of it; for those unrelieved, the physician can manage along the bile-acid-diarrhea direction (drug direction-level, no prescriptions written). "No gallbladder means lifelong diarrhea" is a rumor amplified from a minority's experience into the universal (CONTRADICTED · contradicted by the incidence data).

### 5.2 "People without a gallbladder can't eat oil": falsified

- After cholecystectomy **fat is digested as usual**: the liver's bile-acid secretion is fully preserved; only the "mealtime concentrated-release" warehouse is lost, so early on a large fatty meal may more easily cause diarrhea and bloating (MODEL · the natural corollary of §5.1's mechanism).
- The correct posture is **gradual adaptation**: low fat early after surgery, stepping back up to normal fat amounts over weeks — the great majority recover to "anything can be eaten, only heavy oil and heavy spice in moderation" (INFERENCE · mechanism + post-operative study framing; no evidence supports lifelong oil abstinence). Falsifier: if "no gallbladder, no oil" were true, steatorrhea and malnutrition would be epidemic in the post-cholecystectomy population — cohort data do not support it (CONTRADICTED).
- At the long-term-study level, association studies linking cholecystectomy to metabolic outcomes (such as colorectal-cancer/cardiovascular risk signals in some populations) remain **contested and confounded**; this volume records it honestly as UNKNOWN: no freezing, no panic — maintaining healthy diet and weight is the common denominator anyway (honest-boundary clause).

### 5.3 Blood-glucose management after pancreatic surgery (preview face)

After pancreatic surgery (tumor or necrosis removal), depending on the amount of pancreatic parenchyma resected, **new-onset diabetes or worsening of existing diabetes** can appear (pancreatogenic diabetes, brittle) — this is the hard reason post-operative follow-up carries blood glucose (FACT · direction-level consensus; this volume lists UNKNOWN, no dedicated paper verified; to be expanded if a pancreatic-surgery volume is ever built). The master face of post-operative glucose management interlinks the diabetes map `diab:血糖管理总纲` — no wheel reinvented.

## Face 6 Red-flag face: any of these, go to the emergency department that evening

**Red-flag list (INFERENCE · all items direction-level; any one = seek care immediately, no internet self-diagnosis, no painkiller-first symptom suppression)**:

1. **Right-upper/upper abdominal severe pain lasting hours without relief** (especially with nausea/vomiting, pain to the point of doubling over) — the doorway of biliary colic escalation, acute cholecystitis, pancreatitis;
2. **Fever with chills + yellowing skin/eye whites (jaundice) + abdominal pain** = Charcot's triad, acute cholangitis emergency (§2.2);
3. **Painless jaundice + urine dark as strong tea** — an obstruction-face signal, seen the same day;
4. **Persistent upper-abdominal pain + repeated vomiting after binge eating/drinking** — manage as possible pancreatitis (§3.2);
5. **Persistent vomiting unable to eat, vomiting blood or black stools** — upper-GI obstruction/bleeding direction (`gi:红旗分流优先` interlink);
6. **Clay-colored (gray-white) stools** + dark urine — the "stool evidence" of biliary obstruction;
7. Pallor, cold sweats, confusion, low blood pressure — shock precursors, **call an ambulance, do not wait** (`fa:急症识别` interlink).

The reverse reminder matters equally: **chronic indigestion / occasional brief bloating pain without red flags does not belong on this list** — handle per the GI canon's stratification; no need to rush to the ER every time (avoiding overcorrection).

## Face 7 Falsification face: the six most-circulated lies and their falsifiers

Each item's format: **rumor → falsification → falsifier (what evidence would overturn this falsification)**. A falsification is itself falsifiable — that is the discipline.

1. **"Gallbladder-preserving stone removal is better — keep the organ"** → the honest ledger: gallbladder-preserving stone extraction (endoscopic/choledochoscopic removal keeping the gallbladder) has one core weakness — **stone recurrence** — the stone-forming environment (the gallbladder with imbalanced bile) is unchanged; the literature frames long-term recurrence rates in double-digit percentages (APPROX · Du et al. Wideochir Inne Tech Maloinwazyjne 2020, re-thinking indications and recurrence prevention, PMID 32117490; specific numbers vary widely across studies, not frozen), so mainstream guidelines have not listed it as standard treatment, restricting it to strictly selected scenarios (FACT · not included in routine recommendations by EASL 2016). It is not a scam; it is a **very-narrow-indication technique** — the pit opens only when marketing talk wraps it into a universal choice, "better, non-invasive, organ-preserving" (INFERENCE). Falsifier: long-term (>5–10 year) large-sample controlled studies showing recurrence reduced to near cholecystectomy's "stone-free rates" with non-inferior complications would require this falsification to be rewritten.
2. **"Stones that don't hurt need no attention"** → half true, half false: observation of most asymptomatic stones is safe (§1.2 natural history), but "no attention" is wrong — **attention = stratified assessment + periodic follow-up**; where porcelain gallbladder/≥3cm/polyp features and other indications exist, observation is the wrong option (CONTRADICTED · for the "completely ignore" part; falsifier: should natural history and guideline indications be rewritten by new evidence — e.g., high-risk indications proven to carry no prognostic meaning — this item downgrades).
3. **"After gallbladder removal the person is ruined; digestion is finished"** → the facts: the great majority recover to near-normal eating and life (§5.2); short-term diarrhea is common and mostly relievable (§5.1). Refusing indicated surgery for cholecystitis/gallstones out of fear of being "ruined" is trading a rumor for complication risk (CONTRADICTED; falsifier: long-term cohorts showing epidemic post-operative digestive failure/malnutrition would require this item to be rewritten).
4. **"Pancreatitis recovered, so drinking is fine"** → for alcoholic pancreatitis, abstinence is the only repeatedly validated recurrence intervention — continued drinking = high risk of recurrence and chronicization, and chronic pancreatitis disables through pain while pancreatic insufficiency and cancer risk climb (FACT · guideline direction-level; falsifier: a reliable cohort proving moderate-low drinking does not raise recurrence could partially loosen the "any amount" wording — current evidence does not support it).
5. **"Gallbladder-protecting supplements/secret formulas can flush stones out"** → same source as §1.4: formed stones are not "flushed out" by diet or supplements; what so-called "stone-flushing therapies" actually induce is **biliary colic and even acute pancreatitis** — using a "flushing ritual" to provoke a silent stone into an emergency is this field's most classic self-harm (CONTRADICTED; falsifier: a rigorously controlled trial proving some intervention stably dissolves/passes stones without flare risk — existing evidence is zero, and dissolution drugs themselves carry only extremely narrow indications with month-scale courses). Also: the gallbladder "detox" concept has no anatomical counterpart (the gallbladder is a concentrated-storage bag, not a "toxin vault").
6. **"One CA19-9 test can screen for pancreatic cancer"** → §4.2: double insufficiency of sensitivity and specificity + benign false positives, no guideline recommending it for population screening (CONTRADICTED; falsifier: prospective population studies showing its single-marker sensitivity/specificity reaching screenable thresholds — none exist today).

## Close: the one-page memory card of the biliary-pancreatic face

- Far more people carry stones than imagined (10–15%); **whether to remove depends on stratification, not fear**; observation is not neglect — follow-up is the management.
- **Unrelenting right-upper-quadrant pain, fever with chills plus jaundice, upper-abdominal pain with vomiting after a feast, painless jaundice, clay-colored stools** — five red flags, emergency department that evening.
- Pancreatitis's two big causes (gallstones/alcohol): governing the mouth (regular meals, abstinence) is harder evidence than any "gallbladder-protecting supplement"; necrotizing pancreatitis mortality is double-digit — don't gamble.
- New-onset diabetes + wasting, CA19-9 for follow-up only never screening, high-risk family history deserving targeted surveillance.
- Losing the gallbladder is not being ruined: adapt gradually, oil can be eaten, diarrhea will settle; after pancreatic surgery, remember to bring blood glucose into follow-up.
