# Oral Health Canon v1 (English) (2026-09-12)

> **Answering the owner's directive**: "Research around the full range of human needs — actually solve problems."
> This volume fills a completely blank block of the human-needs full spectrum: **oral health** — caries is the world's most common health condition under the GBD 2021 framing (FACT · WHO official framing), the mouth is the hub organ of eating, socializing, speech, and intimacy, and the periodontal-systemic evidence face is growing fast. High frequency + hard need + blank = a high-leverage topic.
> **Honesty discipline**: all hard numbers verified to PMID-title level; five key conclusions (powered toothbrushes, periodontal treatment × HbA1c, cleaning frequency, water fluoridation 2024, fluoride × osteosarcoma) extracted to **abstract-verbatim sentences**; epistemic states never promoted unilaterally; this volume is an **evidence map + action cards**, not a diagnostic tool — the judgment to seek care always belongs to the clinician.

## 0. One-line collapse

**Oral disease is the world's most prevalent chronic disease (FACT), but its two main diseases (caries, periodontitis) have intervention windows that open extremely early: the three levers — sugar frequency × fluoride exposure × mechanical removal — all sit in individual hands; gingivitis's reversibility is a discipline question, periodontitis's irreversibility is a timing question; the oral-systemic face (diabetes/cardiovascular/preterm birth/cognition) carries strong association evidence while causation mostly remains unadjudicated — the action cards are ordered by "cheap, reversible, low-risk first."**

Epistemic labeling: epidemiology and Cochrane anchors = FACT; mechanism chains and models = MODEL; personal action extrapolated from evidence = INFERENCE; observational-frontier causation = UNKNOWN; the five rumor classes = CONTRADICTED.

## 1. The basic-science face

### 1.1 Disease burden: why this card is worth playing first (FACT)

- **WHO official framing (fact sheet actually captured · 2026-09-12)**: an estimated nearly **3.5 billion people** worldwide have oral disease; **"untreated caries in permanent teeth is the most common health condition" (GBD 2021 framing, original sentence)**; oral cancers combined rank **13th** among cancers globally.
- Implication (INFERENCE): oral disease is "universal" beyond the great majority of systemic diseases, and its prevention levers are cheap to the point of nearly free — fluoride + toothbrush + floss. This is the classic "high prevalence × low intervention cost" maximum-leverage zone.

### 1.2 The four-factor caries model (MODEL · textbook-mature model)

Caries = **bacteria (acid-producing flora, e.g., mutans streptococci) × fermentable sugars (frequency matters more than total amount) × the host tooth (enamel quality, salivary buffering and remineralization capacity) × time** — the chronic result of four elements converging on the tooth surface. The model's value (INFERENCE · directly usable): interrupting **any single edge** prevents caries — fluoride and saliva = the remineralization edge, cleaning = the bacteria edge, sugar-frequency control = the substrate edge. Modern extension: caries is understood as **ecological dysbiosis of the tooth-surface plaque** rather than a single-pathogen infection (MODEL · wired to §3.5 the oral microbiome).

### 1.3 The periodontal disease spectrum: the dividing line between reversible and irreversible (this canon's most important timing concept)

- **Gingivitis**: plaque-driven inflammation of the gums, **completely reversible on plaque removal** (MODEL · textbook grade, mechanism consistent with clinical consensus). The clinical signal = bleeding on brushing.
- **Periodontitis**: inflammation progressing to **attachment loss and alveolar bone resorption — bone already resorbed does not naturally regrow** (MODEL). The action meaning of the dividing line (INFERENCE): **bleeding on brushing = the cheapest early alarm, the signal that "the reversible window is still open"**; by the time teeth loosen, the window has closed.
- **The microbiological anchor of plaque biofilm (FACT · title-level)**: Socransky et al. *Microbial complexes in subgingival plaque*. J Clin Periodontol 1998 (PMID 9495612) — subgingival plaque is organized in "complex" structure (the red/orange complexes associate most tightly with periodontitis); a 2025 meta-taxonomic update study exists (PMID 40467115); lineage characterization is evolving (FACT · existence; specific lineages = model layer, not expanded).
- **Why biofilm must be removed mechanically (MODEL→INFERENCE)**: a biofilm = a matrix-encased microbial aggregate; antibiotics and mouthwashes struggle to penetrate the intra-membrane gradients; **physical disruption (brushing/interdental cleaning) is the only daily-repeatable, selection-pressure-free removal method**. Action implication: mouthwash is an adjunct and **cannot replace brushing** — "using mouthwash instead of brushing" is the classic false substitute.

## 2. The evidence-based action face (the key action-card evidence base)

### 2.1 Fluoride toothpaste: the first anti-caries lever (FACT · Cochrane anchor)

**Anchor**: Walsh T, Worthington HV, Glenny AM, et al. *Fluoride toothpastes of different concentrations for preventing dental caries.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2019;(3):CD007868 (PMID 30829399 · title-level verified).

- Conclusion face (review framing · title-level): fluoride toothpaste is effective against caries, and a **dose-response of higher concentration → stronger anti-caries effect** holds; the effect above the conventional baseline of 1000 ppm is solid (abstract numbers not extracted verbatim → the concentration-threshold statement follows the review's public framing · INFERENCE).
- **Amounts by age (action card · INFERENCE · consensus framing of pediatric dental guidelines)**:
  - <3 years: a rice-grain-sized smear (about 1000 ppm);
  - 3–6 years: pea-sized (about 1000 ppm);
  - >6 years and adults: standard 1000–1500 ppm fluoride toothpaste, covering the brush head each time.
- Key discipline (INFERENCE): **after brushing, spit — do not rinse** — leaving fluoride on the tooth surfaces (fluoride's anti-caries mechanism is local retention promoting remineralization, not swallowing).
- "Fluoride toothpaste causes cancer" is a **CONTRADICTED** rumor — see §5.4.

### 2.2 Brushing method and timing (MODEL→INFERENCE)

- **The Bass angled-brush method** (MODEL · historical classic: proposed by C.C. Bass in the 1948s; the original not verified through this round's channel → honestly noted): bristles at 45° toward the gum line, small horizontal vibrations, then sweep vertically. The point is not force; it is **complete coverage + not injuring the gums** — hard horizontal scrubbing = the common self-injury source of wedge-shaped cervical defects and gum recession (MODEL).
- **Twice a day × 2 minutes; the bedtime session is non-negotiable** (INFERENCE · during sleep salivary flow drops = the weakest shift for the remineralization system).
- **Don't brush immediately after acidic food or drink** (MODEL · extrapolated from in-vitro/observational evidence):
  - In-vitro anchor: *Interaction between toothpaste abrasivity and toothbrush filament stiffness on the development of erosive/abrasive lesions in vitro*. Int Dent J 2017 (PMID 28574173 · title-level) — acid-softened enamel surface + mechanical abrasion = stacked damage;
  - Observational anchor: *Is Toothbrush Bristle Stiffness Associated with Erosive Tooth Wear in Adolescents?* Caries Res 2021 (PMID 34365453 · title-level) — bristle stiffness associated with erosive tooth wear at the population level.
  - **Action card**: after acid exposure (carbonated drinks/juice/vinegar/gastric reflux) **wait about 30 minutes, rinse with water, then brush**; for people with reflux disease, treat the reflux first (INFERENCE).
- **Powered toothbrushes (small but real · Cochrane as it is)**: Yaacob M, et al. *Powered versus manual toothbrushing for oral health.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2014;(6):CD002281 (PMID 24934383 · **abstract verbatim**): moderate-certainty evidence shows powered toothbrushes significantly reduce plaque and gingivitis versus manual, with a **small** benefit magnitude (a relative reduction in gingivitis of roughly 6%–11%); **rotary-oscillating heads carry the most evidence**. Action implication (INFERENCE): powered = an increment worth buying, but **not a necessity** — technique + fluoride + coverage come before equipment.

### 2.3 Interdental cleaning: floss vs water flosser (the evidence face as it is · this section is an anti-marketing zone)

- **Floss**: Sambunjak D, et al. *Flossing for the management of periodontal diseases and dental caries in adults.* Cochrane 2011 (PMID 22161438 · title-level) — included trials were weak in quality; the benefit evidence is **weak and low-certainty**; the **2019 update was labeled WITHDRAWN** (PMID 31013348 · FACT — withdrawn because the author team no longer updated it, not because conclusions were overturned; honestly distinguished). The **biological plausibility of interdental cleaning is beyond dispute**: brushing cannot reach the interdental surfaces, and interdental caries hold a high share (MODEL).
- **Interdental brushes**: cleaning efficiency in periodontal populations is listed by most guidelines as superior to floss (INFERENCE · anchor not independently verified this round → UNKNOWN, gap left).
- **Water flosser (oral irrigator)**: has its place as an **adjunct** for orthodontic/implant/fixed-bridge populations (INFERENCE · mechanism = mechanical irrigation); its RCT anchor evidence face was not verified this round (UNKNOWN · not expanded).
- **Action card**: interdental cleaning at least once a day; the tool you can sustain is the best tool (adherence beats the tool debate · INFERENCE).

### 2.4 Fluoridated water and community caries prevention: the evidence history of effect and controversy (FACT · the new 2024 Cochrane anchor)

**Anchor**: *Water fluoridation for the prevention of dental caries.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2024 (PMID 39362658 · **abstract verbatim**):
- The authors' conclusion (extracted and rendered): contemporary studies indicate that initiating community water fluoridation may lead to a slightly larger decline in dmft and a slightly higher increase in the proportion of caries-free children, **but the effect sizes are smaller than in studies before 1975**; contemporary research carries an "insufficient evidence" problem; the certainty of the association with dental fluorosis is **low** (inherited from the 2015 edition).
- The evidence history, as it is (FACT+INFERENCE): fluoridation was one of the 20th century's biggest public-health controversies — **the effect exists but its marginal benefit shrank after fluoride toothpaste became widespread** (the 2024 Cochrane verdict's direction); dental fluorosis (white patches/staining of tooth surfaces) is a **real cosmetic side effect**, not "poisoning."
- The safety-controversy face: the most direct contemporary test of fluoride × osteosarcoma = the JADA 2021 case-control study (PMID 33745682 · **abstract verbatim**): topical fluoride OR 0.94 (95%CI 0.60-1.46), supplemental fluoride OR 0.78 (95%CI 0.46-1.33), the authors' conclusion "topical fluoride or dietary fluoride supplements were not associated with increased osteosarcoma risk" (CONTRADICTED the carcinogenesis rumor · see §5.4). The US National Research Council (NRC) 2006 report *Fluoride in Drinking Water* (title verified to exist via search) concerned research gaps in the high-exposure range (far above water-fluoridation concentrations) along the bone/neurological directions — **"research gaps exist" ≠ "routine fluoridation concentrations are harmful"** (INFERENCE · two different things, not to be mixed).

### 2.5 Cleaning frequency: the evidence face vs the commercial push (FACT · this canon's most counter-intuitive section)

**Anchor**: Lamont T, Worthington HV, Clarkson JE, Beirne PV. *Routine scale and polish for periodontal health in adults.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2018;(12):CD004625 (PMID 30590875 · **abstract verbatim**):
- The original conclusion (extracted and rendered): for regularly attending adults without severe periodontitis, **routine scaling (professional cleaning) over 2–3-year follow-up showed "small or no difference" for gingivitis, probing depth, and oral-health-related quality of life (high-certainty evidence)**; the evidence for differences between 6-month and 12-month rhythms was "uncertain" (very low certainty); cleaning does reduce calculus, but the clinical importance of the small reduction was "uncertain."
- Implication (INFERENCE): **"everyone must get a cleaning every six months" is a commercial convention, not an evidence requirement**; high-risk groups (periodontitis history/diabetes/smoking/preconception) get individualized recall by periodontal specialty assessment; low-risk healthy adults can lengthen the interval. **"Cleanings damage the enamel" is a CONTRADICTED rumor** (§5.2) — but brief post-cleaning sensitivity is real and transient (MODEL).
- Boundary (honest): this review addresses "routine scale and polish" in **asymptomatic regularly attending populations**; subgingival scaling for diagnosed periodontitis is treatment, not a "cleaning," with its own evidence system (UNKNOWN · not expanded in this volume).

## 3. The oral-systemic face (this canon's hardcore)

### 3.1 Periodontitis × diabetes: the hardest edge of the bidirectional relationship (FACT · abstract verbatim)

**Anchor**: Simpson TC, et al. *Treatment of periodontitis for glycaemic control in people with diabetes mellitus.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2022;(4):CD004714 (PMID 35420698 · **abstract verbatim**):
- **Moderate-certainty evidence (30 studies · 2443 people): periodontal treatment (subgingival scaling) produced an absolute HbA1c reduction of 0.43% (95%CI -0.59 to -0.28) at 3–4 months; 0.30% at 6 months (12 studies · 1457 people); 0.50% at 12 months (a single study · 264 people)**.
- Interpretation (INFERENCE; all three directly usable):
  1. 0.43% ≈ the glucose-lowering magnitude of a weak oral drug — **periodontal treatment is a systematically underestimated adjunctive lever in diabetic glucose management**;
  2. The effect decays over time → **periodontal treatment is not a one-time event; inflammation control needs maintenance**;
  3. The reverse edge (diabetes → periodontitis more severe and harder to control) is an association-grade evidence face (MODEL · mechanism = advanced glycation end-products × neutrophil function × wound healing).
- Action card: a diabetes diagnosis = an indication for periodontal examination; poor response of periodontitis to treatment = check blood sugar (both directions hold · INFERENCE).

### 3.2 Periodontal disease × cardiovascular: strong association, causation unadjudicated (FACT · honest boundary-drawing)

**Anchor**: Lockhart PB, et al. *Periodontal disease and atherosclerotic vascular disease: does the evidence support an independent association?* AHA scientific statement. Circulation 2012;125:2520-2544 (PMID 22514251 · title-level).
- The AHA statement's position (the title is the question; the statement framing = FACT): periodontal disease and atherosclerotic vascular disease show a **consistent association**, but **independent causation is not established** — confounding by shared causes (smoking/diabetes/socioeconomic position) not fully removed (MODEL · statement logic).
- Subsequent Mendelian-randomization and similar studies have still produced no adjudicated causation (UNKNOWN · no new evidence face rewriting that conclusion was verified this round).
- Action implication (INFERENCE): **no need to wait for causal adjudication** — controlling periodontal disease and controlling classic cardiovascular risk factors (blood pressure/lipids/smoking) have zero conflict and zero substitution; the strong-causation pitch of "treat gum disease to prevent heart attacks" = down-weight.

### 3.3 Pregnancy periodontal disease × preterm birth: stable association, split intervention evidence (FACT+UNKNOWN · as it is)

- Association face (FACT · title-level): *Relationship between periodontal disease and preterm birth. A systematic review and meta-analysis.* Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal 2024 (PMID 39396142).
- Intervention face (honestly split):
  - Lopez RCT (J Periodontol 2002 · PMID 12211502 · title-level): periodontal treatment "may reduce" the risk of preterm low birth weight (a single trial · positive);
  - Reanalysis of the large OPT trial data (Int J Epidemiol 2018 · PMID 29868830 · title-level): after accounting for selection bias the association weakened — **whether treatment lowers preterm birth remains unadjudicated** (UNKNOWN).
- Action card (INFERENCE · holds regardless of causation): **preconception/first-trimester periodontal examination + treatment is safe and worthwhile** — pregnancy gingivitis is genuinely common (MODEL); the safety of dental treatment during pregnancy (including scaling under local anesthesia) is professional consensus; "you can't see the dentist while pregnant" = a CONTRADICTED-grade misconception (cognitive face).

### 3.4 Chewing × cognition: the observational frontier (INFERENCE · causation open)

A triple observational anchor set (all title-level):
- Qi X, et al. *Dose-Response Meta-Analysis on Tooth Loss With the Risk of Cognitive Impairment and Dementia.* J Am Med Dir Assoc 2021;22:2039-2045 (PMID 34579934) — **dose-response**: the more teeth lost, the higher the risk;
- Fang WL, et al. tooth loss × dementia meta of 21 observational studies. BMC Psychiatry 2018 (PMID 30342524);
- Li L, et al. meta of cohort studies. Front Neurol 2023 (PMID 37139053).
- Epistemic state (honest): all of it **observational** — malnutrition/inflammation/shared educational and socioeconomic position could all explain it; **causation unadjudicated (UNKNOWN)**; "restore the teeth to prevent dementia" is currently a candidate hypothesis, not a conclusion. Frontier hypothesis face: chewing → hippocampal blood flow / occlusal proprioceptive input (METAPHOR/MODEL boundary · not to be mixed).
- Action implication (INFERENCE · low cost, no regret): **keeping teeth = keeping chewing = keeping intake quality** — the direct edge from tooth restoration in older adults to nutritional intake is harder than the indirect "prevent dementia" edge.

### 3.5 The oral microbiome (wired to the microbiome scale)

The mouth is the body's second-largest microbial habitat; tooth-surface plaque = the canonical spatially structured biofilm (§1.3); periodontitis/caries = dominant-population shifts under the dysbiosis model (MODEL · the Socransky 1998 anchor + a 2025 meta-taxonomic update exists). **Interlink with the microbiome-scale healing definition document**: `docs/healing/U08-microbiome-healing-definition-20260826.md` (the three causal edges — broad-spectrum-antibiotic overuse × ultra-processed diets × chronic stress — apply isomorphically). Action implication (INFERENCE): **broad-spectrum antibiotics are not first-line for periodontitis** (systemic antibiotics only as adjuncts in specific severe/aggressive cases · INFERENCE); mechanical removal first = also the discipline of microbiome management.

## 4. Special scenarios

### 4.1 Children (FACT anchors + MODEL mechanisms)

- **Primary-tooth caries is not "they'll be replaced anyway, ignore it"** (a CONTRADICTED-grade misconception; mechanism = MODEL): caries in primary teeth progresses fast (thin enamel); pain and infection impair eating and nutrition; early loss of primary teeth → space loss and occlusal disorder in the permanent dentition; caries activity itself carries across dentitions (primary-tooth caries is one of the strongest predictors of permanent-tooth caries · MODEL).
- **Pit-and-fissure sealants**: Ahovuo-Saloranta A, et al. *Sealants for preventing dental decay in the permanent teeth.* Cochrane 2013;(3):CD001830 (PMID 23543512 · title-level) — the evidence for sealing permanent molars' fissures against caries holds (face: seal the first permanent molars on eruption).
- **Fluoride varnish**: Marinho VC, et al. *Fluoride varnishes for preventing dental caries in children and adolescents.* Cochrane 2013 (PMID 23846772 · title-level) — varnish prevents caries effectively (professionally applied at intervals).
- Action card (INFERENCE): start fluoride-toothpaste brushing at the eruption of the first tooth; evaluate sealants when the first permanent molars erupt (around age 6); **parents help with brushing until age 6–8** (insufficient fine motor control = the main cause of missed surfaces · MODEL).

### 4.2 Orthodontics: the medical vs cosmetic boundary (MODEL→INFERENCE)

- **Medical indications** (function face): severe malocclusion affecting bite function/chewing/speech, deep overbite with traumatic occlusion, craniofacial factors of obstructive sleep-breathing problems, cleft lip-and-palate sequence treatment — treatment has functional gain (MODEL).
- **Cosmetic indications** (preference face): mild-to-moderate misalignment as the chief demand — **legitimate but a consumer decision**, needing no medicalized packaging (INFERENCE · informed self-determination).
- The crossing cost (FACT-grade common knowledge + MODEL): fixed appliances = an explosion of plaque-retention surfaces → **elevated risk during orthodontics of caries (demineralized white-spot lesions) and gingivitis** — oral-hygiene discipline during orthodontics (water flosser/interdental brush adjuncts) is part of the treatment, not optional.

### 4.3 Implants/root canals: evidence-based considerations of saving vs extracting (INFERENCE · a decision tree)

The proprioception and bone-maintenance function of the natural tooth–root–periodontal ligament system has no perfect replacement (MODEL); modern dentistry's tooth-saving capacity far exceeds lay intuition (root canal retreatment/post-and-core crowns/hemisection, etc. · MODEL). The decision tree (INFERENCE · for the conversation with the dentist; not self-diagnosis):
1. **Is the crown repairable?** Shallow/moderate caries → fill; deep caries reaching the pulp without periapical lesion → root canal + crown usually saves the tooth;
2. **Is the periodontal foundation there?** Severe attachment loss + bone resorption to the apex → even with a repaired crown, the periodontal foundation is lost;
3. **Root fracture / recurrent large periapical lesions** → the extraction indication rises;
4. **The post-extraction restoration path**: implant/bridge/removable denture each carry their indications and maintenance costs; implants = mature but **not "one and done forever"** (peri-implantitis = the implant's periodontitis · MODEL; maintenance discipline same as natural teeth);
5. **Cost and preference** enter (legitimate variables · INFERENCE).

### 4.4 Wisdom teeth: asymptomatic should not mean "everyone gets them out" (a CONTRADICTED-grade misconception + evidence history)

- **Anchor**: Song F, et al. *The effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of prophylactic removal of wisdom teeth.* Health Technol Assess 2000 (PMID 10932022 · title-level) — a large UK HTA assessment: **the effectiveness and cost-effectiveness of prophylactic removal of asymptomatic wisdom teeth were not supported** (assessment framing).
- Mainstream guideline stance (INFERENCE · NICE line): asymptomatic, disease-free impacted wisdom teeth = watch rather than prophylactically extract; **extraction indications = already diseased or a clear threat**: recurrent pericoronitis, unrestorable caries, root resorption of the adjacent tooth, dentigerous cyst, etc. (MODEL).
- Evidence history, honestly: this round's channel could not title-verify a Cochrane 2020 wisdom-teeth review (exact-title query returned 0 hits · recorded in the receipt) — this section's conclusion rests on the HTA 2000 anchor; the causation of "extracting wisdom teeth improves lower-incisor crowding" is likewise unproven (MODEL · crowding is multifactorial).

### 4.5 Oral cancer red flags (wired to the red-flag system · FACT epidemiology + INFERENCE referral)

- Epidemiological anchor (FACT): oral cancers combined = the 13th most common cancer worldwide (WHO fact sheet actually captured); the main risk factors = tobacco, alcohol, areca nut (IARC-grade evidence · FACT common-knowledge framing), with HPV-associated oropharyngeal cancer rising (MODEL).
- **The red-flag list for seeking care** (standalone card in §6 · INFERENCE · aligned to the NICE NG12 suspected-cancer referral system — NG12's existence verified via search; item wording not verified verbatim → honestly noted):
  - An oral ulcer **unhealed for >3 weeks**;
  - Oral **white patches/red patches** (red patches especially = higher risk · MODEL) or unexplained new growths on the tongue/floor of mouth;
  - An **unexplained lump** in the mouth or neck that persists;
  - Unexplained oral bleeding, tongue numbness, limited mouth opening, difficulty swallowing.
- Action discipline: a red flag ≠ a diagnosis; it = **seeing a doctor within the time limit** (the referral system's meaning is precisely turning "wait and see" into "the three-week red line" · INFERENCE).

## 5. The falsification face (five rumors · the CONTRADICTED ledger)

| # | Rumor | Verdict | Evidence and anchors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **"A toothache is not a disease"** | CONTRADICTED (as a reason for not taking it seriously) | Caries = the world's most common health condition under the GBD 2021 framing (WHO fact sheet · FACT); pain = a **late signal** that the decay has reached the pulp (MODEL) — "no pain" ≠ "no disease"; regular check-ups are the real early screening |
| 5.2 | **"Cleanings damage the enamel"** | CONTRADICTED | Cochrane high-certainty evidence: routine scale and polish shows small or no difference for adult periodontal health (PMID 30590875 · abstract verbatim), with no "enamel damage" evidence face; post-cleaning sensitivity is real but transient (MODEL); an ultrasonic scaler tip gliding along the tooth surface ≠ grinding (MODEL) |
| 5.3 | **"Whitening toothpastes make you whiter and whiter"** | CONTRADICTED (effective on extrinsic stains, ineffective on the teeth's intrinsic color, and excess = harm) | Mechanism = abrasives polishing away extrinsic stains (MODEL); dentin wear rises with whitening toothpastes' hydrogen-peroxide concentration and abrasivity (Am J Dent 2023 · PMID 37076293; 2024 · PMID 38458975 · title-level); intrinsic color is set by dentin, which abrasives cannot change — intrinsic whitening goes through in-office bleaching (INFERENCE) |
| 5.4 | **"Fluoride toothpaste/fluoridated water causes cancer"** | CONTRADICTED (the osteosarcoma direction) | JADA 2021 case-control (PMID 33745682 · abstract verbatim): topical fluoride OR 0.94 (0.60-1.46), supplemental fluoride OR 0.78 (0.46-1.33), the authors' conclusion "not associated with increased osteosarcoma risk"; the NRC 2006 report's high-exposure research gaps ≠ harm at routine concentrations (INFERENCE · honest boundary-drawing) |
| 5.5 | **"Asymptomatic wisdom teeth should all be extracted"** | CONTRADICTED (as a blanket policy) | UK HTA 2000 (PMID 10932022): prophylactic removal unsupported on effectiveness and cost-effectiveness; mainstream guidelines = watch asymptomatic teeth, extract on disease indications (§4.4); extraction itself carries a surgical risk face (nerve injury/dry socket · MODEL) — a benefit-less prophylactic exposure does not stand |

> Anti(G) self-reflexive clause: this table itself is falsifiable — if **new evidence** appears for any rumor (e.g., a stronger-designed fluoride-carcinogenesis study, a quantified cleaning-damages-enamel study), the corresponding row must be downgraded immediately with an erratum (append-only, history not deleted).

## 6. The action card (standalone · ordered by cost-effectiveness) and the red-flag list for seeking care (standalone)

### Action cards · daily and periodic (all INFERENCE · cheap, reversible, low-risk first)

1. **Fluoride toothpaste twice a day × 2 minutes** (>6 years and adults 1000-1500 ppm; amounts by age in §2.1); the bedtime session is non-negotiable; after brushing, spit — do not rinse.
2. **Interdental cleaning at least once a day** (floss or interdental brushes; the tool you can sustain = the best tool).
3. **After acidic food or reflux, wait about 30 minutes before brushing**; rinse with water first.
4. **Bleeding on brushing = the gingivitis alarm**: intensify cleaning; if it doesn't recede in 1–2 weeks → see the dentist (the reversible-window discipline).
5. **A powered toothbrush = a small but real increment** (Cochrane moderate certainty), not a necessity.
6. **Sugar's frequency > its total amount** (MODEL): sweets gathered into one meal beat sipping spread across the day; don't slow-sip sugary drinks through a straw.
7. **Individualized cleaning rhythm**: regularly attending adults without severe periodontitis need not be bound to "once every six months"; periodontitis history/diabetes/smoking/preconception = recall by the specialty.
8. **Diabetes = an indication for periodontal examination**; poor periodontal treatment response = check blood sugar (both directions).
9. **Preconception/first-trimester periodontal check**; "you can't see the dentist while pregnant" is a misconception.
10. **Children**: fluoride toothpaste from the first tooth's eruption; parents help brush until 6–8 years old; seal the first permanent molars; regular fluoride varnish.
11. **Wisdom teeth**: watch asymptomatic ones; extract only on disease indications.
12. **Mouthwash = an adjunct**; it never replaces mechanical removal.

### The red-flag list for seeking care (time-limit discipline · the referral system)

| Red flag | What the time limit means |
|---|---|
| Oral ulcer unhealed >3 weeks | The suspected-cancer referral red line (the NICE NG12 system) |
| White/red patches, unexplained new growths, unexplained lumps | Oral & maxillofacial surgery promptly |
| Unexplained oral bleeding, tongue numbness, limited mouth opening/swallowing difficulty | Prompt evaluation |
| Spontaneous pain/night pain, sustained cold-heat stimulus pain | The pulpitis face — the earlier the visit, the higher the chance of saving the tooth |
| Toothache with fever/facial swelling/limited mouth opening | Infection-spread risk — emergency-grade |
| Traumatic tooth avulsion | **The 30-minute reimplantation window** — hold the crown, rinse, re-seat it or keep it in milk, and go immediately |
| A loose tooth (non-traumatic) | A late periodontitis signal — attachment/bone already lost, the salvage period |
| Markedly worsening gum bleeding and swelling in pregnancy | Dental visits in pregnancy are safe and necessary |
