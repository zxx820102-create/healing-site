# Family First Aid Canon v1 — In the golden 4 minutes there is no doctor, only you

> **For whom**: all 8 billion people. It assumes no medical background and no equipment on scene.
> **One-line summary**: scene safety → call 120 (mainland China's medical emergency line; readers elsewhere: use your local emergency number — *(translator calibration)*) → do only the actions you are certain are safe → of those who must go to hospital, not a single one stays home.
> **Epistemic-state statement**: every key number and step in this booklet carries an epistemic label. Guideline anchors = ILCOR "2024 International Consensus on Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Emergency Cardiovascular Care Science With Treatment Recommendations" (DOI:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001288 / 10.1016/j.resuscitation.2024.110414) and the adult basic life support portion of the AHA "2025 Guidelines for CPR and Emergency Cardiovascular Care" (Part 7 · DOI:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001369); the choking first-aid flow is the AHA/ILCOR 2025 update (relayed consistently by Xinhua, CCTV, provincial Red Cross chapters and others; the guideline's original page could not be verified directly — labeled FACT·relayed). Anything unverifiable is labeled UNKNOWN; no numbers are invented.
> **Legal boundary (direction-level description · not legal advice)**: Article 184 of the Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (the "Good Samaritan clause" (好人条款)): **a voluntary emergency rescuer does not bear civil liability for harm caused to the person helped** (full text verified directly on the Ministry of Justice official site · FACT). Direction-level meaning: in mainland China, voluntary good-faith rescuers are protected by this article. This booklet is not a legal opinion; for a specific case consult a legal professional.

## ① General principles: the first survivor is the rescuer

1. **Scene safety first (FACT)**: charging in before you can see the scene clearly turns one casualty into two. For electrocution, cut the power first or flick the wire away with a dry insulating object; for a gas leak, open doors and windows first — no lights, no flame, no doorbell; for a traffic accident, set out warnings before rescuing; for a drowning person, do not enter the water — throw flotation objects and poles from shore first — "being able to swim" is not a permit to jump in (drowning-rescue anchor: the Xiangtan municipal government first-aid guide · People's Daily Health Times).
2. **Do not move the injured (MODEL)**: unless there is a secondary hazard on scene (fire, collapse, traffic, toxic environment), do not move the injured — especially when spinal injury is suspected (a fall from height, a car crash, severe neck pain), wait for professional immobilization. The safety baseline for necessary escapes: "log-roll as one unit — head, neck and trunk on one axis."
3. **When calling 120 and doing nothing else is the right move (MODEL)**: ① the environmental hazard is not cleared; ② you are not sure what to do — the 120 dispatcher can coach you through CPR and bleeding control by phone; ③ spinal injury suspected with no hazard — hold the position and wait for the ambulance. **When calling 120, state clearly**: the location (the district first, then the road, then the landmark), what happened, how many are injured, your phone number — and **let them hang up first**.
4. **Checking responsiveness**: tap both shoulders and shout loudly; for infants, tap the soles of the feet. No response + no normal breathing (or only agonal (gasping) respiration) = treat as cardiac arrest and start CPR (FACT · AHA 2025 anchor).
5. **The boundary of first aid**: first aid means "keeping the casualty alive until professionals arrive," not "curing them." Every action in this booklet ends at the same sentence: **anyone who must go to hospital, goes to hospital** (the red lines close each section).

## ② CPR: the golden 4 minutes — compressions never stop

**Applies to**: adults and children/infants with no response + no normal breathing. **Untrained → do compression-only CPR (Hands-Only)**; skipping rescue breaths is never worse (FACT · AHA anchor: cpr.heart.org verified directly against the source text).

**Adult steps (FACT · AHA 2025 Part 7 anchor)**:
1. Tap the shoulders and shout to confirm no response; call for help and assign one specific person to call 120 and one specific person to fetch the AED.
2. **Position**: the midpoint of the line between the nipples, the lower half of the sternum (center of the chest).
3. **Posture**: heel of the hand in contact, hands stacked, arms vertical; press using the weight of your upper body.
4. **Depth 5–6 cm** (at least 5 cm / 2 inches, no more than 6 cm / 2.4 inches).
5. **Rate 100–120 compressions/minute** (about the tempo of the song "Stayin' Alive").
6. Let the chest **fully recoil** after each compression; interrupt as little as possible (measured decay of compression quality begins after 90–120 seconds · AHA 2025 Part 7).
7. Trained rescuers cycle 30 compressions + 2 rescue breaths; each breath lasts 1 second with visible chest rise.

**Children and infants — the differences (FACT · MSD Manual Professional (Chinese) verified directly)**:
- Compression depth = **one third of the chest's front-to-back depth**, about 4–5 cm (adolescents weighing >55 kg follow the adult standard).
- Single rescuer: children — one heel of the hand or two; infants — two fingers (with two rescuers, the two-thumb encircling-hands technique may be used).
- Asphyxial hypoxic arrest is common in children and infants, so trained rescuers put more weight on rescue breaths (30:2; infant two-rescuer 15:2).

**The five steps of AED use (FACT · Shenyang Municipal Health Commission / Ezhou CDC public education verified directly + Mayo Clinic)**:
1. **On**: power on; follow the voice prompts.
2. **Pad**: bare the chest and dry the skin; place pads below the right collarbone + outside the left nipple on the mid-axillary line; children use pediatric pads/attenuator (if none, use adult pads and follow the device's prompt).
3. **Clear**: while the rhythm is being analyzed, no one touches the patient; when the AED says "stand clear," it means you.
4. **Shock**: if a shock is advised → confirm nobody is touching → press the shock button.
5. **Continue**: after the shock, **resume chest compressions immediately** — do not stop to look at the result; the AED re-analyzes automatically every 2 minutes. An AED only defibrillates ventricular fibrillation / pulseless ventricular tachycardia; it will not shock "someone who doesn't need a shock" — fear using it wrong less than not using it.

**When to stop**: the casualty breathes / responds (roll them into the recovery position (侧卧位), i.e., the lateral position) · the AED prompts · professionals take over · you are exhausted (FACT).
**Red line for hospital care**: everyone who received CPR must go to hospital; for anyone on whom CPR was started, there is no "they came around, so no hospital."

## ③ Airway obstruction (choking): the 2025 flow — alternate "5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts"

**Applies-to judgment (if they can cough, don't strike · FACT·relayed)**: the person can cough and can make sounds = partial obstruction — **no back blows and no thrusts**; encourage hard coughing and stay beside them; cannot cough, cannot make sounds, hands clutching the throat (the V gesture), face turning blue = severe obstruction — act now. **Never perform a blind finger sweep in a conscious person's mouth.**

**Adults and children over 1 year (FACT·relayed: the AHA/ILCOR 2025 update · relayed consistently by Xinhua 2026-01, CCTV/China Daily 2026-03, and Jiangxi/Shanghai Red Cross public education)**:
1. **5 back blows**: stand to the person's side and slightly behind, have them lean forward with the head lower than the chest, and strike hard between the shoulder blades with the heel of the hand 5 times.
2. **5 abdominal thrusts (the Heimlich maneuver)**: step behind the person, one foot forward between theirs; locating the spot ("scissors-rock-cloth" method): place two fingers (index and middle) at the point two finger-widths above the navel, press the thumb side of your fist against that point, wrap it with the other hand, and deliver **quick inward-and-upward** thrusts 5 times (thrust depth about 4–6 cm).
3. **Alternate the cycle of 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts** until the object comes out. Why it was updated: back blows raise expulsion success and reduce the internal-organ injury risk of abdominal thrusts.
4. **The person loses consciousness**: lay them down at once, call 120, start CPR (the compressions themselves also generate pressure that can expel the object).

**Infants under 1 year (abdominal thrusts forbidden)**: lay the infant face-down along your forearm with the head lower than the trunk; give 5 back blows with the heel of the hand between the shoulder blades; flip face-up and give 5 chest thrusts with the heel of the hand on the lower-middle sternum; alternate the cycle. Attempt removal only if the object is visible and superficial; if you cannot see it, never dig blindly.

**Pregnant people and people with obesity (abdominal thrusts forbidden)**: switch to **chest thrusts** — same position as CPR (lower half of the sternum), thrusting inward, expelling the object by chest-cavity pressure.

**Self-rescue**: press a fist against the point two finger-widths above your navel, wrap it with the other hand, and drive inward and upward; or brace the upper abdomen against the back of a chair or the corner of a table and thrust quickly.

**Red line for hospital care**: even if the object comes out, anyone who received abdominal thrusts should be examined at hospital (thrusts can injure internal organs · Shangguan News quoting an emergency expert · FACT·relayed); anyone who lost consciousness, or on whom CPR was performed, must go to hospital.

## ④ Stroke: BEFAST recognition + never feed water or pills

**The BEFAST mnemonic (FACT · Aroor 2017, the BE-FAST original in the AHA journal Stroke + the American Stroke Association site)**:
- **B**alance — sudden loss of balance or coordination
- **E**yes — sudden blurred vision / visual-field loss / double vision
- **F**ace — facial asymmetry, drooping mouth corner
- **A**rm — one-sided arm weakness or drift
- **S**peech — slurred speech or incomprehension
- **T**ime — call 120 now and **record the exact time symptoms began**

**Background (FACT)**: FAST detects anterior-circulation (carotid-territory) stroke with about 88% sensitivity but easily misses posterior-circulation events (chiefly dizziness/eye symptoms) — BEFAST adds the B and the E.

**The golden time window (FACT · Beijing Tiantan Hospital stroke first-aid public education verified directly)**:
- Intravenous thrombolysis: within **4.5 hours of onset**.
- Mechanical thrombectomy: for some large-vessel occlusion patients this extends to **within 24 hours**.
- Time is brain: brain cells die with every minute of delay — so the T means "go to hospital carrying the onset time."

**Why you must never feed water or pills (FACT · Tiantan Hospital explicitly lists: no feeding food or water, no self-administered aspirin or other drugs)**: ① stroke patients often have swallowing paralysis — anything fed can enter the airway, causing choking or aspiration pneumonia; ② feeding aspirin on your own before CT distinguishes ischemic from hemorrhagic stroke — if it is a brain hemorrhage, that pours fuel on the bleeding; ③ the time spent feeding costs far more than whatever goes down.

**Red line for hospital care**: suspected stroke has no "let's watch it a moment" option — all cases go to hospital immediately; even if symptoms have resolved (a TIA can still progress to a full stroke).

## ⑤ Burns and scalds: Cool–Remove–Soak–Cover–Send (冲·脱·泡·盖·送) — everything else makes it worse

**The five-character formula (FACT · Xinhua / Xinhua Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine / Yantai Laishan government public education verified directly)**:
1. **Cool**: running cool water (about 15–20°C — not ice water) continuously for **at least 15 minutes (15–30 minutes)**; keep cooling even after the pain eases. Cooling still helps within 3 hours of the burn (FACT · a 2025 PubMed cooling review: 20 minutes of cool running water significantly improves outcomes within 3 hours).
2. **Remove**: while cooling, carefully take off clothing and jewelry; cut around clothing that sticks and leave it in place — **do not tear, do not burst blisters**.
3. **Soak**: small-to-medium limb burns may continue soaking in cool water 15–30 minutes (for large burns watch temperature and prevent hypothermia — especially in children).
4. **Cover**: drape clean gauze / plastic wrap **loosely** over the wound. Apply nothing: toothpaste, soy sauce, sesame oil, gentian violet, flour — all of them contaminate the wound, mask the injury, and make debridement more painful for the doctor (FACT · ANZCOR Guideline 9.1.3: no ointments, creams or powders).
5. **Send**: go to hospital.

**Two high-frequency secondary injuries (FACT · McGill / ANZCOR verified directly)**:
- **Ice applied directly = frostbite stacked on a burn**: ice constricts vessels and drives further ischemic tissue death.
- **Never burst a blister yourself**: an intact blister is a sterile dressing; bursting it opens the gate to infection; large or tension blisters are handled by a doctor under sterile conditions.

**Chemical burns (FACT · MSD Manual Home Edition / Taipei Veterans General Hospital / Taipei Fire Department verified directly)**: brush dry powder off the skin first (dry cloth/paper), then flush with large volumes of running water for **≥30 minutes**; clear quicklime before flushing; substances that react violently with water (water-prohibited materials such as metallic sodium/potassium) **must not be flushed with water** — cover with a dry cloth and rush to hospital; for an eye injury, hold the eye open and flush continuously 15–20 minutes or longer.
**Red line for hospital care**: burns of the face, hands, feet, perineum, joints or genitals; burns in infants/toddlers and older adults; any wound larger than the casualty's own palm; all chemical/electrical burns; inhalation injury (coughing fits, hoarse voice, singed nasal hairs) — all go to hospital, no exceptions.

## ⑥ Bleeding and trauma: pressure is king; the tourniquet is the last card

**Direct pressure = the first means of bleeding control (FACT · ANZCOR Guideline 9.1.1 verified directly)**: press a clean dressing (clothing or a towel if none) directly on the wound and keep firm, continuous pressure; if the dressing soaks through, **do not lift it off and replace it** — stack more on top. For major limb bleeding, elevating the limb can accompany the pressure.

**The true cost of a tourniquet (FACT · ANZCOR + the "Expert Consensus on Emergency Tourniquet Use" in a Chinese emergency medicine journal, verified directly)**: use it only for **life-threatening limb bleeding that direct pressure cannot control**. The cost: total ischemia of the limb distal to the band — the longer it stays on, the higher the necrosis and amputation risk, with compartment syndrome and reperfusion injury risks on top. The rules: tie it 5–8 cm above (toward the heart) the wound, avoiding joints; **write down the start time** (on the forehead or on the tourniquet); re-assess periodically as prompted; never cover it up. A tourniquet used = hospital, no exceptions.

**Fractures: immobilize, never reduce (MODEL · multi-source first-aid commons)**: do not try to bend a deformity back; splint across the two joints above and below with a splint/stiff cardboard/plank — no splint, tie the limb to the healthy limb or the trunk; for an open fracture, do not push protruding bone ends back in; cover in a circle with sterile dressing. Spinal injury suspected — do not move; wait for the ambulance.

**Nosebleed: head forward, pinch the soft part — never tilt the head back (FACT · Mayo Clinic / the University of Hong Kong Faculty of Medicine / Taiwan NHIA verified directly)**: sit, lean the body forward, pinch the soft tissues on both sides of the nose (not the bony bridge) with thumb and index finger for a continuous 10–15 minutes (no peeking by letting go); breathe through the mouth; a cold compress on the bridge may help. **The cost of tilting back = blood swallowed into the stomach**, which provokes vomiting and hides the amount of blood loss. Tissue or cotton packed into the nostril may be used to compress (Taiwan NHIA suggestion).
**Red line for hospital care**: bleeding not controlled after 20–30 minutes of pressure; traumatic bleeds (especially with clear watery drainage from the nose = suspected CSF leak); anyone on anticoagulants or with a clotting disorder; recurrent bleeding — all go to hospital.

## ⑦ Poisoning and ingestion: bring the bottle; do not induce vomiting blindly

**The boundaries of induced vomiting (all FACT · MSD verified directly)**:
- **Do not induce vomiting**: ① corrosives (strong acids/strong alkalis/drain cleaner/84 disinfectant (household bleach)) — vomiting again means a second burn, and never neutralize acid with base or vice versa (the reaction's heat worsens injury · MSD Home Edition); ② petroleum products / hydrocarbons (gasoline, kerosene, banana oil (thinner), paint thinner) — aspirating vomit can cause a fatal chemical pneumonia (Merck/MSD Professional: avoid gastric emptying); ③ anyone unconscious or convulsing — aspiration and suffocation.
- Ordinary detergents/pills, person awake, non-corrosive and non-hydrocarbon: self-induced vomiting is still not advised — call 120 or the local poison control line first and follow instructions. **The ipecac syrup era is over (MODEL · the international toxicology community's consensus direction)**.
- **Bring the container**: take the pill bottle/packaging/leftover poison and a sample of vomit to the hospital (MSD / first-aid public education in agreement).
- Folk "neutralizing" antidotes are forbidden (feeding base for acid poisoning; milk and eggs as a universal antidote — both unevidenced and delaying).

**Carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning: ventilate first, then rescue (FACT · the National Health Commission's "Technical Scheme for Health Emergency Response to Acute Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Events" + local government emergency public education, verified directly)**:
1. **Protect yourself first**: before holding your breath and entering low, open doors and windows to ventilate and shut off the gas source; no open flames, no electric sparks.
2. Move the person quickly to fresh air and loosen the collar.
3. Call 120. If consciousness is impaired, the recovery position keeps the airway open; no breathing → CPR.
4. Moderate-to-severe cases (altered consciousness, coma) must go to a hospital with a **hyperbaric oxygen (chamber)**.

**Red line for hospital care**: in principle, every known/suspected poisoning gets professional evaluation — poisoning has no "let's watch it"; CO poisoning can cause delayed encephalopathy even when the person currently "seems fine" — hospital, mandatory.

## ⑧ The falsification face: these pieces of "common sense" kill (each one falsifiable · this round's verification log in the receipt)

1. **Philtrum pressure (掐人中) to revive the unconscious / during a seizure — no evidence and harmful (FACT · People's Daily Health Times verified directly)**: pressing the philtrum changes no disease course; a bruised lip is the light outcome — the lethal one is delaying the breathing check and CPR. For unconsciousness/seizure the right actions are: check breathing, call 120, position safely.
2. **Toothpaste / soy sauce / sesame oil on a burn — delays heat dissipation + contaminates the wound (FACT · ANZCOR/Xinhua)**: every paste, cream and sauce blocks heat loss and covers the injury. The only correct action: cool.
3. **Head back for a nosebleed — blood into the stomach (FACT · Mayo / HKU Faculty of Medicine)**: tilting back does not reduce bleeding; it pours blood down the throat — coughing, vomiting, and a hidden blood count. Lean forward + pinch the soft nostrils.
4. **Hanging a drowning person upside down to "drain water" — delays CPR (FACT · the Xiangtan municipal government first-aid guide / Shuguang Hospital affiliated with Shanghai University of TCM, verified directly)**: drainage does not remove water from the lungs (water does not obstruct the airway); it only provokes reflux and aspiration of stomach contents, and burns the most precious minutes of hypoxia correction. What is special about drowning resuscitation: it is fundamentally hypoxia — trained rescuers prioritize rescue breaths (the latest evidence-based recommendation: 5 rescue breaths first, then 30 compressions · People's Daily Health Times); untrained, compression-only CPR is still far better than "draining water."
5. **Swallowing rice balls / drinking vinegar for a fish bone — pressure drives it deep + acetic acid is powerless (FACT · Shenzhen Health Commission case reports / multiple hospital public education, verified directly)**: swallowed rice can push a bone from superficial to deep in the esophagus, even piercing it into the nearby aorta; vinegar crosses the throat in seconds, while softening a bone takes soaking-scale time. Correct: stop swallowing, cough gently; remove with tweezers only if visible and superficial — otherwise ENT with a laryngoscope.
6. **Stuffing things into the mouth / forcing the jaw open / restraining limbs during a seizure — all of it injures (FACT · People's Daily / Xinhuanet verified directly)**: chopsticks or towels break teeth, and broken teeth can obstruct the airway and suffocate; "swallowing the tongue causes death" is a rumor — suffocation is the real risk; restraining limbs causes fractures and dislocations. Correct: clear dangerous objects, cushion the head, lay on the side, **record the seizure start time**; emergency hospital care is needed only if it lasts >5 minutes or seizures repeat; after the seizure let them sleep without waking — guard until fully alert.
7. **Ice on burns — a second frostbite injury (FACT · ANZCOR 9.1.3)**: see ⑤.
8. "'Unconscious means stroke — pinch them awake' — the causes of unconsciousness run from hypoglycemia to brain hemorrhage (MODEL)": no breathing → start CPR; breathing → recovery position and call 120; when you cannot tell, the 120 dispatcher's phone guidance is the only correct option.

## The red-line master table for hospital care (runs through the whole booklet · one-page quick reference)

| Scenario | After first aid, must go to hospital | Wait / follow professionals only |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPR/AED | anyone on whom CPR was started: 100% to hospital | during resuscitation, no instruction = no stopping |
| Airway obstruction | after abdominal thrusts (organ-injury check); anyone who lost consciousness | ① infants/pregnant/obesity cases: professional handling first |
| Stroke | everyone, carrying the onset time | thrombolysis/thrombectomy happen only in hospital |
| Burns and scalds | face/hands/feet/perineum/joints · large area · old and young · chemical/electrical/inhalation | water-reactive chemicals: on scene |
| Bleeding | tourniquet users; bleeding past 20–30 minutes of pressure; anticoagulated | suspected spinal injury: do not move |
| Poisoning/CO | everyone; moderate-to-severe CO → a hyperbaric oxygen chamber | water-reactive substances, corrosives: gastric emptying decisions |
