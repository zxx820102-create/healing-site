# Breast Health Canon v1 (2026-09-12)

> The one-sentence overview: **the breast is an organ that remodels itself all life long under hormonal rhythm — most of what you can feel is not cancer; screening is a two-sided ledger that "buys certainty and pays in false positives and overdiagnosis"; only red flags (inflammatory breast cancer, bloody discharge) are worth racing time for, and in almost every other scenario "follow-up on schedule" is the safety net itself.**

The breast's bulk is fat and gland: 15–20 lobes arranged like a bunch of grapes, the ducts are the grape stems converging on the nipple, and the Cooper's ligaments between the glands are the "suspension straps." How much gland there is determines whether the tissue feels firm or soft on palpation, and whether imaging calls it "dense." It remodels under hormonal command all through life — development at puberty, the pre-period swelling of every cycle, the commissioning of lactation, the exit after menopause. Precisely because it keeps changing, the breast report is the heaviest anxiety zone of routine checkups; and rumors such as "massage dissolves the lump," "no pain means no problem," and "mammography causes cancer" push the anxiety in the wrong direction. This canon has seven faces: basics, screening, nodules and reports, care-seeking red flags, anxiety, living, falsification. Epistemic states are labeled item by item (the five states FACT/MODEL/INFERENCE/UNKNOWN/CONTRADICTED), the falsification face attaches a falsifier (what evidence could overturn it) to every entry, and all PMIDs were verified one by one against Europe PMC REST — none from memory.

## Face 1. The basics face: reading an organ that "changes all life long"

### 1.1 Two exams: the structural exam and the functional exam

Breast checkups come as two exams: the **structural exam** (ultrasound / mammography / MRI looking at form) and the **functional exam** (hormones and risk background). The great majority of anxiety comes from one word on the structural exam — "nodule," "lump," "calcification" — yet the first triage lever of judgment is always **morphological features**, not functional indicators. This is isomorphic with the thyroid canon: stratify first, decide second [FACT · consensus].

### 1.2 The menstrual cycle is the first background variable

In reproductive-age women breast tissue rises and falls with the cycle: **in the 1–2 weeks before the period, swelling, tenderness and graininess increase and palpation gets "worse"; after the period it softens and "clears" again** — this is normal physiology under hormonal tide, not disease [FACT · textbook-grade]. Therefore:

- **The best window for palpation and follow-up is about one week after the period ends**, when the background is "cleanest" [MODEL · textbook direction-grade];
- Many "new lumps" first felt just before the period shrink after the period, or even disappear — but **whether they disappeared is judged by the same doctor comparing images, not by self-reassurance** [MODEL].

*falsifier: if cycle correlation with swelling/texture change is lost in objective records (symptom diary + imaging), this section is revised.*

### 1.3 The benign–malignant spectrum on one chart

```
  normal glands ─→ non-proliferative change ─→ usual hyperplasia ─→ atypical hyperplasia ─→ carcinoma in situ ─→ invasive cancer
  (the vast majority of people stop on the left half; the Hartmann 9,087-woman cohort: non-proliferative lesions are 67%
    of benign biopsies, overall RR 1.56, atypical hyperplasia RR 4.24 — risk is a "staircase," not a "cliff")
  PMID 16034008 · NEJM 2005 · EPMC abstract verified [FACT]
```

Common benign members (textbook spectrum · direction-grade [MODEL]):

| Member | Typical portrait | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Fibroadenoma | Common in young women · well-circumscribed · freely mobile · a small bead that slips under the finger | Most watched with follow-up · surgery in a tiny minority |
| Simple cyst | A "blister of water" in the gland · ultrasound settles it in one shot | Aspirated only if symptomatic · otherwise observed |
| Intraductal papilloma | Often arrives as single-duct discharge | Needs evaluation (see the red-flag face) |
| Fat necrosis / scar | Appears after trauma or surgery | Imaging + biopsy if needed to settle it |

### 1.4 Discussion card for your doctor (basics face)

- "Which of the categories above does what I felt / what's on my report belong to?"
- "For my age band, what is the ranked likelihood of each possibility?"
- "Which week of my menstrual cycle should the next follow-up land in?"

## Face 2. The screening face: when to check, what to check, and what it costs

### 2.1 First, fix the language: screening ≠ diagnosis

Screening is systematic testing of **people without symptoms**; a positive screen is only "the ticket into the diagnostic program," and the verdict comes from diagnostic imaging ± biopsy. Sensitivity = the share of the sick who are caught; specificity = the share of the healthy who are let go; PPV moves with prevalence — **the same report means different things in populations with different risk** [FACT · interlink with chk:screen-not-diagnose].

### 2.2 Age stratification: the evidence moves, and bodies disagree (shown as it is)

Take the US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) as an example — you can watch the evidence evolve:

| Version | Recommendation (average-risk women) | Epistemic state |
|---|---|---|
| USPSTF 2016 (PMID 26757170 · abstract verified) | Mammography every 2 years for ages 50–74 (B); individualized decision for 40–49 (C); insufficient evidence at ≥75 (I); insufficient evidence for DBT (I) | FACT |
| USPSTF 2024 (PMID 38687503 · abstract verified) | Mammography every 2 years for ages 40–74 (B · moderate certainty); starting age lowered from 50 to 40 | FACT |

Other bodies (the American Cancer Society among them) differ from USPSTF on starting age and frequency (earlier starts / annual schemes exist) [MODEL · this round did not verify the current details of those bodies word-for-word; the bodies' current versions prevail]. China has a national screening guideline (T/CPMA 014-2020, published in Chinese Journal of Oncology 2021, PMID 33472314 · bibliographic record verified; the specific age-band parameters were not verified word-for-word [MODEL]), plus a systematic review of Chinese screening practice (PMID 38321439 · bibliographic record verified, abstract fetch unsuccessful [MODEL]).

**Three invariants** (steadier than any single number) [INFERENCE · cross-guideline synthesis]:

1. **Ages 40–74 are the common coverage band of all mainstream schemes**; the disagreements concentrate on "whether to start at 40–49" and on frequency;
2. **Average-risk people under 40 do not get population-wide mammography screening** — baseline prevalence is low, PPV is lower, and the false-positive bill is dearer; but **symptoms go down the red-flag pathway regardless of age**;
3. **High-risk people (BRCA mutations / family history / chest radiation in youth) take a separate pathway: earlier start + tighter interval + MRI added** (see 2.6) — they are not part of the average-risk stratification table.

No "everyone should be screened" assertion is made: this canon gives no one-line conclusion of the form "everyone should start at age X," because the guideline disagreement is real — and that is exactly where the "discuss with your doctor" frame earns its keep.

### 2.3 The tool evidence ledger: who actually has mortality evidence

- **Mammography is the only screening tool with randomized-trial mortality endpoints**. The UK Independent Review (Marmot, Lancet 2012 · PMID 23117178 · abstract verified): meta-analysis of 11 RCTs, relative risk of breast-cancer death in the invited arm **0.80 (95% CI 0.73–0.89) = a relative 20% reduction** [FACT].
- **But the same evidence base is not unanimous**: the Canadian CNBSS 25-year follow-up (BMJ 2014 · PMID 24519768 · abstract verified) found that once adjuvant therapy was widespread, breast-cancer mortality was **HR 0.99 (0.88–1.12)** in the screened arm versus the control arm — no added benefit from screening. Both results are true: **the era and the level of treatment change screening's marginal value** [FACT + INFERENCE · the two trials differ in population/era/control design; that the difference in conclusions relates to the spread of adjuvant therapy is one mainstream explanation · direction-grade].
- **Ultrasound**: in Chinese and Asian practice it is often combined with mammography; it is a supplementary tool for dense breasts (see 2.5). As a standalone screening tool, RCT evidence that it reduces mortality is insufficient [MODEL].
- **MRI**: the mainstay of surveillance in high-risk people. In a 236-carrier case-control study of BRCA1/2 mutation carriers (JAMA 2004 · PMID 15367553 · abstract verified): MRI sensitivity **77%** vs mammography **36%**, ultrasound **33%**, clinical palpation **9.1%**; all four combined 95% [FACT].
- **Digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT)**: combined with digital mammography it drops the recall rate from 107 to 91 per 1,000 and raises detection (JAMA 2014 · PMID 25058084 · abstract verified, more than 450,000 screening rounds) [FACT · the mortality endpoint still awaits more research — the 2016 USPSTF had rated it evidence grade I].

### 2.4 The two-sided ledger: benefits and costs booked together (interlink with can:overdiagnosis-cost)

**Benefit side** [FACT · the three paragraphs above]: relative death risk down about 20% (RCT meta-analysis gauge); the absolute amount varies with baseline risk [INFERENCE].

**Cost side** (every line verified directly):

1. **False positives and recall anxiety**: the chain recall → extra views/ultrasound → biopsy carries marked psychological consequences (PMID 11873891 · see Face 5) [FACT];
2. **Overdiagnosis** (detecting indolent lesions that would never have surfaced in a lifetime): the Marmot panel estimated **19% (15–23) of screen-detected cancers** (long-run gauge 11%) [PMID 23117178 · FACT]; a contemporary US cohort estimated **15.4% (9.4–26.5) ≈ 1 in every 7 screen-detected cases** (Ann Intern Med 2022 · PMID 35226520 · abstract verified) [FACT]; the CNBSS gauge **22%** (1 case per 424 women screened) [PMID 24519768 · FACT]. Three numbers, three methods, one direction [INFERENCE: overdiagnosis is real, and its magnitude has an interval];
3. **The radiation ledger** (both sides written out): modeling estimates that annual screening from 40–74 induces about **125** radiation-associated breast cancers and **16** deaths per 100,000 women — while earlier detection avoids about **968** deaths in the same period (Ann Intern Med 2016 · PMID 26756460 · abstract verified) [FACT]. Radiation is not zero, but framed against the benefit it is no reason to "not dare to check" (see falsification face, item 4).

### 2.5 Dense breasts: why "a normal mammogram" is not a talisman against death

- Women with glandular density ≥75% versus <10%: breast-cancer risk **OR 4.7 (3.0–7.4)**; cancer detected within 12 months of a negative mammogram (interval cancer) **OR 17.8 (4.8–65.9)** — high density both raises risk and masks lesions (NEJM 2007 · PMID 17229950 · abstract verified) [FACT].
- Mechanistically kin: the Berg prospective series, 111 cases (Radiology 2004 · PMID 15486214 · abstract verified): mammography sensitivity falls from **100%** in fatty breasts to **45%** in extremely dense breasts; within non-fatty breasts ultrasound and MRI are more sensitive but carry more false positives [FACT].
- Asian women run a higher density share overall [MODEL · direction-grade · interlink with fem:breast-density-face] — which explains ultrasound's high standing in domestic practice; "who gets supplemental ultrasound/MRI" is a stratification question: mortality evidence for supplemental screening in average-risk dense breasts is not yet sufficient [MODEL]; the high-risk take the separate 2.6 pathway [FACT · framework].

### 2.6 The high-risk pathway (framework; no prescriptions)

Known BRCA1/2 mutations, strong family history, chest-wall radiation in youth and the like → genetic counseling for assessment; surveillance generally means "earlier start + annual mammography ± MRI ± ultrasound" [MODEL · framework-grade; specific starting ages and combinations defer to the specialist and current guidelines]. Working out whether you yourself are high-risk deserves attention more than arguing about the average population's starting age [INFERENCE].

### 2.7 Discussion card for your doctor (screening face)

- "Given my age + family history + density, roughly which band is my risk in?"
- "Should I screen every how many years? Starting from what age? Why these numbers and not others?"
- "What is my breast density? Should its effect on report confidence go into my record?"
- "If this round's result is abnormal, what is the next link in the test chain? At each step, how large are the false-positive / overdiagnosis costs?"
- "Which personal risk-raising factors of mine (radiation history / family history / genes) should be put on file?"

## Face 3. The nodule-and-report face: what the words on the report actually say

### 3.1 Lesson one: most of what you feel, or what is found, is benign

Better imaging makes more and more "somethings" visible — isomorphic with the surge in thyroid nodule detection (thyroid volume: palpation 2–6% vs high-frequency ultrasound 19–67% · interlink with thy:nodule-detection-bias) [FACT · same-class mechanism]. On the breast side: lesions classified "probably benign" had malignancy probabilities of **0.5%** (Sickles, 3,184-case prospective · Radiology 1991 · PMID 2014293) and **1.49%** (a single-center BI-RADS 3 ultrasound cross-section · PMID 40008467) on the two directly verified data points [FACT · within each cohort]. New lesions after menopause, hard, fixed, and progressively enlarging carry rising weight — so "most are benign" does not mean "rule yourself benign" [MODEL].

### 3.2 The report vocabulary, translated into plain words [MODEL · communication language]

| Report word | Plain words | Panic calibration |
|---|---|---|
| Hypoechoic nodule | A darker patch than its surroundings on ultrasound — only a statement that it "exists" | Judge together with morphology |
| Well-circumscribed / regular shape | Key words of the benign portrait | Minus (panic) point |
| Ill-defined / irregular shape / taller-than-wide | A portrait that needs a closer look | Plus point · but not a verdict |
| Microcalcifications / clustered calcifications | Salt-grain calcium deposits — mammography's home turf | Judge morphology + distribution jointly |
| Blood-flow signal | A blood-supply description · alone it makes no verdict | Don't read it alone |
| Axillary lymph-node morphology | Whether there is cortical thickening / structural abnormality | Red-flag interlock item |

### 3.3 BI-RADS: a language for communication, not a verdict

BI-RADS 0–6 is a **communication grading** between radiology and the clinic. Understanding it is for not panicking, not for diagnosing yourself [FACT · framework] (interlink with chk:nodule-breast-birads):

| Level | Meaning (direction-grade) | Usual destination |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | Negative / benign (e.g., simple cyst) | Routine screening rhythm |
| 3 | Probably benign (framework threshold of malignancy probability <2% · observed 0.5–1.5%) | **Short-interval follow-up (e.g., 6 months)** — follow-up is a formal decision, not a brush-off |
| 4a/4b/4c | Low to moderate suspicion, probability rising in steps | Biopsy to settle it |
| 5 | Highly suspicious | Biopsy · prepare along the malignant pathway |
| 6 | Already biopsy-proven | Treatment pathway |

The key plain words: **"Category 3 = recheck in six months" is not the doctor kicking the can — it is the internationally shared triage strategy** — among Sickles' 3,184 cases, all 17 later-proven cancers were stage 0/I, and all surfaced first through imaging change during follow-up (PMID 2014293) [FACT · this is precisely how the safety net works]. The specific level assigned defers to your radiologist; different machines/doctors can drift by half a level [MODEL].

### 3.4 Triple assessment: how a lump reaches a verdict

The assessment framework for a palpable lump = **clinical palpation + imaging + needle biopsy pathology**, three lines of evidence cross-checking each other (the "triple assessment" · textbook framework [MODEL]). The directional iron rule [FACT · framework]: **imaging establishes character first; anti-inflammatory drugs or watchful waiting come after**; a new non-inflammatory lump is not self-treated with "anti-inflammatories and observation" (interlink with fem:breast-lump-path).

### 3.5 The long-run ledger after a benign diagnosis

A benign biopsy is not "a needle taken for nothing": the Mayo 9,087-woman cohort (PMID 16034008) gives the staircase — non-proliferative lesions (67% of the total) carry roughly mildly elevated risk; usual hyperplasia higher; **atypical hyperplasia RR 4.24**, sustained for ≥25 years [FACT]. The reading [INFERENCE]: a benign diagnosis = entering the "staircase follow-up" roster, not entering a countdown; even at RR 4.24, the absolute annual risk is still a small number (baseline × 4 ≠ doom) [MODEL · individualized conversion needed].

### 3.6 Discussion card for your doctor (nodule face)

- "What is my category? What interval of malignancy probability does it correspond to?"
- "Why not biopsy now / why biopsy right now?" (you should be able to hear the reason in both directions)
- "How long is the follow-up interval? What does follow-up compare against?"
- "Which type does my benign pathology report name (non-proliferative / proliferative / atypical)? What does that mean for my follow-up strategy?"

## Face 4. The red-flag face: when waiting is not an option

> Why the red-flag face exists: so that rare but lethal portraits are **recognized**, not so that everyone panics over common benign portraits. IBC (inflammatory breast cancer) is rare — the epidemiologic-review gauge is "a rare and virulent" breast-cancer type (PMID 16735783 · bibliographic record + abstract verified) [FACT · the specific share figures differ across sources; no single-point figure is given here].

### 4.1 Inflammatory breast cancer (IBC) alert [dedicated section · must-read]

The portrait [FACT · PMID 16735783: diffuse edema (peau d'orange) plus redness are its characteristic presentation, fast progression, poor prognosis]:

- **Rapid breast enlargement, redness, warmth, swelling, peau-d'orange skin changes**, often **without a clearly palpable lump**;
- Progression is counted in **weeks** (several weeks to several months), not a slow drag of many months;
- **The most dangerous misunderstanding = treating it as ordinary mastitis with antibiotics alone**: if redness and swelling do not recede after anti-infective treatment, or keep worsening → **you must return for escalated evaluation** (imaging ± biopsy, including skin biopsy) [MODEL · clinical-pathway direction-grade];
- Why it cannot be dragged: IBC's biological behavior is virulent, and the diagnostic pathway itself (clinical + biopsy confirmation) depends on someone "thinking of it" — delay = staging cost [FACT · the poor-prognosis direction + MODEL · the pathway].

*falsifier: if population data show that delay in imaging/biopsy evaluation of "red-swollen" presentations is unrelated to stage, this escalation-pathway statement is revised.*

### 4.2 Nipple discharge triage

Discharge itself is common (2–5% of women's care visits) and is one of breast cancer's first symptoms (5–12% of cases), but **most causes are benign** — the goal of evaluation is to separate pathological from physiological (Am J Med 2015 review · PMID 25447625 · abstract verified) [FACT].

Directional portrait [MODEL · textbook gauge]:

- **Favoring physiological / multifocal**: bilateral, multi-duct, only on pressure, clear or milky, squeeze-related;
- **Favoring pathological**: **one side, one duct, spontaneous (flows by itself, no squeezing), bloody or serosanguineous**, especially persistently from one duct opening → get duct-directed evaluation;
- A small nodule palpable near the areola edge with discharge → consider the intraductal papilloma spectrum [MODEL];
- **Bloody ≠ cancer**: most bloody discharge still has a benign cause (duct ectasia / papilloma) — but it is a "must be evaluated" portrait, not a "must panic" portrait [MODEL + FACT (most causes benign)].

### 4.3 The physical-sign red-flag list (the care-seeking verb = promptly, not ER panic)

[MODEL · clinical direction-grade]

- A newly appearing lump that is **hard, fixed, ill-defined** (especially new after menopause);
- **Skin dimpling / tethering**, **new nipple retraction or a shift in its direction**;
- **Eczema-like changes of nipple and areola that do not heal with treatment** (Paget disease of the breast needs evaluation);
- **Hard axillary / supraclavicular lymph nodes**;
- Any lesion **progressively enlarging** (whatever the previous conclusion was).

### 4.4 Mastitis vs IBC: a direction-of-difference table

| Dimension | Lactation mastitis [MODEL · direction-grade] | Suspected IBC [FACT · portrait + MODEL · pathway] |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Lactating / history of milk stasis | Any age · usually not lactating |
| Extent | Focal, may have tender induration | Diffuse red-swollen-hot, fast progression |
| Systemic | May have fever / marked pain | Systemic toxic signs often absent |
| Response to treatment | Improves with standard care | **Standard anti-infective treatment fails = escalate immediately** |

Interlink cue with the infection volume (inf): the standard management of lactation mastitis and lactation support live in the lactation-specific material; this canon carries only one line — "redness and swelling you cannot tell apart = do not wait indefinitely inside antibiotics" [FACT · pathway-grade].

### 4.5 Discussion card for your doctor (red-flag face)

- "For my presentation, is IBC / pathological discharge on the differential list? Why is / why isn't it?"
- "For anti-infective treatment, how many days do we set as the limit? If it hasn't improved by then, which escalation pathway do we take?"
- "If a biopsy is done, which layer of evidence is sampled (image-guided core / skin / ductoscopy)?"

## Face 5. The anxiety face: the waiting, and life after a "benign verdict"

### 5.1 A recall is not a verdict, but the anxiety is real

A follow-up cohort study (J Public Health Med 2001 · PMID 11873891 · abstract verified): women ultimately "cleared" after recall had significantly **worse** psychological outcomes at 1, 5 and 11 months after evaluation than those released at first screen, and an echo was still measurable 3 years later, before the next screen [FACT]. This is not fragility — it is one of the real price tags of this screening tool: **anxiety is a legitimate cost item already entered in the "screening bill"** (interlink with can:overdiagnosis-cost), not your personal failure [INFERENCE].

### 5.2 When anxiety is normal, and when it needs care

Anxiety's function = pinning attention on a threat; its pathological boundary = **intensity/duration mismatched to the threat, impossible to switch off, avoidance starting to impair function** (interlink with mood:anxiety_function) [MODEL]. Translated to the breast scenario:

- A week of poor sleep and looping thoughts while waiting for results = the normal band [MODEL];
- Three months on, still unable to open checkup text messages, avoiding follow-up, self-palpating on repeat into insomnia = a psychological event that needs care — take the mood-volume pathway [MODEL];
- The fear-avoidance loop "catastrophize → avoid checking → more anxious" holds in the breast scenario too (isomorphic interlink with pain:fear_avoidance, the chronic-pain loop) [MODEL · analogy transfer labeled].

### 5.3 The waiting-period protocol (an actionable seven days)

[MODEL · psychological-support framework]

1. **Write the questions down** and bring them into the clinic (see each face's discussion card) — turn rumination into a list;
2. **Do not consult the internet alone**: symptom searching delivers you with precision to the worst-outcome page [INFERENCE · the reverse-survivorship mechanism];
3. **No monthly self-palpation ritual** (see Face 6) — high-frequency self-checks during the waiting period only amplify noise;
4. **Book people, book things**: calendar density is the cheap lever against uncertainty [MODEL];
5. When the result comes, whatever it is, **ask the next action first**, the meaning second — action-anxiety weighs less than meaning-anxiety [MODEL · psychology direction-grade].

### 5.4 Discussion card for your doctor (anxiety face)

- "If the result is benign, how long is my psychological burden within the normal range? When should I seek help?"
- "Are there patient-support resources / a follow-up coordinator I can be given?"
- "Before the next follow-up, what changes would warrant coming early? Everything else, I live normally."

## Face 6. The living face: what the evidence says you can do

### 6.1 Awareness, not a self-exam ritual

The Shanghai randomized trial of 266,000 women (JNCI 2002 · PMID 12359854 · abstract verified; Chinese-language report PMID 16117898 · bibliographic record verified): **intensive monthly self-exam instruction did not reduce breast-cancer mortality**; the self-exam arm instead found more benign lesions and underwent more benign biopsies [FACT] (interlink with fem:bse-vs-imaging). The right posture [MODEL · consensus direction]:

- **Know your own normal**: know the normal texture and symmetry of your post-period week;
- **Change means care-seeking**: only new, persistent, one-sided, progressive change escalates;
- Awareness is **a tool that lowers the threshold for seeing a doctor**, not a substitute for screening tools.

> Falsifier feedback (2026-09-13  · machine CANDIDATE): the falsifier-week organ's counter-evidence search (Europe PMC · mode=affirm: this claim is a negative claim, so counter-evidence = positive-benefit evidence) signaled 3 hits (representative PMID 41885785 · 41610119 · 40773464), all with signal phrases of the "educational intervention improves self-exam practice/awareness," "statistically significant" kind. Before human review this does not change the epistemic state: the hits above are all "education → behavior/awareness" endpoints; no benefit evidence with a "self-exam instruction → mortality" endpoint was seen, so they do not constitute same-endpoint counter-evidence to this section's mortality conclusion. ### 6.2 The lifestyle direction ledger (direction-grade throughout · no prescription doses)

[MODEL · epidemiologic direction-grade summary; individual decisions in person]

- **Alcohol**: intake is positively associated with risk; direction = less is better;
- **Weight and exercise**: postmenopausal overweight is associated with rising risk; regular physical activity's direction = protective;
- **Lactation**: cumulative breastfeeding duration's direction = protective (while carrying the standard-care needs of lactation mastitis, see 4.4);
- **Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT)**: excess risk grows with duration of use and falls after stopping (interlink with fem:mht-breast-excess [FACT]); whether to use it is an individual decision of symptom burden × baseline risk (interlinks with fem:mht-decision-window and fem:menopause-root) — **this canon writes no hormone prescriptions of any kind**;
- **Follow-up adherence**: write the agreed recheck dates into the calendar — the single most controllable thing an ordinary person holds over their own risk [INFERENCE].

### 6.3 What not to do (interlocks with the falsification face)

- **No massaging to "dissolve the lump"**: no clinical evidence base [UNKNOWN · qualified evidence body lacking]; applying external force to a breast with suspected IBC/inflammatory mass points the direction = clearly wrong [MODEL · delayed-diagnosis risk]; delaying care because the massage's "it worked" bodily soothing reassures you = negative value [INFERENCE];
- No squeezing "nodules," no hot-compress improvisation on lumps, no faith in "nodule-dissolving" tea folk remedies [MODEL · as above];
- No self-diagnosis from photos/AI [MODEL · unvalidated];
- No copying someone else's plan onto yourself (density, category, pathology type differ; pathways differ) [FACT · framework].

### 6.4 The pain ledger

Cyclical pre-period swelling = the physiological face of hormonal rhythm (1.2). Among people with isolated pain as the sole complaint and normal checkups, mammography's malignancy detection rate is about **0.6%** — the same order as the baseline screening population (Clin Imaging 2026 · 5 years, 6,670 cases · PMID 42664924 · abstract verified) [FACT]. Direction [MODEL]: exclude first + reassure, rather than repeated imaging "in search of total reassurance"; a supportive bra, charting pain–cycle correlation, and in-person visits for analgesia as needed are a reasonable order [MODEL · no prescriptions].

### 6.5 Discussion card for your doctor (living face)

- "Given my age and family history, which one or two lifestyle changes are worth the most for me?"
- "I am taking / considering hormone-type drugs (contraception / MHT / menopause plans) — how does that affect my breast follow-up rhythm?"
- "What screening lead time do the women in my family need?"
